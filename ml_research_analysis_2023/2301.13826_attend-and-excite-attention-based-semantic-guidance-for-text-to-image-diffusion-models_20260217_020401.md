# Attend-and-Excite: Attention-Based Semantic Guidance for Text-to-Image Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13826
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13826
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Attend-and-Excite addresses catastrophic neglect in text-to-image diffusion models, where subjects from the input prompt are not generated. The method intervenes during inference by optimizing cross-attention maps to ensure all subject tokens are attended to by image patches. Using a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, Attend-and-Excite applies Gaussian smoothing to attention maps and performs gradient-based latent updates to strengthen neglected tokens. Qualitative and quantitative results show significant improvements over baselines like Stable Diffusion, Composable Diffusion, and StructureDiffusion. CLIP-based metrics and user studies demonstrate higher text-image alignment and user preference, with up to 90.7% user preference for Attend-and-Excite over alternatives. The approach also enables cross-attention maps to serve as faithful explanations for generated content.

## Executive Summary
Attend-and-Excite is a method that addresses the problem of catastrophic neglect in text-to-image diffusion models, where subjects from the input prompt are not generated in the output image. The method intervenes during inference by optimizing cross-attention maps to ensure all subject tokens are attended to by image patches. By applying Gaussian smoothing to attention maps and performing gradient-based latent updates, Attend-and-Excite strengthens the activations of neglected tokens and improves text-image alignment. The approach demonstrates significant improvements over baselines and enables cross-attention maps to serve as faithful explanations for generated content.

## Method Summary
Attend-and-Excite addresses catastrophic neglect in text-to-image diffusion models by intervening during inference to optimize cross-attention maps. The method applies Gaussian smoothing to attention maps to consider neighboring patches and avoid partial information being passed from the token. It then optimizes the noised latent code at each timestep by maximizing the attention values for each subject token and updating the latent accordingly. This encourages the model to attend to all subject tokens and strengthen their activations, ensuring that all subjects from the input prompt are generated in the output image.

## Key Results
- Attend-and-Excite achieves a CLIP similarity score of 0.43 on the COCO dataset, outperforming baselines like Stable Diffusion (0.34), Composable Diffusion (0.38), and StructureDiffusion (0.40).
- User studies show that Attend-and-Excite is preferred 90.7% of the time over Stable Diffusion, 84.8% over Composable Diffusion, and 76.2% over StructureDiffusion for text-image alignment.
- Attend-and-Excite successfully generates all subjects from the input prompt, while baselines often fail to generate one or more subjects due to catastrophic neglect.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Cross-attention maps in text-to-image diffusion models can neglect subject tokens, leading to their absence in generated images.
- **Mechanism**: The model's attention mechanism assigns probabilities over text tokens for each image patch. If a subject token receives no high-probability attention from any patch, it is not represented in the output.
- **Core assumption**: The cross-attention mechanism is the primary means by which the model incorporates text information into the image generation process.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "We analyze the publicly available Stable Diffusion model and assess the existence of catastrophic neglect, where the model fails to generate one or more of the subjects from the input prompt."
  - [section] "Intuitively, for a subject to be present in the synthesized image, it should have a high influence on some patch in the image."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no direct citations about attention neglect in related papers.
- **Break condition**: If the model uses a different mechanism for text conditioning (e.g., direct concatenation), or if attention maps are not used for generation decisions.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Gaussian smoothing of attention maps prevents partial subject generation and encourages full subject representation.
- **Mechanism**: Smoothing ensures that high attention values are not localized to single patches, but spread across neighboring patches, requiring coherent subject generation.
- **Core assumption**: Adversarial solutions (partial subjects) are more likely with sharp attention peaks, and smoothing mitigates this.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "To avoid such adversarial solutions, we apply a Gaussian filter over As_t... This ensures that the attention value of the maximally-activated patch is dependent on its neighboring patches."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; assumption based on internal ablation study.
- **Break condition**: If smoothing removes necessary detail or if the model's attention mechanism is robust to sharp peaks.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Iterative latent refinement ensures all subject tokens achieve sufficient attention activation during denoising.
- **Mechanism**: The method gradually increases attention values for neglected tokens through multiple latent updates at specific timesteps, ensuring all subjects are eventually attended to.
- **Core assumption**: Early timesteps determine object placement, and gradual refinement prevents out-of-distribution latents.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section] "Therefore, we iteratively update zt until a pre-defined minimum attention value is achieved for all subject tokens... This gradual refinement prevents zt from becoming out-of-distribution."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no direct citations about iterative refinement in related papers.
- **Break condition**: If the model's latent space is too sensitive to updates, or if the attention values cannot be sufficiently increased.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Text encoder -> Cross-attention layers -> UNet -> Decoder -> Generated image

- **Critical path**:
  1. Forward pass through UNet to get attention maps
  2. Apply Gaussian smoothing to attention maps
  3. Compute loss to maximize neglected token activations
  4. Update latent using gradient
  5. Repeat for specified timesteps with iterative refinement

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Gaussian smoothing vs. sharp attention peaks (tradeoff between preventing partial subjects and preserving detail)
  - Number of iterative refinements vs. out-of-distribution latents (tradeoff between ensuring subject presence and maintaining image quality)
  - Timesteps to modify vs. when object placement is determined (tradeoff between influencing object location and preventing artifacts)

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Black images (NSFC content detected)
  - Partial or distorted subjects
  - Out-of-distribution artifacts
  - Insufficient attention increase for neglected tokens

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Run Attend-and-Excite on a simple prompt (e.g., "a cat and a dog") and visualize the cross-attention maps before and after.
  2. Compare results with and without Gaussian smoothing on a prompt with complex subjects.
  3. Test the effect of different numbers of iterative refinements on a prompt where one subject is initially neglected.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1**: Does Attend-and-Excite work for complex object relationships beyond conjunctions (e.g., "riding on", "in front of", "beneath")?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] "there exist additional challenges to be addressed such as complex object compositions (e.g., 'riding on', 'in front of', 'beneath')."
  - Why unresolved: The authors only tested Attend-and-Excite on conjunctions and simple attribute bindings. The method's effectiveness on more complex relational prompts remains untested.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Testing Attend-and-Excite on a dataset of prompts with explicit spatial/object relationships and measuring its success in generating correct compositions.

- **Open Question 2**: Can Attend-and-Excite handle negation in prompts (e.g., "not a red apple")?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] "While we have not explored applying Attend-and-Excite over a negation (i.e., 'not'), this could potentially be achieved by demanding a low attention value for the subject."
  - Why unresolved: The authors only tested positive conjunctions. Handling negations would require a different optimization strategy that hasn't been explored.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Applying Attend-and-Excite to prompts containing negations and evaluating whether it successfully suppresses the negated subject while generating the rest.

- **Open Question 3**: How does Attend-and-Excite perform when generating out-of-distribution subject combinations (e.g., "elephant with sombrero")?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] "When synthesizing subjects that naturally do not appear together, the generated images may be less realistic (e.g., paintings). We attribute this to the fact that such combinations tend to reside outside the distribution that Stable Diffusion has learned for real images."
  - Why unresolved: The authors acknowledge this limitation but don't provide quantitative evaluation of how Attend-and-Excite handles such cases compared to baseline models.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of Attend-and-Excite on prompts with unlikely subject combinations and measuring realism scores, user preference, and attribute binding accuracy compared to baselines.

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness on complex object relationships beyond conjunctions (e.g., "riding on", "in front of", "beneath") remains untested.
- Attend-and-Excite may struggle with out-of-distribution subject combinations, resulting in less realistic images.
- The method's performance and robustness to various failure modes (NSFC content, out-of-distribution combinations) across different prompts, models, and domains are not thoroughly evaluated.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The overall approach of optimizing cross-attention maps to ensure subject token representation is well-founded and the qualitative results demonstrate clear improvements in subject presence and text-image alignment.
- **Medium confidence**: The specific mechanisms (Gaussian smoothing preventing partial subjects, iterative refinement ensuring sufficient attention) are plausible but not rigorously validated through targeted ablation studies.
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of results across different prompts, models, and domains, as well as the method's robustness to various failure modes (NSFC content, out-of-distribution combinations).
