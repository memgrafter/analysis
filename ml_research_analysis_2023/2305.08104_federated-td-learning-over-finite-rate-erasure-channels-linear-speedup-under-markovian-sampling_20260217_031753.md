# Federated TD Learning over Finite-Rate Erasure Channels: Linear Speedup under Markovian Sampling
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08104
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08104
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First non-asymptotic analysis of quantization and erasures in federated RL with linear speedup

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses federated policy evaluation with linear function approximation under communication constraints modeled by finite-capacity channels with Bernoulli erasures. The proposed QFedTD algorithm enables agents to exchange quantized TD update directions over unreliable links, capturing both limited bandwidth and packet loss. Under Markovian sampling, the authors provide a finite-sample analysis showing that QFedTD achieves a linear speedup in sample complexity relative to the single-agent case, with convergence rate depending on the erasure probability and quantization distortion.

## Method Summary
QFedTD is a federated temporal difference learning algorithm that incorporates quantization and handles erasure channels. Agents compute local TD updates and quantize them before transmission. The quantization noise is modeled as unbiased with bounded variance. The algorithm does not require projection steps and handles three sources of randomness: Markov samples, quantization, and erasures. The analysis provides finite-sample bounds with improved O(τ) dependence on mixing time compared to prior O(τ²) bounds.

## Key Results
- Achieves linear speedup in sample complexity relative to single-agent case
- Improved mixing time dependence from O(τ²) to O(τ)
- First non-asymptotic analysis of quantization and erasures in federated RL
- Theoretical findings corroborated by synthetic experiments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm works by carefully bounding the error terms from Markovian sampling, quantization, and erasures simultaneously. The key insight is that under certain conditions on quantization noise and erasure probabilities, the federated aggregation can still achieve linear speedup despite communication constraints. The Markovian sampling structure is leveraged to provide tighter bounds on the convergence rate.

## Limitations
- Assumes unbiased quantization noise with bounded variance
- Stringent assumptions on independence and boundedness of error sources
- Synthetic experiments lack real-world validation
- Analysis limited to linear function approximation

## Confidence
- Convergence rate analysis under Markovian sampling: High
- Handling of quantization and erasure errors: Medium
- Practical applicability and generalization: Low
