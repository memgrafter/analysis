# Naver Labs Europe (SPLADE) @ TREC Deep Learning 2022

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12574
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12574
- Reference count: 1
- Naver Labs Europe used SPLADE models with ensembling and re-ranking for TREC Deep Learning 2022, submitting 32 runs

## Executive Summary
Naver Labs Europe's TREC Deep Learning 2022 submissions built on their 2021 strategy, employing SPLADE-based first-stage retrieval enhanced with ColBERTv2 and DocT5 ensembles, followed by multi-stage re-ranking with hard negatives. A notable addition was Rocchio-based first-stage ranking and a MonoT5 re-ranker initialized from T0pp. The team submitted 32 runs across passage and document tasks, maintaining competitive performance but observing an increased gap between first-stage and re-ranked results compared to previous years. The analysis revealed inconsistencies in ensembling effectiveness and challenges with specific query types, raising questions about the cost-benefit trade-off of their multi-stage approach.

## Method Summary
The Naver Labs Europe team employed a multi-stage retrieval and ranking approach for TREC Deep Learning 2022. First-stage retrieval combined SPLADE with ensemble methods including ColBERTv2 and DocT5, with Rocchio-based ranking applied to improve initial results. Second-stage re-ranking used ensembles of re-rankers trained with hard negatives from SPLADE, including a MonoT5 model initialized from T0pp. The system processed queries through multiple ranking stages, with final results submitted across both passage and document tasks. This approach maintained competitive performance while revealing challenges in ensemble consistency and increasing cost-benefit gaps between first-stage and re-ranked results.

## Key Results
- The strategy maintained competitive performance but showed an increased gap between first-stage and re-ranked runs compared to previous years
- While improvements over first-stage models were evident, the cost-benefit of re-ranking remains a concern
- Analysis revealed inconsistencies in ensembling and challenges in handling certain query types, indicating room for more intelligent ensemble strategies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The multi-stage approach works by leveraging complementary strengths of different retrieval models - SPLADE provides efficient sparse representations, while ColBERTv2 and DocT5 capture different semantic aspects. The use of hard negatives in training re-rankers improves their ability to distinguish between relevant and highly relevant documents. Rocchio-based first-stage ranking enhances initial retrieval quality by expanding queries with relevant terms. The ensemble approach aggregates multiple model predictions to achieve more robust and comprehensive ranking, though this comes at increased computational cost.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
SPLADE -> ColBERTv2/DocT5 ensemble -> Rocchio ranking -> SPLADE hard negative re-ranking -> MonoT5 re-ranking -> final results

**Critical Path**
The critical path follows: query input → first-stage retrieval (SPLADE + ensemble) → Rocchio expansion → second-stage re-ranking (SPLADE hard negatives) → final MonoT5 re-ranking → output results

**Design Tradeoffs**
The primary tradeoff involves computational cost versus ranking effectiveness. Multi-stage re-ranking significantly improves results but at the expense of increased processing time and resources. The ensemble approach provides robustness but introduces complexity in model coordination and weight optimization. Rocchio-based expansion improves recall but may introduce noise for sparse-label queries.

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor performance on queries with sparse labels where Rocchio expansion adds noise
- Inconsistent ensemble behavior where first-stage models outperform re-rankers on specific queries
- Degradation of SPLADE EFFICIENT V performance on TREC datasets compared to MS MARCO
- Increased computational cost without proportional gains in effectiveness for certain query types

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do efficient SPLADE models (e.g., SPLADE EFFICIENT V) perform well on MS MARCO but struggle significantly on TREC datasets despite using the same architecture?
- Basis in paper: Point 5 in analysis section notes this discrepancy and states "We are still not sure of the reason for this decrease in performance"
- Why unresolved: The paper explicitly acknowledges uncertainty about the cause of this performance gap between datasets
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of query characteristics, relevance distributions, and evaluation metrics between MS MARCO and TREC datasets to identify systematic differences

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the inconsistency in ensembling performance be addressed, particularly for queries like 2002533 where first-stage models outperformed rerankers despite overall improvements?
- Basis in paper: Point 5 in TREC DL 2022 analysis section notes "There seems to be a lack of consistency on our ensembling process" and identifies specific query-level inconsistencies
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but doesn't propose or test solutions for more intelligent ensemble strategies
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of query-adaptive ensembling methods that could automatically select or weight models based on query characteristics

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between first-stage retrieval quality and reranking effectiveness, given the increasing cost-benefit gap observed?
- Basis in paper: Point 1 in TREC DL 2022 analysis states "The gap between the first stage and reranked runs increased compared to previous years" and point 2 notes the cost concern
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the growing gap but doesn't propose solutions or determine optimal stopping points
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies measuring the marginal returns of reranking across different first-stage quality levels and cost constraints

### Open Question 4
- Question: Would applying Rocchio consistently across all query types improve overall performance, or are there specific query characteristics that determine its effectiveness?
- Basis in paper: Point 4 in TREC DL 2022 analysis confirms Rocchio improves results from SPLADE, but analysis section point 1 notes it helps "when labels are not sparse (i.e. outside of MSMARCO v1 dev)"
- Why unresolved: The paper shows Rocchio helps but doesn't analyze when it helps most or if it could be selectively applied
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic analysis of query features (length, complexity, label density) to predict Rocchio effectiveness and develop selective application strategies

## Limitations

- Lack of clarity on the effectiveness of Rocchio-based first-stage ranking versus standard SPLADE retrieval
- Uncertainty about the relative contribution of each ensemble component (ColBERTv2, DocT5, and SPLADE)
