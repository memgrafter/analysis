# Dual policy as self-model for planning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.04440
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04440
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Dual policy agent uses distilled policy as self-model for planning, showing stable training and faster inference compared to shared policy agent in predator-prey environment.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates using a distilled policy network as a self-model for planning in reinforcement learning. The authors compare a shared policy agent (using model-free policy as self-model) with a dual policy agent (using separate distilled policy for planning) in a predator-prey environment. The dual policy agent demonstrates similar performance to the shared policy agent but with more stable training. The distilled policy enables faster inference during Monte Carlo Tree Search planning and captures reflexive behaviors that the model-free policy cannot simulate.

## Method Summary
The method involves training three types of agents: simple (model-free only), shared policy (model-free + world model), and dual policy (model-free + world model + distilled policy). Agents are trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for the model-free policy, mean squared error loss for the world model, and negative log-likelihood for the distilled policy. The dual policy agent uses Monte Carlo Tree Search with the distilled policy as the self-model for planning, while the shared policy agent uses its model-free policy for planning.

## Key Results
- Dual policy agent shows similar performance to shared policy agent in predator-prey environment
- Dual policy agent demonstrates more stable training across episodes
- Distilled policy enables faster inference during planning compared to using the full model-free policy
- Dual policy agent captures reflexive behaviors (e.g., fleeing when predator is too close) that the model-free policy cannot simulate

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using a distilled policy as a self-model enables faster inference during planning.
- Mechanism: The distilled policy is a separate, smaller network trained to mimic the model-free policy's behavior. Because it is smaller, each forward pass during MCTS planning requires fewer computations, reducing planning latency.
- Core assumption: The distilled policy can accurately approximate the model-free policy's action distribution while being architecturally simpler.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "distilled policy allows for faster inference during planning"
  - [section] "having multiple networks may stabilize training... the inference time for planning can be faster if distilled policy is smaller than the model-free policy"
- Break condition: If the distilled policy becomes too small to accurately capture the model-free policy's action distribution, planning accuracy degrades and the speed advantage is nullified.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The dual policy setup stabilizes training compared to using the model-free policy alone.
- Mechanism: Having separate networks for model-free actions and planning creates redundancy similar to ensemble methods or double Q-learning. This separation reduces overfitting to short-term rewards and improves generalization during planning.
- Core assumption: Separate networks reduce correlated errors and provide diverse value estimates.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "dual policy agent shows similar performance to the shared policy agent but with more stable training"
  - [section] "having multiple networks may stabilize training for planning, as previous works with multiple networks such as ensemble models... showed improved stability for model-free learning"
- Break condition: If the distilled policy is poorly trained or diverges from the model-free policy, the stability benefit disappears and may introduce additional noise.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The distilled policy captures reflexive behaviors that the model-free policy cannot simulate, enabling more accurate planning.
- Mechanism: Reflexive actions (e.g., fleeing when predator is too close) are hard-coded and not learned by the model-free policy. The distilled policy learns from past trajectories, including these reflexive behaviors, providing a more complete self-model for planning.
- Core assumption: Reflexive behaviors are part of the agent's historical action distribution and can be distilled.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "dual policy agent also learns a more comprehensive understanding of its own behaviors, including reflexive actions that cannot be simulated by the model-free policy alone"
  - [section] "agents might possess parts of action policies that they cannot simulate for planning purposes, such as reflexive and other innate responses to emergency scenarios... The distilled policy learns from its past actions, thus gaining a comprehensive understanding of the agent's action policies including the reflexive behavior"
- Break condition: If reflexive behaviors are too rare or context-specific, the distilled policy may fail to learn them reliably, leading to planning errors in those states.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)
  - Why needed here: MCTS is used to explore future trajectories during planning by sampling actions from the self-model. Understanding its mechanics is critical to grasping how the distilled policy accelerates planning.
  - Quick check question: In MCTS, what is the purpose of the "selection" phase versus the "expansion" phase?

- Concept: Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE)
  - Why needed here: GAE is used to compute advantage estimates for both training the model-free policy and evaluating trajectories during MCTS planning. Knowing how it balances bias and variance is important for interpreting training stability.
  - Quick check question: How does the lambda parameter in GAE affect the bias-variance tradeoff?

- Concept: Knowledge Distillation
  - Why needed here: The distilled policy is trained via knowledge distillation from the model-free policy's action distributions and value estimates. Understanding distillation helps explain why the distilled policy can be smaller yet still effective.
  - Quick check question: In knowledge distillation, what role does the temperature parameter play in shaping the softened probability distribution?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Model-free policy network -> World model network -> Distilled policy network -> MCTS planner

- Critical path:
  1. Train model-free policy via PPO using real environment rollouts
  2. Train world model to predict next state and reward
  3. Train distilled policy via knowledge distillation and action prediction loss
  4. During planning, use MCTS with distilled policy and world model to generate action candidates
  5. Execute top action from MCTS in the real environment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Smaller distilled policy → faster inference but risk of underfitting
  - Larger distilled policy → better accuracy but slower planning
  - Separate networks → potential stability gains but increased memory/compute cost
  - MCTS depth → more thorough planning but higher computational cost

- Failure signatures:
  - Training instability: Large variance in success/death/timeout proportions across episodes
  - Planning inefficiency: Long inference times despite distilled policy
  - Poor exploration: Agent repeatedly fails in larger maps or gets stuck in local optima
  - Reflexive behavior mismatch: Agent fails to react appropriately to predator proximity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare shared policy agent vs. dual policy agent in the base predator-prey environment; measure success, death, and timeout proportions
  2. Vary distilled policy size (number of parameters) while keeping model-free policy fixed; measure planning inference time and performance
  3. Test both agents in progressively larger maps; evaluate exploration capability via success proportion

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the dual policy approach generalize to more complex and varied environments beyond the predator-prey scenario?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that their findings were validated within a single environment, which presents a limit to the generalizability of their findings. They suggest that future work could extend this investigation to a variety of environments to solidify the observed benefits and uncover any environment-specific characteristics.
- Why unresolved: The current study is limited to one specific environment, and the performance and benefits of the dual policy approach in different contexts remain untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the dual policy approach in a diverse set of environments, including those with different dynamics, action spaces, and reward structures, would provide evidence of its generalizability and robustness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the implications of using a distilled policy as a self-model in multi-agent settings, particularly in terms of 'theory of mind' and predicting other agents' behaviors?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest that beyond the context of single agent behavior, the dual policy paradigm could also be explored in multi-agent settings in terms of 'theory of mind', understanding how an agent's self-model could help it predict and adapt to the behaviors of other agents.
- Why unresolved: The current study focuses on single-agent environments, and the role of self-models in multi-agent scenarios, especially in terms of social cognition and predicting others' actions, has not been explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing the dual policy approach in multi-agent environments and analyzing its impact on agents' ability to predict and adapt to others' behaviors would provide insights into its potential for enhancing social cognition in AI systems.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the size and complexity of the distilled policy network affect its performance as a self-model, and what are the trade-offs between inference speed and planning accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors found that smaller distilled policies consistently outperform larger ones in terms of planning inference speed, suggesting that smaller networks are more efficient at distilling knowledge from the model-free policy. However, they did not observe a clear pattern correlating the size of the model-free policy with performance.
- Why unresolved: While the study shows that smaller distilled policies offer faster inference, the optimal size and complexity of the distilled network for balancing inference speed and planning accuracy remain unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with a wider range of network architectures for both the model-free and distilled policies, and analyzing the trade-offs between inference speed, planning accuracy, and overall performance, would provide insights into the optimal design of the distilled policy network.

## Limitations

- Network architecture details for model-free, distilled, and world model networks are not fully specified, making precise reproduction difficult
- Empirical validation is limited to a single predator-prey environment without ablation studies on distilled policy size or MCTS hyperparameters
- Claims about reflexive behavior capture rely on qualitative observations rather than quantitative metrics

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Faster inference from smaller distilled policy network; MCTS planning framework validity
- **Medium confidence**: Training stability improvements from dual policy setup; comprehensive behavior understanding through distillation
- **Low confidence**: Specific claims about reflexive behavior capture; generalizability beyond predator-prey environment
