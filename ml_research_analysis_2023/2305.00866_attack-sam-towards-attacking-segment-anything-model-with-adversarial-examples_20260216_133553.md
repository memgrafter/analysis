# Attack-SAM: Towards Attacking Segment Anything Model With Adversarial Examples

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00866
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00866
- Reference count: 17
- First study to investigate adversarial robustness of Segment Anything Model (SAM)

## Executive Summary
This work introduces Attack-SAM, the first comprehensive study on adversarial attacks against Segment Anything Model (SAM). The authors propose a mask removal attack using CLIP-MSE loss to push predicted mask values below a negative threshold, effectively eliminating object detections. Experiments demonstrate that SAM is highly vulnerable to white-box attacks (FGSM, PGD), achieving near-zero IoU scores, while showing partial robustness in black-box transfer settings where small objects prove more resistant.

## Method Summary
The attack formulation uses CLIP-MSE loss to encourage SAM's predicted mask values to fall below a negative threshold (Negth), removing object detections. FGSM and PGD attacks generate adversarial perturbations with magnitude 8/255 for white-box and 16/255 for black-box settings. The method evaluates mask removal success using mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) between clean and adversarial predictions. Attacks are tested with random point prompts and varying numbers of attack points (K) in "segment everything" mode.

## Key Results
- SAM achieves near-zero mIoU (<0.01) under PGD-10 white-box attacks
- Black-box transfer shows partial robustness, with small objects being more resistant
- Attack success rate increases with the number of attack points (K)
- CLIP-MSE loss effectively removes masks by pushing predictions below threshold

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SAM's mask predictions are sensitive to small perturbations in pixel values.
- Mechanism: The CLIP-MSE loss encourages pixel values in the segmentation map to drop below a negative threshold, effectively removing masks by flipping the sign of predictions.
- Core assumption: The model's decision boundary is linear enough that small perturbations can flip the sign of many pixel predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experiments show SAM is vulnerable to white-box attacks (FGSM, PGD), achieving near-zero IoU (e.g., mIoU < 0.01 with PGD-10)."
  - [section] "To reduce the randomness effect, it is desired to make the predicted values lower than a certain thresholdNegth."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: Related papers focus on attacking SAM but don't provide mechanism-level details.
- Break condition: If the model's internal activations are highly non-linear or thresholded before mask output, small perturbations may not propagate effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Black-box attacks show partial robustness due to limited perturbation transfer.
- Mechanism: Perturbations optimized on a surrogate model do not fully transfer to SAM, especially for small objects with fewer affected pixels.
- Core assumption: Transferability is inversely related to object size due to perturbation magnitude constraints.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In black-box transfer settings, SAM exhibits some robustness, with small objects being more resistant."
  - [section] "We find that those small objects tend to be more robust against adversarial attacks in the black-box setting."
  - [corpus] No direct mechanism evidence; must be inferred from results.
- Break condition: If the surrogate model is structurally similar to SAM, transferability may increase across object sizes.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Prompt transfer vulnerability decreases as attack covers more points.
- Mechanism: Attacking a single point leaves other prompt regions relatively unaffected; multi-point attacks distribute perturbation across the image, increasing overall vulnerability.
- Core assumption: SAM's mask generation for each prompt is largely independent, so attacking one does not automatically compromise others.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "When the generated perturbation mainly focuses on removing the mask around the select attack point, it can be less effective in other regions."
  - [section] "As shown in Figure 2(c) and Figure 2(d), the attack success rate increases as we increase the number of attack pointK."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No mechanism-level discussion in related papers.
- Break condition: If SAM's internal representations share features across prompts, single-point attacks could still have widespread effects.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image → SAM model → logits → mask → IoU → attack loss → gradient → perturbation
- Critical path: Image → SAM model → logits → mask → IoU → attack loss → gradient → perturbation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Threshold choice (Negth) affects attack strength vs. perceptibility
  - Perturbation magnitude (8/255 vs 16/255) trades white-box success for black-box transferability
  - Single vs multi-point attacks balance attack focus vs. coverage
- Failure signatures:
  - High IoU after attack indicates failed mask removal
  - Perceptible artifacts suggest perturbation budget exceeded
  - Inconsistent black-box transfer suggests surrogate model mismatch
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run FGSM attack with ϵ=8/255, Negth=-0.5, measure mIoU drop.
  2. Repeat with PGD-10, compare white-box vs black-box transfer.
  3. Vary K (number of attack points) and measure attack success in "segment everything" mode.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the specific mechanisms behind SAM's partial robustness to black-box attacks, particularly for small objects?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that small objects tend to be more robust against adversarial attacks in the black-box setting and notes this as an area for future investigation.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide an explanation for why small objects exhibit this robustness, only noting it as an observation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments analyzing the perturbation magnitudes on small versus large objects, and studies on how object size affects the effectiveness of adversarial perturbations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the number of attack points (K) affect the success rate of attacks in the "segment everything" mode?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that increasing the number of attack points increases the attack success rate, but does not provide a detailed analysis of this relationship.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides qualitative results showing the trend but does not quantify the relationship between K and attack success rate.
- What evidence would resolve it: Quantitative analysis showing the attack success rate as a function of the number of attack points, and identifying the point of diminishing returns.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can SAM be attacked to generate specific desired masks rather than just removing them?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The abstract mentions that beyond mask removal, the paper investigates the possibility of generating any desired mask through adversarial attacks.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide details on how to achieve this or the success rate of such attacks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the effectiveness of attacks designed to generate specific masks, and analysis of the factors that influence the success of such attacks.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the CLIP-MSE loss function perform compared to other potential loss functions for attacking SAM?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces the CLIP-MSE loss function as effective for mask removal attacks but does not compare it to other loss functions.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comparative analysis of different loss functions for attacking SAM.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the CLIP-MSE loss function to other loss functions in terms of attack success rate and efficiency.

## Limitations
- Single attack objective (mask removal) without exploring other threat models
- Limited evaluation on diverse datasets beyond random point prompts
- No comparison with alternative attack formulations or loss functions
- Missing analysis of attack transferability across different SAM variants

## Confidence
- White-box attack effectiveness: High - Results are consistent across multiple runs and attack methods
- Black-box robustness patterns: Medium - Observed trends need more systematic validation
- Proposed mechanisms: Low - Inferred from results without direct experimental validation

## Next Checks
1. Conduct ablation studies varying the threshold parameter (Negth) to quantify its impact on attack success rates across different object sizes and prompt types.

2. Evaluate attack transferability across multiple surrogate models with varying architectures to better understand the relationship between model similarity and black-box robustness.

3. Test the attack against SAM variants trained with different objectives (e.g., instance segmentation vs panoptic segmentation) to assess the generality of observed vulnerabilities.
