# Logically at Factify 2: A Multi-Modal Fact Checking System Based on Evidence Retrieval techniques and Transformer Encoder Architecture

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03127
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03127
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Weighted average F1 score of 0.79 on De-Factify 2023 Factify 2 dataset, placing 3rd among 9 participants

## Executive Summary
This paper presents Logically's submission to the De-Factify 2023 challenge on multimodal fact checking. The system employs a two-stage approach with evidence retrieval and cross-modal veracity prediction using a Transformer Encoder architecture. Evidence retrieval uses SBERT-based semantic search at sentence level, while the veracity model combines CLIP cross-modal embeddings and Word2Vec text embeddings with self-attention mechanisms. The system achieves a weighted average F1 score of 0.79 on both validation and blind test sets, placing 3rd among 9 participants with a small margin to the top system.

## Method Summary
The system uses a two-stage approach for multimodal fact checking. First, evidence retrieval identifies relevant passages from documents using SBERT-QA semantic search at the sentence level, retrieving top-5 passages based on cosine similarity with claim text. Second, a cross-modal veracity prediction model combines CLIP embeddings for multimodal inputs and Word2Vec embeddings for text, processed through separate Transformer Encoder blocks with self-attention mechanisms. The outputs are concatenated and passed through an MLP classifier to predict one of five veracity categories. The model is trained using cross-entropy loss with AdamW optimizer, batch size 16, and early stopping based on validation loss.

## Key Results
- Achieved weighted average F1 score of 0.79 on both validation and blind test sets
- Placed 3rd among 9 participants in De-Factify 2023 challenge
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot capabilities of pre-trained cross-modal models like CLIP for fact checking tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-trained cross-modal models like CLIP enable strong zero-shot capabilities for multimodal fact checking
- Mechanism: CLIP maps visual and text embeddings into a common semantic space, allowing the model to compute similarity scores between claim images and document text/images without requiring fine-tuning
- Core assumption: Cross-modal semantic alignment at the global level is sufficient for determining entailment relationships in fact checking
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "pre-trained cross-modal models like CLIP have strong zero-shot capabilities for multimodal fact checking tasks"
  - [section 3.4] "The cross-modal encoder is implemented with a pre-trained CLIP model that aims to map visual and text embeddings into a common space"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct citations supporting CLIP's effectiveness in fact checking specifically
- Break condition: If the cross-modal alignment fails to capture nuanced semantic relationships between claim and evidence, or if domain-specific visual concepts are not well represented in CLIP's training data

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Sentence-level evidence retrieval using SBERT-QA improves veracity prediction accuracy
- Mechanism: SBERT-QA encodes claim and document text into 768-dimensional vectors, computes cosine similarity to rank passages, and selects top-K relevant sentences as evidence
- Core assumption: Semantic similarity between claim and evidence passages is a reliable proxy for evidential relevance
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "SBERT model based on the MPNet architecture [39] and is trained on a Question-Answer (QA) dataset with 215M QA pairs from diverse sources"
  - [section 5.1] "SERT-QA based model achieves big marginal improvement at both paragraph and sentence level"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - limited comparison to other evidence retrieval methods in the corpus
- Break condition: If semantic similarity does not correlate with actual evidential support, or if SBERT-QA fails to capture domain-specific terminology and context

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Transformer Encoder architecture with self-attention mechanisms effectively models higher-order interactions between multimodal inputs
- Mechanism: Two separate Transformer encoders process CLIP and Word2Vec embeddings, capturing contextual relationships through multi-head self-attention before classification
- Core assumption: Self-attention mechanisms can identify hidden relationships between vector entities regardless of modality
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.5] "The self-attention mechanisms of the TE encoder allows for simple but powerful reasoning that can identify hidden relationships between vector entities, regardless of whether they are visual or textual in nature"
  - [section 5.1] "two transformer encoders...consisting of N identical sequential blocks of a multi-head attention (MHA) and a fully connected feed-forward network (FFN)"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - transformer architectures are well-established in NLP but specific application to multimodal fact checking is less documented
- Break condition: If the self-attention mechanism cannot effectively model the complex relationships between multimodal inputs, or if the fixed architecture cannot adapt to varying input complexities

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Evidence Retrieval (SBERT-QA) → Embedding Layer (CLIP + Word2Vec) → Transformer Encoder → MLP Classifier
- Critical path: Claim → Evidence Retrieval → Embedding → Transformer Encoder → Classification
- Design tradeoffs: Sentence-level vs paragraph-level evidence retrieval; CLIP vs fine-tuned vision models; Word2Vec vs contextual embeddings
- Failure signatures: Poor evidence retrieval leading to insufficient context; embedding mismatch between modalities; attention mechanism failing to capture relevant relationships

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the model handle multimodal claims that contain sarcasm, irony, or misleading context, which are common in real-world misinformation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the need for techniques that can effectively handle more complex and nuanced real-world scenarios, such as those involving sarcasm, irony, and misleading context.
- Why unresolved: The current system is evaluated on the Factify 2 dataset, which may not fully capture the complexity of real-world multimodal misinformation that often involves nuanced language and visual cues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the system on datasets containing multimodal claims with sarcasm, irony, and misleading context, and comparing performance to human fact-checkers.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the system be improved to learn finer-grained cross-modal representations by jointly modeling intra- and inter-modality relationships and aligning vision regions with sentence words or entities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies the need for more effective techniques for multimodal modeling and explainability, particularly in regards to learning finer-grained cross-modal representations.
- Why unresolved: The current system uses CLIP for cross-modal alignment at a coarse-grained level, ignoring finer-grained cross-modal alignment at region-word level.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing models that can jointly model intra- and inter-modality relationships, and evaluating their performance on multimodal fact-checking tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the system be adapted to handle large amounts of textual and multimodal information from multiple sources and domains for claim verification in real-world applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions the need for techniques that can effectively handle large amounts of textual and multimodal information from multiple sources and domains for claim verification.
- Why unresolved: The current system is evaluated on a specific dataset and may not scale well to the diverse and noisy information encountered in real-world fact-checking scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the system on large-scale, multi-source datasets and evaluating its performance in terms of accuracy, efficiency, and robustness.

## Limitations

- The system relies on CLIP's zero-shot capabilities, which may not capture domain-specific visual concepts not well-represented in its training data
- Exact hyperparameter values for Transformer Encoder blocks and MLP classifier details are not specified, making exact reproduction challenging
- The SBERT-QA evidence retrieval approach lacks direct comparison to alternative methods or validation that semantic similarity correlates with evidential relevance
