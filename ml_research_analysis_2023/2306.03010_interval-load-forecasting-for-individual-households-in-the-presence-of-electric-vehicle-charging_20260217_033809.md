# Interval Load Forecasting for Individual Households in the Presence of Electric Vehicle Charging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03010
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03010
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: LSTM-BNN generates uncertainty-aware interval forecasts for household electricity consumption with EV charging

## Executive Summary
This study addresses the challenge of forecasting household electricity consumption in the presence of electric vehicle (EV) charging, which introduces significant variability and randomness into load patterns. The proposed solution is a Long Short-Term Memory Bayesian Neural Network (LSTM-BNN) that generates interval forecasts by leveraging dropout-based Bayesian inference to capture prediction uncertainty. The approach is trained and evaluated using real-world data from four households with EV charging, comparing performance under lockdown and non-lockdown conditions during the COVID-19 pandemic. Results show that LSTM-BNNs achieve accuracy comparable to standard point forecasts while providing the added benefit of uncertainty quantification through prediction intervals.

## Method Summary
The study proposes an LSTM-BNN model for interval load forecasting that uses dropout layers during both training and inference to approximate Bayesian inference. The model takes historical load data and EV charging patterns as inputs to predict future electricity consumption with associated uncertainty intervals. Training incorporates dropout at each layer, and during inference, multiple stochastic forward passes generate a distribution of predictions from which prediction intervals are derived. The model is evaluated using real-world data from four households with EV charging, comparing performance under lockdown and non-lockdown conditions during the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Key Results
- LSTM-BNNs achieve accuracy comparable to standard point forecasts while providing uncertainty quantification through prediction intervals
- No significant impact of lockdown periods on model performance, suggesting EV charging randomness outweighs pandemic effects
- The approach demonstrates effective handling of the inherent uncertainty introduced by EV charging behavior

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The LSTM-BNN approach works by combining the temporal pattern recognition capabilities of LSTM networks with Bayesian uncertainty quantification through dropout-based inference. During training, dropout layers prevent overfitting while learning to represent uncertainty in the model weights. During inference, multiple stochastic forward passes with active dropout generate a distribution of predictions, from which statistically valid prediction intervals can be derived. This approach naturally captures the inherent randomness of EV charging behavior while maintaining the ability to learn temporal patterns in household electricity consumption.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Data Preprocessing -> LSTM-BNN Model -> Dropout-Based Inference -> Prediction Intervals

Critical Path:
Input data → LSTM layers → Dropout layers → Dense output layer → Multiple stochastic forward passes → Prediction interval calculation

Design Tradeoffs:
- Uses dropout for uncertainty quantification rather than traditional Bayesian methods to reduce computational complexity
- Employs LSTM architecture to capture temporal dependencies rather than simpler feedforward networks
- Generates prediction intervals rather than point estimates to provide uncertainty information
- Focuses on 24-hour ahead forecasts with hourly resolution for practical utility

Failure Signatures:
- Poor performance on households with highly irregular EV charging patterns
- Overly conservative or overly narrow prediction intervals
- Inability to capture long-term temporal dependencies
- Performance degradation during extreme weather conditions or special events

## Limitations
- Study uses only four households with EV charging, limiting generalizability to broader populations
- COVID-19 lockdown comparison does not account for other potential confounding factors
- Performance evaluation metrics may not fully capture all aspects of forecast quality
- Focus on 24-hour ahead forecasts may not represent other forecasting horizons or granularities
- Practical utility of uncertainty intervals for real-world applications is not explicitly demonstrated
