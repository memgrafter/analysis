# Evaluating GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 Models on Brazilian University Admission Exams

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17003
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17003
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: GPT-4 with Chain-of-Thought achieved 87% accuracy on ENEM 2022, surpassing GPT-3.5 by 11 points

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 models on ENEM, a high-stakes Brazilian university entrance exam. The exam poses challenging tasks for language models due to its multidisciplinary nature, requiring understanding of information from diverse domains. The study analyzes responses generated by GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 models using different prompt strategies, including zero-shot, few-shot, and few-shot with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompts. The results show that GPT-4 with CoT achieved an accuracy of 87% on the 2022 exam, surpassing GPT-3.5 by 11 points. The study also establishes GPT-4 as the new state-of-the-art model for tackling the ENEM challenge and provides insights into the effectiveness of various prompting strategies.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 models on ENEM exams using zero-shot, few-shot, and few-shot with Chain-of-Thought prompting strategies. Models were accessed via OpenAI API with temperature=0 and max tokens=4000. The ENEM Challenge dataset (2009-2017, 916 questions after filtering) and ENEM 2022 dataset (88 questions after filtering) were used. Few-shot examples were drawn from ENEM 2022. Model outputs were parsed using regex rules for zero-shot experiments, and accuracy was calculated across different knowledge domains.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 with CoT achieved 87% accuracy on ENEM 2022, surpassing GPT-3.5 by 11 percentage points
- Mathematical questions showed the highest improvement with CoT prompting (50.00% → 72.73%)
- GPT-4's zero-shot accuracy reached 94.56%, significantly higher than GPT-3.5 models (77.41% to 82.88%)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting significantly improves GPT-4's ability to solve complex, multi-step reasoning problems on the ENEM exam.
- Mechanism: By prompting the model to first generate a step-by-step explanation before arriving at the final answer, CoT decomposes complex problems into smaller, more manageable parts. This structured reasoning process allows the model to better handle questions requiring deep understanding of underlying principles, especially in domains like mathematics and natural sciences.
- Core assumption: The model can generate valid reasoning chains that align with human logic when explicitly instructed to do so, and these chains actually improve final answer accuracy rather than just adding noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The results show that GPT-4 with CoT achieved an accuracy of 87% on the 2022 exam, largely surpassing GPT-3.5 by 11 points."
  - [section] "By utilizing few-shot prompts, the accuracy of the model increased by a mere one percentage point. However, we observed a substantial improvement of over 6 percentage points when using the few-shot CoT prompt, with mathematical questions showing the highest improvement (50.00% → 72.73%), followed by natural science questions (76.92% → 88.46%)."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus doesn't provide direct comparison of CoT vs non-CoT performance on ENEM.
- Break condition: The model fails to generate coherent reasoning chains, or the reasoning chains lead to incorrect conclusions despite appearing logical.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5 by a substantial margin on the ENEM exam, establishing it as the new state-of-the-art model for this task.
- Mechanism: GPT-4's larger model size, more extensive training data, and improved architecture enable it to better understand and integrate information from diverse domains, which is crucial for the multidisciplinary nature of ENEM questions. The model can handle complex reasoning tasks that require comprehension across multiple fields of knowledge.
- Core assumption: The performance difference is due to genuine model capability improvements rather than memorization of training data, especially given the 2022 exam was administered after the model's training cutoff.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "On the 2022 edition, the best-performing model, GPT-4 with CoT, achieved an accuracy of 87%, largely surpassing GPT-3.5 by 11 points."
  - [section] "The average zero-shot accuracy for the GPT-3.5 models spanned from 77.41% to 82.88%, whereas the gpt-4 model exhibited a pronounced increase, attaining an accuracy of 94.56%."
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - related papers show GPT-4 performing well on specialized exams, supporting the general trend of improved performance.
- Break condition: The performance gap narrows significantly when using more sophisticated prompting strategies with GPT-3.5, or when evaluating on tasks that specifically target GPT-4's unique capabilities.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The ENEM exam's multidisciplinary nature poses unique challenges for language models, requiring integration of knowledge across diverse domains.
- Mechanism: Questions often require understanding of multiple fields simultaneously (e.g., statistics and biology), which pushes language models to not only retrieve relevant information but also synthesize it into coherent solutions. This complexity makes the exam a good benchmark for evaluating a model's ability to handle real-world reasoning tasks.
- Core assumption: The exam questions genuinely require integration of knowledge across domains rather than being solvable through domain-specific knowledge alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This exam poses challenging tasks for LMs, since its questions may span into multiple fields of knowledge, requiring understanding of information from diverse domains. For instance, a question may require comprehension of both statistics and biology to be solved."
  - [section] "The ENEM exam, presented in Brazilian Portuguese, poses a unique challenge for LMs, as it requires a deep understanding of various fields of knowledge and the ability to integrate information from diverse domains."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus doesn't provide examples of multidisciplinary questions from ENEM.
- Break condition: The exam questions can be solved by independently applying knowledge from single domains without genuine integration.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well do the evaluated language models generalize to other high-stakes, multidisciplinary exams beyond ENEM, particularly in different languages and educational systems?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The study evaluates models on a specific Brazilian exam but notes the lack of similar studies in other languages and educational contexts.
- Why unresolved: The research is limited to Portuguese and a single exam format, with no cross-linguistic or cross-cultural validation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the same models and prompting strategies on exams from other countries (e.g., SAT, Gaokao, Abitur) in different languages would provide comparative performance data.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between the number of few-shot examples and the complexity of Chain-of-Thought explanations for maximizing accuracy across different knowledge domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study uses three fixed few-shot examples and explores CoT but doesn't systematically vary these parameters.
- Why unresolved: The research uses a static configuration without exploring the full parameter space of few-shot examples and CoT depth.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies varying the number of few-shot examples (1-10) and CoT complexity levels while measuring performance across all ENEM domains.

### Open Question 3
- Question: To what extent did the GPT models memorize ENEM answers during training versus developing genuine reasoning capabilities, and how can we distinguish between these mechanisms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that GPT-4 performed worse on 2022 data than on historical data, raising concerns about memorization.
- Why unresolved: The study observes performance patterns suggesting memorization but lacks methods to definitively distinguish memorization from reasoning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments with novel questions that share surface features with training data but require different reasoning, or analysis of attention patterns when solving known vs. novel problems.

## Limitations
- The study excludes image-based questions and those requiring advanced mathematical reasoning or chemical knowledge, limiting generalizability
- Few-shot examples were drawn from the same year (2022) as the evaluation set, raising concerns about potential data contamination
- The research does not explore whether performance gains from CoT prompting represent genuine reasoning improvements or simply more elaborate pattern matching

## Confidence
- High Confidence: GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5 on ENEM questions when using appropriate prompting strategies
- Medium Confidence: Chain-of-Thought prompting provides substantial benefits for GPT-4 on complex reasoning tasks
- Low Confidence: The claim that ENEM's multidisciplinary nature uniquely challenges language models is weakly supported

## Next Checks
1. **Data Contamination Verification**: Conduct an analysis to determine whether ENEM 2022 questions were present in the training data of evaluated models, particularly for GPT-4. This would involve checking for near-identical question matches in publicly available model training corpora.

2. **Reasoning Quality Assessment**: Implement a secondary evaluation that assesses not just answer accuracy but also the logical coherence and validity of CoT-generated reasoning chains. This would help distinguish between genuine reasoning improvements and more elaborate guessing strategies.

3. **Cross-Lingual Generalization Test**: Evaluate the same prompting strategies on the English-language versions of ENEM-style questions (or similar multidisciplinary exams in English) to determine whether observed performance differences are language-specific or reflect broader model capability differences.
