# Computably Continuous Reinforcement-Learning Objectives are PAC-learnable

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.05518
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05518
- **Reference count**: 13
- **Key outcome**: Establishes sufficient conditions (uniform continuity and computability) for PAC-learnability of reinforcement learning objectives and applies them to three specific objectives.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes theoretical foundations for determining when reinforcement learning objectives are PAC-learnable. The authors prove that uniform continuity is a sufficient condition for PAC-learnability in the information-theoretic setting, while computability is sufficient in the computation-theoretic setting. The paper demonstrates these results by applying them to three specific objectives from the literature: simple reward machines, Bozkurt et al.'s LTL-in-the-limit objectives, and geometric linear temporal logic. The work provides both verification tools for existing objectives and guidance for designing new PAC-learnable objectives.

## Method Summary
The paper uses theoretical proof-based methods to establish sufficient conditions for PAC-learnability. For the information-theoretic setting, it shows that uniformly continuous objectives can be reduced to finite-horizon cumulative reward problems, which are known to be PAC-learnable. For the computation-theoretic setting, it demonstrates that computable objectives can be approximated by rational sequences, implying uniform continuity, and provides algorithms that can compute both the required horizon bounds and objective values. The authors apply these conditions to specific objectives by verifying their uniform continuity or computability properties.

## Key Results
- Uniform continuity of an objective guarantees PAC-learnability in the information-theoretic setting
- Computability of an objective guarantees PAC-learnability in the computation-theoretic setting
- Three specific objectives from the literature are proven to be PAC-learnable under these conditions
- The sufficient conditions provide both verification tools and design principles for reinforcement learning objectives

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Uniform continuity of an objective guarantees PAC-learnability in the information-theoretic setting.
- **Mechanism**: Uniform continuity ensures that for any desired accuracy ε, there exists a finite horizon H beyond which all extensions of a sequence prefix produce values within ε of each other. This allows reduction to a finite-horizon cumulative reward problem, which is known to be PAC-learnable.
- **Core assumption**: The objective function's domain forms a metric space where distance is defined by longest common prefix length, and the objective is measurable and bounded.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "we prove that if an objective given as an oracle is uniformly continuous, then it is PAC-learnable"
  - [section 4.2]: Proof constructs finite-horizon problem and invokes ORLC algorithm
  - [corpus]: Weak (no corpus papers directly address this specific uniform continuity mechanism)
- **Break condition**: If the objective has discontinuities or requires infinite horizon to distinguish values, the reduction fails and PAC-learnability is not guaranteed.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Computability of an objective guarantees PAC-learnability in the computation-theoretic setting.
- **Mechanism**: A computable objective can be approximated by rational sequences, which implies uniform continuity. This allows both the information-theoretic PAC-learnability and the construction of a computable learning algorithm that computes the horizon bound and objective values.
- **Core assumption**: The objective can be computed by a Type-2 Turing machine producing fast-converging Cauchy sequences.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "for the analysis that considers computational complexity, we prove that if an objective is computable, then it is PAC-learnable"
  - [section 4.4]: Combines uniform continuity result with computable algorithm construction
  - [corpus]: Weak (corpus papers discuss computability but not this specific reinforcement learning application)
- **Break condition**: If the objective is not computable (e.g., requires solving undecidable problems), no PAC-learning algorithm can exist.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Reduction from general objectives to finite-horizon cumulative rewards preserves PAC-learnability.
- **Mechanism**: The paper constructs a lifted MDP where each state represents a history prefix, assigns rewards based on the objective's value at horizon H, and uses existing PAC algorithms for finite-horizon problems.
- **Core assumption**: The lifted state space is finite (since original MDP has finite states and actions) and the reward function is well-defined.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [section 4.2]: Detailed construction of lifted MDP and reduction proof
  - [abstract]: "we reduce κ to a finite-horizon cumulative rewards problem"
  - [corpus]: Weak (corpus doesn't contain similar reduction proofs)
- **Break condition**: If the horizon H grows too quickly with ε, the reduction may not be computationally feasible, though this doesn't affect information-theoretic PAC-learnability.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Objective specification module -> Uniform continuity check -> Horizon computation -> Lifted MDP construction -> PAC algorithm wrapper -> Policy extraction

- **Critical path**:
  1. Receive objective specification
  2. Check uniform continuity or computability
  3. Compute horizon bound H for given ε
  4. Construct lifted MDP with history-dependent rewards
  5. Invoke finite-horizon PAC algorithm
  6. Extract and return policy

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Information-theoretic vs computation-theoretic settings: The former ignores computation cost but requires oracle access, while the latter requires actual algorithms
  - Horizon bound growth: Uniform continuity guarantees existence of H but doesn't bound how fast H grows with ε
  - Reduction overhead: The lifted MDP may have exponential state space in H, affecting practical efficiency

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Objective not uniformly continuous: PAC-learnability not guaranteed in information-theoretic setting
  - Objective not computable: PAC-learnability fails in computation-theoretic setting
  - Reduction produces intractable MDP: Algorithm may not terminate in practice despite theoretical PAC-guarantee
  - Horizon bound too large: Makes the reduction impractical even if theoretically valid

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test uniform continuity verification on simple reward machines with known continuity properties
  2. Implement the reduction from reward machines to finite-horizon problems and verify policy quality
  3. Compare sample complexity of the reduction-based approach versus direct algorithms for known objectives

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: Are there practical reinforcement learning objectives beyond those mentioned in the paper that are not uniformly continuous or computable but are still PAC-learnable?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper mentions that their conditions are sufficient but not necessary, providing an example of a non-uniformly continuous objective that is PAC-learnable.
- **Why unresolved**: While the paper provides one example, it doesn't systematically explore other potential objectives that might fall into this category.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Identifying and analyzing other reinforcement learning objectives that are not uniformly continuous or computable but can be shown to be PAC-learnable.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How do the sample complexity and computational complexity bounds for PAC-learnable objectives scale with respect to the parameters of the objectives (e.g., number of states, actions, or formula size)?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper mentions that the sample complexity depends only on the parameters known to the algorithm, but it doesn't provide specific scaling relationships.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper focuses on establishing sufficient conditions for PAC-learnability rather than analyzing the specific complexity bounds.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Deriving and analyzing the exact scaling of sample complexity and computational complexity for various PAC-learnable objectives.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: Can the results of this paper be extended to more general reinforcement learning settings, such as partially observable MDPs (POMDPs) or multi-agent reinforcement learning?
- **Basis in paper**: [inferred] The paper focuses on standard MDPs and doesn't discuss extensions to other reinforcement learning settings.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper's results are specific to the MDP setting and don't directly address more complex scenarios.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Adapting the concepts of uniform continuity and computability to POMDPs or multi-agent settings and establishing sufficient conditions for PAC-learnability in those contexts.

## Limitations

- The uniform continuity condition may be overly restrictive, potentially excluding learnable objectives that are continuous but not uniformly continuous
- The computability requirement assumes access to Type-2 Turing machines, which may not reflect practical implementation constraints
- The reduction to finite-horizon problems can result in state spaces that grow exponentially with the required horizon H, making the approach computationally intractable for objectives requiring large horizons

## Confidence

- **High**: The theoretical framework connecting uniform continuity to PAC-learnability in the information-theoretic setting
- **Medium**: The computability-to-PAC-learnability reduction in the computation-theoretic setting, as it relies on Type-2 Turing machine assumptions
- **Medium**: The reduction from general objectives to finite-horizon cumulative rewards problems
