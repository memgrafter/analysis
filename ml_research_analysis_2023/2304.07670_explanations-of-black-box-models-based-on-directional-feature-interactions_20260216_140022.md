# Explanations of Black-Box Models based on Directional Feature Interactions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07670
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07670
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper extends univariate feature explanations to bivariate explanations, capturing directional feature interactions in black-box models as a directed graph. The method generalizes to any removal-based explanation, with experiments on Shapley values. Analyzing the directed explanation graph reveals most influential features, directionally redundant features (whose presence negates other features' influence), and mutually redundant features (interchangeable features). The authors prove transitivity of mutual redundancy for Shapley explanations. Experiments on various datasets show superior performance in identifying feature interactions and maintaining post-hoc accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method to extend univariate feature explanations to bivariate explanations that capture directional feature interactions in black-box models. The approach represents these interactions as a weighted directed graph, where each element represents the importance of one feature conditioned on another being present. The method generalizes to any removal-based explanation framework and is specifically demonstrated using Shapley values. By analyzing this graph, the method can identify the most influential features, mutually redundant features (interchangeable features), and directionally redundant features (whose presence negates the influence of others).

## Method Summary
The method extends univariate Shapley value explanations to bivariate explanations by conditioning the utility function on the presence of each feature. For each feature i, the utility is restricted to cases where i is present, and the univariate explanation method is applied to this restricted utility, creating a d×d bivariate explanation matrix. This matrix forms a directed graph where edges represent feature importance relationships. The graph is analyzed to identify strongly connected components (mutually redundant features) and PageRank scores (most influential features). The method generalizes to any removal-based explanation and includes theoretical proofs of transitivity for mutual redundancy in Shapley explanations with monotone utility functions.

## Key Results
- Transitivity of mutual redundancy is proven for Shapley explanations with monotone utility functions
- The directed explanation graph enables identification of influential features, directionally redundant features, and mutually redundant features
- Experiments on CIFAR10, IMDB, Census, Divorce, Drug, and gene expression data show superior performance in identifying feature interactions compared to state-of-the-art methods
- Post-hoc accuracy is maintained or improved after masking identified redundant features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bivariate Shapley explanations extend univariate removal-based explanations by capturing directional feature interactions through a weighted directed graph.
- Mechanism: For each feature i, the method computes a column of the bivariate explanation matrix by restricting the utility function to cases where i is present, then applies the univariate explanation method to this restricted utility. This creates a d×d matrix where element E2(u)ij represents the importance of feature j conditioned on feature i being present.
- Core assumption: The underlying utility function is well-defined for subset functions and can be meaningfully restricted to cases where specific features are present.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We extend univariate explanation to a higher-order; this enhances explainability, as bivariate methods can capture feature interactions in black-box models, represented as a directed graph."
  - [section 4] "the bivariate explanationE2 :U→ Rd×d as ad×d matrix: theith column of this matrix isE(ui), i.e., the univariate explanation applied to utility functionui"
- Break condition: If the utility function cannot be meaningfully restricted or if the univariate explanation method does not generalize to restricted utility functions, the bivariate construction fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The directed explanation graph enables discovery of mutual and directional redundancy through graph analysis.
- Mechanism: Features i and j are mutually redundant when E2(u)ij = E2(u)ji = 0, forming strongly connected components in the redundancy graph H. Directional redundancy occurs when E2(u)ij = 0 but E2(u)ji ≠ 0, identifying sink nodes in H.
- Core assumption: Zero values in the bivariate explanation matrix indicate true redundancy rather than approximation error or noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Analyzing this graph enables us to discover groups of features that are equally important (i.e., interchangeable), while the notion of directionality allows us to identify the most influential features."
  - [section 4.1] "Deﬁnition 4.2. Giveni,j ∈D, featuresi,j are mutually redundant ifE2(u)ij =E2(u)ji = 0."
- Break condition: If approximation errors in computing E2(u) produce spurious zeros, the redundancy detection becomes unreliable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Shapley explanation maps lead to transitive mutual redundancy, enabling identification of equivalence classes of interchangeable features.
- Mechanism: Theorem 1 proves that for Shapley explanations with monotone utility functions, if features i and j are mutually redundant and j and k are mutually redundant, then i and k are also mutually redundant, making the redundancy graph H transitive.
- Core assumption: The utility function is monotone (more features never hurt) and Shapley value computation is exact or sufficiently accurate.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "In the following theorem however, we prove that the Shapley explanation mapE indeed leads to the transitivity of mutual redundancy and, thereby, the graphH"
  - [section 4.1] "Corollary 1.1. GraphH for Shapley explanation map with a monotone utility function is transitive."
- Break condition: If the utility function is not monotone or if approximation errors in Shapley computation are too large, transitivity may not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input black-box model f, input x, and utility function u → Utility restriction per feature → Univariate explanation computation → Bivariate matrix construction → Graph formation → Redundancy analysis → Output explanations
- Critical path: Input → Utility restriction per feature → Univariate explanation computation → Bivariate matrix construction → Graph formation → Redundancy analysis → Output explanations
- Design tradeoffs: Exact Shapley computation is intractable for many features, requiring approximation methods that trade accuracy for speed. The method generalizes to any univariate explainer but performance depends on that explainer's properties.
- Failure signatures: If the bivariate matrix contains mostly zeros or has unusual sparsity patterns, this may indicate issues with the utility function or approximation method. If the redundancy graph has no strongly connected components, mutual redundancy may not exist in the data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the transitivity property of mutual redundancy in Shapley explanations hold for non-monotone utility functions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Theorem 1 and Corollary 1.1 discuss transitivity for monotone utility functions
- Why unresolved: The proof relies on the monotonicity property, which ensures non-negative marginal gains. For non-monotone utilities, marginal gains can be negative, potentially breaking the transitivity chain.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal proof or counterexample showing whether transitivity holds for a specific non-monotone utility function, such as the Gini index or cross-entropy loss.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of redundancy threshold γ impact the trade-off between identifying true feature interactions and introducing false positives?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 5 mentions the sensitivity analysis of graph H to parameter γ and its effect on graph density and post-hoc accuracy
- Why unresolved: While the paper suggests using cross-validation or examining density curves to select γ, a more systematic approach or theoretical bounds on γ's impact are not provided.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different γ values across various datasets and black-box models, or a theoretical analysis linking γ to the signal-to-noise ratio in feature interactions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the directed explanation graph G be used to identify higher-order feature interactions beyond pairwise interactions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper extends univariate explanations to bivariate explanations, capturing directional feature interactions as a directed graph. This suggests the possibility of extending the method to capture higher-order interactions.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on bivariate explanations and their analysis. The extension to higher-order interactions is mentioned but not explored in detail.
- What evidence would resolve it: An algorithm for constructing a hypergraph from the directed explanation graph, where hyperedges represent groups of mutually redundant features. Experiments comparing the performance of this hypergraph-based approach to existing methods for detecting higher-order interactions.

## Limitations
- Computational cost of exact Shapley calculations for high-dimensional data requires approximation methods that may introduce errors
- Method assumes zero values in bivariate matrix indicate true redundancy, which may not hold with approximation errors
- Performance depends on the properties of the underlying univariate explanation method and monotonicity of the utility function
