# SwinRDM: Integrate SwinRNN with Diffusion Model towards High-Resolution and High-Quality Weather Forecasting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03110
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03110
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Achieves superior performance to IFS on Z500, T850, T2M, and TP at lead times up to 5 days using a two-step strategy: SwinRNN+ at 1.40625° resolution followed by diffusion-based super-resolution to 0.25°.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of achieving high-resolution, high-quality medium-range global weather forecasting, where current data-driven models underperform compared to traditional numerical weather prediction systems. The authors propose SwinRDM, a two-step approach that first trains an improved SwinRNN+ model at moderate resolution (1.40625°) and then applies a diffusion-based super-resolution model to generate high-resolution (0.25°) forecasts. This strategy circumvents the prohibitive computational cost of direct high-resolution training while achieving superior performance to the state-of-the-art Integrated Forecast System (IFS) on key atmospheric variables at lead times up to 5 days.

## Method Summary
SwinRDM employs a two-step strategy: First, an improved SwinRNN+ model is trained at 1.40625° resolution using 6 historical frames to predict 20 future frames, with architectural improvements including a single-scale design with higher feature dimensions and multi-layer feature aggregation. Second, a diffusion-based super-resolution model is trained to map the 1.40625° SwinRNN+ outputs to 0.25° resolution forecasts, conditioned on both the low-resolution input and the time step. The diffusion model learns to iteratively denoise Gaussian noise while preserving the coarse structure from SwinRNN+ and generating realistic fine-scale details. The approach leverages ERA5 reanalysis data (1979-2018) at 6-hour intervals, with sub-sampling for different model components.

## Key Results
- Achieves lower latitude-weighted RMSE than IFS on Z500, T850, T2M, and TP at lead times up to 5 days.
- Maintains low FID scores across all lead times, outperforming bilinear interpolation and SwinIR for super-resolution quality.
- Shows higher CSI for precipitation forecasting compared to IFS, indicating better skill in predicting precipitation events.
- Demonstrates the effectiveness of the two-step strategy in achieving high-resolution forecasts without the prohibitive computational cost of direct high-resolution training.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The diffusion model enables the capture of high-resolution, fine-scale weather features that are lost when training directly on high-resolution data. By learning a reverse denoising process conditioned on low-resolution SwinRNN+ outputs, it can generate high-frequency details that are consistent with the coarse structure provided by the SwinRNN+ predictions.

### Mechanism 2
The SwinRNN+ architecture's single-scale design with higher feature dimensions outperforms the original multi-scale SwinRNN at 1.40625° resolution. Removing the multi-scale hierarchy reduces memory overhead, allowing larger feature maps that increase representational capacity, while feature aggregation from multiple transformer layers improves gradient flow and captures multi-level context.

### Mechanism 3
Conditioning the diffusion model on both the low-resolution prediction and the time step enables temporally coherent super-resolution across forecast horizons. The lead time conditioning allows the diffusion model to adjust the fine-scale generation process to account for the systematic smoothing that occurs in recurrent forecasts as lead time increases.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Historical frames -> SwinRNN+ encoder (single-scale Swin Transformer blocks) -> SwinRNN+ decoder (feature aggregation) -> 1.40625° forecast -> Diffusion SR model (U-Net) -> 0.25° forecast
- **Critical path**: 1) Encode historical frames to initialize hidden states, 2) Recurrently predict future frames at 1.40625°, 3) Sample a future frame and feed to diffusion SR model with lead time, 4) Diffusion model outputs 0.25° forecast
- **Design tradeoffs**: Single-scale SwinRNN+ vs multi-scale (memory vs receptive field coverage), Diffusion SR vs direct high-res training (computational cost vs potential fidelity loss), Lead time conditioning (simplicity vs expressiveness for temporal degradation)
- **Failure signatures**: Loss of fine-scale detail in diffusion outputs (check conditioning dimensionality and noise schedule), Degraded performance at longer lead times (inspect hidden state propagation and feature aggregation), Memory bottlenecks during SwinRNN+ training (verify single-scale design and feature dimension scaling)
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Train SwinRNN+ with feature dimension 256 vs 512; compare RMSE on Z500/T850 at 1.40625°, 2) Replace diffusion SR with bilinear upsampling; measure FID degradation over lead times, 3) Remove feature aggregation; compare training stability and final accuracy

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. How can the historical context information extraction in SwinRNN+ be improved to enhance short-range forecasting performance?
2. Can the diffusion-based super-resolution model in SwinRDM be extended to handle ensemble forecasting for multiple atmospheric variables simultaneously?
3. How does the performance of SwinRDM compare to traditional numerical weather prediction models at very long lead times (e.g., 7-10 days)?

## Limitations
- The evaluation focuses on only 4 atmospheric variables at short lead times (≤5 days), leaving questions about generalization to other variables and longer horizons.
- The diffusion model's conditioning on lead time as a scalar variable may be oversimplified, potentially limiting fine-scale realism for longer forecasts.
- Computational cost trade-offs are incompletely characterized - the paper claims diffusion SR is "cheaper" but provides no detailed FLOPs comparison.

## Confidence
- **Medium** for the core two-step architecture claim (SwinRNN+ + diffusion SR)
- **Low** for the specific architectural claims about SwinRNN+ improvements (single-scale design, feature aggregation)
- **Medium** for performance claims against IFS
