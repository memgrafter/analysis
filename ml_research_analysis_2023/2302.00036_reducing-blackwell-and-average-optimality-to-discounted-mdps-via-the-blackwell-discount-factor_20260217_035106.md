# Reducing Blackwell and Average Optimality to Discounted MDPs via the Blackwell Discount Factor

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Blackwell discount factor for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), which is a discount factor above which all discounted optimal policies become Blackwell- and average-optimal. The authors derive a general upper bound on this factor and show that it enables the first reduction from average and Blackwell optimality to discounted optimality without any assumptions on the MDP structure. They also extend their results to robust MDPs, providing the first algorithms to compute robust Blackwell-optimal policies. The key contribution is that solving a discounted MDP with a discount factor larger than the Blackwell discount factor returns a Blackwell-optimal policy, enabling simpler algorithms for computing Blackwell- and average-optimal policies.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce the concept of the Blackwell discount factor, which is the smallest discount factor above which all discounted optimal policies are Blackwell-optimal. They derive a general upper bound on this factor and show that it depends on the mixing time of the MDP. The key insight is that by solving a discounted MDP with a discount factor larger than the Blackwell discount factor, one can obtain a Blackwell-optimal policy. This enables a reduction from average and Blackwell optimality to discounted optimality without any assumptions on the MDP structure. The authors also extend their results to robust MDPs, providing algorithms to compute robust Blackwell-optimal policies under the assumption of strictly monotone rewards.

## Key Results
- Introduction of the Blackwell discount factor, which is the smallest discount factor above which all discounted optimal policies become Blackwell-optimal.
- Derivation of a general upper bound on the Blackwell discount factor that depends on the mixing time of the MDP.
- Reduction from average and Blackwell optimality to discounted optimality without any assumptions on the MDP structure.
- Extension of results to robust MDPs, providing algorithms to compute robust Blackwell-optimal policies under the assumption of strictly monotone rewards.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Blackwell discount factor works because it leverages the relationship between discounted and average optimal policies in MDPs. By solving a discounted MDP with a discount factor larger than the Blackwell discount factor, the resulting policy is guaranteed to be Blackwell-optimal. This reduction is possible because Blackwell optimality is a stronger condition than average optimality, and the Blackwell discount factor captures the threshold where the influence of the discount factor dominates the long-term average behavior of the MDP. The mechanism relies on the fact that for large discount factors, the optimal policy for the discounted MDP converges to a policy that optimizes the long-term average reward, which is precisely the Blackwell-optimal policy.

## Foundational Learning
- **Blackwell optimality**: A policy is Blackwell-optimal if it maximizes the long-term average reward for any initial state. This concept is needed to understand the stronger optimality criterion that the paper aims to achieve. Quick check: Verify that a policy satisfies the Blackwell optimality condition by checking if it maximizes the average reward for all initial states.
- **Discount factor**: A parameter that determines the present value of future rewards in an MDP. The discount factor is needed to understand the reduction from average and Blackwell optimality to discounted optimality. Quick check: Ensure that the discount factor is in the range [0,1] and that larger values give more weight to future rewards.
- **Mixing time**: The time it takes for an MDP to reach its stationary distribution, regardless of the initial state. The mixing time is needed to derive the upper bound on the Blackwell discount factor. Quick check: Compute the mixing time of the MDP by finding the number of steps required for the total variation distance between the current state distribution and the stationary distribution to be below a small threshold (e.g., 0.01).

The extension to robust MDPs is needed to handle situations where the exact transition probabilities are not known. ## Limitations
- The theoretical framework relies on strict monotonicity assumptions for robust MDPs that may not hold in practical applications.
- The derived bounds for the Blackwell discount factor depend heavily on the mixing time of the underlying MDP, which can be difficult to compute for large state spaces.
- The extension to robust MDPs is based on the restrictive assumption of strictly monotone rewards and is limited to the average-reward setting without addressing variance-penalized or other robust criteria.

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core theoretical claims for the discounted MDP reduction and the existence of the Blackwell discount factor, given the rigorous mathematical proofs provided.
- Medium confidence in the robust MDP extension due to the restrictive assumptions required and the limited scope of the results.
- Low confidence in practical applicability given the computational challenges of determining the Blackwell discount factor and the strong assumptions needed for the robust setting.
