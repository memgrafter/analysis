# NoCoLA: The Norwegian Corpus of Linguistic Acceptability

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.07790
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07790
- **Reference count:** 12
- **Key outcome:** NoCoLA introduces two new datasets for evaluating Norwegian language models' grammatical understanding: NoCoLAclass for binary classification and NoCoLAzero for zero-shot evaluation.

## Executive Summary
NoCoLA provides the first comprehensive benchmark for Norwegian grammatical acceptability evaluation, enabling detailed error analysis and comparison of language models. The datasets are derived from the ASK corpus of Norwegian learner essays and cover nine error types including inflection, word choice, spelling, and punctuation. NoCoLAclass achieves around 80% accuracy across Norwegian models, with NorBERT3 models performing best, while NoCoLAzero shows Norwegian models achieving ~90% accuracy in distinguishing acceptable sentences. The dual approach of supervised classification and zero-shot evaluation provides complementary insights into language model capabilities.

## Method Summary
The NoCoLA datasets are created by extracting error-annotated sentences from the ASK corpus of Norwegian learner essays. NoCoLAclass is a supervised binary classification task using BERT-style models fine-tuned on 116,195 training sentences. NoCoLAzero is a zero-shot evaluation task that uses pseudo-log-likelihood scoring to compare acceptable and unacceptable sentence pairs (99,115 pairs total) without additional training. Both datasets maintain detailed error type annotations from the original corpus, enabling fine-grained analysis of model performance across different grammatical phenomena.

## Key Results
- NoCoLAclass achieves approximately 80% accuracy across Norwegian language models
- NorBERT3 models perform best on NoCoLAclass classification task
- NoCoLAzero shows Norwegian models achieving ~90% accuracy in zero-shot acceptability discrimination
- Datasets cover nine error types including inflection, word choice, spelling, and punctuation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The NoCoLA datasets leverage naturally occurring learner errors rather than artificially constructed minimal pairs, providing a more realistic distribution of grammatical mistakes.
- Mechanism: By extracting error-annotated sentences from the ASK corpus of Norwegian learner essays, the datasets capture actual patterns and frequencies of grammatical errors made by language learners.
- Core assumption: Learner corpora contain sufficient diversity and representativeness of grammatical error types to serve as a comprehensive benchmark for language models.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Both datasets are derived from the ASK corpus of Norwegian learner essays." [section] "The non-acceptable sentences are either collected from the linguistics literature by experts as in the English CoLA...or these sentences are collected from natural texts, usually based on the language of language learners."
- Break condition: If learner error distributions change significantly over time or fail to represent grammatical phenomena important for native speaker text processing.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The dual approach of supervised classification (NoCoLAclass) and zero-shot evaluation (NoCoLAzero) provides complementary insights into language model capabilities.
- Mechanism: NoCoLAclass measures fine-tuning ability and practical applicability, while NoCoLAzero isolates the language model's innate grammatical knowledge by requiring no additional training.
- Core assumption: Supervised fine-tuning can artificially inflate performance metrics, making it necessary to have an independent zero-shot evaluation method.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "NoCoLAclass is a supervised binary classification task...NoCoLAzero is a purely diagnostic task for evaluating the grammatical judgement of a language model in a completely zero-shot manner"
- Break condition: If language models develop capabilities that make the distinction between fine-tuned and zero-shot performance negligible.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The error type categorization enables fine-grained analysis of language model weaknesses across specific grammatical phenomena.
- Mechanism: By maintaining detailed error type annotations from the original ASK corpus (inflection, word choice, spelling, punctuation, etc.), researchers can identify which specific grammatical aspects language models struggle with.
- Core assumption: Error type categorization from learner corpora is sufficiently precise and meaningful for benchmarking purposes.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The datasets cover nine error types including inflection, word choice, spelling, and punctuation." [section] "We have merged some of the error-codes so that we have a medium-grained way of understanding the performance of the models on the different types of errors found in NoCoLAzero."
- Break condition: If error type categories are too coarse or fine to provide actionable insights, or if certain error types are underrepresented.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** NoCoLA evaluation system consists of dataset loading modules, preprocessing pipelines (detokenization, anonymization), evaluation metrics (accuracy, MCC), and model-specific scoring functions (PLL for MLM, log-probability for CLM)
- **Critical path:** Load dataset → preprocess sentences → tokenize with model → compute scores → aggregate results by error type → report overall metrics
- **Design tradeoffs:** Using learner corpora provides realistic error distributions but may not cover all native speaker grammatical phenomena; zero-shot evaluation is more diagnostic but less practical than fine-tuned classification
- **Failure signatures:** Low performance on specific error types may indicate tokenizer issues or corpus bias; poor zero-shot results suggest limited grammatical knowledge; high variance across runs indicates instability

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the performance differences between language models on NoCoLA reflect their underlying training data composition and tokenization strategies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that NB-BERT models are relatively weak on punctuation-related errors, which is attributed to the large version being trained on uncased data, and that ScandiBERT performs comparably to Norwegian models except for spelling errors.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies specific performance differences across error types, it does not provide a systematic analysis of how different training data characteristics influence model performance on various grammatical phenomena.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study comparing language models' performance across different error types while controlling for training data characteristics would clarify the relationship between these factors and grammatical understanding.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the NoCoLA datasets be effectively extended to evaluate other Norwegian dialects or language variants beyond Bokmål?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper explicitly states that its work is limited to the most widely used written standard for Norwegian, namely Bokmål.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses exclusively on Bokmål and does not investigate whether the dataset construction methodology would be equally effective for other Norwegian dialects or variants like Nynorsk.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating equivalent datasets for other Norwegian variants using the same methodology as NoCoLA would demonstrate the generalizability of the approach.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do the diagnostic capabilities of NoCoLAzero compare to other zero-shot evaluation methods in revealing specific linguistic weaknesses of language models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that NoCoLAzero is a purely diagnostic task for evaluating grammatical judgment in a zero-shot manner.
- Why unresolved: While the paper presents NoCoLAzero as a diagnostic tool and shows error-type breakdowns, it does not benchmark its effectiveness against other zero-shot evaluation methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using multiple zero-shot evaluation methods on the same set of language models would clarify NoCoLAzero's relative diagnostic capabilities.

## Limitations

- The datasets are derived from learner essays, which may not fully represent native speaker grammatical acceptability judgments
- The performance differences between models could be influenced by training corpus composition and tokenizer effectiveness rather than purely grammatical knowledge
- The datasets focus specifically on Norwegian language, preventing direct cross-linguistic comparisons with other CoLA-style benchmarks

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The dataset creation methodology and basic evaluation results are well-documented and reproducible
- **Medium Confidence:** The assertion that Norwegian models achieve ~90% accuracy on NoCoLAzero requires validation across a broader range of models and runs
- **Low Confidence:** The interpretation that error type analysis directly indicates specific model weaknesses may be confounded by tokenizer behavior and training data distribution
