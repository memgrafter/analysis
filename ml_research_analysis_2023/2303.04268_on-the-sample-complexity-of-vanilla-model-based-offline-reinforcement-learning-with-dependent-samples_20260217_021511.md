# On the Sample Complexity of Vanilla Model-Based Offline Reinforcement Learning with Dependent Samples

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04268
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04268
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Derives high-probability polynomial sample complexity bounds for vanilla model-based offline RL with dependent samples, requiring partial or uniform coverage

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the sample complexity of vanilla model-based offline reinforcement learning (RL) with dependent samples in the infinite-horizon discounted-reward setting. The authors address the bias introduced by dependent samples in offline RL and derive sufficient conditions on the number of sample paths required for off-policy evaluation and optimization. They introduce a robust MDP approach to handle estimation uncertainty and propose a deterministic-favored estimator that outperforms the sample mean estimator for almost-deterministic MDPs. The work provides the first theoretical analysis of vanilla model-based offline RL with dependent samples, filling a gap in the literature.

## Method Summary
The method constructs a robust MDP model that includes the true MDP with high probability, addressing the bias from dependent samples in offline RL. A concentration bound for random stopping times is applied to overcome the bias in transition probability estimates. The authors derive sufficient conditions on the number of sample paths needed for vanilla model-based off-policy evaluation and optimization under partial or uniform coverage assumptions. They also propose a deterministic-favored estimator for transition probabilities that performs better than the sample mean estimator in almost-deterministic MDPs.

## Key Results
- High-probability polynomial sample complexity bound for vanilla model-based off-policy evaluation with dependent samples
- Extension of results to off-policy optimization under uniform coverage
- Comparison of sample complexity with vanilla importance sampling in infinite-horizon setting
- Deterministic-favored estimator outperforms sample mean estimator for almost-deterministic dynamics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The sample mean estimator for transition probabilities is biased in the offline RL setting because sample transitions are dependent on each other.
- Mechanism: Sample transitions follow the dynamics of the MDP and are collected as paths. This creates dependencies where future samples depend on previous samples, breaking the independence assumption required for unbiased estimation.
- Core assumption: The dataset consists of sample paths collected under a behavior policy, where transitions are generated sequentially following MDP dynamics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We analyze the sample complexity of vanilla model-based offline RL with dependent samples"
  - [section]: "The sample-mean estimator is the maximum likelihood estimator of the transition probabilities. However, this estimator is biased since the outcomes of the future samples are dependent on the previous samples."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers focus on importance sampling and value estimation but don't directly address sample mean bias with dependent samples.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The robust MDP approach bounds the value function estimation error by accounting for uncertainty in transition probability estimates.
- Mechanism: A robust MDP includes the true MDP within a confidence set with high probability. This allows computing upper and lower bounds on the value function that account for estimation uncertainty.
- Core assumption: We can construct a confidence set around empirical transition frequencies that contains the true transition probabilities with high probability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We overcome the first source of bias by using a robust MDP (Nilim and El Ghaoui 2005) that includes the true MDP with high probability"
  - [section]: "A way to overcome the bias in the value function estimation is to use a robust MDP model that uses a possible set of transition probabilities"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers mention robust MDPs but don't detail the specific construction used here.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The deterministic-favored estimator outperforms the sample mean estimator for almost-deterministic MDPs with limited stochasticity.
- Mechanism: When a state-action pair has a dominant next state (probability ≥ γ(1-ε) for small ε), the deterministic-favored estimator boosts the estimate of the most frequent outcome, reducing MSE compared to the sample mean.
- Core assumption: The MDP has limited stochasticity where each state-action pair has a dominant next state.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we provide an estimator that outperforms the sample-mean estimator for almost deterministic dynamics that are prevalent in reinforcement learning"
  - [section]: "Let X be a categorical random variable with support {1, ..., K} and probability distribution [p1, ..., pK]. The sample mean estimator of pi is ˆpSMi = Ni/N... We define the deterministic-favored estimator as ˆpDFi = Ni + √N 1d(i)/(N + √N)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers don't discuss deterministic-favored estimators.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the sample complexity of vanilla model-based offline RL scale when using the deterministic-favored estimator instead of the sample mean estimator?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces a deterministic-favored estimator that outperforms the sample mean estimator for almost-deterministic dynamics and shows it has lower MSE in the regime of low number of samples.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides a theoretical analysis of the deterministic-favored estimator's performance compared to the sample mean estimator for a single categorical random variable. It does not analyze how this translates to the overall sample complexity of model-based offline RL.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis showing the sample complexity of vanilla model-based offline RL using the deterministic-favored estimator, and a comparison with the sample complexity using the sample mean estimator.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the sample complexity bounds for vanilla model-based offline RL be tightened further?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper provides sample complexity bounds for vanilla model-based offline RL, but these bounds may not be tight. - Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a lower bound on the sample complexity of vanilla model-based offline RL, so it is unclear whether the upper bounds are tight.
- What evidence would resolve it: A lower bound on the sample complexity of vanilla model-based offline RL that matches the upper bound, or an algorithm that achieves a better sample complexity.

### Open Question 3

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison of the sample complexity of vanilla model-based offline RL to that of other offline RL methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: A thorough comparison of the sample complexity of vanilla model-based offline RL to that of other offline RL methods, including pessimistic model-based approaches and model-free methods.

## Limitations

- The analysis critically depends on the behavior policy having sufficient coverage of the state-action space, which may not hold in many practical offline RL scenarios
- The paper assumes known behavior policy, limiting applicability to settings where this assumption fails
- The deterministic-favored estimator's theoretical advantage is proven only for specific MDP structures with limited stochasticity, and empirical validation across diverse MDPs is needed

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The fundamental insight that sample mean estimators are biased with dependent samples in offline RL settings is well-established. The concentration inequalities used for handling random stopping times are standard tools with proven guarantees.

**Medium Confidence**: The robust MDP approach for bounding value function estimation error follows established methods, though the specific construction and its practical performance need validation. The comparison with importance sampling in infinite-horizon settings is theoretically sound but relies on assumptions about policy distribution shifts.

**Low Confidence**: The deterministic-favored estimator's practical advantage over sample mean across diverse MDPs requires empirical validation. The sample complexity bounds, while polynomial, may be conservative in practice and their tightness needs verification.
