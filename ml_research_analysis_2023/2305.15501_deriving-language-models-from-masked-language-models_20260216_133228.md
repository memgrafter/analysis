# Deriving Language Models from Masked Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15501
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15501
- Reference count: 23
- Primary result: The Arnold-Gokhale approach outperforms MRF, HCB, and standard MLM methods in language modeling metrics, with the derived model occasionally surpassing the original MLM's performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses a fundamental limitation of masked language models (MLMs): while MLMs excel at conditional predictions, they do not define an explicit joint distribution over language, making exact evaluation metrics like perplexity intractable. The authors propose methods to derive explicit joint distributions from MLMs by leveraging the insight that MLM training implicitly learns conditional marginals of language. They evaluate several approaches, including Markov random field constructions and the Hammersley–Clifford–Besag theorem, ultimately finding that an Arnold-Gokhale-based method that finds near-compatible joints provides the best performance, occasionally even outperforming the original MLM.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes four methods to derive joint distributions from MLMs: (1) MLM construction using the MLM's own joint predictions, (2) MRF construction assuming conditional independence, (3) HCB construction using the Hammersley–Clifford–Besag theorem for compatible conditionals, and (4) AG construction using Arnold and Gokhale's algorithm to find near-compatible joints. The evaluation uses BERTBASE and BERTLARGE on SNLI and XSUM datasets with two masked positions, measuring pairwise and unary perplexity, and conditional KL divergence. The AG method iteratively updates the joint distribution to minimize the KL divergence between the MLM's conditionals and the derived joint's conditionals.

## Key Results
- The AG approach outperforms MRF, HCB, and standard MLM methods in virtually all metrics across both datasets and masking schemes
- The derived AG model occasionally outperforms the original MLM in unary perplexity
- Improvements are most pronounced when masked tokens are close together in the sequence
- MRF and HCB approaches suffer from high pairwise perplexity, especially with contiguous masking

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
MLMs implicitly learn conditional marginals of language, enabling construction of explicit joint distributions. The MLM training objective approximates the conditional marginals of the true data distribution. By leveraging these learned conditionals, one can reconstruct a joint distribution that better captures dependencies between tokens than the MLM's own factorized approximation.

### Mechanism 2
The Arnold-Gokhale approach constructs near-compatible joint distributions that outperform other methods. The AG algorithm finds a joint distribution whose unary conditionals are closest to those of the MLM, effectively regularizing the conditionals to be compatible. This regularization can lead to improved language modeling performance, even surpassing the original MLM in some cases.

### Mechanism 3
The Hammersley–Clifford–Besag construction can recover the true joint distribution if the MLM's unary conditionals are compatible. The HCB theorem provides a way to reconstruct a joint distribution from its unary conditionals, given a pivot point. If the MLM's unary conditionals are compatible, the HCB construction will yield the true joint distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MLM -> Unary conditionals -> MRF/HCB/AG construction -> Derived joint distribution -> Evaluation metrics
- Critical path: 1) Obtain unary conditionals from MLM for masked positions, 2) Construct joint distributions using proposed methods, 3) Evaluate constructed joint distributions, 4) Compare performance of different methods
- Design tradeoffs: MRF construction is simple but makes unrealistic independence assumptions; HCB construction requires compatible conditionals and a pivot point; AG construction is more complex but can handle incompatible conditionals
- Failure signatures: High perplexity scores indicate poor language modeling performance; large KL divergence values suggest derived joint's conditionals deviate significantly from MLM's conditionals; inability to compute exact distributional properties for full sequences
- First 3 experiments: 1) Implement MRF construction and evaluate on small dataset, 2) Implement HCB construction and compare to MRF on same dataset, 3) Implement AG construction and assess performance relative to other methods, focusing on cases where MLM conditionals are likely incompatible

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does regularizing MLM conditionals to be compatible during training improve language modeling performance? The authors found AG approach sometimes outperformed original MLM, suggesting compatibility regularization might be beneficial. Unresolved because paper only studied compatibility after MLM training, not modifying training objective. Evidence: Training MLM with additional compatibility loss term and comparing performance to standard MLM training.

### Open Question 2
How does quality of derived joints vary with distance between masked tokens? Authors analyzed how token distance affects pairwise NLL, finding improvements most pronounced when masked tokens are close together. Unresolved because paper showed this effect but didn't explain underlying reasons or explore longer sequences. Evidence: Detailed analysis of joint quality variation with token distance for longer sequences, and theoretical analysis of why nearby tokens show stronger dependency.

### Open Question 3
Can insights from pairwise joint derivation be extended to derive full-sequence language models from MLMs? Authors noted methods are intractable for full sequences and focused on pairwise setting. Unresolved because paper established derived joints can outperform MLMs in pairwise settings but didn't address scaling to full sequences without sampling. Evidence: Tractable method for deriving full-sequence distributions from MLMs that maintains or improves upon MLM performance.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses exclusively on pairwise distributions (two masked tokens), not addressing full sequence modeling
- Comparison limited to two specific MLM architectures (BERTBASE and BERTLARGE), limiting generalizability
- Relatively small evaluation set (1000 examples per dataset) and limited exploration of diverse domains or longer sequences

## Confidence
- High confidence: Mathematical framework for deriving joint distributions, proof that MLM training approximates conditional marginals, and core theoretical results about compatibility and HCB theorem
- Medium confidence: Empirical superiority of AG approach over MRF and HCB methods, demonstrated on limited datasets and model architectures
- Medium confidence: Claim that AG model can occasionally outperform original MLM, requiring further validation across diverse scenarios

## Next Checks
1. Extend evaluation to longer sequences: Test whether derived joint distributions maintain advantages when extended beyond pairwise cases to three or more masked tokens
2. Cross-architecture validation: Apply methodology to GPT-style autoregressive models and smaller MLMs to determine generalizability beyond BERTBASE and BERTLARGE
3. Real-world task performance: Evaluate derived models on downstream language understanding tasks (e.g., GLUE benchmark) to assess whether improved distributional properties translate to practical performance gains beyond perplexity metrics
