# Impact of translation on biomedical information extraction from real-life clinical notes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02042
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02042
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Native French biomedical concept extraction outperforms translation-based approaches with F1 score 0.51 vs 0.39-0.38

## Executive Summary
This study compares biomedical concept extraction from French clinical notes using native French models versus translation-based approaches with English models. The research evaluates three normalization methods (deep multilingual model, CODER, and MedCAT) across both approaches. Results show that native French methods achieve significantly better performance (F1 0.51) compared to English translation methods (F1 0.39 and 0.38). Translation quality emerges as a critical limiting factor, with BLEU scores of 0.31-0.51 for biomedical texts. Error analysis reveals that UMLS synonym mismatches and abbreviations contribute significantly to performance gaps between the approaches.

## Method Summary
The study employs a comparative evaluation framework using real French clinical notes from a French hospital. The French approach uses French language models for Named Entity Recognition (NER) and normalization directly on French text. The English approach translates French text using opus-mt-fr-en (with and without biomedical fine-tuning) and applies English NER and normalization models. Three normalization methods are evaluated: a deep multilingual model, CODER, and MedCAT. Document-level evaluation measures the proportion of correctly predicted CUIs per document. Translation quality is assessed using BLEU scores on biomedical test sets.

## Key Results
- Native French approach achieves global F1 score of 0.51, significantly outperforming English methods (0.39 and 0.38)
- Translation quality remains a limiting factor with BLEU scores of 0.31-0.51 for biomedical texts
- Synonym mismatches in UMLS and abbreviations contribute to performance gaps between approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Translation degrades medical concept extraction performance compared to native models
- Mechanism: Translation introduces semantic drift and loss of domain-specific terminology fidelity, causing NER and normalization errors
- Core assumption: French clinical texts contain domain-specific expressions that don't translate cleanly to English while preserving medical meaning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Translation quality remains a limiting factor, with BLEU scores of 0.31-0.51 for biomedical texts"
  - [section 4.3] "Fine-tuned version of opus-mt-fr-en on biomedical datasets led to the best results with a BLEU score of 0.51"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus shows related work on translation-based approaches but no direct BLEU validation for these specific models
- Break condition: If translation models achieve near-perfect BLEU scores (>0.9) on biomedical texts, the performance gap would likely disappear

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: UMLS synonym mismatches cause normalization errors independent of translation
- Mechanism: Multiple CUIs exist for semantically equivalent terms, and models may select wrong CUI even with correct mention extraction
- Core assumption: The UMLS contains synonymous terms with different CUIs that create ambiguity for normalization models
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Error analysis reveals that synonym mismatches in UMLS and abbreviations contribute to performance gaps"
  - [section 5.1.2] "24% and 39% of the terms found by the deep normalization algorithm and CODER respectively were actually synonyms but with two different UMLS CUIs"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus mentions UMLS-based normalization but doesn't quantify synonym mismatch impact
- Break condition: If UMLS were restructured to use single CUI per concept regardless of synonym variations

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning translation models on biomedical data improves translation quality and downstream extraction
- Mechanism: Domain-specific fine-tuning adapts translation models to medical terminology and phrasing patterns
- Core assumption: Translation models benefit from domain adaptation similar to other NLP models
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "A fine-tuned version of opus-mt-fr-en on the 2016 and 2019 Biomedical Translation shared tasks was also tested"
  - [section 4.3] "Fine-tuning on biomedical datasets led to the best results with a BLEU score of 0.51"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - corpus includes papers on domain adaptation of translation models
- Break condition: If fine-tuning provides negligible BLEU improvement (<0.05) on biomedical test sets

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - French text → Translation (if using English approach) → NER → Entity mentions → Normalization → CUIs
  - French text → NER → Entity mentions → Normalization → CUIs (native French approach)

- Critical path:
  1. French text → Translation (if using English approach)
  2. Translated text → NER → Entity mentions
  3. Entity mentions → Normalization → CUIs
  4. Document-level CUI prediction comparison

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Translation + English models vs Native French models: Translation adds error but leverages mature English tools
  - Single-step vs two-step normalization: MedCAT combines NER+normalization but less flexible than separate modules
  - Fine-tuning vs off-the-shelf: Fine-tuning improves domain performance but requires additional data and computation

- Failure signatures:
  - Low BLEU scores indicate translation problems that cascade to NER/normalization
  - High NER precision but low normalization recall suggests synonym/CUI mapping issues
  - Inconsistent performance across semantic groups indicates model bias or data imbalance

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare BLEU scores of baseline vs fine-tuned translation models on biomedical test sets
  2. Evaluate NER performance on gold-standard mentions to isolate normalization errors
  3. Test synonym resolution by manually verifying CUI assignments for ambiguous terms

## Limitations

- Narrow scope: Only French clinical notes evaluated, limiting generalization to other languages
- Translation approaches: Study doesn't explore alternative translation methods like multilingual models or back-translation
- Evaluation granularity: Document-level evaluation may mask important mention-level performance differences

## Confidence

- High confidence: Native French approach outperforming English translation methods (F1 0.51 vs 0.39-0.38)
- Medium confidence: Translation quality as primary limiting factor
- Low confidence: UMLS synonym mismatches as major error source
