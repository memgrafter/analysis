# Revisiting Sentence Union Generation as a Testbed for Text Consolidation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.15605
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15605
- **Reference count:** 18
- **Key outcome:** Sentence union task proposed as testbed for multi-text consolidation; PRIMERA achieves best results but only 29.7% fully correct unions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces sentence union generation as a testbed for multi-text consolidation, where models must merge two related sentences containing overlapping information into a single, non-redundant sentence. The authors create a new dataset of over 1,800 sentence pairs using refined crowdsourcing methods and establish a comprehensive evaluation protocol combining human assessment with automatic metrics like ROUGE, NLI, and compression rate. They evaluate state-of-the-art models (T5, PRIMERA, GPT-3) and find that while PRIMERA performs best, all models struggle with the task, highlighting the complexity of multi-text consolidation and identifying key challenges for future research.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces sentence union generation as a testbed for multi-text consolidation, where given two related sentences with overlapping information, the model must generate a single sentence containing all information without redundancy. The authors create a dataset from existing consolidation sources (ECB+, MultiNews, DUC/TAC) with 1,087 training, 349 dev, and 477 test examples. They establish evaluation protocols using human assessment (coverage, faithfulness, redundancy, fluency) and automatic metrics (ROUGE1, NLI using DeBERTa xxlarge v2 with MNLI threshold 0.5, and compression rate difference ∆CR). The models are fine-tuned on this dataset and evaluated using both human and automatic metrics, with detailed error analysis to understand consolidation challenges.

## Key Results
- PRIMERA achieves the best performance but only 29.7% of generated unions are fully correct
- Models struggle with uni-directional entailment relationships and incorrect entity/predicate attachments
- Human evaluation shows models often fail to properly consolidate information, with significant coverage and faithfulness issues
- Automatic metrics correlate imperfectly with human judgment, highlighting the need for comprehensive evaluation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The union task forces models to learn fine-grained informational relation extraction between sentences.
- **Mechanism:** By requiring the output to contain exactly all information from both source sentences without redundancy, the model must distinguish between equivalent, uni-directional entailing, and disjoint content spans.
- **Core assumption:** The semantic overlap between sentences is rich enough to require learning subtle lexical and syntactic entailment patterns.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "This approach enables targeted investigation of information union capabilities of models..."
  - [section 3]: "Identifying relevant informational links is crucial for producing a union, as demonstrated by the example in Fig. 2."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; dataset contains varied redundancy levels but the corpus neighbor analysis shows low FMR overlap with other consolidation works, suggesting niche focus.
- **Break condition:** If sentences are too disjoint or too similar, the consolidation signal becomes too weak to train on.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Fine-tuning large pretrained models on union data transfers cross-document consolidation skills to downstream tasks.
- **Mechanism:** Pretrained models already encode cross-sentence coherence; fine-tuning on union data refines this to handle multi-document consolidation without saliency bias.
- **Core assumption:** Cross-document pretraining (e.g., PRIMERA) improves consolidation ability beyond standard seq2seq models.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "we evaluate state-of-the-art language models on the task, along with a detailed analysis of their capacity to address multi-text consolidation challenges..."
  - [section 6]: "PRIMERA (Xiao et al., 2022), which was pretrained in a cross-document fashion... achieves state-of-the-art results over multi-document summarization datasets."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence; FMR scores suggest limited direct overlap with other consolidation datasets.
- **Break condition:** If the union dataset is too small or too homogeneous, transfer gains plateau.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The controlled annotation protocol yields higher-quality unions than crowdsourced consensus methods.
- **Mechanism:** Splitting the task into base selection, highlight identification, and writing reduces cognitive load and enforces consistency.
- **Core assumption:** Separating relation identification from writing improves precision in capturing entailment and equivalence relations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "we provide refined annotation methodology and tools for careful creation of a sentence union corpus."
  - [section 5.1]: "Our controlled process, which separates the identification of informational relations from the writing phase, results in higher quality sentence unions..."
  - [corpus]: Neighbor analysis shows no overlap with other union datasets, so no external quality comparison possible.
- **Break condition:** If workers skip too many examples, dataset size and diversity suffer.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Textual entailment and semantic equivalence detection
  - **Why needed here:** Union generation requires identifying when two spans convey the same information or when one entails the other.
  - **Quick check question:** Given sentences "The cat sat on the mat" and "A feline rested atop the rug", can you mark all spans that are semantically equivalent?

- **Concept:** Redundancy detection in text
  - **Why needed here:** The output must avoid repeating information even if expressed differently in source sentences.
  - **Quick check question:** In "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog. The brown fox leaps above the idle canine", which spans are redundant?

- **Concept:** Cross-document coreference resolution
  - **Why needed here:** Sentences from different documents may refer to the same entity with different surface forms.
  - **Quick check question:** If sentence 1 mentions "President Biden" and sentence 2 mentions "Joe Biden", should they be treated as the same entity in the union?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Input sentence pair -> Encoder (cross-doc pretrained transformer) -> Decoder (seq2seq model) -> Union sentence -> Evaluation (ROUGE/NLI/∆CR)

- **Critical path:**
  1. Load and tokenize sentence pair with special separator
  2. Encode into hidden states
  3. Decode union sentence autoregressively
  4. Evaluate with automatic metrics (ROUGE, NLI, ∆CR)
  5. Iterate fine-tuning on training split

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Longer input sequences improve context but increase memory cost
  - In-context learning (GPT-3) avoids fine-tuning but is less reliable
  - Fine-tuning on union data improves precision but risks overfitting to limited dataset size

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High NLI but low ROUGE → model outputs paraphrases not matching reference
  - High ROUGE but high ∆CR → model concatenates without consolidation
  - Low human scores despite high automatic metrics → automatic metrics insufficient

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Fine-tune T5-large on 25% of training data; evaluate on dev set with ROUGE/NLI/∆CR
  2. Fine-tune PRIMERA on 50% of training data; compare consolidation scores to T5
  3. Test GPT-3 with optimized prompt on full test set; compare human vs. automatic evaluation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the compression rate of union sentences change when moving from two-sentence inputs to three or more sentences?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper mentions that future research could expand the dataset to include consolidation beyond two input sentences, suggesting this hasn't been explored yet.
- **Why unresolved:** The current dataset and experiments are limited to two-sentence unions, so the impact of increasing input complexity hasn't been measured.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Creating a dataset with three or more sentences and comparing compression rates and model performance to the current two-sentence setup.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** Do explicit text consolidation structures improve multi-text consolidation performance in large language models?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper suggests examining "the use of explicit text consolidation structures for improving multi-text consolidation in large language models" as future research.
- **Why unresolved:** The current models rely on implicit consolidation without any structural guidance.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Training models with explicit consolidation structures (e.g., dependency trees, semantic graphs) and comparing their performance to baseline models.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How well do union generation models generalize across different domains beyond news articles?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper acknowledges that "Our dataset is also domain specific, in that all the sentences are taken from news sources. This might result in challenging cross-domain generalization."
- **Why unresolved:** The current dataset is limited to news sources, so domain adaptation hasn't been tested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Testing union generation models on datasets from other domains (scientific literature, social media, legal documents) and measuring performance degradation.

## Limitations
- Dataset size is limited (only 1,800 examples), potentially limiting model generalization
- Dataset is domain-specific (news sources), raising cross-domain generalization concerns
- Human evaluation was conducted on only 99 examples, which may not be representative

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in annotation methodology and dataset quality (supported by controlled creation process and detailed error analysis)
- **Medium confidence** in model evaluation results (limited by small dataset and potential overfitting to specific consolidation patterns)
- **Low confidence** in claims about downstream transfer benefits (no experiments validating application to other consolidation tasks)
