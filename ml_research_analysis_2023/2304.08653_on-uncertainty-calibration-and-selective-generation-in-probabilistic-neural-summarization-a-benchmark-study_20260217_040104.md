# On Uncertainty Calibration and Selective Generation in Probabilistic Neural Summarization: A Benchmark Study

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.08653
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08653
- **Reference count**: 19
- **Key outcome**: Probabilistic deep learning methods consistently improve summarization quality, uncertainty calibration, and selective generation performance.

## Executive Summary
This paper conducts an extensive benchmark study of probabilistic deep learning calibration methods applied to large language models for text summarization. The authors evaluate six probabilistic methods (SNGP, BE, MCD, DE, SNGP+MCD, SNGP+DE) across three summarization datasets (XSUM, CNN/DM, RedditTIFU) using the T5-base model. They demonstrate that probabilistic methods consistently improve summarization quality, uncertainty calibration, and enable effective selective generation where models can abstain from low-quality summaries. The study reveals important failure patterns, showing that multi-model methods like Deep Ensembles can cause underconfidence on simple tasks while performing well on challenging ones.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes T5-base on three summarization datasets, then applies probabilistic methods that generate multiple samples from the model's posterior distribution and average predictions to mitigate overconfidence. The methods include single-model approaches (SNGP with Gaussian process last layer, Bayesian Embedding, Monte Carlo Dropout) and multi-model approaches (Deep Ensembles, SNGP combinations). Inference uses beam search with beam_size=3, and length-normalized log-probabilities serve as uncertainty estimates. The evaluation pipeline measures summarization quality (ROUGE-1/2/L), uncertainty calibration (ECE), and selective generation performance through quality vs abstention curves.

## Key Results
- Probabilistic methods consistently improve ROUGE scores across all three datasets compared to deterministic T5 baseline
- SNGP and SNGP combinations achieve the best trade-off between summarization quality and uncertainty calibration
- Well-calibrated models successfully perform selective generation, abstaining from low-quality summaries based on uncertainty thresholds
- Deep Ensembles cause underconfidence on simple CNN/DM dataset while improving performance on challenging XSUM and RedditTIFU

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Probabilistic methods improve summarization quality by averaging multiple plausible predictions from the model posterior, mitigating individual model overconfidence.
- Mechanism: Methods like SNGP, BE, MCD, and DE generate multiple samples from the model's posterior predictive distribution and average the outputs, creating an ensemble-like effect that smooths overconfident predictions.
- Core assumption: Averaging multiple samples from the model posterior leads to predictions that better reflect true uncertainty and improve both quality and calibration.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] states "probabilitistic DNNs generates and multiple plausible predictions from its posterior predictive and report the average, thereby mitigating the overconﬁdence"; [section 2] notes methods "can be applied to large neural models without sacriﬁcing performance".
- Break condition: If the model posterior is too narrow or samples are highly correlated, averaging will not reduce overconfidence.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Improved uncertainty calibration enables better selective generation by allowing models to abstain from low-quality summaries.
- Mechanism: Well-calibrated models assign higher uncertainty to low-quality outputs, enabling selective generation through thresholding on predicted log-probabilities.
- Core assumption: Strong rank correlation exists between model-predicted uncertainty and actual summary quality (ROUGE scores).
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] shows "probabilistic methods consistently improve the model's generation and uncertainty quality, leading to improved selective generation performance"; [section 4.4] explains selective generation procedures and expected high uncertainty for low-quality outputs.
- Break condition: If rank correlation between uncertainty and quality is weak or negative, selective generation will fail.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SNGP and its combinations (SNGP+MCD, SNGP+DE) consistently achieve the best trade-off between summarization quality and uncertainty calibration.
- Mechanism: SNGP transforms neural networks into approximate Gaussian process models, better reflecting distance between test examples and training set for improved uncertainty estimates.
- Core assumption: Gaussian process last layer in SNGP captures meaningful uncertainty signals that correlate with summary quality.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] reveals "notable failure patterns of probabilistic methods widely-adopted in NLP community (e.g., Deep Ensemble and Monte Carlo Dropout)"; [section 3] describes SNGP's transformation into approximate Gaussian process model.
- Break condition: If dataset is too simple (e.g., CNN/DM), single models already perform well and ensembles may cause underconfidence.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: T5 base model → probabilistic method wrapper (SNGP, BE, MCD, DE, SNGP+*) → beam search decoder → evaluation pipeline (ROUGE, ECE, selective generation curves)
- **Critical path**: Data → T5 fine-tuning → probabilistic method application → inference with beam search → evaluation (quality + calibration + selective generation)
- **Design tradeoffs**: Single-model methods (SNGP, BE, MCD) are faster but may underfit complex tasks; multi-model methods (DE, SNGP+DE) are more accurate but computationally expensive; SNGP adds Gaussian process layer increasing memory usage but improving calibration.
- **Failure signatures**: CNN/DM shows no benefit from multi-model methods → task is too simple; MCD degrades performance on Reddit → high linguistic diversity breaks MCD assumptions; Deep ensembles cause underconfidence on easy tasks → overconfidence in single model is beneficial.
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Run deterministic T5 baseline on XSUM, CNN/DM, Reddit → establish ROUGE and ECE baselines; 2) Apply SNGP to T5 → compare ROUGE, ECE, and selective generation vs baseline; 3) Apply SNGP+MCD → compare ROUGE, ECE, and selective generation vs SNGP alone.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How do uncertainty calibration methods perform on even larger language models (e.g., T5-XXL or GPT-3)? The paper only tested T5 base model and mentioned methods "can be applied to larger models" in a footnote without verification.
- **Open Question 2**: Do calibration improvements generalize to other generation tasks beyond summarization, such as machine translation or dialogue? The paper focuses exclusively on summarization benchmarks without testing other NLP tasks.
- **Open Question 3**: How does the choice of beam size affect calibration and selective generation performance? The paper states they used beam_size=3 but does not explore how different beam sizes impact results.
- **Open Question 4**: Can calibration methods be effectively combined with other training objectives beyond MLE to improve performance? The paper notes MLE tends to overfit and may limit calibration improvements but does not explore alternative training objectives.

## Limitations

- Multi-model methods show no benefit on simple CNN/DM dataset, suggesting effectiveness depends on task difficulty and single model overconfidence.
- Monte Carlo Dropout degrades performance on highly diverse, informal text (RedditTIFU), indicating method effectiveness varies with dataset characteristics.
- Computational cost of multi-model approaches (Deep Ensembles, SNGP combinations) is not discussed, raising deployment concerns.
- Analysis is limited to T5-base architecture, restricting generalizability to other model families or larger variants.

## Confidence

- **Summarization Quality Improvements**: High confidence - Multiple ROUGE score improvements reported across three datasets and six methods with consistent patterns.
- **Uncertainty Calibration Gains**: Medium confidence - ECE improvements reported but computation details for sequence-level predictions not fully specified, and improvement magnitude varies across datasets.
- **Selective Generation Performance**: Medium confidence - Rank correlation between uncertainty and ROUGE scores shows promise, but depends on length-normalized log-probabilities as uncertainty proxy.
- **Method Effectiveness Rankings**: Medium confidence - SNGP combinations consistently rank well, but failure patterns (MCD on Reddit, DE on CNN/DM) show effectiveness is context-dependent.

## Next Checks

1. **Dataset Difficulty Validation**: Replicate study using progressively more difficult summarization tasks (XSUM → RedditTIFU → more complex dataset) to confirm multi-model methods only show benefits on sufficiently challenging tasks where single models are overconfident.

2. **Cross-Architecture Generalization**: Apply probabilistic methods to T5-large or other transformer architectures (BART, Pegasus) to verify whether calibration improvements generalize beyond T5-base or are architecture-specific.

3. **Real-World Deployment Test**: Implement selective generation system in realistic setting where model must decide whether to generate or abstain on new inputs, measuring actual quality-quality trade-off achieved in practice versus theoretical curves.
