# Uncertainty Quantification in Deep Neural Networks through Statistical Inference on Latent Space

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10840
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10840
- Authors:
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: Introduces latent-space Gaussian modeling to detect misclassifications and out-of-distribution samples with >95% TN-OOD and ~75% TP on MNIST.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method to quantify uncertainty in deep neural network classifiers by modeling the latent-space distributions of correctly classified training samples per class. For each class in each layer, a multivariate Gaussian is fit to the latent vectors of correctly classified samples. New inputs are evaluated by computing likelihoods under each model and combining confidences across layers with a smoothstep function. Experiments on MNIST show strong performance in detecting out-of-distribution examples, achieving over 95% true negative detection while maintaining about 75% true positive detection. The method is computationally efficient and does not require multiple models or dropout during inference.

## Method Summary
The approach builds a multivariate Gaussian model for each class in each layer of the network, based on the latent vectors of correctly classified training points. For a new input, it computes the likelihood under each model and combines confidences across layers using a smooth step function. This allows detection of misclassifications and out-of-distribution samples. Experiments on MNIST show that the method outperforms MC-dropout and ensemble baselines, especially in identifying out-of-distribution examples, achieving over 95% true negative detection while maintaining about 75% true positive detection. The method is computationally efficient and does not require multiple models or dropout during inference.

## Key Results
- On MNIST, the method achieves >95% true negative detection and ~75% true positive detection for out-of-distribution samples.
- Outperforms MC-dropout and ensemble baselines in identifying out-of-distribution examples.
- Computationally efficient, requiring no multiple models or dropout during inference.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The method captures epistemic uncertainty by modeling the latent-space distributions of correctly classified training samples per class.
- Mechanism: For each class in each layer, the latent vectors of correctly classified training samples are used to fit a multivariate Gaussian. The likelihood of a new input under each model, combined across layers, reflects how typical the input is relative to known patterns.
- Core assumption: The latent representations of correctly classified samples from the same class form a well-behaved (approximately Gaussian) distribution, and misclassified or out-of-distribution inputs produce latent vectors that fall outside this distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Using the latent-space representation generated by the fraction of training set that the network classifies correctly, we build a statistical model that is able to capture the likelihood of a given prediction."
  - [section] "we build a statistical model that can be used to estimate the likelihood of a certain prediction. The underlying assumption is that the latent representation of a well-classified input vector will be similar to the latent representations in the corresponding latent confidence set, whereas the latent representation of a misclassified input vector will be dissimilar."
- Break condition: If the latent space for a class is multimodal or contains significant overlap between classes, the Gaussian assumption fails and the model may incorrectly estimate likelihoods.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The smoothstep function normalizes log-likelihoods to a confidence score that discriminates between in-distribution and out-of-distribution samples.
- Mechanism: Log-probabilities from the Gaussian models are binned into a histogram; percentiles α and β define low and high confidence thresholds. The smoothstep function maps values in between to a continuous confidence score between 0 and 1.
- Core assumption: The distribution of log-probabilities for correctly classified samples is sufficiently separated from those of misclassified or out-of-distribution samples that percentile thresholds can be meaningfully chosen.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we construct a histogram of the log-probabilities... and use it to identify the α-th percentile q_l,k_α and the β-th percentile q_l,k_β. Log-probabilities below or equal to q_l,k_α are assigned a confidence value of zero, while those above or equal to q_l,k_β are assigned a confidence value of one."
- Break condition: If the overlap between correctly classified and misclassified log-probabilities is large, percentile-based thresholds cannot reliably separate them.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multiplying layer-wise confidences under an independence assumption yields a final confidence that is sensitive to out-of-distribution inputs.
- Mechanism: Each layer produces a confidence score; these are multiplied together to obtain a final confidence, assuming statistical independence across layers.
- Core assumption: The latent representations in different layers are statistically independent given the class label.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we assume that the probability distribution of the latent representations of the same input vector but in different layers are statistically independent."
- Break condition: If the independence assumption is violated (e.g., strong correlations between layers), the product of confidences may misrepresent the true uncertainty.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preparation -> Model training -> Latent extraction -> Confidence set building -> Gaussian fitting -> Percentile calculation -> Inference

- Critical path:
  1. Train the base neural network.
  2. Extract latent representations from correctly classified training samples.
  3. Fit Gaussian models and compute percentiles.
  4. Evaluate new inputs by computing confidence scores.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Gaussian assumption vs. more flexible distributions (e.g., mixture models).
  - Independence assumption vs. modeling inter-layer correlations.
  - Fixed confidence threshold (0.5) vs. adaptive thresholds based on operating conditions.

- Failure signatures:
  - High false positive rate: Confidence scores are not discriminating between correctly and incorrectly classified samples.
  - High false negative rate: Model fails to detect out-of-distribution samples.
  - Computational bottleneck: Gaussian fitting and percentile calculation scale poorly with very large latent spaces.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a small feedforward network (e.g., 2 hidden layers, 1024 nodes each) on MNIST, then apply the uncertainty quantification method to a held-out test set.
  2. Repeat the experiment with a deeper network (e.g., 4 hidden layers, 256 nodes each) and compare results.
  3. Remove one class from the training set, train the network, and test the method's ability to detect out-of-distribution samples corresponding to the removed class.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the method perform on datasets with more complex, non-Gaussian latent space distributions, and what alternative priors might be more appropriate?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge the method assumes Gaussian distributions in latent space and note it might not hold in more complex scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only tested on MNIST, a relatively simple dataset. No tests were conducted on datasets with known non-Gaussian latent structures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the method on datasets known for non-Gaussian latent representations (e.g., certain image or text datasets) and comparing performance with methods using alternative priors.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the assumption of statistical independence across layers affect the quality of uncertainty estimates in very deep networks, and can this assumption be relaxed or validated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state the assumption of independence among layer probabilities is strong and could impact results in networks with many layers.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses relatively shallow networks (2-4 layers) and does not test deeper architectures where this assumption might break down.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the method on very deep networks and testing variants that model layer dependencies, then comparing uncertainty quality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the method be extended to regression tasks where labels are continuous, and what strategy would be used to define similarity for building statistical models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose extending the method to continuous-value prediction problems and suggest using a statistical model based on training vectors with similar predictions.
- Why unresolved: The method is only demonstrated for classification; no experiments or formal framework for regression is provided.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing and testing the method on regression benchmarks, defining and validating similarity metrics, and comparing with existing UQ methods for regression.

## Limitations
- The Gaussian assumption for latent-space distributions may fail for complex, multimodal data.
- The independence assumption across layers is strong and may not hold in deep networks.
- Experimental validation is limited to MNIST, a simple dataset with low intra-class variability.

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the core mechanism of using latent-space likelihoods for uncertainty quantification, as the statistical foundation is sound and the method is clearly defined.
- **Medium confidence** in the comparative performance claims against MC-dropout and ensemble baselines, given the limited dataset scope and lack of ablation studies on architectural choices (layer count, node numbers, percentile thresholds).
- **Low confidence** in the scalability and robustness of the method to more complex datasets or networks with deeper or convolutional architectures, as these were not tested.

## Next Checks
1. Test the method on a more complex dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10 or Fashion-MNIST) with higher intra-class variability to assess robustness of the Gaussian assumption.
2. Conduct an ablation study to determine the sensitivity of performance to the number of layers used, the choice of percentile thresholds (α, β), and the independence assumption.
3. Compare the method's calibration and reliability against modern uncertainty quantification approaches such as evidential deep learning or ensemble methods on the same tasks.
