# Sparsified Model Zoo Twins: Investigating Populations of Sparsified Neural Network Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13718
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13718
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MP outperforms VD up to 80% sparsity, with original and sparsified performance highly correlated (r > 90%)

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates sparsification methods across large populations of neural networks by creating "sparsified model zoo twins" from 24 original model zoos. Two popular sparsification methods—Magnitude Pruning (MP) and Variational Dropout (VD)—are applied across 23,920 models, resulting in 1,726,000 documented model states. The study finds both methods perform robustly, with MP outperforming VD up to 80% sparsity. The authors demonstrate that original and sparsified model performance are highly correlated, enabling selection of good sparsification candidates before pruning. All models and sparsified versions are publicly available at modelzoos.cc.

## Method Summary
The study applies Magnitude Pruning (MP) and Variational Dropout (VD) to 24 model zoos containing 23,920 models across six datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, USPS, CIFAR-10, and STL-10). MP is applied at sparsity levels from 10% to 90% with 15 epochs of fine-tuning, while VD runs for 25 epochs with reduced learning rate, pruning weights with α≥3. The authors evaluate test accuracy, sparsity ratios, generalization gaps, and model agreement between original and sparsified models. Hyper-representations are learned on the weight spaces using an autoencoder with a novel disentanglement loss to analyze the structure of sparsified model populations.

## Key Results
- MP outperforms VD up to 80% sparsity, with both methods showing robust performance across populations
- Original and sparsified model performance are highly correlated (Pearson's r > 90%), enabling pre-selection of good sparsification candidates
- Larger layers consistently achieve higher sparsity ratios across all sparsified model zoos
- Hyper-representations reveal disentangled latent structures for accuracy and sparsity in the model population

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Model zoo generation (original models) -> Sparsification application (MP and VD) -> Performance evaluation (accuracy, sparsity, agreement) -> Hyper-representation learning (AE with novel loss) -> Correlation analysis

- Critical path:
  1. Load original model zoos
  2. Apply sparsification methods at multiple sparsity levels
  3. Fine-tune pruned models (MP only)
  4. Evaluate performance metrics
  5. Compute agreement between original and sparsified models
  6. Train hyper-representations on weight spaces
  7. Analyze correlation and latent structure

- Design tradeoffs:
  - MP vs VD: MP is simpler and faster but may degrade at very high sparsity; VD is more complex but potentially more stable at extreme sparsification levels.
  - Fine-tuning vs no fine-tuning: MP requires fine-tuning which adds computational cost but recovers accuracy; VD integrates training into the sparsification process.

- Failure signatures:
  - If accuracy drops sharply at moderate sparsity levels, either the sparsification method is too aggressive or the model zoo lacks redundancy.
  - If agreement between original and sparsified models is low, the sparsification method may be removing functionally important parameters.
  - If hyper-representation reconstruction fails, the weight space may lack sufficient structure for meaningful analysis.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply MP and VD to a small subset of models from one zoo and verify accuracy retention at 50% sparsity.
  2. Compare layer-wise sparsity patterns between MP and VD on the same subset to confirm the "larger layers pruned more" hypothesis.
  3. Compute agreement between original and sparsified models to validate the high agreement claim before scaling up.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How do the two sparsification methods (MP and VD) perform on other types of neural network architectures beyond the small CNNs used in this study?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The study focuses exclusively on small CNNs trained on image classification tasks, with no exploration of other architectures.
  - Why unresolved: The paper explicitly states that the results are limited to small CNNs and does not investigate other network types like transformers, RNNs, or larger CNNs.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Direct application and comparison of MP and VD on diverse architectures (transformers, RNNs, larger CNNs) across various tasks would provide the needed evidence.

- Question: What is the relationship between layer size and sparsity in deeper or wider neural networks?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The study finds that larger layers consistently achieve higher sparsity in small CNNs, but this pattern may not hold for deeper or wider networks.
  - Why unresolved: The paper only examines small CNNs with a fixed architecture, so the relationship between layer size and sparsity in more complex architectures remains unknown.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic analysis of sparsity patterns across layers in deeper and wider neural networks would clarify this relationship.

- Question: How does the performance correlation between original and sparsified models change with different training datasets and domains?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The study shows high correlation between original and sparsified performance across six image datasets but doesn't explore other domains.
  - Why unresolved: The paper only tests on image classification datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, USPS, CIFAR-10, STL-10) without investigating other domains like NLP or speech.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Testing the correlation on datasets from different domains (NLP, speech, reinforcement learning) would reveal if the high correlation generalizes beyond image tasks.

## Limitations
- Results are limited to small CNN architectures and may not generalize to other model families like transformers or larger vision networks
- The exact implementation details of Variational Dropout and original training schedules are not fully specified, potentially affecting reproducibility
- The study focuses exclusively on unstructured pruning methods, missing insights from structured approaches that might better suit hardware acceleration

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The robust performance of MP and VD across populations (60-80% agreement, r > 0.90 correlation) - supported by extensive experiments across 23,920 models
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that larger layers consistently achieve higher sparsity - observed pattern but lacks mechanistic explanation and may vary with architecture
- **Medium Confidence**: The disentanglement of latent representations for accuracy and sparsity - qualitative findings from hyper-representations need quantitative validation on held-out data
