# Transferring Neural Potentials For High Order Dependency Parsing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10469
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10469
- Reference count: 3
- This paper addresses high-order dependency parsing by integrating biaffine neural potentials with graphical models.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles high-order dependency parsing by leveraging biaffine neural potentials within a factor graph framework. The method integrates first-order biaffine scores with high-order constraints such as grandparents and consecutive siblings, using dual decomposition and ADMM for MAP inference. The model is trained end-to-end, coupling neural arc scores with global structural constraints. Experiments on the English Universal Dependencies dataset show that while first-order parsing achieves up to 93.2% accuracy, high-order parsing yields lower results (65.3%) due to fewer higher-order constraints used for speed.

## Method Summary
The approach uses biaffine scores as unary arc potentials in a factor graph, combining them with high-order features (grandparents and consecutive siblings) represented as binary potentials. MAP inference is performed via dual decomposition with ADMM to enforce global constraints. The model is trained end-to-end with Adam (or SGD) for 10 epochs, batch sizes 5-20, on the English Universal Dependencies dataset (sentences up to 60 tokens). Dual decomposition and ADMM are used to solve the inference problem and couple neural potentials with structural constraints.

## Key Results
- First-order parsing achieves up to 93.2% accuracy on UD English.
- High-order parsing with limited constraints yields 65.3% accuracy, attributed to fewer higher-order constraints for speed.
- End-to-end training couples neural biaffine scores with global structural constraints, showing the feasibility of integrating neural potentials with high-order graphical models.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by transferring neural biaffine potentials into a factor graph and using dual decomposition with ADMM to solve MAP inference while enforcing high-order structural constraints. This allows the model to leverage global dependencies (like grandparent and sibling relationships) that are not captured by first-order parsing alone. The end-to-end training couples neural scores with these constraints, enabling the model to learn from both local and global information.

## Foundational Learning
- **Biaffine scoring**: Computes arc scores between head and dependent representations using multi-layer perceptrons. Needed for accurate first-order arc prediction.
  - Quick check: Verify that biaffine scores match those from standard parsers on validation data.
- **Factor graph construction**: Encodes dependencies as nodes and potentials as factors, including unary (arc) and binary (high-order) potentials.
  - Quick check: Ensure factor graph structure matches intended constraints (grandparents, siblings).
- **Dual decomposition**: Decomposes global inference into sub-problems for each constraint type, then coordinates solutions.
  - Quick check: Monitor convergence of dual variables and agreement between sub-problems.
- **ADMM algorithm**: Solves the decomposed inference by alternating updates to primal and dual variables.
  - Quick check: Track objective value and constraint satisfaction over iterations.
- **End-to-end training**: Jointly updates neural parameters and constraint weights using backpropagation.
  - Quick check: Monitor loss and accuracy on both first-order and high-order metrics.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:**
Biaffine network (head, dependent MLPs) -> Unary potentials (arc scores) -> Factor graph (grandparent, sibling constraints) -> Dual decomposition + ADMM (MAP inference) -> End-to-end training (Adam/SGD)

**Critical path:**
Neural biaffine scores → Unary potentials → Factor graph → Dual decomposition/ADMM → MAP parse

**Design tradeoffs:**
- High-order constraints improve global structure but increase inference complexity; limiting them for speed reduces accuracy.
- End-to-end training couples neural and structural learning but requires careful tuning of dual decomposition hyperparameters.
- Using only grandparent and sibling constraints simplifies the factor graph but may miss other useful high-order features.

**Failure signatures:**
- Training fails to converge or accuracy drops if high-order constraints are too sparse or not well-chosen.
- Poor generalization on longer sentences (>60 tokens) due to limited training data and computational complexity.

**First experiments:**
1. Train and evaluate only the biaffine parser (first-order) on UD English to verify 93.2% accuracy baseline.
2. Add grandparent constraints to the factor graph and verify that dual decomposition with ADMM converges and improves over first-order.
3. Add sibling constraints and measure impact on accuracy and inference time.

## Limitations
- The drop to 65.3% accuracy for high-order parsing is attributed to fewer constraints for speed, but the paper does not provide details on how constraints were selected or their impact on parsing quality.
- The exact neural network architecture and dual decomposition/ADMM hyperparameters are not specified, making faithful reproduction difficult.
- The model's performance on longer sentences (beyond 60 tokens) is not reported, raising questions about scalability and generalization.

## Confidence
- First-order biaffine parser accuracy (93.2%): High
- High-order parsing accuracy (65.3%) and explanation: Low
- End-to-end joint training with dual decomposition/ADMM: Medium

## Next Checks

3. Train and evaluate on sentences of increasing length (e.g., 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 tokens) to confirm that accuracy degradation is not due to handling longer sentences rather than constraint limitations.
