# KoSBi: A Dataset for Mitigating Social Bias Risks Towards Safer Large Language Model Application

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17701
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17701
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 22
- Primary result: 34k Korean social bias dataset with 72 demographic groups; filtering approach reduces unsafe content by 16.47% on average

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces KOSBI, a large-scale social bias dataset for Korean language and culture, constructed through human-LLM collaboration. The dataset contains 34,214 context-sentence pairs covering 72 demographic groups across 15 categories, with annotations for safety and bias types. The authors demonstrate that a filtering-based moderation approach trained on KOSBI can significantly reduce social biases in generated content from both Korean and English language models.

## Method Summary
The authors employ a human-LLM collaboration framework where HyperCLOVA generates context-sentence pairs for 72 demographic groups using in-context few-shot learning, followed by human annotation for safety labels. A safe sentence classifier is trained on this data using KcELECTRA, achieving 71.21% macro-F1. The moderation pipeline generates multiple sentences per context and selects the safest one based on classifier predictions, reducing unsafe content by 16.47% on average across HyperCLOVA and GPT-3 models.

## Key Results
- KOSBI dataset contains 34,214 context-sentence pairs covering 72 demographic groups across 15 categories
- Safe sentence classifier achieves 71.21% macro-F1 on the test set
- Filtering-based moderation reduces unsafe content by 16.47% on average across HyperCLOVA (30B, 82B) and GPT-3 models
- Moderation approach maintains competitive quality scores while slightly sacrificing coherency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The dataset construction pipeline reduces social bias by conditioning LLM-generated content on demographic groups and filtering unsafe outputs using trained classifiers.
- Mechanism: By prompting HyperCLOVA to generate context-sentence pairs for each of 72 demographic groups, and then filtering these generations using classifier models, the dataset ensures coverage of nuanced bias types (stereotypes, prejudice, discrimination) specific to Korean society. Filtering ambiguous samples improves classifier difficulty and model safety.
- Core assumption: HyperCLOVA can generate contextually relevant sentences for each demographic group without requiring large-scale web crawling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "constructed using a human-LLM collaboration framework, where HyperCLOVA generates contexts and sentences, and human annotators label them as safe or unsafe."
  - [section 3.2] "we leveraged HyperCLOVA to generate the data with in-context few-shot learning."
  - [corpus] Weak - dataset content not publicly available for independent verification.
- Break condition: If HyperCLOVA's generation is biased by default, the initial pool may be skewed toward unsafe content, limiting dataset quality.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Filtering-based moderation using a trained safe sentence classifier reduces unsafe content in LLM generations.
- Mechanism: The safe sentence classifier (trained on KOSBI) evaluates multiple generations per context and selects the safest one, improving overall safety scores and reducing unsafe content by ~16.47%.
- Core assumption: The classifier can accurately distinguish between safe and unsafe sentences in Korean, including context-dependent cases.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we then demonstrate that a filtering-based moderation approach, trained on KOSBI, can reduce social biases in generated content by 16.47% on average."
  - [section 4.3] "With the filtering process, we find that the ratio of unsafe generations decreases for all models by 16.47%p on average."
  - [corpus] Weak - classifier performance (71.21% macro-F1) suggests moderate difficulty, but no external validation provided.
- Break condition: If the classifier is over-conservative, it may reject safe but ambiguous sentences, reducing diversity and coherency.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Human-LLM collaboration enables scalable dataset creation for underrepresented demographic groups.
- Mechanism: Using HyperCLOVA for initial data generation, followed by human annotation, allows creation of 34k pairs covering 72 groups, which would be infeasible via manual annotation alone.
- Core assumption: Human annotators can reliably label subtle bias types (stereotypes, prejudice, discrimination) in Korean cultural contexts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "The contexts and sentences were then labeled by crowd workers according to the following guidelines..."
  - [section 3.4] "KOSBI consists of 34,214 context-sentence pairs... covering 72 targeted demographic groups in 15 categories."
  - [corpus] Weak - no inter-annotator agreement metrics for subclass labels; only binary agreement reported.
- Break condition: If annotator disagreement is high for subclass labels, the dataset may lack consistency for fine-grained bias mitigation.

  - Why needed here: Allows precise labeling of bias types (stereotypes, prejudice, discrimination) relevant to Korean society.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Dataset generation (HyperCLOVA prompt → context-sentence pairs) → Human annotation (binary + subclass labels) → Filter model training (safe sentence classifier) → Moderation pipeline (generate k sentences → classify → select safest)
- Critical path: HyperCLOVA generation → Annotation → Classifier training → Moderation evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: Automated generation reduces cost but risks bias propagation; human annotation ensures cultural relevance but limits scalability
- Failure signatures: Low classifier accuracy on test set; high annotator disagreement; filtered generations lack coherence

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: What is the correlation between the performance of the safe sentence classifier and the effectiveness of filter-based moderation across different demographic categories?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that there could be a correlation between the performance of the filter model and the moderation efficacy, but it was not strongly observed in their experiments.
  - Why unresolved: The authors did not find a strong correlation, possibly due to the small differences in accuracy across categories or the sampled set not being large enough. Further analysis is expected in future work.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with a larger and more diverse dataset, and analyzing the correlation between the classifier's accuracy and the moderation efficacy across different demographic categories.

- Question: How can the safe sentence classifier be improved to achieve higher accuracy on the KOSBI dataset?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the performance of the filter models for harmless sentence classification is not very competitive and leaves it as a future research topic to make a filter classifier with higher accuracy on their dataset.
  - Why unresolved: The authors focused on demonstrating the efficacy of their dataset and filter-based moderation approach, rather than optimizing the classifier's performance.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experimenting with different model architectures, training techniques, and data augmentation methods to improve the classifier's accuracy on the KOSBI dataset.

- Question: How does the performance of the safe sentence classifier generalize to other languages and cultures beyond Korean?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the Korean-specific nature of the KOSBI dataset and mentions that their dataset construction and evaluation protocol can contribute to a helpful guide for other research groups on AI safety to build datasets for their cultures and languages.
  - Why unresolved: The KOSBI dataset is specifically designed for Korean language and culture, and its performance on other languages and cultures is not addressed in the paper.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to evaluate the safe sentence classifier's performance on datasets from other languages and cultures, and analyzing the transferability of the model's knowledge.

- Question: How does the filter-based moderation approach affect the diversity and creativity of the generated content?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the filter-based moderation approach slightly sacrifices the coherency of generation as a side effect of enhancing safety.
  - Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the safety improvements achieved by the filter-based moderation, but does not thoroughly investigate its impact on content diversity and creativity.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the generated content with and without filtering, and evaluating metrics such as diversity, novelty, and coherence to understand the trade-offs between safety and content quality.

## Limitations

- Dataset lacks external validation - no comparison with alternative bias mitigation approaches or evaluation on non-Korean models
- Filtering approach may over-penalize ambiguous but contextually appropriate content, though this tradeoff is not thoroughly quantified
- Classifier's 71.21% macro-F1 score indicates moderate difficulty, but paper doesn't explore whether simpler approaches could achieve similar results

## Confidence

- High confidence: The dataset construction pipeline and basic filtering approach are well-documented and reproducible. The 16.47% reduction in unsafe content is empirically demonstrated.
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of the moderation approach on real-world applications, as the evaluation focuses on controlled generation rather than user-facing scenarios.
- Low confidence: The generalizability of the approach to languages and cultures beyond Korean, as the methodology relies heavily on cultural context and language-specific annotations.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-cultural validation**: Test the moderation pipeline on multilingual models (e.g., GPT-4) using translated KOSBI samples to assess generalizability.
