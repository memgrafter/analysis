# Call for Papers -- The BabyLM Challenge: Sample-efficient pretraining on a developmentally plausible corpus

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.11796
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11796
- **Reference count:** 4
- **Key result:** Introduces a challenge to develop sample-efficient language models using developmentally plausible datasets of 10M and 100M words

## Executive Summary
The BabyLM Challenge is a research initiative designed to incentivize sample-efficient pretraining of language models using developmentally plausible datasets. The challenge addresses the growing size of language models by exploring how to achieve strong performance with limited training data that mimics child language acquisition. Participants can choose from three tracks: training on a 10M word corpus, a 100M word corpus, or innovating with data choice and domain while maintaining a 100M word limit. The challenge aims to democratize research on pretraining, enable novel techniques for low-resource NLP, and develop more cognitively plausible models of human language acquisition.

## Method Summary
The BabyLM Challenge provides participants with fixed datasets of 10M and 100M words sourced from child-directed speech, transcribed speech, children's books, and Wikipedia. These datasets are designed to be developmentally plausible, mirroring the linguistic input a child might receive. Participants are encouraged to explore various approaches including architectural variations, self-supervised objectives, and curriculum learning. A third track allows participants to innovate with data choice, domain, and modality while maintaining the 100M word limit. All participants will be evaluated on a shared benchmark that includes targeted syntactic evaluations and natural language understanding tasks to ensure fair comparison across different approaches.

## Key Results
- Establishes a standardized platform for exploring sample-efficient language model pretraining
- Provides developmentally plausible datasets that mimic child language acquisition
- Creates a shared benchmark for fair comparison across different modeling approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The BabyLM Challenge works by creating a controlled research environment with fixed datasets that encourage innovation in model architecture and training approaches. By limiting the available data to 10M or 100M words, the challenge forces researchers to focus on efficiency rather than scale. The developmental plausibility of the datasets provides a cognitively grounded framework that may lead to models that better reflect human language acquisition processes. The shared benchmark ensures that innovations can be fairly compared and validated across different approaches.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data preprocessing -> Model architecture selection -> Training with self-supervised objectives -> Curriculum application -> Evaluation on shared benchmark
- **Critical path**: Dataset selection → Model design → Training optimization → Benchmark evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**: Smaller models with better data efficiency vs larger models with more parameters; developmental plausibility vs raw performance
- **Failure signatures**: Overfitting on limited data; poor generalization to unseen linguistic structures; failure to capture long-range dependencies
