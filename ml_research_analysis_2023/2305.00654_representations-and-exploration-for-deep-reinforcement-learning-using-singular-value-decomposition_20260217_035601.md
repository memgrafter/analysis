## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenges of representation learning and exploration in deep reinforcement learning, particularly for environments with rich observations and partial observability. The proposed De-Rex method uses singular value decomposition (SVD) to learn state representations that preserve transition structure and capture state visitation frequency for exploration bonuses. The approach is extended to partially observable settings using predictive state representations and employs mini-batch optimization with double-sampling for unbiased gradients. Experiments on DM-Lab-30 and DM-Hard-8 environments demonstrate significant improvements over baselines.

## Method Summary
De-Rex combines representation learning with exploration by decomposing a weighted transition matrix using SVD to obtain state representations that preserve transition structure. The method learns a function fθ(s) such that the singular value decomposition of the expected outer product DPπ = fθ(s)fθ(s)ᵀpπ(s) captures both transition dynamics and visitation frequency. For exploration, De-Rex uses the inverse of singular values to compute pseudo-counts that serve as exploration bonuses. The approach is extended to partially observable environments using predictive state representations, where histories and tests replace states in the SVD. Mini-batch optimization with double-sampling ensures unbiased gradient estimates for the decomposition loss.

## Key Results
- De-Rex outperforms baselines in representation quality on DM-Lab-30 environments
- Method solves nearly four times more hard exploration tasks than competitors on DM-Hard-8
- Unified framework provides both representation learning and exploration without negative sampling or target networks

## Limitations
- Generalizability concerns to extremely large state spaces where SVD may become computationally prohibitive
- Stability issues with double-sampling approach in high-variance environments
- Reliance on POMDP assumptions of low-rank dynamics may limit applicability to domains with complex transition structures
