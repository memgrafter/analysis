# Benchmarking Robustness of Deep Reinforcement Learning approaches to Online Portfolio Management

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.10950
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10950
- Reference count: 19
- The paper benchmarks robustness of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithms for online portfolio management.

## Executive Summary
This paper benchmarks 64 DRL models for online portfolio management using various combinations of market representations, reward functions, and learning algorithms on S&P500 stock data. While most models achieve similar returns of 23-28% over two years, their performance degrades significantly on out-of-distribution data, indicating overfitting. The best-performing approach uses daily prices, episodic rate of return reward, and DDPG algorithm, but all models show poor generalization and declining performance over time, suggesting DRL algorithms are not yet robust enough for reliable portfolio management.

## Method Summary
The study trains and evaluates 64 DRL models using combinations of four market representations (daily prices, sliding window, sparse window, technical indicators), four reward functions (daily returns, episodic returns, rate of return, Sortino), and four DRL algorithms (DDPG, PPO, A2C, SAC). Models are trained on S&P500 data from 2010-2022 using 60-day random train/validation splits, then backtested on 2021-2022 data with 20 diverse assets. Hyperparameters are optimized using Optuna TPE, and performance is evaluated using Rate of Return, Sortino ratio, Maximum Drawdown, CVaR, Information Ratio, IR trend, and IR quotient metrics.

## Key Results
- Most models achieve similar returns (23-28% over two years) despite different configurations
- Performance degrades significantly on out-of-distribution data, indicating overfitting
- Best-performing approach uses daily prices, episodic rate of return reward, and DDPG algorithm
- All models show declining Information Ratio over time, suggesting poor generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Daily prices with episodic Rate of Return reward and DDPG algorithm yields the best performance.
- Mechanism: This combination minimizes state complexity while maximizing reward stationarity, allowing the DDPG algorithm to converge more reliably.
- Core assumption: The financial market can be effectively represented with daily OHLCV values and recent history is less relevant than current state.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The best-performing approach uses daily prices, episodic rate of return reward, and DDPG algorithm."
  - [section] "The highest impact comes from the representations, where the daily prices performed best, followed closely by the continuous sliding window."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus about specific combination of daily prices, episodic RoR, and DDPG.
- Break condition: If market dynamics become highly non-stationary requiring longer-term context, or if asset correlations become more important than individual prices.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Overfitting occurs when DRL agents learn to select the same assets regardless of market conditions.
- Mechanism: Agents memorize asset performance patterns from training data rather than learning generalizable decision rules, leading to poor out-of-distribution performance.
- Core assumption: The training data contains sufficient asset performance patterns that agents can memorize rather than learning underlying market dynamics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "They find that while most models achieve similar returns (23-28% over two years), their performance degrades significantly on out-of-distribution data, indicating overfitting."
  - [section] "We have found that agents tend to overfit, picking the same assets regardless of market variations."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus about overfitting mechanisms.
- Break condition: If training data is sufficiently diverse and agents are trained with regularization techniques that prevent memorization.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The low volume of data available in OLPS limits learning efficiency of popular algorithms.
- Mechanism: With only ~2500 data points for 20 years of history, algorithms cannot explore the state space sufficiently to learn robust policies.
- Core assumption: Deep RL algorithms require large volumes of data to learn generalizable policies, similar to other domains like robotics or games.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "OLPS has limited data, with roughly 2500 points for 20 years of history. This is a limiting factor that may have motivated some authors to augment their data."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence in corpus about data volume limitations.
- Break condition: If data augmentation techniques or transfer learning can effectively increase the usable training data, or if algorithms can be modified to work efficiently with limited data.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data pipeline (OHLCV data preprocessing → normalization → market representations) → RL environment (state/action space definition → reward functions → episode termination) → Agent architecture (neural network design → algorithm selection → hyperparameter tuning) → Evaluation framework (performance metrics → robustness metrics → backtesting procedure)
- Critical path: Data preprocessing → market representation selection → agent training with hyperparameter optimization → evaluation on held-out test period → robustness analysis
- Design tradeoffs:
  - State complexity vs. generalization: More complex representations (sliding windows, indicators) may capture more information but can lead to overfitting with limited data
  - Reward stationarity vs. immediate feedback: Episodic rewards provide more stable learning signals but delay feedback on individual actions
  - Exploration vs. exploitation: Different algorithms balance this tradeoff differently, affecting convergence and final performance
- Failure signatures:
  - High variance in performance across seeds indicates unstable training
  - Declining IR trend over time suggests overfitting to training data
  - Low IR quotient indicates poor generalization to out-of-distribution data
  - High maximum drawdown relative to returns suggests inadequate risk management
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare performance of different market representations (daily prices vs. sliding window vs. indicators) with a fixed algorithm and reward function
  2. Test different reward functions (daily returns vs. episodic RoR vs. Sortino ratio) with a fixed market representation and algorithm
  3. Evaluate generalization by training on one time period and testing on a different period, measuring IR quotient and trend

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do DRL algorithms show poor generalization to out-of-distribution data in online portfolio management?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that agents learned to pick winning assets during training but these were no longer the best performing at later dates, leading to degradation in backtesting performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but doesn't deeply explore the underlying causes or potential solutions to improve generalization.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing improved generalization through techniques like data augmentation, regularization, or ensemble methods, or theoretical analysis explaining the root causes of poor generalization.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the low volume of historical data in OLPS limit the learning efficiency of DRL algorithms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that OLPS has limited data (roughly 2500 points for 20 years) and points to learning efficiency limits as a cause of poor robustness.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide detailed analysis of how data scarcity specifically impacts DRL training or what data-efficient approaches could work better.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of DRL performance with different data augmentation techniques, synthetic data generation, or transfer learning approaches in OLPS.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What alternative reward functions or learning objectives could lead to more robust portfolio management strategies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests various reward functions (daily returns, episodic returns, rate of return, Sortino ratio) but finds all models degrade over time, suggesting current objectives may be insufficient.
- Why unresolved: While the paper evaluates multiple reward functions, it doesn't explore more sophisticated objectives that might capture long-term stability or risk-adjusted performance better.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing superior robustness with alternative reward structures like multi-objective optimization, hierarchical rewards, or reward functions that explicitly penalize overfitting to training data.

## Limitations

- Limited baseline comparison with established financial benchmarks beyond simple Mean-Variance Optimization
- Unclear distinction between robust learning and memorization in the overfitting diagnosis
- Insufficient analysis of whether data scarcity is the primary bottleneck versus other factors

## Confidence

- Medium confidence in overfitting diagnosis
- Medium confidence in data limitations claim
