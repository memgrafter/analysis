# Multi-source Semantic Graph-based Multimodal Sarcasm Explanation Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16650
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16650
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 10
- The paper proposes a new approach for multimodal sarcasm explanation generation using object-level semantic metadata and external knowledge from ConceptNet.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TEAM, a novel approach for multimodal sarcasm explanation generation (MuSE) that generates natural language explanations for why multimodal social posts are sarcastic. The key innovation lies in using object-level semantic metadata extracted from images instead of global visual features, combined with external knowledge from ConceptNet, to construct a multi-source semantic graph that models semantic relations among the input caption, image, and external knowledge. Experiments on the MORE dataset demonstrate that TEAM significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on various evaluation metrics including BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore, and Sent-BERT.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach consists of four main components: vision-based object-level semantic extraction that retrieves object class and attribute metadata from images using Faster-RCNN, external related knowledge acquisition that retrieves relevant concepts from ConceptNet based on the caption and object metadata, multi-source semantic graph construction that models semantic relations among all sources, and sarcasm explanation generation using a BART decoder. The semantic graph captures intra-modal and cross-modal relations through GCN layers, which then support the BART encoder-decoder architecture in generating coherent sarcasm explanations. The model is trained on the MORE dataset using standard cross-entropy loss.

## Key Results
- TEAM significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on MORE dataset with improvements of 5.2% on BLEU-4 and 4.8% on ROUGE-L
- Object-level semantic metadata extraction proves more effective than global visual features for sarcasm reasoning
- External knowledge integration from ConceptNet contributes to better explanation generation performance
- The multi-source semantic graph effectively captures semantic relations across modalities

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image → Object-level Semantic Extraction → External Knowledge Acquisition → Multi-source Semantic Graph → GCN-based Reasoning → BART Decoder → Sarcasm Explanation
- Critical path: The complete pipeline from image input through semantic graph construction to final explanation generation
- Design tradeoffs:

  - Poor sarcasm explanation quality could indicate issues with object-level extraction, external knowledge relevance, or semantic graph construction
  - Model instability or slow training may suggest problems with GCN implementation or graph construction

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would incorporating embedded text in images (OCR samples) affect multimodal sarcasm explanation model performance?
- Basis in paper: explicit - "Our model performs worse on OCR samples than on Non-OCR samples" and "our model currently ignores the text embedded in the image"
- Why unresolved: The paper explicitly states that ignoring embedded text is a limitation but does not explore how incorporating it would improve performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing model performance on OCR samples with and without incorporating embedded text

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different knowledge concepts contribute differently to sarcasm reasoning, and how can this be leveraged in the model?
- Basis in paper: explicit - "Ignore that different knowledge concepts may contribute differently to the sarcasm reasoning"
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions this as a limitation but does not explore methods to weigh or prioritize knowledge concepts
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing performance improvements when assigning different weights to knowledge concepts based on their relevance to sarcasm

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would model performance change if a full connection graph is used instead of the proposed semantic relations?
- Basis in paper: explicit - "w/-FullGraph performs worse than TEAM, which verifies the utility of proposed semantic relations"
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows proposed semantic relations are better than full connection graph, it does not explore other graph structures or their impact on performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing performance using different graph structures (e.g., different types of relations, varying levels of connectivity)

## Limitations

- The semantic graph construction process is described conceptually but not fully specified, making it difficult to assess whether claimed semantic relations are meaningfully captured
- Ablation studies comparing object-level features versus global visual features are not presented, limiting confidence in claimed superiority of object-level approach
- External knowledge integration's impact is not thoroughly analyzed, leaving uncertainty about whether retrieved concepts genuinely contribute to sarcasm understanding or merely add noise

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The overall approach architecture (four-component pipeline) and general experimental methodology are well-described and reproducible
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of using object-level semantic metadata over global features is supported by strong experimental results but lacks detailed ablation analysis
- **Medium Confidence**: The integration of external knowledge from ConceptNet shows performance improvements, but the specific contribution and relevance of retrieved concepts are not thoroughly validated
