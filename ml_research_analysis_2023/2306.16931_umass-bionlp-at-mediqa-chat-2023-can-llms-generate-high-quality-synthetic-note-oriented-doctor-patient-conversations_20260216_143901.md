# UMASS_BioNLP at MEDIQA-Chat 2023: Can LLMs generate high-quality synthetic note-oriented doctor-patient conversations?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16931
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16931
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Proposed doctor-patient loop system achieves ROUGE scores of 56.48 vs 53.29 for baseline, with better concept recall (51.23 vs 45.69) and medical information coverage

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel multi-agent LLM cooperation system called the "doctor-patient loop" for generating high-quality synthetic doctor-patient conversations from clinical notes. The system segments clinical notes into sections, extracts medical concepts using MedSpaCy, and employs multiple ChatGPT instances playing distinct roles in a structured conversation loop. With fact-checking and post-editing modules, the approach generates conversations that achieve higher ROUGE scores and concept recall compared to standard ChatGPT and GPT-4 outputs, while maintaining medical accuracy and conversation quality.

## Method Summary
The system segments clinical notes into section headings, extracts UMLS concepts as keywords, and runs a structured loop between two ChatGPT instances (doctor and patient roles). Each section generates a focused dialogue segment based on its keywords. A fact-checking module ensures consistency with the source clinical note, and post-editing refines the conversation. All section dialogues are concatenated to form the complete conversation. The approach addresses API token limits by processing notes section-by-section while maintaining overall coherence.

## Key Results
- ROUGE-1/2/L scores of 56.48 (vs 53.29 for baseline), showing better content overlap
- Concept recall of 51.23 (vs 45.69 for baseline), indicating better medical information coverage
- Maintained conversation quality while achieving superior medical content coverage

- Claim: Multi-agent loop with distinct roles improves dialogue coherence and medical content coverage

- Core assumption: Role specialization allows focused contextually appropriate responses while maintaining continuity through prompt history
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions "multiple ChatGPT instances playing doctor and patient roles in a loop"; section describes iterative turn-based questioning

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Clinical note segmentation enables targeted conversation generation within API token limits
- Mechanism: Notes divided into section headings (history of present illness, medications, etc.), with separate prompts for each section allowing longer conversations from shorter segments
- Core assumption: Section-based segmentation preserves medical context while enabling generation of longer overall conversations
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions segmentation; section describes dissecting clinical notes into headings and constructing section-specific prompts
- Break condition: Segmented conversations fail to maintain coherence when concatenated or critical information lost between segments

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fact-checking and post-editing modules ensure medical accuracy and conversation quality
- Mechanism: Additional ChatGPT instances monitor for hallucination, completeness, and coherence, filtering and refining generated content to align with source clinical notes
- Core assumption: LLM-based quality control can effectively identify and correct errors while preserving natural dialogue flow

- Break condition: Fact-checking incorrectly flags valid information or fails to catch actual hallucinations

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Clinical note → Segmentation → Keyword extraction → Doctor-Patient loop → Fact-checking → Post-editing → Final conversation

- Critical path: Clinical note → Segmentation → Keyword extraction → Doctor-Patient loop → Fact-checking → Post-editing → Final conversation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - API token limits vs. conversation completeness: Requires segmentation approach
  - Model accuracy vs. computational cost: Multiple ChatGPT calls increase latency
  - Fact-checking strictness vs. natural flow: Over-correction may make conversations mechanical
  - Keyword focus vs. conversational breadth: May miss relevant information not captured in keywords

- Failure signatures:
  - Repetitive question patterns despite keyword variation
  - Inconsistent medical terminology across conversation turns
  - Missing critical information from source clinical notes
  - Hallucinated symptoms or treatments not present in source material
  - Mechanical, unnatural dialogue flow

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test single-segment generation: Input one clinical note section and evaluate doctor-patient conversation quality and medical concept coverage
  2. Evaluate fact-checking effectiveness: Generate conversations with intentional errors and measure detection/removal accuracy
  3. Compare segmentation strategies: Test different section boundaries to optimize information retention and conversation coherence
