# Value function estimation using conditional diffusion models for control

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07290
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07290
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 39
- One-line primary result: DVF matches or outperforms strong offline RL baselines on low-quality data tasks like Mountain Car, Maze 2D, and PyBullet environments.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Diffused Value Function (DVF), a novel method for estimating value functions using conditional diffusion models. DVF addresses the challenge of learning from low-quality data, such as videos without actions or rewards, by training a diffusion model to predict future states conditioned on the current state, policy, and time offset. This model is trained on state sequences without requiring action or reward labels. The value function is then estimated by sampling future states from the diffusion model and weighting them by rewards predicted by a separate reward model. The paper demonstrates the effectiveness of DVF on various tasks, including Mountain Car, Maze 2D, and PyBullet environments. Results show that DVF can match or outperform strong offline RL baselines, particularly on low-quality data.

## Method Summary
DVF learns a conditional diffusion model from state sequences to predict future states given the current state, policy, and time offset. This diffusion model captures the state visitation measure for multiple controllers. A separate reward predictor is trained on labeled state-action pairs. Value functions are estimated by Monte Carlo sampling from the diffusion model and averaging rewards of sampled states. The method can work with state sequences alone (without actions/rewards) and scales to high-dimensional observations.

## Key Results
- DVF matches or outperforms strong offline RL baselines like CQL on PyBullet tasks
- Successfully learns from low-quality data (state sequences without actions/rewards)
- Scales to long-horizon tasks with high-dimensional observations
- Avoids compounding prediction errors through independent diffusion sampling

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DVF avoids the compounding prediction error of classical model-based RL by predicting future states independently via diffusion instead of autoregressive unrolls.
- Mechanism: Instead of iteratively predicting st+1, st+2, ..., st+k using a transition model (which compounds errors), DVF directly samples st+k from a diffusion model conditioned on st, policy, and time offset k. Each sample is independent, so errors don't accumulate.
- Core assumption: The diffusion model can accurately represent the joint distribution over multi-step transitions, including the effect of the policy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "DVF represents multi-step transitions implicitly, it avoids having to predict high-dimensional observations at every timestep and thus scales to long-horizon tasks with high-dimensional observations."
  - [section 4.2]: "Because DVF samples each time offset independently, there is also no concern of compounding model error as the horizon increases."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. Related work discusses diffusion for planning, but no direct anchor on error compounding avoidance.
- Break condition: If the diffusion model cannot accurately represent the multi-step transition distribution (e.g., due to policy conditioning mismatch or high variance), the value estimates will be biased.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DVF enables learning from low-quality data (state sequences without actions/rewards) by decomposing value estimation into separately learnable components.
- Mechanism: The value function is factorized into an occupancy measure (learned via diffusion from state sequences), a reward model (learned from labeled data), and a policy representation. These can be trained independently, so missing action/reward labels in the offline data are not a blocker for the occupancy measure.
- Core assumption: The policy representation (scalar or sequential) is sufficient to distinguish different controllers in the data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "This model can be efficiently learned from state sequences (i.e., without access to reward functions nor actions), and subsequently used to estimate the value of each action out-of-the-box."
  - [section 3.1]: "This step can be performed on large amounts of demonstration videos without the need of any action nor reward labels."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence. No direct anchor on decomposition into learnable components from incomplete data.
- Break condition: If the policy representation cannot disambiguate controllers (e.g., similar policies have same representation), the occupancy measure will be conflated, leading to incorrect value estimates.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DVF avoids expensive rollouts and TD learning by using Monte Carlo sampling from the diffusion model to estimate value functions.
- Mechanism: Instead of unrolling a model for multiple steps or bootstrapping with TD updates, DVF samples n future states from the diffusion model and averages their rewards to estimate the value function. This is O(1) per sample and avoids temporal dependencies.
- Core assumption: The diffusion model samples are unbiased estimates of the true occupancy measure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "DVF can be used to efficiently capture the state visitation measure for multiple controllers."
  - [section 3.2]: "We circumvent the computational overhead by unrolling one step of Bellman backup" and using sampled future states.
  - [section 4.1]: "Fig. 2 shows that all three quantities exhibit strong positive correlation" between true returns, estimated value, and reward at sampled states.
- Break condition: If the diffusion model samples are biased (e.g., due to poor training or policy conditioning), the Monte Carlo estimate will be systematically wrong.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and value functions
  - Why needed here: DVF is built on decomposing the value function into occupancy measure and reward components; understanding MDPs is essential to grasp this decomposition.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between the state value function V(s) and the state-action value function Q(s,a)?

- Concept: Diffusion models and denoising autoencoders
  - Why needed here: DVF uses a denoising diffusion probabilistic model to learn the occupancy measure; understanding how diffusion models work (forward/reverse processes, noise prediction) is crucial.
  - Quick check question: In a diffusion model, what is the role of the denoising network during sampling?

- Concept: Policy representation and conditioning
  - Why needed here: DVF needs to condition the diffusion model on the policy to estimate values for different controllers; understanding how policies can be represented (scalar vs sequential) and embedded is key.
  - Quick check question: Why might a sequential policy representation be more robust than a scalar one when dealing with offline data from many policies?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: State sequences -> Diffusion model training -> Reward model training -> Monte Carlo sampling -> Value estimation -> (Optional) Policy optimization
- Critical path: Data -> Diffusion model training -> Reward model training -> Value estimation -> (Optional) Policy optimization
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Diffusion model vs autoregressive model: Diffusion avoids compounding errors but may be less sample-efficient to train
  - Scalar vs sequential policy representation: Scalar is compact but doesn't scale; sequential is flexible but higher dimensional
  - Number of Monte Carlo samples (n): More samples -> better estimate but higher compute cost
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor value estimation: Check if diffusion model samples are diverse and cover true occupancy measure; check reward model accuracy
  - Unstable policy optimization: Check if policy representation is sufficient to distinguish controllers; check if value estimates are correlated with true returns
  - High variance in value estimates: Increase number of Monte Carlo samples or check if diffusion model is overfitting
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train diffusion model on synthetic MDP with known policy occupancy measure; check if sampled states match true distribution
  2. Train reward model on labeled data; check correlation between predicted and true rewards on diffusion samples
  3. Combine diffusion and reward models to estimate value function; compare against ground truth on small MDP

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of DVF compare to other state-of-the-art model-based and model-free offline RL algorithms on complex robotic tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that DVF matches and sometimes outperforms strong offline RL baselines like CQL on PyBullet tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares DVF to a limited set of baselines (CQL, BC) on a subset of tasks. A more comprehensive comparison to other leading algorithms on a wider range of complex robotic tasks is needed to fully assess DVF's competitiveness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing DVF's performance against a broader set of state-of-the-art offline RL algorithms on a diverse set of complex robotic tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of policy representation (scalar vs. sequential) impact DVF's performance and scalability to large policy sets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses two policy representations - scalar and sequential - and mentions that sequential representation is used for offline datasets due to the limitations of scalar representation with large policy sets.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a detailed analysis of how the choice of policy representation affects DVF's performance across different types of tasks or policy sets of varying sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing the performance of DVF using scalar and sequential policy representations across tasks with different policy sets (e.g., varying numbers of policies or policies with different structures).

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the limitations of DVF in terms of the types of MDPs it can handle effectively, and how can these limitations be addressed?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that DVF operates on observations directly and requires tuning the noise schedule for each task, implying potential limitations in handling diverse MDPs. It also discusses challenges in online learning due to non-stationarity.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't provide a comprehensive analysis of the types of MDPs where DVF might struggle or potential strategies to overcome these limitations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies identifying the types of MDPs where DVF's performance degrades, along with proposed modifications or extensions to address these limitations.

## Limitations
- Scalability to complex policies: The scalar policy representation may not scale well to large numbers of diverse controllers
- Reward model dependency: Value estimates are fundamentally limited by the accuracy of the separate reward model
- Theoretical grounding: Limited theoretical guarantees for value function accuracy

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core mechanism of using diffusion models to represent multi-step transitions and avoid compounding errors
- Medium confidence: The claim about learning from low-quality data without actions or rewards
- Medium confidence: The computational efficiency claims relative to traditional model-based RL

## Next Checks
