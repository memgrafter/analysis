# A Parametric Similarity Method: Comparative Experiments based on Semantically Annotated Large Datasets

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04123
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04123
- Reference count: 40
- This paper presents a parametric semantic similarity method (SemSimp) designed to measure the similarity of digital resources based on a reference ontology.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SemSimp, a parametric semantic similarity method for measuring the similarity of digital resources using a reference ontology. The method computes concept weights using either extensional methods (concept frequency, annotation frequency) or intensional methods (top-down topology-based, intrinsic information content) and normalizes similarity scores between 0 and 1. Through extensive experiments on ACM and APS datasets, SemSimp demonstrated superior performance compared to six representative semantic similarity methods, with the annotation frequency weighting and geometric average normalization configuration achieving the best results.

## Method Summary
SemSimp measures semantic similarity between digital resources annotated with concepts from a reference ontology. The method computes concept weights using four approaches (concept frequency, annotation frequency, top-down topology, and intrinsic information content) and normalizes similarity scores using four factors (maximum, minimum, arithmetic average, or geometric average of annotation vector cardinalities). It uses the Hungarian algorithm to find maximum weighted matching between concept pairs, enabling polynomial-time computation of semantic similarity. The method was evaluated on 1,103 ACM papers and 52,762 APS papers using two indicators: statistical confidence in special issue cohesion and Pearson correlation with human expert judgment.

## Key Results
- SemSimp with annotation frequency weighting and geometric average normalization outperformed six other semantic similarity methods
- The method showed better correlation with human judgment than existing approaches
- Results were consistent across both computer science (ACM) and physics (APS) domains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The parametric normalization factor improves similarity stability across annotation vectors of different cardinalities.
- Mechanism: SemSimp divides the maximum weighted matching sum by a normalization factor (max, min, ave, or gav) that adjusts for vector size differences. This prevents bias toward either longer or shorter annotations.
- Core assumption: The annotation vectors represent the same underlying semantic content regardless of size, and the normalization factor can correct for size imbalances.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "it leverages a reference ontology and taxonomic reasoning, encompassing different approaches for weighting the concepts of the ontology."
  - [section]: "Choosing the normalization factor as the arithmetic average implies a compromise between the two above cases."
- Break condition: If the normalization factor over-corrects, similarity values may lose discriminative power or converge toward uniform scores.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Extensional weighting methods (CF, AF) outperform intensional ones (TD, IIC) when sufficient annotated data is available.
- Mechanism: Extensional methods compute concept weights based on actual document frequencies, capturing real-world usage patterns in the dataset, while intensional methods rely solely on ontology structure.
- Core assumption: The dataset is large enough and representative to reflect true concept importance through frequency distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "weights can be computed by considering either the available digital resources or the structure of the reference ontology of a given domain."
  - [section]: "Extensional methods compute concept weights by taking into account both the topology of the ISA hierarchy and the content of the annotated dataset."
- Break condition: When dataset size is small or biased, extensional methods may produce unreliable weights, causing performance degradation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Geometric average normalization (gav) balances Pearson correlation and confidence metrics better than arithmetic average.
- Mechanism: Gav is more sensitive to small values, making it better suited for preserving differences when vectors differ greatly in size, which correlates better with human judgment.
- Core assumption: Human similarity judgments are more sensitive to relative differences in smaller vectors than absolute differences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "one of the configurations of SemSimp, using annotation frequency weighting and geometric average normalization, outperformed the other assessed methods."
  - [section]: "the difference between G and A configurations with respect to I2 is relevant and cannot be ignored."
- Break condition: If vector sizes are uniformly distributed or human judgment treats size differences uniformly, gav may not provide advantage.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does SemSimp perform when comparing digital resources with sparse or noisy annotations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that SemSimp uses semantic annotations from a reference ontology, but does not extensively discuss performance with sparse or noisy annotations.
- Why unresolved: The experiments focused on well-annotated ACM and APS datasets, where annotation quality is assumed to be high.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing SemSimp on datasets with known annotation sparsity or noise and comparing results to methods designed for such cases.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does SemSimp scale to very large ontologies with millions of concepts and annotations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper states that SemSimp is polynomial in the size of the larger annotation vector, but does not discuss performance with extremely large ontologies.
- Why unresolved: The experiments used ontologies with a few thousand concepts, which may not represent scalability challenges.
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking SemSimp on ontologies with millions of concepts and varying annotation densities.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can SemSimp effectively compare digital resources across different domains or ontologies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the APS experiment was performed in the physics domain, but does not discuss cross-domain comparisons.
- Why unresolved: The experiments compared resources within the same domain (computer science or physics) using their respective ontologies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing SemSimp on datasets where resources are annotated with concepts from different ontologies and evaluating its ability to identify semantic similarity across domains.

## Limitations

- The evaluation relies on a single domain (ACM Computing Classification System) and one external dataset (APS Physics), limiting generalizability across different ontology structures and annotation practices
- The human judgment correlation (I2) involves only three expert annotators, raising concerns about inter-rater reliability and potential bias
- The paper does not address computational complexity analysis, making it unclear how SemSimp scales to larger ontologies or datasets

## Confidence

- High confidence: SemSimp's core mechanism using Hungarian algorithm for maximum weighted bipartite matching is well-established
- Medium confidence: The superiority of AF+gav configuration over other methods is supported by experimental results but needs validation on additional domains
- Low confidence: Claims about performance advantages in physics domain are based on a single dataset without comparative statistical analysis
