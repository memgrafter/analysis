# Expediting Distributed DNN Training with Device Topology-Aware Graph Deployment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06126
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06126
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: TAG achieves up to 4.56x speed-up over baselines, generalizes to unseen models and device topologies without re-training

## Executive Summary
TAG is an automatic DNN deployment system that accelerates distributed training over scattered resources by considering both the DNN computation graph and device topology. It combines a heterogeneous GNN with MCTS to identify optimized deployment strategies, and explores lossless gradient compression via SFB to reduce communication overhead. TAG demonstrates significant speed-ups over baselines and can generalize to unseen models and device topologies without re-training.

## Method Summary
TAG uses a heterogeneous GNN combined with MCTS to explore deployment strategies for DNN training on heterogeneous GPU clusters. The GNN learns embeddings from a unified graph representation containing both computation nodes and device nodes, producing prior probabilities that guide MCTS search. MCTS iteratively builds strategies using these priors and refines them through simulator feedback. TAG also applies SFB optimization to reduce communication overhead by identifying low-rank structures in gradient tensors. The system automatically handles OOM errors and generalizes to unseen models and topologies.

## Key Results
- TAG achieves up to 4.56x speed-up over baselines
- Generalizes to unseen models and device topologies without re-training
- Reduces communication overhead through SFB optimization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Heterogeneous GNN + MCTS combination enables generalizable strategy generation across unseen device topologies.
- Mechanism: The GNN takes a unified graph representation of both computation graph and device topology, learning embeddings that guide MCTS search. MCTS progressively builds partial strategies using the GNN's prior probabilities, while the simulator provides runtime feedback to refine strategies iteratively.
- Core assumption: Runtime feedback features (execution time, idle time, memory usage) are informative for improving GNN predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We novelly combine both the DNN computation graph and the device topology graph as input to a graph neural network (GNN), and join the GNN with a search-based method to quickly identify optimized distributed training strategies."
  - [section] "The key to enabling TAG to generalize to different device topologies, we use a heterogeneous graph as input, that contains both computation nodes and device nodes."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence on GNN-MCTS combination effectiveness.
- Break condition: If runtime feedback features are not informative, the GNN will not learn generalizable policies.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SFB optimization as a local combinatorial problem reduces communication overhead without affecting training convergence.
- Mechanism: For each gradient tensor in a replicated op group, TAG formulates a min-cut-like integer linear program to decide which subgraph to duplicate. This transforms the problem of choosing between AllReduce/PS and SFB into an optimization that considers computation and communication costs.
- Core assumption: Low-rank structures exist in gradient tensors that can be exploited by SFB.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "To reduce communication in a heterogeneous cluster, we further explore a lossless gradient compression technique and solve a combinatorial optimization problem to automatically apply the technique for training time minimization."
  - [section] "SFB deals with gradients of the parameters, whose only consumer is the ApplyGradient op...We formulate the SFB decision for each gradient as a mixed-integer optimization problem that can be efficiently solved using off-the-shelf solvers."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence on SFB effectiveness in this context.
- Break condition: If low-rank structures don't exist in gradients, SFB won't provide communication benefits.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Interactive strategy refinement with runtime feedback produces feasible strategies for unseen models.
- Mechanism: Instead of one-shot strategy generation, TAG simulates execution of current strategy and uses the resulting runtime trace (execution time, idle time, memory usage) to guide generation of better strategies. This allows automatic handling of OOM errors by adjusting memory usage through model parallelism.
- Core assumption: Simulated execution traces are representative of real execution performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "TAG repeatedly simulates the execution of a strategy and collects the corresponding simulated execution trace, and then tries to generate a better strategy based on the runtime feedback for the existing strategy."
  - [section] "The interactive strategy exploration process also helps TAG produce feasible (e.g. not causing OOM errors) strategies for unseen models."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence on simulation accuracy.
- Break condition: If simulator's performance estimates are inaccurate, strategies may not perform as expected.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Graph Analyzer -> Profiler -> GNN -> MCTS -> Simulator -> Compiler -> Execution
- Critical path: Graph → GNN → MCTS → Simulator → Strategy refinement → Compiler → Execution
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Larger GNN vs. faster inference
  - More MCTS iterations vs. strategy quality
  - More detailed simulator vs. runtime overhead
  - SFB optimization complexity vs. communication savings
- Failure signatures:
  - GNN produces poor priors → MCTS explores suboptimal strategies
  - Simulator underestimates communication time → Strategies don't meet performance targets
  - SFB solver fails to find optimal solution → Missed communication savings
  - Profiler data is inaccurate → Poor performance estimates
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test TAG on a simple DNN (e.g., VGG) on a homogeneous cluster and compare with DP-NCCL baseline
  2. Test TAG on the same model on a heterogeneous cluster (different GPU types) and verify speed-up
  3. Test TAG's generalizability by applying to a model not seen during GNN training

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the performance of TAG scale with larger DNN models (e.g., GPT-3 size) compared to existing systems?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates TAG on models up to BERT-Large (2.3B parameters), but mentions it as a future direction to extend TAG to support pipeline parallelism for larger models.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experimental results for extremely large models like GPT-3.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Running TAG on GPT-3-sized models and comparing training time/speedup to baselines.

- Question: How does TAG's performance change when trained on device topologies with more complex network structures (e.g., hierarchical, non-uniform bandwidth)?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states TAG can handle various device topologies, but only evaluates on simple, flat topologies.
  - Why unresolved: No experiments are conducted on complex topologies.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating TAG on hierarchical and non-uniform bandwidth topologies, comparing performance to baselines.

- Question: How does the performance of TAG's GNN component change when trained on datasets with a wider variety of DNN architectures?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses only 6 representative DNN models for GNN training, which may not cover the full diversity of DNN architectures.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the impact of GNN training data diversity on performance.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Training TAG's GNN on a more diverse set of DNN architectures and comparing performance on unseen models.

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation on complex, hierarchical device topologies
- Potential overfitting of GNN to training models/topologies
- Uncertainty about existence of low-rank gradient structures across diverse DNN architectures
