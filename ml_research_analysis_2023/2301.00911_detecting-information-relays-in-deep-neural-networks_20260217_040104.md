# Detecting Information Relays in Deep Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00911
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00911
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: A new information-theoretic metric called "relay information" successfully identifies functional modules in neural networks, correlating with node essentiality

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a novel information-theoretic metric called "relay information" to identify functional modules in artificial neural networks. The metric quantifies how much information specific groups of neurons relay from inputs to outputs, allowing researchers to pinpoint computational modules within trained networks. The authors validate their approach on MNIST handwritten digit classification using both standard and composite network architectures, demonstrating that their greedy search algorithm can efficiently identify minimal informative subsets that are functionally critical to network performance.

## Method Summary
The method calculates relay information (IR) as the shared information between inputs and outputs conditioned on the state of non-relaying neurons, isolating information flow through specific neuron sets. A greedy shrinking subset aggregation algorithm iteratively removes neurons contributing least to IR, exploiting the fact that smaller sets have lower information than their supersets. The approach is validated by comparing IR-identified modules with knockout analysis results on MNIST classification tasks using both standard and composite network architectures.

## Key Results
- Relay information successfully identifies functional modules in neural networks, with identified sets being critical for network function
- The greedy algorithm correctly identifies minimal informative subsets in composite networks, finding less than 0.9% of all subsets with higher information content
- Relay information correlates with node essentiality, demonstrating that high-IR neurons are functionally critical to network performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Relay information (IR) identifies computational modules by quantifying how much information specific neuron groups pass from inputs to outputs
- Mechanism: IR measures shared information between inputs and outputs conditioned on the state of non-relaying neurons, isolating information flow through specific neuron sets
- Core assumption: Information in neural networks is not perfectly redundant across all neurons
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The relay information measures how much information groups of neurons that participate in a particular function (modules) relay from inputs to outputs."
  - [section 2.3] "The relay information measures how much information a set of nodes relays between inputs and outputs."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus neighbors focus on relay nodes in wireless networks, not neural network information flow
- Break condition: Perfect redundancy where removing any subset doesn't change information content

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The greedy shrinking subset aggregation algorithm efficiently finds minimal relay sets without exhaustive enumeration
- Mechanism: Start with all neurons in relay set, iteratively remove neurons that contribute least to IR, exploiting that smaller sets have lower information than their supersets
- Core assumption: Smaller neuron sets carry less information than larger sets that contain them (absent redundancy)
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.4] "the number of tests required to find the set with the largest amount of information can be significantly reduced by taking advantage of the fact that a smaller subset cannot have more information than its superset."
  - [section 3.1] "Fewer than 0.9% of all subsets have a higher information content than the one identified by the algorithm."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence - corpus doesn't address greedy algorithms for neural networks
- Break condition: Redundant information encoding where different neuron combinations carry identical information

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Relay information correlates with node essentiality, meaning relay neurons are functionally critical
- Mechanism: Neurons identified as high-IR relays cause network failures when knocked out, demonstrating their functional importance
- Core assumption: Information flow through specific neurons translates to functional necessity
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We also show that the functionality of modules correlates with the amount of relay information they carry."
  - [section 3.1] "Training the full network via backpropagation is not expected to create modules... Indeed, we find information to be relayed in an unstructured fashion... nodes that are positively identified as relays do not necessarily seem to be essential."
  - [section 3.2] "Figure 8 shows the result of an ANOVA that for all numerals and for both models (full and composite). The correlation coefficient between sensitivity and set size is about half (or smaller) the correlation coefficient between sensitivity and relay information."
- Break condition: Distributed representations where information is encoded across many redundant neurons

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer (784 nodes) -> Hidden layer (20 nodes) -> Output layer (10 nodes) OR Input layer (784 nodes) -> 10×2 hidden nodes -> Output layer (10 nodes)
- Critical path:
  1. Train neural network on MNIST
  2. Calculate relay information for all neuron subsets
  3. Apply greedy shrinking algorithm to find minimal relay sets
  4. Perform knockout analysis to validate functional importance
  5. Correlate IR with node essentiality
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Exhaustive vs. greedy search: Exhaustive is accurate but exponential, greedy is efficient but may miss redundant sets
  - Binning strategy: Median vs. k-means clustering for discretizing continuous activations
  - Network architecture: Modular vs. fully connected affects information distribution patterns
- Failure signatures:
  - Greedy algorithm fails to converge or finds non-minimal sets (indicating redundancy)
  - Low correlation between IR and knockout effects (suggesting distributed representations)
  - High variance in IR estimates across runs (sampling issues)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run relay information analysis on a simple XOR network to verify algorithm works on known problems
  2. Compare IR results between the composite network (known modules) and full network (unknown modules)
  3. Perform knockout analysis on top IR-scoring neurons to validate functional importance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of neural network architecture, training method, and hyperparameters affect the distribution of relay information across nodes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "We conjecture that our method applies to all other variances of deep learning. However, we also conjecture that the way in which information is distributed across the network will depend on the method and parameters of the optimization procedure."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests a limited set of architectures and training methods (ADAM optimizer, mean squared error loss, tanh activation), so the generalizability to other settings is unknown
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing the relay information metric across a wide range of neural network architectures, training algorithms, and hyperparameters

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the greedy algorithm's performance depend on the size of the dataset and the amount of redundancy among neurons?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "we expect that the failure rate depends on the task being studied, the data set size as well as the amount of redundancy among neurons."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests on the MNIST dataset and does not systematically vary the dataset size or neuron redundancy
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the dataset size and neuron redundancy while evaluating the greedy algorithm's accuracy in identifying minimal informative sets

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the relay information metric be extended to identify functional modules in recurrent neural networks, convolutional layers, or generative models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests that "it would be interesting to study if this method also applies to, for example, time series classification, recurrent neural networks, convolutional layers, or even generative tasks."
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests the metric on feedforward networks for image classification
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the relay information metric to recurrent, convolutional, and generative neural network architectures and comparing the identified modules to known functional units

## Limitations
- The greedy algorithm may fail to identify correct minimal sets when information is redundantly encoded across neurons
- Entropy estimation from finite samples introduces potential bias, particularly for higher-order interactions among neurons
- The method's applicability to deep networks with many layers and non-linear activation functions beyond tanh is unproven

## Confidence
- High confidence: The mathematical framework for relay information is sound and the greedy algorithm's efficiency is well-established
- Medium confidence: The correlation between relay information and node essentiality is demonstrated but needs validation on more complex architectures
- Low confidence: The method's applicability to deep networks with many layers and non-linear activation functions beyond tanh
