# THiFLY Research at SemEval-2023 Task 7: A Multi-granularity System for CTR-based Textual Entailment and Evidence Retrieval

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01245
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01245
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Achieves F1-scores of 0.856 (Task A) and 0.853 (Task B) in NLI4CT task

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a multi-granularity system designed for Clinical Trial Report (CTR) based textual entailment and evidence retrieval in the NLI4CT task. The system leverages both sentence-level and token-level encoding to handle two distinct tasks: textual entailment (Task A) and evidence retrieval (Task B). A key innovation is the integration of SciFive, a T5-based model pre-trained on medical corpus, to enhance numerical inference capabilities. The system employs model ensembling and joint inference to improve stability and consistency, achieving state-of-the-art performance with F1-scores of 0.856 and 0.853 on the respective tasks.

## Method Summary
The proposed system utilizes a multi-granularity inference network (MGNet) that processes CTRs through both sentence-level and token-level encoders. The sentence-level encoder employs a BiLSTM or Transformer to capture broader contextual information, while the token-level encoder uses BiLSTM or max-pooling for detailed local features. SciFive, pre-trained on medical corpus, is specifically leveraged to handle numerical inference challenges. Joint inference is implemented via a transformer encoder to improve consistency between the two tasks. Model ensembling further enhances the stability of predictions.

## Key Results
- F1-score of 0.856 on textual entailment (Task A)
- F1-score of 0.853 on evidence retrieval (Task B)
- Best performance on both subtasks in the NLI4CT task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The system's effectiveness stems from its multi-granularity approach, which captures both high-level sentence semantics and fine-grained token-level details. The integration of SciFive for numerical inference addresses a critical challenge in medical CTR analysis. Joint inference ensures consistency between related hypotheses, while model ensembling reduces variance and improves robustness.
