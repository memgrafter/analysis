# Linguistically Informed ChatGPT Prompts to Enhance Japanese-Chinese Machine Translation: A Case Study on Attributive Clauses

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15587
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15587
- Reference count: 0
- Improves Japanese-to-Chinese machine translation of attributive clauses by over 35% using linguistically informed prompts

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of translating Japanese attributive clauses into Chinese using machine translation tools. Japanese attributive clauses have complex syntactic structures that commercial MT tools struggle with, particularly "nominative" inner relation attributive clauses. The authors propose a two-step prompt strategy that combines linguistic theory with ChatGPT to improve translation quality. The approach analyzes the semantic role of modified nouns and restructures sentences based on linguistic principles before translation, achieving significant improvements in translation accuracy.

## Method Summary
The method involves a two-step prompt strategy using ChatGPT. First, a pre-edit prompt identifies the noun modified by the attributive clause and restructures the sentence by placing this noun in the subject position of the attributive clause, separating it into two sentences. Second, a translation prompt converts the restructured Japanese sentence into Chinese. This approach leverages linguistic knowledge about semantic roles and their hierarchical relationships to determine optimal noun placement. The method operates in zero-shot scenarios without requiring fine-tuning or additional labeled examples.

## Key Results
- Average translation quality score improved by over 35% compared to baseline
- Effective for "nominative" inner relation attributive clauses in Japanese-Chinese translation
- Demonstrates significant improvements using zero-shot prompting without fine-tuning
