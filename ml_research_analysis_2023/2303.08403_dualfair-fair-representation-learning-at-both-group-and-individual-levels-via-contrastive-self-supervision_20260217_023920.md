# DualFair: Fair Representation Learning at Both Group and Individual Levels via Contrastive Self-supervision

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08403
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08403
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: DualFair achieves high prediction accuracy (AUC up to 0.91) while reducing bias metrics (e.g., demographic parity distance reduced to 0.03, counterfactual parity distance to 0.06)

## Executive Summary
DualFair is a self-supervised fair representation learning framework that jointly addresses group fairness and counterfactual fairness. It uses a cyclic variational autoencoder to generate counterfactual samples and employs fairness-aware contrastive learning to create embeddings that are indistinguishable across protected groups while keeping counterfactual pairs close. Self-knowledge distillation is used to preserve representation quality. Experiments on six real-world datasets show DualFair achieves high prediction accuracy while significantly reducing bias metrics, outperforming state-of-the-art fair representation learning baselines.

## Method Summary
DualFair addresses algorithmic fairness through a two-stage approach. First, a cyclic variational autoencoder (C-VAE) generates counterfactual samples by learning to reconstruct original data while swapping sensitive attributes. Second, a main model employs fairness-aware contrastive learning with InfoNCE loss to create group-invariant embeddings and maintains counterfactual pairs close together. The framework incorporates self-knowledge distillation with TabMix augmentation to preserve representation quality. The model is trained using Adam optimizer with a batch size of 128 for 200 epochs on the main model and 600 epochs on the generator.

## Key Results
- Achieves AUC up to 0.91 on classification tasks while maintaining high prediction accuracy
- Reduces demographic parity distance to 0.03 and counterfactual parity distance to 0.06
- Outperforms state-of-the-art fair representation learning baselines on six real-world datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DualFair works by jointly optimizing for both group fairness (treating different demographic groups equally) and counterfactual fairness (ensuring fairness in hypothetical scenarios). The cyclic VAE generates counterfactual samples that capture the causal relationships between features, while the contrastive learning objective enforces that embeddings are indistinguishable across groups. The self-knowledge distillation with TabMix augmentation preserves representation quality by regularizing the learning process and preventing information loss during debiasing.

## Foundational Learning
- Variational Autoencoders (VAEs): Generative models that learn to reconstruct input data while regularizing the latent space; needed for counterfactual sample generation; quick check: verify reconstruction quality and latent space structure
- Contrastive Learning: A self-supervised learning approach that learns representations by comparing similar and dissimilar pairs; needed for creating group-invariant embeddings; quick check: monitor alignment and uniformity metrics during training
- Counterfactual Fairness: A fairness notion that requires models to make the same predictions in the actual and counterfactual worlds; needed for addressing individual-level fairness; quick check: verify counterfactual sample quality through correlation analysis

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:**
C-VAE (Generator) -> TabMix Augmentation -> Main Model (DualFair) -> Prediction Heads

**Critical Path:**
Data Preprocessing -> C-VAE Training -> Main Model Training (with contrastive loss and self-knowledge distillation) -> Evaluation

**Design Tradeoffs:**
- Accuracy vs. Fairness: The framework balances prediction performance with fairness constraints through careful loss function design
- Group vs. Individual Fairness: Joint optimization for both fairness notions requires balancing competing objectives
- Sample Quality vs. Generation Speed: Higher quality counterfactual samples may require more complex generation methods

**Failure Signatures:**
- Poor counterfactual sample quality leading to ineffective fairness improvements (check correlation matrices)
- Collapse of contrastive learning where embeddings become constant (monitor alignment/distribution loss balance)
- Over-regularization causing loss of predictive power (compare pre-debiasing and post-debiasing accuracy)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How sensitive is the performance of DualFair to the choice of temperature parameter 𝜏 in the contrastive loss, and what is the optimal range for this parameter across different datasets?
- How does the quality of counterfactual samples generated by C-VAE impact the downstream fairness and prediction performance, and can alternative counterfactual generation methods (e.g., using causal graphs) improve results?
- Can DualFair be extended to handle more complex fairness scenarios, such as intersectional fairness (considering multiple sensitive attributes simultaneously) or fairness in the presence of label bias?

## Limitations
- The paper does not provide specific hyperparameter values for temperature parameter τ in the contrastive loss
- TabMix augmentation implementation details (masking ratio, replacement sampling method) are not fully specified
- The ablation study is limited, analyzing only one component at a time rather than exploring different combinations of fairness constraints
