# Interpretable Sentence Representation with Variational Autoencoders and Attention

## Executive Summary
This thesis addresses the challenge of interpretability in deep learning-based natural language processing by developing novel architectures that separate linguistic information into understandable concepts. The work proposes two main models: ADVAE, which controls syntactic roles in sentences, and QKVAE, which separates semantic and syntactic information in latent representations. Both models leverage Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Transformer architectures to achieve interpretable sentence representations without requiring annotated data. The research demonstrates that it is possible to enhance the interpretability of state-of-the-art language models while maintaining competitive performance on standard benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The thesis develops two main approaches for interpretable representation learning in NLP. The first model, ADVAE, builds upon conditional VAEs by incorporating syntactic role information through attention mechanisms, allowing explicit control over syntactic properties during generation. The second model, QKVAE, extends the Transformer architecture by modifying the self-attention mechanism to separately encode semantic and syntactic information in different components of the latent representation. Both models are trained on unannotated text data, with interpretability emerging from the architectural constraints rather than supervised labels. The evaluation combines quantitative performance metrics with qualitative assessments of the learned representations' interpretability.

## Key Results
- QKVAE achieves competitive performance compared to supervised models, matching the performance of a supervised model using 50K annotated samples
- ADVAE successfully controls syntactic roles in generated sentences through its interpretable latent space
- QKVAE demonstrates improved syntactic role disentanglement capabilities compared to baseline models
- Both models show that interpretable representations can be learned without annotated data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The thesis leverages the probabilistic framework of Variational Autoencoders to encourage disentangled representations, while attention mechanisms provide a natural way to separate different types of linguistic information. By constraining the latent space to encode specific linguistic concepts (syntactic roles, semantic content), the models learn to organize information in interpretable ways. The attention mechanism in Transformers is particularly well-suited for this task because it naturally captures different types of dependencies in text, which can be exploited to separate semantic and syntactic information.

## Foundational Learning
- Variational Autoencoders: Probabilistic models that learn latent representations by maximizing a lower bound on the data likelihood, useful for learning interpretable representations
- Transformer self-attention: Mechanism that computes weighted combinations of input elements, naturally capturing different types of dependencies in text
- Conditional generation: Techniques for controlling generated outputs by conditioning on specific attributes or properties
- Disentanglement learning: Methods for separating different factors of variation in learned representations
- Syntax-semantics separation: The linguistic distinction between sentence structure (syntax) and meaning (semantics)

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Input text -> Encoder (Transformer/VAE) -> Latent space (separated concepts) -> Decoder (Transformer) -> Output text

Critical path: The encoder-decoder architecture with VAE regularization is the core mechanism for learning interpretable representations. The attention mechanisms in both encoder and decoder are critical for achieving the separation of linguistic concepts.

Design tradeoffs: The models balance between reconstruction accuracy and representation interpretability, with VAE regularization encouraging disentangled latent spaces. The separation of semantic and syntactic information requires architectural constraints that may limit expressiveness compared to standard models.

Failure signatures: If the interpretability claims don't hold, the latent space may not show clear separation of concepts, or the controlled generation may not produce the expected syntactic variations. Poor reconstruction quality could indicate that the interpretability constraints are too restrictive.

## Limitations
- Interpretability claims rely heavily on qualitative evaluations and anecdotal evidence rather than systematic human studies
- Specific experimental details and statistical significance of results are not fully elaborated
- The models' performance on diverse linguistic phenomena beyond tested datasets is unclear
- Practical scalability and computational efficiency for real-world applications are not addressed
