# Evaluating Inter-Bilingual Semantic Parsing for Indian Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13005
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13005
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Inter-bilingual semantic parsing of 11 Indian languages into English logical forms, with machine-translated data showing performance gaps compared to monolingual parsing

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces IE-SEMPARSE, a novel inter-bilingual semantic parsing task for Indian languages, where utterances are parsed into English logical forms rather than native language forms. The dataset is constructed by machine-translating existing English semantic parsing datasets into 11 Indian languages, with human validation confirming translation quality for complex utterances. Experiments across multiple multilingual models demonstrate that inter-bilingual parsing is more challenging than monolingual or multilingual approaches, though translation-finetuned models can achieve comparable performance. The results show clear performance differences between high-resource and low-resource languages, with training strategies that incorporate all available data yielding the best results.

## Method Summary
The authors construct the IE-SEMPARSE dataset by machine-translating English utterances from mTOP, multilingual-TOP, and multi-ATIS++ datasets into 11 Indian languages (Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, and Telugu). Human evaluation validates translation quality for complex utterances (29% of the dataset), while BLEU scores confirm similarity to existing human-translated data. The study evaluates three semantic parsing paradigms: monolingual (same language utterance and logical form), multilingual (same language utterance, English logical form), and inter-bilingual (target language utterance, English logical form). Multiple training strategies are tested, including translation-finetuning and data augmentation approaches.

## Key Results
- Inter-bilingual semantic parsing is more challenging than monolingual or multilingual parsing across all evaluated models
- Translation-finetuned models can achieve performance comparable to monolingual models despite the added complexity
- High-resource languages show better performance than low-resource languages in inter-bilingual parsing
- Train All strategies, which leverage data from all languages, yield the best overall performance through data augmentation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Inter-bilingual semantic parsing introduces additional complexity by requiring models to map utterances in Indian languages to English logical forms, effectively adding a translation component to the semantic parsing task. The approach leverages the structural similarities between Indian languages and English logical forms while benefiting from cross-lingual transfer through multilingual pretraining. Translation-finetuning helps models better handle the language mismatch by adapting to the specific translation patterns in the dataset. The Train All strategy succeeds by exposing models to a broader distribution of linguistic patterns across all languages, improving generalization.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Semantic Parsing** - Converting natural language utterances into machine-readable logical forms; needed for task definition and evaluation metrics
2. **Multilingual Pretraining** - Training models on multiple languages simultaneously; needed to enable cross-lingual transfer for Indian languages
3. **Translation Finetuning** - Adapting pretrained models to specific translation patterns; needed to improve handling of language mismatches in inter-bilingual parsing
4. **Data Augmentation** - Using data from multiple languages to improve model robustness; needed for the Train All strategy's success
5. **BLEU Score** - Automated metric for translation quality; needed to validate machine-translated dataset quality
6. **Exact Match Evaluation** - String-based metric for semantic parsing accuracy; needed for consistent performance measurement

## Limitations

- Heavy reliance on machine translation for dataset creation introduces potential quality issues
- Human evaluation was limited to 150 examples across 5 languages, not covering all 11 languages
- Performance gaps between monolingual and inter-bilingual parsing are observed but underlying linguistic factors are not thoroughly investigated
- Exact match metric may be overly sensitive to minor differences that don't affect semantic equivalence

## Confidence

- **Dataset Quality Claims**: Medium - Human validation confirms quality for sampled subset, but machine translation covers majority of dataset
- **Model Performance Claims**: High - Clear patterns across multiple models and languages with consistent results
- **Language Resource Claims**: Medium - Supported by data but lacks detailed analysis of underlying mechanisms
