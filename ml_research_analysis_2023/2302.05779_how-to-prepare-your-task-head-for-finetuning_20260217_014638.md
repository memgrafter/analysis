# How to prepare your task head for finetuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05779
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05779
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Choice of task head influences feature adaptation during fine-tuning; mild adaptation often outperforms full/no adaptation

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how the choice of task head during fine-tuning affects feature adaptation in pretrained models. Through theoretical analysis and experiments, it demonstrates that the initial training accuracy/loss (energy) at the start of fine-tuning determines the extent of feature adaptation. The authors find that mild feature adaptation often leads to better downstream performance than either full adaptation or no adaptation at all. Practical guidelines are provided, including stopping head probing earlier, using label smoothing, and adjusting head capacity to control feature adaptation and improve generalization.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a two-stage approach called HP-FT (Head Probing followed by Fine-Tuning). First, the backbone is frozen while the randomly initialized task head is trained for τ epochs to establish initial energy. Then, the entire network is jointly fine-tuned. The authors sweep τ to control energy, use label smoothing to manipulate energy levels, and test different head capacities (linear vs MLP). Feature adaptation is measured by distances (ℓ₂, cosine, dot product) between initial and final features. Experiments use pretrained backbones (ResNet18/34/50) on ImageNet/CIFAR and downstream datasets including CIFAR10/100, STL10, Flowers102, and DomainNet.

## Key Results
- Initial training accuracy/loss (energy) determines the amount of feature adaptation during fine-tuning
- Mild feature adaptation often yields better downstream performance than either full adaptation or no adaptation
- Practical guidelines (early stopping head probing, label smoothing, adjusting head capacity) help control feature adaptation and improve generalization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Initial training accuracy/loss (energy) determines the amount of feature adaptation during fine-tuning
- Mechanism: Higher initial energy leads to less feature adaptation by reducing the gap between predictions and labels, thus limiting backbone updates
- Core assumption: Empirical neural tangent kernel changes slowly during fine-tuning
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions energy determines feature adaptation; section provides mathematical decomposition showing energy term in learning dynamics

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Mild feature adaptation often yields better downstream performance than either full adaptation or no adaptation
- Mechanism: Dot product between resulting and original features first increases then decreases with energy, indicating optimal middle ground
- Core assumption: Direction term plays important role especially at beginning of fine-tuning
- Evidence anchors: Abstract describes non-trivial trend in feature changes with energy; section shows Euclidean/cosine distances increase while dot products first increase then decrease

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Task head architecture influences feature adaptation amount
- Mechanism: Low-capacity heads prevent high training accuracy, leading to more feature adaptation; high-capacity heads might distort pretrained features
- Core assumption: Head capacity relative to backbone influences training prediction after head probing
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions adjusting head capacity as practical guideline; section discusses linear head limitations in preserving pretrained features

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pretrained backbone → Task head → Fine-tuning process
- Critical path: 1. Pretrain backbone on large dataset 2. Attach randomly initialized task head 3. Perform head probing for τ epochs 4. Fine-tune entire network 5. Evaluate downstream performance
- Design tradeoffs: Higher capacity heads achieve higher training accuracy but might distort pretrained features; lower capacity heads preserve features but might not learn downstream task well
- Failure signatures: Poor downstream performance indicates either too much or too little feature adaptation depending on initial accuracy/loss
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Sweep τ from 0 to large value using linear head, observe downstream validation accuracy
  2. Use label smoothing during head probing, compare downstream performance with baseline
  3. Try MLP head instead of linear head, evaluate impact on feature adaptation and downstream performance

## Limitations

- Theoretical analysis relies on NTK slow change assumption that may not hold in all practical scenarios
- Relationship between head capacity and feature adaptation is qualitative without clear capacity thresholds
- Experiments primarily focus on vision tasks with standard backbones, limiting generalizability to other domains
- Optimal stopping point for head probing likely depends on specific dataset and backbone requiring extensive tuning

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Initial training accuracy/loss (energy) determines feature adaptation amount - well-supported by theoretical and experimental evidence

**Medium confidence**: Mild feature adaptation yields better performance than full/no adaptation - supported by experiments but may not hold universally across all tasks

**Low confidence**: Practical guidelines (early stopping, label smoothing, capacity adjustment) are general recommendations with variable effectiveness requiring further validation
