# Selecting Features by their Resilience to the Curse of Dimensionality

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02455

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an unsupervised feature selection method that ranks features by their resilience to the curse of dimensionality. The approach leverages intrinsic dimensionality estimation to identify features that can effectively discriminate data subsets of varying sizes. The method is designed to select features that weaken the impact of high dimensionality on learning tasks. Experiments on real-world datasets demonstrate competitive performance against established feature selection techniques, with proposed approximations enabling scalability to large datasets.

## Method Summary
The proposed method adapts recent work on intrinsic dimensionality estimation to feature selection. It ranks features based on their ability to discriminate between data points and their connection to low intrinsic dimensionality. The approach is unsupervised and does not require labeled data. An approximation is introduced to handle large-scale datasets consisting of millions of points, making the method practical for real-world applications. The core idea is that features which maintain discrimination ability across different data subset sizes are more resilient to the curse of dimensionality and thus more meaningful for learning procedures.

## Key Results
- The proposed feature selection method is competitive with and often outperforms established feature selection methods on real-world datasets.
- The method successfully scales to large datasets through the proposed approximation technique.
- Features selected by the method are shown to be connected to low intrinsic dimensionality, making them more meaningful for learning tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by identifying features that maintain their ability to discriminate between data points across different subset sizes. This resilience to the curse of dimensionality indicates that these features capture the most important variations in the data structure. By focusing on features with low intrinsic dimensionality, the method selects those that represent the data's underlying manifold structure, making them more informative for downstream learning tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Feature Discrimination Module -> Intrinsic Dimensionality Estimation -> Resilience Ranking -> Feature Selection

Critical Path:
1. Compute pairwise distances between data points
2. Estimate intrinsic dimensionality for each feature
3. Calculate resilience scores based on discrimination across subset sizes
4. Rank features by resilience and select top-k features

Design Tradeoffs:
- Exact computation provides more accurate results but is computationally expensive
- Approximation enables scalability but may introduce selection errors
- Unsupervised approach is broadly applicable but may miss task-specific feature importance

Failure Signatures:
- Poor performance when data has very high intrinsic dimensionality
- Reduced effectiveness on datasets with many irrelevant or redundant features
- Approximation errors may lead to suboptimal feature selection on extremely large datasets

First Experiments:
1. Compare feature rankings on synthetic datasets with known intrinsic dimensionality
2. Evaluate selected features' impact on clustering performance
3. Test the approximation's accuracy vs. exact computation on medium-sized datasets
