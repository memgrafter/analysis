# From axioms over graphs to vectors, and back again: evaluating the properties of graph-based ontology embeddings

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16519
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16519
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: The properties of graph projection methods (totality, injectivity, simplicity) significantly impact the accuracy of ontology embeddings for axiom prediction tasks

## Executive Summary
Ontology embeddings enable machine learning on graph representations of ontologies for axiom prediction and reasoning tasks. This study systematically evaluates how different graph projection techniques affect embedding performance across three biomedical ontologies. The research demonstrates that projection method properties like injectivity and totality directly determine prediction accuracy, with OWL2Vec* excelling at subclass axioms while Onto2Graph performs better for existential restrictions. RDF projections introduce noise from blank nodes that reduces accuracy. The findings establish that graph projection choices are critical for successful ontology embedding applications.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates ontology embeddings by first applying different graph projection methods (OWL2Vec*, Onto2Graph, and RDF) to transform ontologies into graph structures. These graphs are then embedded using knowledge graph embedding techniques (primarily TransE). Axiom prediction is performed by scoring edges in the embedded graph and ranking potential axioms from the deductive closure. Performance is measured using mean rank, hits at {1, 10, 100}, and ROC AUC metrics across two large biomedical ontologies (Gene Ontology and Food Ontology) with synthetically removed axioms.

## Key Results
- OWL2Vec* excels at predicting subclass axioms but introduces scoring ambiguities due to non-injective projections
- Onto2Graph outperforms for existential restrictions by better capturing complex logical structures through injective mappings
- RDF projections suffer from noise introduced by blank nodes, reducing overall prediction accuracy
- Projection method properties (totality, injectivity, simplicity) directly correlate with embedding performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Graph projection properties directly determine the performance of ontology embedding for axiom prediction.
- Mechanism: The choice of projection method affects how well the logical structure of ontologies is preserved in the graph representation, which in turn impacts the accuracy of downstream machine learning tasks like axiom prediction.
- Core assumption: The graph projection method must accurately capture the logical relationships in the ontology for effective embedding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our experiments demonstrate that non-injective projections like OWL2Vec* can lead to ambiguities in scoring, while injective methods like Onto2Graph provide clearer axiom inference."
  - [section 4.3]: "OWL2Vec* can parse complex axioms... and generates edges... that Onto2Graph does not generate..."
  - [corpus]: The corpus includes related papers on ontology embedding and reasoning, supporting the relevance of the mechanism.
- Break condition: If the projection method fails to preserve essential logical relationships, the embedding will not accurately represent the ontology, leading to poor axiom prediction performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The injectivity of a graph projection method impacts the clarity of axiom inference.
- Mechanism: Injective projections ensure that each axiom maps to a unique subgraph, allowing for unambiguous scoring and inference of axioms from the graph embedding.
- Core assumption: Injective projections provide a one-to-one mapping between axioms and subgraphs, facilitating clear inference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We find that there are substantial differences between different projection methods, and both the projection of axioms into nodes and edges as well ontological choices in representing knowledge will impact the success of using ontology embeddings to predict axioms."
  - [section 4.3]: "Furthermore, powl2vec* is not injective because different axioms will produce the same edge..."
  - [corpus]: The corpus includes papers on knowledge graph embedding and reasoning, supporting the relevance of injectivity in graph projections.
- Break condition: If the projection is not injective, multiple axioms may map to the same subgraph, leading to ambiguity in scoring and inference.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The totality of a graph projection method affects the completeness of the embedding.
- Mechanism: Total projections utilize all axioms in the ontology, ensuring that the embedding captures the full logical structure, while partial projections may miss important relationships.
- Core assumption: Total projections provide a more complete representation of the ontology, leading to better axiom prediction performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Methods that embed ontologies in graphs (graph projections) have different formal properties related to the type of axioms they can utilize..."
  - [section 3.1]: "A graph projection for O = (Σ, Ax) is a function p that maps an ontology into a relational graph G... The function p may be total or partial with respect to O depending on whether it is defined for all axioms in the set Ax or only for some axioms."
  - [corpus]: The corpus includes papers on ontology representation and reasoning, supporting the relevance of totality in graph projections.
- Break condition: If the projection is partial, important axioms may be excluded from the graph, leading to incomplete embeddings and poor axiom prediction performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Ontology -> Graph Projection -> Graph Embedding -> Axiom Prediction

- Critical path:
  1. Load ontology and apply graph projection method
  2. Generate graph embeddings using a graph embedding technique (e.g., TransE)
  3. Use the embeddings to predict new axioms
  4. Evaluate the accuracy of axiom predictions

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Injective vs. non-injective projections: Injective projections provide clearer inference but may be more computationally expensive
  - Total vs. partial projections: Total projections capture more information but may introduce noise, while partial projections are more focused but may miss important relationships

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor axiom prediction accuracy: Indicates issues with the graph projection or embedding method
  - Ambiguity in scoring: Suggests problems with injectivity or the uniqueness of axiom representations
  - Incomplete embeddings: Points to issues with the totality of the projection method

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare the performance of injective and non-injective graph projection methods on a simple ontology
  2. Evaluate the impact of total vs. partial projections on axiom prediction accuracy
  3. Test different graph embedding techniques (e.g., TransE, TransR) to determine their effect on axiom prediction performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do non-injective graph projections impact the interpretability of predicted axioms in ontology embeddings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses that non-injective projections like OWL2Vec* can lead to ambiguities in scoring because different axioms may generate the same subgraph, leading to multiple axioms receiving the same score.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a challenge but does not provide a solution or method to address the ambiguity in scoring.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the performance of axiom prediction when using methods to disambiguate or prioritize among multiple axioms with the same score.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the effect of different graph embedding methods (e.g., TransR vs. TransE) on the performance of ontology embeddings for axiom prediction?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that TransR uses a different embedding for relations, which are embedded in a different space than nodes, and it evaluates the effect of using TransR instead of TransE in the RDF projection.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides a brief comparison between TransE and TransR for RDF projections and suggests that further evaluation of different knowledge graph embedding approaches is needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive comparison of multiple graph embedding methods across different ontology projection methods and axiom types.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of ontology projection method affect the scalability of axiom prediction in large ontologies?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the performance of different projection methods on large biomedical ontologies (GO and FoodOn) but does not explicitly address scalability issues.
- Why unresolved: While the paper evaluates performance on large ontologies, it does not discuss the computational efficiency or scalability of the methods as the size of the ontology increases.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments measuring the time and computational resources required for axiom prediction as the size of the ontology grows, comparing different projection methods.

## Limitations

- The study relies on synthetic axiom removal to evaluate prediction accuracy, which may not fully capture real-world performance where axioms are missing due to modeling gaps rather than arbitrary removal
- The comparison focuses on three specific projection methods, potentially missing other approaches that could perform differently
- Hyperparameter optimization details for the embedding models are not fully specified, which may affect reproducibility

## Confidence

- High confidence in the core finding that projection properties significantly impact embedding performance
- Medium confidence in the relative ranking of specific methods (OWL2Vec* vs Onto2Graph) due to the synthetic nature of the evaluation
- Medium confidence in the mechanism explanations, as they are largely theoretical rather than empirically validated through ablation studies

## Next Checks
