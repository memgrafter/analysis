# Regular Time-series Generation using SGM

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08518
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08518
- Reference count: 33
- This paper introduces TSGM, a score-based generative model framework for time-series generation that achieves state-of-the-art results on discriminative and predictive scores.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents TSGM, a novel framework for time-series generation using score-based generative models. The key innovation is a conditional score network that learns the gradient of the conditional log-likelihood with respect to time, combined with an RNN-based autoencoder. The method addresses the unique challenges of time-series data by preserving autoregressive dependencies through a carefully designed denoising score matching loss. TSGM outperforms existing baselines including TimeGAN on multiple real-world datasets while providing rigorous ablation studies to validate the design choices.

## Method Summary
TSGM combines an RNN-based autoencoder with a conditional score network into a unified framework for time-series generation. The encoder maps time-series data into a latent space, while the conditional score network learns to estimate the gradient of the conditional log-likelihood. A custom denoising score matching loss is derived specifically for the time-series generation task, accounting for the fully recurrent nature where each observation depends on all previous ones. The method is trained in two stages: first pre-training the encoder-decoder pair, then training the conditional score network using the denoising score matching objective.

## Key Results
- TSGM achieves state-of-the-art performance on discriminative and predictive scores across multiple datasets
- The method outperforms TimeGAN and other baselines in generating realistic time-series samples
- Rigorous ablation studies confirm the efficacy of the model design choices

- Core assumption: The encoder can reliably embed time-series data into a latent space where the conditional likelihood structure is preserved and learnable by the score network.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Encoder -> Latent Space -> Conditional Score Network -> Reverse SDE -> Decoder -> Generated Samples
- Critical path: Encoder → Latent Space → Conditional Score Network → Reverse SDE → Decoder → Generated Samples
- Design tradeoffs:
  - RNN vs Transformer for encoder: RNNs preserve temporal order naturally but may struggle with long-range dependencies
  - U-Net depth: Deeper networks can capture more complex patterns but increase computational cost
  - Sampling steps: More steps improve sample quality but increase generation time
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor discriminative score: Generated samples are distinguishable from real data
  - Poor predictive score: Generated samples don't preserve temporal patterns needed for forecasting
  - Training instability: Loss doesn't converge or shows high variance
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train encoder-decoder pair only and evaluate reconstruction quality to ensure latent space preserves temporal information
  2. Train conditional score network with pre-trained encoder-decoder and evaluate score matching loss convergence
  3. Generate samples and evaluate discriminative and predictive scores against baselines to confirm performance improvements

## Limitations
- The paper lacks detailed architectural specifications for the U-Net modifications required for 1D time-series processing, making exact replication challenging
- No ablation studies on the impact of different weight functions λ(s) in the denoising score matching loss, leaving uncertainty about optimal choices
- Limited comparison with newer time-series generation methods beyond TimeGAN and VAE-based approaches

## Confidence
- High confidence in the overall framework design combining RNN autoencoder with conditional score network
- Medium confidence in the denoising score matching formulation, as the derivation is provided but not extensively validated
- Low confidence in hyperparameter sensitivity and robustness, as only limited sensitivity studies are presented
