# How robust accuracy suffers from certified training with convex relaxations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06995
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06995
- Reference count: 40
- This paper shows that certified training with convex relaxations consistently achieves worse standard and robust error than adversarial training across multiple datasets and threat models.

## Executive Summary
This paper systematically compares adversarial training (AT) and certified training (COAP, IBP, FAST-IBP, CROWN-IBP) for robust neural network training on image datasets. The authors find that certified training methods consistently underperform adversarial training, achieving worse standard and robust error across MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Tiny ImageNet datasets for both ℓ∞ and ℓ2 threat models. They identify three key factors contributing to this performance gap: the shape of the perturbation set, the perturbation budget, and the margin of the data distribution. The paper introduces the concept of "unstable neurons" - neurons whose pre-activation bounds straddle zero during perturbations - as the central mechanism explaining why certified training performs worse.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates adversarial training using 10-step Auto-PGD inner optimization and certified training using various convex relaxation methods with their specific schedules and parameters. All models are evaluated using AutoAttack (AA+) for empirical robust error. The study covers three datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Tiny ImageNet) with CNN, ResNet, and WideResNet architectures respectively, testing both ℓ∞-ball and ℓ2-ball threat models with varying perturbation budgets. The authors introduce the "unstable neurons" metric to quantify how many neurons have uncertain activation states during training, which they correlate with the performance gap between training paradigms.

## Key Results
- Certified training with convex relaxations achieves worse standard and robust error than adversarial training across all tested datasets and threat models
- The performance gap increases with larger perturbation budgets and smaller data margins
- The number of unstable neurons during training correlates strongly with the error gap between certified and adversarial training
- COAP fails to converge for larger perturbation budgets, while IBP-based methods scale better but still underperform AT

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Convex relaxations become looser when more neurons have uncertain activation states (unstable neurons)
- Mechanism: Unstable neurons occur when pre-activation bounds straddle zero, forcing the convex relaxation to over-approximate the feasible region. This over-approximation introduces error into both training and evaluation.
- Core assumption: The number of unstable neurons directly correlates with the tightness of the convex relaxation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that in most tasks and for both ℓ∞-ball and ℓ2-ball threat models, certified training with convex relaxations suffers from worse standard and robust error than adversarial training"
  - [section] "Given a neural network fθ and a dataset D = {(xi, yi)}n i=1, we define the number of unstable neurons as UnstNeur(fθ) = 1 mn nX i=1 mX j=1 I{ℓθ j(xi) ≤ 0 ≤ uθ j(xi)}"
  - [corpus] "Gaussian Loss Smoothing Enables Certified Training with Tight Convex Relaxations" suggests convex relaxation tightness is a key factor in certified training performance
- Break condition: If the network architecture or input data ensures all neurons are either always active or always inactive, unstable neurons cannot occur.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Perturbation set alignment with the signal direction increases unstable neurons and worsens performance
- Mechanism: When the perturbation set aligns with the shortest path to the decision boundary (signal direction), more neurons cross the activation threshold during perturbations, increasing the number of unstable neurons. This leads to looser convex relaxations and larger error gaps.
- Core assumption: The signal direction is the direction along the shortest path to the decision boundary.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we define the signal direction s(x, y) for a data point (x, y) ∈ X × Y and the robust Bayes optimal classifier f∗ : X → Y, as the direction along the shortest path to the decision boundary"
  - [section] "we can observe how for both the CIFAR-10 and the concentric spheres datasets, the number of unstable neurons increases with larger alignment"
  - [corpus] "RNN-Guard: Certified Robustness Against Multi-frame Attacks for Recurrent Neural Networks" indicates perturbation direction is crucial for robustness
- Break condition: If the perturbation set is orthogonal to all signal directions or the data distribution has no clear signal direction.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Larger perturbation budgets and smaller margins increase unstable neurons, worsening certified training performance
- Mechanism: Both larger perturbation budgets and smaller margins force the decision boundary to become more complex, requiring more piecewise linear regions. This complexity increases the number of neurons that straddle activation thresholds, leading to more unstable neurons and looser convex relaxations.
- Core assumption: The complexity of the decision boundary is directly related to the number of piecewise linear regions needed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We interpret these findings as follows: both smaller margin and larger perturbation budget increase the model complexity of the decision boundary with good robust training accuracy"
  - [section] "both smaller margin and larger perturbation budget increase the model complexity of the decision boundary with good robust training accuracy"
  - [corpus] "Enhancing the Antidote: Improved Pointwise Certifications against Poisoning Attacks" suggests margin plays a crucial role in robustness guarantees
- Break condition: If the perturbation budget is so small that no neuron becomes unstable, or the margin is so large that the decision boundary is trivial.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but based on the findings, several important questions arise:

- Whether the performance gap between certified training with convex relaxations and adversarial training persists on larger datasets like ImageNet
- How the shape of the decision boundary mediates the relationship between unstable neurons and the error gap
- Whether modifications to convex relaxations can reduce the error gap without sacrificing computational efficiency

## Limitations
- The study is limited to image classification tasks and may not generalize to other domains like NLP
- The analysis is primarily observational rather than establishing causal relationships between unstable neurons and performance gaps
- Theoretical guarantees of convex relaxations are not directly tested against their empirical performance gap

## Confidence
- General performance gap finding: **High**
- Unstable neurons as primary mechanism: **Medium**
- Applicability across all architectures/tasks: **Low**
