# Purposeful and Operation-based Cognitive System for AGI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13556
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13556
- Reference count: 4
- One-line primary result: Proposes MOM, a cognitive model for AGI that extends previous models by introducing operational models and will

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes MOM (Model Of Models), a cognitive architecture for AGI that extends previous models by introducing operational models (frames/classes) and will. The model is based on the duality principle and a holistic approach that combines top-down and bottom-up learning, generalization and specialization. The core idea is to represent knowledge as a dynamic semantic network of models and instances, where models are abstract classes and instances are concrete objects.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a hybrid approach combining deep learning and symbolic manipulation to create a dynamic operational memory of models and instances. The model learning process involves empirical (from examples) and rule-based approaches, as well as consolidation over time to create stable patterns. The architecture aims to unify static knowledge representations with dynamic operational memory through action connections that can produce new elements, enabling the creation and updating of hierarchies on the fly.

## Key Results
- Introduces operational models (frames/classes) and will as extensions to previous cognitive models
- Proposes a hybrid Neuro-Symbolic system combining empirical and rule-based learning approaches
- Presents consolidation as a mechanism to transform chaotic knowledge into stable, discrete patterns

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: MOM unifies static knowledge representations with dynamic operational memory by introducing operational models and will
- Mechanism: Static semantic networks become dynamic through action connections that can produce new elements, enabling creation and updating of hierarchies on the fly
- Core assumption: Knowledge representation must be both interpretable (symbolic) and flexible (adaptive), achieved by combining top-down rule-based learning with bottom-up empirical learning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "introduces operational models (frames/classes) and will"
  - [section]: "operationability introduces a new type of association to objects: actions that act upon them, thus producing new knowledge elements"
  - [corpus]: Weak - neighbor papers discuss world models and cognitive architectures but do not directly support MOM's specific duality and operational model claims
- Break condition: If action space is too large or actions lack admissibility constraints, the model could become computationally intractable or produce irrelevant hierarchies

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Model learning in MOM combines empirical and expertise approaches to form a hybrid Neuro-Symbolic system
- Mechanism: Empirical learning is bottom-up from examples (induction), while rule-based learning is top-down via abstract language (deduction)
- Core assumption: Complex concepts can be learned either through examples or rules, and best results come from combining both approaches
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "two contrary but completing learning approaches in AI are combined: empirical, i.e. from examples (induction), and expertise (rule-based)"
  - [abstract]: "a holistic approach is advocated for AGI designing and cognition under constraints or efficiency is proposed, in the form of reusability and simplicity"
  - [corpus]: Weak - neighbor papers mention cognitive architectures but do not directly support MOM's hybrid learning mechanism
- Break condition: If rule-based component is too rigid or empirical component lacks sufficient examples, the model may fail to generalize or overfit

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Consolidation transforms chaotic, continuous knowledge into stable, discrete patterns over time
- Mechanism: Through repetition and sparsification, the model reduces hypothesis space from infinite possibilities to a small set of consistent patterns
- Core assumption: The brain and AGI agents must compress vast amounts of information into manageable, reusable patterns to function efficiently
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "consolidation is about transforming from chaos to some stable order of patterns, or from a continuous realm to a discrete one"
  - [abstract]: "reaching this mature state is described via a cognitive evolution from infancy to adulthood, utilizing a consolidation principle"
  - [corpus]: Weak - neighbor papers discuss world models and cognitive architectures but do not directly support MOM's consolidation mechanism
- Break condition: If consolidation is too aggressive, the model may lose important details; if too lenient, it may never achieve efficient pattern recognition

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Duality Principle
  - Why needed here: Ensures that every cognitive aspect has an inverse, enabling bidirectional processing and balance between extremes
  - Quick check question: Can you identify a cognitive function in MOM that lacks a clear inverse operation?

- Concept: Hybrid Learning (Empirical + Rule-based)
  - Why needed here: Allows MOM to learn both symbolic and sub-symbolic knowledge, combining strengths of connectionist and symbolic AI
  - Quick check question: How would you design an experiment to test whether MOM's hybrid learning outperforms purely empirical or purely rule-based learning?

- Concept: Consolidation and Reusability
  - Why needed here: Enables efficient learning by compressing knowledge into stable patterns and reusing existing models instead of generating new ones
  - Quick check question: What metrics would you use to measure the effectiveness of consolidation in MOM?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Perception → Model Learning (Empirical/Rule-based) → Operational Memory Update → Consolidation → Reusability
- Critical path: Knowledge representation flows from perception through hybrid learning to operational memory, then undergoes consolidation and reusability for efficient storage and retrieval
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Flexibility vs. Interpretability: MOM aims for both but may struggle with computational complexity
  - Abstraction vs. Detail: Balancing high-level models with low-level instances
  - Consolidation vs. Adaptability: Too much consolidation may hinder learning new patterns
- Failure signatures:
  - Memory explosion: If consolidation fails, the operational memory may grow uncontrollably
  - Inconsistent models: If hybrid learning is unbalanced, models may conflict or fail to generalize
  - Admissibility errors: If action constraints are too loose or too tight, the model may produce irrelevant or impossible actions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test hybrid learning by training MOM on a dataset with both symbolic rules and empirical examples (e.g., math problems with worked solutions and raw data)
  2. Evaluate consolidation by measuring memory usage and pattern stability over time as MOM processes a stream of information
  3. Assess reusability by comparing the number of new models generated vs. reused models as MOM learns more tasks
