# A Comparative Study on Generative Models for High Resolution Solar Observation Imaging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07169
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07169
- Reference count: 40
- We study generative models for high resolution solar EUV imaging, comparing GANs (StyleGAN, ProjectedGAN) and diffusion models (ADM) on 1024x1024 SDO/AIA 193Å data. Standard StyleGAN2/3 fail to reproduce fine-scale details like coronal loops, while diffusion models succeed. ProjectedGAN achieves best FID=2.2 with multi-scale discriminators, pre-trained frozen features, and cross-scale/channel mixing. Human expert evaluation confirms generated samples are indistinguishable from real images. Ablations show multi-scale mechanisms and pre-training are critical. Diffusion models obtain higher FID (15.2) despite good visual quality, likely due to pixel distribution mismatch. Code, models, and workflows are open-sourced.

## Executive Summary
This study compares generative models for high-resolution solar EUV imaging, focusing on reproducing fine-scale details like coronal loops. While standard StyleGAN architectures fail to capture these features, ProjectedGAN achieves state-of-the-art results with FID=2.2 through multi-scale discriminators, pre-trained frozen features, and cross-scale/channel mixing. Diffusion models generate visually superior fine-scale details but suffer from higher FID scores due to pixel distribution mismatches. Human expert evaluation confirms the high quality of Generated ProjectedGAN samples.

## Method Summary
The study trains StyleGAN2/3, ProjectedGAN, and ADM diffusion models on 1024x1024 SDO/AIA 193Å solar images (40K samples). Data is preprocessed with log transform and normalization to [0,1]. ProjectedGAN uses a StyleGAN2 backbone with frozen EfficientNet-Lite0 feature extractor, cross-scale/channel mixing, and four multi-scale discriminators. Models are trained using distributed training on supercomputers. Evaluation includes FID, rFID, KID, CLIP-RN50-FID, precision, recall, and human expert assessment.

## Key Results
- ProjectedGAN achieves best FID=2.2 on solar EUV images through multi-scale discriminators and pre-trained frozen features
- Standard StyleGAN2/3 fail to reproduce fine-scale details like coronal loops, while diffusion models succeed
- Human expert evaluation confirms ProjectedGAN samples are indistinguishable from real images
- Diffusion models obtain higher FID (15.2) despite visually superior fine-scale details due to pixel distribution mismatch

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Multi-scale discriminators are essential for capturing fine-scale details in solar images. The multi-scale discriminator setup processes features from different layers of a pre-trained feature network, allowing independent handling of fine and coarse scales. This structure ensures that features at all scales contribute to the generative process. If the multi-scale structure is removed or replaced with a single-scale discriminator, fine-scale details like coronal loops are not properly reproduced.

### Mechanism 2
Pre-trained frozen feature extractors are crucial for generating high-quality solar images. A frozen pre-trained feature network (like EfficientNet-Lite) extracts features from both real and generated images. These features are mixed across scales and processed by multiple discriminators, providing a stable and rich representation for the generative model. If the feature extractor is randomly initialized or unfrozen, the quality of generated images degrades significantly.

### Mechanism 3
Cross-scale and cross-channel mixing are necessary for proper fine-scale generation in solar images. Cross-scale mixing interpolates and sums feature representations from coarser to finer scales, while cross-channel mixing embeds feature channels into a higher-dimensional space. These techniques ensure that information flows effectively across scales and channels. If cross-scale or cross-channel mixing is disabled, the generated images show clear artifacts and loss of fine-scale details.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Generator (StyleGAN2) -> Feature Network (EfficientNet-Lite0, frozen) -> Cross-scale/channel mixing -> Multi-scale discriminators (4) -> Loss calculation
- Critical path: 1. Extract features from real and generated images using frozen pre-trained feature network. 2. Mix features across scales and channels. 3. Process mixed features with multiple discriminators. 4. Update generator and discriminators based on the loss.
- Design tradeoffs: Pre-trained vs. randomly initialized feature extractor: Pre-trained provides better performance but may introduce domain shift. Multi-scale vs. single-scale discriminators: Multi-scale captures fine details better but increases computational cost. Cross-scale vs. no cross-scale mixing: Cross-scale improves quality but adds complexity.
- Failure signatures: Poor fine-scale details (missing coronal loops), distorted solar disk shape, high FID scores despite good visual quality
- First 3 experiments: 1. Train basic StyleGAN2 model to observe failure in fine-scale detail generation. 2. Train ProjectedGAN with all components to achieve high-quality results. 3. Perform ablation studies by disabling individual components to understand their contribution.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does the failure of basic StyleGAN architectures to generate fine-scale details in solar images stem from inherent dataset properties (e.g., pixel intensity distributions, texture patterns) rather than just architectural limitations? The paper notes that StyleGANv2/3 work well on natural face images but fail on solar images, suggesting dataset-specific challenges. Controlled experiments ablating dataset properties would be needed to isolate their impact.

### Open Question 2
Can alternative evaluation metrics better capture the quality of fine-scale details in scientific image generation, addressing the limitations of FID observed in this study? The paper highlights that FID scores for diffusion models (15.2) are higher than ProjectedGAN (2.2), despite diffusion models producing visually superior fine-scale details. Development of domain-specific evaluation metrics tailored to scientific image features would help resolve this discrepancy.

### Open Question 3
How do temporal and multi-spectral information from the SDO dataset impact the performance of generative models for solar image generation? The study acknowledges that it only trains on static solar images of a specific wavelength, without leveraging temporal or multi-spectral data available in the SDO dataset. Comparative experiments training on multi-spectral or temporally sequenced data would reveal potential benefits.

## Limitations
- Limited ablation evidence for multi-scale discriminators' necessity, with quantitative comparisons for each ablation not provided
- Human evaluation lacks detail on rater expertise and inter-rater reliability
- Domain gap between ImageNet-pretrained features and solar images remains unvalidated

## Confidence

**High confidence**: ProjectedGAN achieves best FID (2.2) among tested GAN variants on this specific dataset; diffusion models show different FID behavior due to pixel distribution characteristics.

**Medium confidence**: Multi-scale discriminators and pre-trained frozen features are necessary components for high-quality solar image generation; cross-scale/channel mixing contributes to coronal loop reproduction.

**Low confidence**: ImageNet-pretrained features are optimal for solar domain transfer; the specific architectural choices are uniquely suited to solar imaging versus other fine-scale generation tasks.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation quantification**: Systematically disable each component (multi-scale discriminators, cross-scale mixing, frozen features) and measure FID changes with statistical significance testing across multiple runs.

2. **Feature extractor comparison**: Replace EfficientNet-Lite0 with randomly initialized network and with fine-tuned version, measuring both FID and qualitative generation quality for coronal structures.

3. **Domain adaptation validation**: Train the same ProjectedGAN architecture on non-solar fine-scale datasets (e.g., microscopy, remote sensing) to test whether multi-scale mechanisms are solar-specific or generally beneficial for fine-detail generation.
