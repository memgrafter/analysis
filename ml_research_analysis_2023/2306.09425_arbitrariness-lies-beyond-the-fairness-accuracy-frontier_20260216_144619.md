# Arbitrariness Lies Beyond the Fairness-Accuracy Frontier

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09425
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09425
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art fairness interventions can arbitrarily increase predictive multiplicity even while improving fairness-accuracy metrics.

## Executive Summary
This paper reveals a critical limitation of fairness interventions in machine learning: while they improve group fairness metrics, they can significantly increase predictive multiplicity—where multiple models achieve similar fairness and accuracy but disagree on individual predictions. The authors demonstrate both theoretically and empirically that this multiplicity can be arbitrarily high under strict fairness and accuracy constraints. They propose an ensemble method that provably reduces this disagreement while maintaining fairness, showing that ensembles of as few as 30 models can reduce score variability to baseline levels.

## Method Summary
The paper combines theoretical analysis with empirical evaluation across three real-world datasets (HSLS, ENEM, Adult). The authors train baseline models (RF, GBM, LR) with different random seeds, apply five fairness interventions (Reduction, Fair Projection, Reject Options, EqOdds, Leveraging), and measure predictive multiplicity through ambiguity and score standard deviation. They then propose an ensemble algorithm that aggregates multiple fair models to reduce multiplicity. The theoretical component proves that fairness constraints can enlarge the solution space, creating multiple equally optimal models with different decision boundaries.

## Key Results
- Fairness interventions significantly increase per-sample score standard deviation, especially for the most affected individuals
- Ensembling with as few as 30 models reduces score variability to baseline levels while maintaining fairness
- Predictive multiplicity can be arbitrarily high even under strict fairness and accuracy constraints
- The phenomenon occurs across multiple real-world datasets and fairness intervention methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fairness interventions can increase predictive multiplicity even when they improve group fairness metrics.
- Mechanism: Adding fairness constraints to the optimization problem enlarges the solution space rather than shrinking it, creating multiple equally optimal models with different decision boundaries.
- Core assumption: The fairness constraint creates non-convexities that allow multiple distinct classifiers to satisfy both fairness and accuracy requirements.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We demonstrate that state-of-the-art fairness interventions may improve group fairness metrics at the expense of exacerbating arbitrariness."
  - [section 3] "We demonstrate that, counter-intuitively, adding a fairness constraint can enlarge the set of optimal models, thereby increasing predictive multiplicity."
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss multiplicity and arbitrariness but don't specifically address this mechanism.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Ensembling reduces predictive multiplicity by concentrating the predictions of multiple models.
- Mechanism: The ensemble acts as a smoothing operation where individual model disagreements are averaged out, leading to more consistent predictions across samples.
- Core assumption: The constituent models are sufficiently diverse but still achieve similar accuracy and fairness levels.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose an ensemble algorithm that reduces multiplicity while maintaining fairness and accuracy."
  - [section 4.1] "we prove that any two different ensembling methods agree for fixed individuals with high probability."
  - [corpus] Weak - related work discusses ensemble methods but not specifically for reducing multiplicity in fairness interventions.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Predictive multiplicity can be arbitrarily high even under strict fairness and accuracy constraints.
- Mechanism: The Rashomon set (set of models with similar performance) can be constructed to have maximum ambiguity while maintaining fairness by carefully partitioning error regions.
- Core assumption: The hypothesis class is unconstrained or sufficiently rich to allow such constructions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3] "Proposition 3.1 (Existence of Rashomon Set with Worst Case Ambiguity)... there exists an empirical Rashomon set... satisfying Overall Accuracy Equality perfectly but with maximal ambiguity."
  - [section 3] "Example 2... adding a fairness constraint can enlarge the set of optimal threshold classifiers to two classifiers... with indistinguishable accuracy and fairness levels (Right) but different decision regions."
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers discuss arbitrariness but don't provide this specific construction.

## Limitations

- The theoretical construction assumes unconstrained hypothesis classes, which may not reflect practical model families
- Empirical evaluation focuses on three datasets with binary group attributes, limiting generalizability
- The study only examines binary classification settings and does not explore multi-class or continuous fairness notions

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The empirical observation that fairness interventions increase score variance is well-supported across multiple datasets and methods
- **Medium confidence**: The theoretical proof of arbitrarily high multiplicity under fairness constraints, while mathematically sound, relies on idealized assumptions about hypothesis classes
- **Medium confidence**: The ensemble solution effectively reduces multiplicity, though the mechanism for why 30 models is sufficient could benefit from deeper theoretical justification
