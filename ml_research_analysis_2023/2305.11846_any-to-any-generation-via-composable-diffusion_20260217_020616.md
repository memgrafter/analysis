# Any-to-Any Generation via Composable Diffusion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11846
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11846
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: State-of-the-art or competitive performance on multimodal generation with any-to-any conditioning capabilities

## Executive Summary
Composable Diffusion (CoDi) introduces a novel generative AI model capable of simultaneously processing and generating arbitrary combinations of modalities including text, image, video, and audio. Unlike existing models limited to specific input-output modality pairs, CoDi uses a "Bridging Alignment" strategy to efficiently align multimodal embeddings via a text bridge and "Latent Alignment" to synchronize generation across modalities in a shared latent space. This enables zero-shot multi-condition generation and any-to-any joint output synthesis without training on all combinations.

The model achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on unimodal tasks while demonstrating superior coherence in multimodal joint generation. Trained on large-scale paired datasets (e.g., LAION-400M, AudioSet, WebVid), CoDi outperforms independent modality generation in alignment metrics and shows strong capabilities in text-to-image, image-to-video, audio captioning, and multimodal joint generation tasks.

## Method Summary
CoDi employs a novel composable generation strategy built upon latent diffusion models (LDMs) for each modality. The method uses "Bridging Alignment" to align prompt encoders across modalities using text as a universal bridge through contrastive learning, reducing training complexity from O(n²) to O(n). "Latent Alignment" synchronizes generation across modalities by projecting latent variables into a shared space via modality-specific environment encoders and training cross-attention modules to attend across modalities. The architecture enables incremental training where individual modality LDMs are trained first, followed by alignment components, allowing zero-shot joint generation of modality combinations not present in training data.

## Key Results
- Text-to-image FID of 11.26, achieving state-of-the-art performance
- Audio captioning SPIDEr of 0.480, demonstrating strong performance
- Superior video-audio coherence in joint generation (0.240 vs 0.255 for independent generation)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Bridging Alignment
- **Claim**: Efficiently aligns multimodal embeddings using text as a universal bridge modality
- **Mechanism**: Trains modality-specific prompt encoders (text, image, video, audio) via contrastive learning anchored on text, reducing training objectives from O(n²) to O(n)
- **Core assumption**: Text is sufficiently present and aligned in paired datasets across all modalities
- **Evidence**: Trained audio and video prompt encoders on audio-text and video-text paired datasets using contrastive learning with text and image encoder weights frozen
- **Break condition**: If text is not sufficiently present or aligned in paired datasets for some modality

### Mechanism 2: Latent Alignment
- **Claim**: Enables any-to-any joint generation by aligning environment encoders across modalities in a shared latent space
- **Mechanism**: Projects latent variables of different modalities into a shared latent space via modality-specific environment encoders; trains cross-attention modules to attend across modalities
- **Core assumption**: Environment encoders can be trained to align latent spaces such that interpolation enables zero-shot joint generation
- **Evidence**: Demonstrated zero-shot joint generation of modalities not present in training data through shared latent space alignment
- **Break condition**: If latent spaces cannot be aligned sufficiently, cross-attention will fail to produce coherent multimodal outputs

### Mechanism 3: Composable Architecture
- **Claim**: Allows incremental training and reuse of modality-specific models while maintaining generation quality
- **Mechanism**: Trains individual modality LDMs independently first; then trains cross-attention and environment encoders only for joint tasks while freezing LDMs
- **Core assumption**: Individual LDMs trained on large-scale modality-specific data are sufficient quality foundations for cross-modal tasks
- **Evidence**: Maintained strong performance on unimodal tasks while adding joint generation capabilities
- **Break condition**: If modality-specific LDMs are not trained to high quality, composable generation will degrade

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Text/Image/Video/Audio encoders -> LDMs (UNet-based diffusers) -> Environment encoders -> Shared latent space -> Cross-attention modules -> VAE autoencoders

- **Critical path**: 
  1. Train individual LDMs with bridging alignment prompt encoders
  2. Train environment encoders and cross-attention for joint tasks
  3. Freeze LDMs, train only cross-attention and environment encoders
  4. Inference: condition on any modality combination, generate any output combination

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Composability vs. end-to-end training: Incremental training enables reuse but may limit fine-grained cross-modal interaction
  - Bridging alignment vs. direct pairwise alignment: Reduces training complexity but relies on text as a universal bridge
  - Latent space alignment vs. feature space alignment: Enables zero-shot joint generation but requires careful encoder training

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor alignment metrics between modalities → misaligned cross-attention
  - Degraded FID scores in single-modality tasks → LDMs not trained sufficiently
  - Inconsistent outputs across modalities → environment encoders misaligned

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test bridging alignment: generate image from text+audio prompt and measure quality vs. text-only
  2. Test latent alignment: jointly generate video+audio from text and compare coherence vs. independent generation
  3. Test composability: train video+audio joint generation and then generate video+text to check zero-shot transfer

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper explicitly mentions limitations around potential biases and stereotypes in generated outputs if training data contains such biases, but does not detail other open questions beyond these known limitations.

## Limitations

- Reliance on text as a universal bridge modality may limit performance when paired datasets lack strong cross-modal alignment
- Zero-shot joint generation claims depend heavily on latent space alignment quality, which is difficult to verify without extensive ablation studies
- Incremental training approach may not fully capture complex cross-modal interactions compared to end-to-end training

## Confidence

- **Bridging Alignment mechanism**: High confidence - well-supported by contrastive learning literature and architectural description
- **Latent Alignment effectiveness**: Medium confidence - strong theoretical foundation but limited empirical validation across all modality combinations
- **Composable architecture benefits**: Medium confidence - demonstrated through quantitative metrics but requires more extensive ablation studies

## Next Checks

1. Conduct systematic ablation studies removing Bridging Alignment to quantify the impact on cross-modal generation quality across different modality pairs
2. Evaluate latent space alignment quality using similarity metrics between generated modalities in joint generation tasks
3. Compare incremental training approach against end-to-end training on a subset of modality combinations to assess performance trade-offs
