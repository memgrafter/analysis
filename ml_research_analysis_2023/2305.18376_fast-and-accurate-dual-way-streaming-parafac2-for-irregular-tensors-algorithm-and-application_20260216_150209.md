# Fast and Accurate Dual-Way Streaming PARAFAC2 for Irregular Tensors -- Algorithm and Application

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18376
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18376
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Dash achieves up to 14.0× faster speed than existing PARAFAC2 decomposition methods for newly arrived data while maintaining lower local errors for newly arrived tensors.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of analyzing irregular tensors in a dual-way streaming setting, where both new rows of existing matrices and new matrices arrive over time. The authors propose Dash, a fast and accurate PARAFAC2 decomposition method specifically designed for this setting. Dash achieves high efficiency by dividing terms related to old and new data, computing only the terms for new data, and avoiding naive computations with old data. It also incorporates a forgetting factor to allow the method to follow recent data movements, making it particularly effective for anomaly detection in real-world datasets.

## Method Summary
Dash tackles dual-way streaming PARAFAC2 decomposition by splitting the loss function into terms involving old data (Xₖ,old, Uₖ,old) and new data (Xₖ,new, Uₖ,new), then computing only the new-data terms while loading precomputed old-data terms from previous updates. The algorithm uses closed-form update rules for Uₖ, Sₖ, and V matrices that only require operations on new data, with helper matrices (cₖ,old, Dₖ,old, Fold, Gold) cached from previous updates. A forgetting factor λ scales the contribution of old data in the loss function, making factor matrices fit more to recently arrived data while gradually discarding older information. The method is initialized using SPARTan and updates incrementally as new data arrives.

## Key Results
- Dash achieves up to 14.0× faster speed than existing PARAFAC2 decomposition methods for newly arrived data
- Dash achieves lower local errors for newly arrived tensors compared to competitors
- Dash successfully detects various anomalies in real-world datasets, including economic crises like the Dot-com Bubble and the Subprime Mortgage Crisis

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer (New data Xₖ,new and pre-existing factor matrices Uₖ,old, Sₖ,old, Vold) -> Helper matrix cache (cₖ,old, Dₖ,old, Fold, Gold) -> Update engine (Closed-form update rules for Uₖ,new, Sₖ, V) -> Output layer (Updated factor matrices Uₖ, Sₖ, V and new helper matrices for next update)

- Critical path: New data -> Update Uₖ -> Update Sₖ -> Update V -> Cache new helper matrices

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory vs. Speed: Caching helper matrices requires memory but avoids recomputation
  - Freshness vs. Stability: Forgetting factor λ trades off between adapting to recent changes and maintaining stable long-term patterns
  - Accuracy vs. Efficiency: Incremental updates may be less accurate than full recomputation but much faster

- Failure signatures:
  - Memory overflow: Cache of helper matrices grows too large
  - Divergence: Factor matrices fail to converge with small λ values
  - Inaccuracy: Global error increases significantly despite low local errors

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Benchmark running time vs. data size on synthetic irregular tensor with known ground truth
  2. Vary forgetting factor λ to observe trade-off between local and global reconstruction errors
  3. Test anomaly detection on synthetic data with injected anomalies at different time points

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How can the forgetting factor be adjusted adaptively to better handle varying characteristics of newly arrived data in a dual-way streaming setting?
- What are the implications of using a forgetting factor on the uniqueness and interpretability of the PARAFAC2 decomposition results?
- How does the performance of Dash compare to other streaming tensor decomposition methods in terms of handling concept drift and change points in the data?

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of the forgetting factor mechanism is not thoroughly validated across different types of data patterns, and the scalability analysis is limited to specific update cycles.
- The claims about anomaly detection performance are based on visual inspection of trends rather than quantitative metrics, and the comparison with baselines may be implementation-dependent.
- Memory requirements for caching helper matrices are not discussed, which could be significant for large-scale applications.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of dividing terms between old and new data for computational efficiency is well-supported by the mathematical formulation and update rules presented.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the forgetting factor in balancing recent versus historical data importance is demonstrated empirically but lacks theoretical guarantees or extensive sensitivity analysis.
- **Low Confidence**: The claims about anomaly detection performance are based on visual inspection of trends rather than quantitative metrics, and the comparison with baselines may be implementation-dependent.
