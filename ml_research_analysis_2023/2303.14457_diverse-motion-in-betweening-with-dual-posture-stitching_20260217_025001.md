# Diverse Motion In-betweening with Dual Posture Stitching

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14457
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14457
- Reference count: 40
- Generates diverse motion in-betweening from only two input frames using dual-posture stitching approach

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenging problem of motion in-betweening using only two frames (start and target) - a scenario where existing methods struggle due to limited input information. The authors propose a dual-posture stitching approach that generates forward motion from the start frame and backward motion from the target frame using two adversarial autoregressive networks based on conditional variational autoencoders (CVAEs). These sequences are then stitched together in the middle. The method introduces novel stitching loss functions and CVAE adaptations including latent interpolation, bi-directional aligning, and phase modulation to achieve high-quality transitions. Experiments demonstrate the approach generates more diverse and higher quality motion sequences compared to existing methods while achieving perfect alignment with target frames.

## Method Summary
The proposed method tackles motion in-betweening by generating sequences in both forward and backward directions from the two input frames. Two separate CVAE-based autoregressive networks are trained: one generates motion forward from the start frame, and the other generates motion backward from the target frame. The forward generator takes the start frame and random noise to predict subsequent frames, while the backward generator does the reverse from the target frame. These two sequences are then stitched together at their middle points. To enable high-quality stitching, the authors introduce a novel stitching loss and make several adaptations to the CVAE framework including latent interpolation between forward and backward latent codes, bi-directional aligning to ensure compatibility, and phase modulation to improve temporal coherence.

## Key Results
- Achieves higher motion quality and more diverse results than existing methods on LaFAN1 and Human3.6m datasets
- Perfect alignment to target frames with improved naturalness metrics (NPSS scores)
- Significant reduction in foot sliding compared to baseline methods
- Superior performance across quantitative metrics including Frechet Motion Distance (FMD)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The dual-posture stitching approach works by leveraging the complementary strengths of forward and backward motion prediction. By generating sequences from both endpoints simultaneously, the method can better capture the motion context and constraints from both the start and target positions. The stitching mechanism allows each network to focus on generating high-quality motion in its respective direction without the burden of reaching a distant target. The CVAE adaptations ensure that the latent spaces of the forward and backward generators are compatible, enabling smooth interpolation and alignment. The adversarial training further refines the generated motions to be more realistic and diverse.

## Foundational Learning
- **Conditional Variational Autoencoder (CVAE)**: A generative model that learns to produce outputs conditioned on input data while maintaining a probabilistic latent space. Why needed: Enables controlled generation of motion sequences while maintaining diversity through random sampling. Quick check: Verify that the model can generate multiple plausible sequences from the same input by varying the latent code.

- **Autoregressive Networks**: Neural networks that generate sequences by predicting each element based on previous elements. Why needed: Essential for modeling the temporal dependencies in motion sequences where each frame depends on previous frames. Quick check: Confirm that the network can accurately predict the next frame given a sequence of previous frames.

- **Adversarial Training**: A training framework where a generator network is trained to fool a discriminator network that tries to distinguish real from generated data. Why needed: Improves the realism and diversity of generated motions by encouraging the generator to produce outputs indistinguishable from real motion data. Quick check: Evaluate whether the discriminator can reliably distinguish between real and generated motions during training.

- **Motion Stitching**: The process of combining two motion sequences at a junction point while maintaining continuity. Why needed: Allows combining forward and backward generated sequences while preserving smooth transitions and temporal coherence. Quick check: Verify that the stitched motion shows no discontinuities or unnatural transitions at the junction point.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Input Frames -> Forward Generator -> Forward Sequence; Input Frames -> Backward Generator -> Backward Sequence; Forward Sequence + Backward Sequence -> Stitching Module -> Output Sequence

Critical Path: Input frames are processed by their respective generators to create forward and backward sequences, which are then aligned and stitched together through the stitching module to produce the final output.

Design Tradeoffs: The method trades computational complexity (running two generators and a stitching module) for improved quality and diversity. Using separate generators for forward and backward motion allows each to specialize but requires careful alignment during stitching.

Failure Signatures: Poor stitching quality manifests as discontinuities or unnatural transitions at the middle point. Inadequate forward/backward prediction shows as sequences that deviate significantly from realistic motion patterns or fail to reach appropriate intermediate positions.

First Experiments:

2. Evaluate stitching quality with synthetic sequences where ground truth middle point is known
3. Assess latent space interpolation between forward and backward codes to verify smooth transitions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the proposed method be extended to generate diverse and high-quality transitions for more complex motions, such as dancing and martial arts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that they mainly focus on locomotion and hope to investigate the performance of the proposed method on more complex motions, like dancing and martial arts, in future work.
- Why unresolved: The current method is evaluated on locomotion datasets (Human3.6m and LaFAN1) and its performance on more complex and expressive motions is not demonstrated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results on datasets containing dancing or martial arts motions, showing the method's ability to generate diverse and high-quality transitions for these complex motions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the proposed method be adapted to predict the optimal length of transitions in real-world applications where animators cannot directly judge the required transition length?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that in inference, the length of the transition is determined by the user, but animators cannot directly judge how long transitions are in real cases. They mention the need for the model to help predict transition lengths.
- Why unresolved: The current method requires the user to specify the transition length, which may not be practical in real-world applications where the optimal length is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: An extension of the method that can automatically determine the optimal transition length based on the input frames or motion context, along with experimental results demonstrating its effectiveness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the proposed method be improved to allow intuitive control over the generation process using high-level semantic information, such as motion styles or action types?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that they hope to explore the use of high-level semantic information (e.g., motion styles or action types) to control the generation in future work, which will better meet the animators' needs.
- Why unresolved: The current method relies on random sampling in the CVAE latent space, which does not allow for intuitive control over the generated motions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A modified version of the method that incorporates high-level semantic information to guide the generation process, along with qualitative and quantitative results showing improved control and alignment with desired motion styles or action types.

## Limitations
- Performance may degrade on highly dynamic or acrobatic motions not well-represented in training data
- Computational overhead of running two generators and stitching module compared to single-direction approaches
- Limited evaluation on complex motion types beyond basic locomotion (walking, running)

## Confidence

- Perfect target frame alignment: Medium
- Superior performance across all motion types: Low
