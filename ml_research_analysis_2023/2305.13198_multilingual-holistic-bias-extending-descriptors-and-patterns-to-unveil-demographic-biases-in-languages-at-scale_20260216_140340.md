# Multilingual Holistic Bias: Extending Descriptors and Patterns to Unveil Demographic Biases in Languages at Scale

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13198
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13198
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: This paper extends the HOLISTIC BIAS dataset to 50 languages (MULTILINGUAL HOLISTIC BIAS), covering 13 demographic axes. Machine translation evaluation reveals that translation quality for EN-to-XX is 8 spBLEU better when using masculine human references compared to feminine, and XX-to-EN is 4 spBLEU better when using masculine sources. ## Executive Summary
This paper presents MULTILINGUAL HOLISTIC BIAS, a dataset that extends the HOLISTIC BIOTAX English taxonomy to 50 languages across 13 demographic axes. The dataset consists of 20,459 sentences generated from 118 descriptors and three patterns, translated by human translators with gender alternatives for gendered languages. The authors evaluate machine translation quality using NLLB-200 and find significant bias favoring masculine translations, with 8 spBLEU difference when evaluating English-to-other-languages translations. They also analyze sentence embeddings using LASER and LaBSE encoders, finding that masculine translations are significantly closer to English neutral sentences for most languages, indicating systematic bias in multilingual embeddings.

## Method Summary
The authors generate English source sentences from templates and descriptors, then human translate to 50 languages with gender alternatives. They evaluate translation quality using the NLLB-200 model in both directions (EN-to-XX and XX-to-EN) and compute spBLEU scores comparing model output to human references. For semantic similarity analysis, they embed sentences using LASER and LaBSE encoders and compute cosine similarities between English neutral sentences and their gendered translations. The analysis covers 13 demographic axes and includes paired t-tests to detect significant differences in semantic distances.

## Key Results
- Translation quality for EN-to-XX is 8 spBLEU better when evaluating with masculine human references compared to feminine
- For most languages, masculine translations are significantly closer to English neutral sentences in embedding space than feminine translations
- Certain demographic axes (socioeconomic, cultural, body-type) show systematically lower translation quality than others (nationality, age, sexual orientation)

- Component map: NLLB-200 model (3B parameters) -> LASER/LaBSE encoders -> sacrebleu implementation -> HOLISTIC BIAS templates and descriptors -> Human translation pipeline

- Critical path: 1) Generate English source sentences from templates and descriptors 2) Human translate to 50 languages with gender alternatives 3) Translate using NLLB model in both directions 4) Compute spBLEU scores comparing model output to human references 5) Embed sentences and compute cosine similarities 6) Analyze results across demographic axes

- Design tradeoffs: Using templated sentences provides controlled evaluation but may lack natural language complexity; focusing on gender bias enables clear measurement but may miss intersectional biases; machine translation evaluation captures model behavior but may conflate bias with translation quality

- Failure signatures: High spBLEU variance between masculine and feminine references indicates gender bias; systematic distance differences in embedding space suggest representation bias; lower quality for certain demographic axes indicates data representation issues

- First 3 experiments: 1) Translate a small set of ambiguous English sentences to multiple languages and manually verify gender assignment patterns 2) Compute spBLEU scores for a single language pair using only masculine vs only feminine references to confirm bias magnitude 3) Embed a balanced set of sentences and visualize embedding distances to confirm clustering patterns

## Limitations
- Translation quality evaluation may conflate gender bias with general translation quality issues
- Templated sentences may not capture the full complexity of real-world translation scenarios
- Analysis focuses primarily on binary gender and may miss non-binary or intersectional biases
- Human translation process introduces potential variability not fully quantified

## Confidence
- High confidence: Translation quality differences between masculine and feminine references (8 spBLEU difference for EN-to-XX)
- Medium confidence: Claims about certain demographic axes showing systematically higher bias than others
- Low confidence: Generalization that these findings indicate fundamental bias in multilingual NLP models rather than model-specific artifacts
