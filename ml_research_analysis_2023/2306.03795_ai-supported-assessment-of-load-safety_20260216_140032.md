# AI-Supported Assessment of Load Safety

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03795
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03795
- Reference count: 35
- One-line primary result: AI system achieves >90% precision/recall for distinguishing usable vs unusable images in cargo safety assessment

## Executive Summary
This study demonstrates an AI-supported system for load safety assessment in logistics that can automatically classify images of truck cargo into safe, unsafe, and unusable categories. The system employs a two-stage approach using convolutional neural networks, first filtering out unusable images before assessing cargo safety. The research successfully shows that CNNs can achieve high precision and recall (above 90%) in distinguishing between usable and unusable images through data augmentation techniques applied to a real-world dataset of 5,712 images. However, the classification of cargo as safely or unsafely loaded proved more challenging, with lower performance metrics, indicating the need for further refinement in this area.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated three CNN architectures (ResNet101, InceptionV3, and LogisticNet) on a dataset of 5,712 real-world images from a logistics company. Data augmentation techniques including random flips, rotations, brightness, and color adjustments were applied to prevent overfitting. The models were trained for up to 300 epochs with early stopping based on validation accuracy. A two-stage classification approach was implemented, first distinguishing usable from unusable images, then classifying safe versus unsafe cargo loading. Performance was measured using precision, recall, and F1-score metrics for both binary and multi-class classification tasks.

## Key Results
- InceptionV3 achieved the best performance in distinguishing between usable and unusable images
- System achieved precision and recall above 90% for the usable/unusable classification task
- Safety classification (safe vs unsafe cargo) showed lower performance with F1-scores around 0.5-0.6
- Data augmentation successfully prevented overfitting on the limited dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Data augmentation prevents overfitting and improves model generalization on limited datasets.
- Mechanism: Applying random geometric and photometric transformations (flips, rotations, brightness and color adjustments) artificially expands the dataset, forcing the CNN to learn invariant features rather than memorizing training images.
- Core assumption: The augmented images remain semantically consistent with the original class labels.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Data augmentation aims to prevent possible overfitting and thus improve the training effect, whereby one reason for overfitting is a too-small dataset"
  - [section]: "For the implementation of data augmentation, photometric and geometric methods are used. By not altering the classes of the images, only the random flip on the y axes, slight random rotation, random brightness modification, and random color adjustments are used for data augmentation"
- Break condition: If transformations produce images that no longer represent realistic cargo photos, the model will learn incorrect features and degrade performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Two-stage classification (usable vs unusable images, then safety assessment) simplifies the learning task and improves overall accuracy.
- Mechanism: By first filtering out unusable images, the second-stage model focuses only on high-quality inputs, reducing noise and ambiguity in the training data for safety assessment.
- Core assumption: The first stage can reliably distinguish usable from unusable images with high precision and recall.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "By applying several architectures of convolutional neural networks (CNN), it can be shown that it is possible to distinguish between unusable and usable images for cargo safety assessment"
  - [section]: "This distinction is quite crucial since the truck driver and the loadmaster sometimes provide photos without the essential image features like the case structure of the truck and the whole cargo"
- Break condition: If the first stage misclassifies usable images as unusable (or vice versa), the downstream safety assessment will be compromised.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using multiple CNN architectures with varying complexity allows selection of the optimal model for the task.
- Mechanism: Different architectures (ResNet101, InceptionV3, LogisticNet) have varying receptive fields, depth, and parameter counts, enabling evaluation of which best captures relevant features for the specific dataset.
- Core assumption: The chosen architectures are appropriate for the input image resolution and domain.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Three CNN architectures are used for the experiment, which can be assigned to two categories... two deep ANN were used for the InceptionV3 and the ResNet101. On the other hand, one shallow architecture, called LogisticNet based on the AlexNet architecture"
  - [section]: "InceptionV3 achieved the best performance at distinguishing between usable and unusable images"
- Break condition: If the dataset domain is significantly different from the pretraining domains of these architectures, their performance may not generalize well.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input preprocessing (resize, augmentation) → CNN backbone (ResNet101/InceptionV3/LogisticNet) → Classification head (softmax for 2 or 3 classes) → Loss function (categorical crossentropy) → Optimizer (Adam/SGD)
- Critical path: Data preprocessing → Model training (with early stopping to prevent overfitting) → Evaluation on validation set → Deployment for inference
- Design tradeoffs: Higher resolution inputs improve accuracy but increase computational cost; deeper architectures may overfit on small datasets; simple architectures may lack capacity for complex feature extraction
- Failure signatures: Low validation accuracy with high training accuracy indicates overfitting; confusion between classes I and II suggests ambiguous training examples; poor performance on unusable image detection indicates augmentation issues

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the distinction between "cargo loaded safely" and "cargo loaded unsafely" be improved by refining the dataset to remove boundary cases where human evaluators disagree?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "manual inspection of the results" revealed "an intersection between the classes I) cargo loaded safely and II) cargo loaded unsafely" due to "some boundary cases, where one cloud assesses the cargo shown on the image as both safe or unsafe loaded cargo"
- Why unresolved: The current dataset contains ambiguous cases that confuse the CNN architectures, leading to poor classification performance for this task
- What evidence would resolve it: A cleaned dataset with clear boundaries between safe and unsafe cargo classes, followed by retraining and evaluation showing improved precision and recall for distinguishing between these two classes

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would a hybrid dataset combining computer-generated and real-world images improve the CNN's ability to classify cargo as safely or unsafely loaded?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose "a hybrid dataset, a mixture of computer-generated and real-world images" as a future approach to "increase the number of training images"
- Why unresolved: The current real-world dataset may be too limited or contain insufficient variety of loading scenarios to train robust classifiers for safety assessment
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating CNN architectures on a hybrid dataset showing improved classification performance for the safety assessment task compared to the current real-world dataset alone

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does input image resolution affect the CNN's performance in distinguishing between usable and unusable images for cargo safety assessment?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tested different resolutions (244x244px, 299x299px, 800x800px) for ResNet101 and InceptionV3, noting that "the resolution can significantly impact the model's performance" and "a higher resolution can also lead to better classification accuracy"
- Why unresolved: While higher resolutions showed improved performance, the optimal resolution balancing computational efficiency and accuracy was not determined
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation across a wider range of resolutions showing the point of diminishing returns where additional resolution does not improve classification accuracy

## Limitations

- Performance for safety classification (safe vs unsafe cargo) was significantly lower than for usable/unusable image detection
- Dataset size of 5,712 images may still be limited for training complex deep learning models
- Specific data augmentation parameters and training hyperparameters were not fully specified

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The system's ability to distinguish between usable and unusable images with precision and recall above 90%
- **Medium confidence**: The overall framework of using a two-stage classification approach for load safety assessment
- **Low confidence**: The precision and recall metrics for the safety classification stage (cargo safely vs unsafely loaded)
