# Logic Against Bias: Textual Entailment Mitigates Stereotypical Sentence Reasoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05670
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05670
- Reference count: 12
- Textual entailment models outperform similarity-based models on fairness metrics without explicit de-biasing

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that textual entailment models significantly reduce stereotypical reasoning in sentence representations compared to similarity-based approaches. Through systematic evaluation of BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, SimCSE, and entailment models on bias tests involving gender, profession, emotion, and social group stereotypes, the authors show that entailment models consistently achieve lower bias scores. The key insight is that entailment models learn logical relationships rather than semantic similarity, breaking the co-occurrence patterns that encode stereotypes in pretraining data. Notably, the discrete scoring strategy for entailment models shows particular promise for fairness, outperforming continuous scoring methods across multiple benchmarks.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates five sentence representation models: BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, SimCSE (similarity-based), and textual entailment models. All models are fine-tuned on NLI datasets (SNLI and MNLI) to classify premise-hypothesis pairs as entailment, neutral, or contradiction. The entailment models use two scoring strategies: continuous scores extracted from the entailment probability, and discrete scores derived from discrete label predictions. Models are evaluated on four bias tests: Winogender for gender stereotypes, Social Bias Inference Test for social group bias, Emotions Implicit Association Test for emotion stereotypes, and CrowS-Pairs for nine types of social biases. The iCAT score measures the bias between two groups by comparing the semantic similarities between sentence pairs.

## Key Results
- Entailment models achieve significantly lower bias scores than all similarity-based baselines across all four bias tests
- Discrete scoring strategy consistently outperforms continuous scoring in reducing bias, particularly for gender and profession stereotypes
- Textual entailment models maintain strong performance on sentence representation tasks while reducing bias, unlike explicit de-biasing methods that often sacrifice performance
- Analysis reveals that entailment models produce embeddings that prioritize logic over word similarity, avoiding stereotypical associations present in pretraining corpora

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Textual entailment models reduce stereotypical reasoning by focusing on logical relationships rather than semantic similarity.
- Mechanism: Instead of encoding sentences as similar vectors when they co-occur frequently in training data, entailment models explicitly classify the logical relationship (entail, neutral, contradiction) between premise and hypothesis pairs.
- Core assumption: Social stereotypes are primarily learned through co-occurrence patterns in training data rather than through explicit logical relationships.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "textual entailment models perform well on sentence representation tasks, and are significantly less biased than similarity-based sentence encoders, without incorporating any explicit de-biasing"
  - [section 1]: "textual entailment stands for the case where the premise is true so that the hypothesis is likely to be true. Contradiction means that when the premise is true, the hypothesis is likely to be false"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - while the corpus shows related work on entailment and bias, it doesn't directly support the specific mechanism claim about how entailment breaks similarity-based stereotype learning
- Break condition: If stereotypes are encoded in the training data through logical patterns rather than just co-occurrence, or if the entailment training data itself contains stereotypical associations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Discrete scoring strategy prevents stereotypical reasoning by forcing binary decisions that avoid gradual similarity-based bias accumulation.
- Mechanism: By converting continuous entailment scores into discrete labels (entail=0, neutral=1, contradiction=2), the model avoids subtle similarity-based preferences that accumulate toward stereotypical associations.
- Core assumption: Continuous similarity scores allow gradual bias accumulation while discrete decisions create clearer logical boundaries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "the discrete scoring models are generally better than the continuous scoring method. Although the entailment score of option a is higher than option b, both options can be both classified into the neutral category"
  - [section 5.2]: Analysis showing discrete entailment embeddings appear more random compared to SimCSE embeddings that reflect human intuition
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence for this specific discrete vs continuous scoring mechanism
- Break condition: If the discrete boundary placement itself becomes biased, or if continuous scoring could be modified to avoid bias accumulation

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Entailment models learn logic without preserving textual similarities, leading to fairer performance by removing co-occurrence-based stereotype learning.
- Mechanism: By optimizing for logical relationship classification rather than similarity maximization, entailment models break the connection between frequent co-occurrence and semantic similarity, thereby avoiding stereotype internalization.
- Core assumption: Social stereotypes are encoded in language models primarily through co-occurrence-based similarity learning rather than through logical relationships.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2]: "the prompt embeddings learned by the entailment models contribute to logical reasoning rather than word coherence representation" and "the biases are caused by the similarity-based learning objectives"
  - [abstract]: "the explicit logic learning with textual entailment can significantly reduce bias and improve the recognition of social communities, without an explicit de-biasing process"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related papers discuss entailment and bias but don't directly support the specific claim about how breaking similarity learning reduces bias
- Break condition: If logical relationships themselves contain stereotypical patterns, or if similarity-based learning could be modified to avoid stereotype internalization

  - Why needed here: The paper's core argument is that stereotypes are learned through co-occurrence patterns, so understanding this mechanism is essential

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input sentences -> Transformer encoding -> Logical relationship classification -> Scoring strategy application -> Bias evaluation
- Critical path: Input → Transformer encoding → Logical relationship classification → Scoring strategy application → Bias evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Continuous vs discrete scoring: Continuous provides more nuanced information but allows bias accumulation; discrete is more robust to bias but loses granularity
  - Model size vs fairness: Larger models show more bias in similarity-based approaches but entailment models maintain fairness regardless of size
  - Training data composition: Entailment models require NLI-style training data which may be limited compared to general text corpora
- Failure signatures:
  - If discrete scoring shows high variance across similar examples, the model may be making arbitrary decisions
  - If fairness metrics remain low despite entailment training, the training data itself may contain logical stereotypes
  - If performance degrades significantly on non-bias tasks, the entailment focus may be too narrow
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare bias metrics between continuous and discrete scoring on the same entailment model to verify the paper's finding
  2. Test the model on controlled examples where logical relationships are clear but semantic similarity is high to ensure it's learning the intended distinction
  3. Evaluate the model on NLI-style tasks to confirm language understanding ability is preserved while bias is reduced

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do textual entailment models perform on bias reduction when evaluated on non-binary gender terms, given that current benchmarks predominantly focus on binary gender?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper acknowledges that existing gender-related bias studies and corpora mainly focus on binary gender settings due to data limitations, and states they will extend this work to non-binary settings in future work.
- Why unresolved: The authors explicitly note they studied stereotypes including gender biases but only investigated under the binary gender setting because of the limitation of existing benchmarks. They mention this as a limitation and future work direction.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the same entailment models on datasets that include non-binary gender terms and comparing the bias reduction performance against both binary-focused models and explicitly debiased models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of model scale on the effectiveness of textual entailment models for bias reduction, given that the paper only evaluated medium-sized models (~350M parameters)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state they evaluated medium-sized language models with around 350M parameters but have not tested the largest language models yet, noting this as a limitation.
- Why unresolved: The paper only analyzed the predictive bias on medium-sized models and explicitly states they have not tested the largest language models, leaving open whether the bias reduction benefits scale with model size.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating textual entailment models of varying scales (including the largest available models) on the same bias benchmarks to determine if the bias reduction benefits remain consistent, improve, or diminish with scale.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the source of bias in pretraining corpora relate to the effectiveness of textual entailment debiasing, and can we identify specific patterns in pretraining data that correlate with persistent bias?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors analyze that biases are caused by similarity-based learning objectives because such algorithms learn and reflect biases in training corpora, and textual entailment models learn logic without preserving textual similarities, leading to fairer performance.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that textual entailment reduces bias by avoiding similarity-based learning, it does not examine the specific sources or patterns of bias in pretraining corpora that cause the most persistent issues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a detailed analysis of pretraining corpora to identify specific patterns, domains, or linguistic constructions that correlate most strongly with bias persistence, and testing whether textual entailment models are particularly effective against these specific sources.

## Limitations
- Only evaluated medium-sized models (~350M parameters), leaving open whether bias reduction benefits scale with model size
- Focused on binary gender settings due to limitations of existing benchmarks, not addressing non-binary gender biases
- Limited to English language tasks and specific stereotype categories, restricting generalizability to other languages and bias types

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Entailment models outperform similarity-based baselines on fairness metrics across all tested categories. The discrete scoring strategy generally shows lower bias than continuous scoring, with consistent results across different model architectures.

**Medium Confidence**: The mechanism explanation that entailment breaks co-occurrence-based stereotype learning is plausible but not definitively proven. Alternative explanations (such as entailment training requiring different optimization dynamics) could also explain the results.

**Low Confidence**: Claims about the discrete scoring strategy's specific advantage over continuous scoring are based on correlation rather than causal evidence. The paper shows discrete scoring performs better but doesn't conclusively prove why.
