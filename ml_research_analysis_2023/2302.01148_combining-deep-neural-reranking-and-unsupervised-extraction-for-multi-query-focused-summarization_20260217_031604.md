# Combining Deep Neural Reranking and Unsupervised Extraction for Multi-Query Focused Summarization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2302.01148
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01148
- **Reference count:** 17
- **Primary result:** Combining BM25 retrieval with neural reranking and ILP+MMR summarization effectively extracts crisis facts across multiple data streams

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the challenge of extracting and summarizing important facts from disaster-related events across multiple data streams (Twitter, Reddit, Web News, Facebook) while incorporating temporal order. The proposed framework combines retrieval, deep neural reranking, and extractive summarization using Integer Linear Programming (ILP) and Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR). For retrieval, BM25 with query expansion is used. Reranking employs various models including a Bag-of-Entities baseline, pre-trained and fine-tuned QA systems (using BERT-based cross-encoders), and ColBERT. The summarization stage uses ILP for selection and MMR for scoring to ensure diversity and novelty. Automatic evaluation shows strong results, with QA models outperforming the baseline and ColBERT across most metrics. However, comprehensiveness tends to decrease over time, suggesting challenges in maintaining diversity across the event timeline. The approach demonstrates the effectiveness of combining deep neural reranking with unsupervised extraction for multi-query focused summarization.

## Method Summary
The approach uses a three-stage pipeline: (1) BM25 retrieval with query expansion to efficiently retrieve candidate documents, (2) deep neural reranking using Bag-of-Entities, pre-trained/fine-tuned QA models, and ColBERT to refine the candidate pool, and (3) ILP-based selection for diversity optimization combined with MMR scoring for redundancy reduction. The method was evaluated on the CrisisFACTS dataset across Twitter, Reddit, and Web News data streams, with Facebook excluded due to limited access. Synthetic training data was created for fine-tuning QA models on crisis-specific queries.

## Key Results
- QA models outperform Bag-of-Entities baseline and ColBERT across most metrics
- Rouge-2 and BERTScore show strong summarization performance
- Comprehensiveness decreases over time, indicating temporal diversity challenges
- ILP+MMR combination effectively reduces redundancy while maintaining diversity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The combination of lexical retrieval (BM25) with deep neural reranking improves fact extraction from disaster-related events.
- Mechanism: BM25 efficiently retrieves a large pool of candidate documents using lexical matching, which are then refined by deep neural models (QA systems and ColBERT) that capture semantic relationships between queries and documents.
- Core assumption: Initial lexical retrieval provides sufficient recall for downstream neural reranking to identify relevant facts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose a combination of retrieval, reranking, and the well-known Integer Linear Programming (ILP) and Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR) frameworks."
  - [section 2.1] "In the first stage, we employ lexical retrieval approaches to mitigate the infeasible computational costs of deep neural models such as BERT-based cross-encoders."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning QA models on crisis-specific data improves performance over pre-trained models.
- Mechanism: Pre-trained QA models are adapted to the crisis domain using synthetic training data created from the DocEE dataset, allowing them to better understand crisis-related queries and extract relevant facts.
- Core assumption: The synthetic crisis-QA dataset captures the semantic patterns needed for the task despite potential data overlap concerns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "Since the first CrisisFACTS Track does not provide any annotations... we decided to create a synthetic version that reflects the query-focused sentence selection."
  - [section 3.4] "The fine-tuned QA model decreases the performance in two summarization setups and in terms of comprehensiveness. We assume that the adapted QA is biased towards the entities of the Crisis-QA dataset."
  - [corpus] Weak - no neighboring papers directly validate domain adaptation for QA in crisis domains.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ILP and MMR combination ensures diversity and reduces redundancy in the final summary.
- Mechanism: ILP selects a diverse set of facts based on concept coverage while MMR re-ranks the summary to penalize redundancy against both the selected summary and past summaries.
- Core assumption: Entity-based concept representation and TF-IDF similarity adequately capture redundancy and diversity needs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3] "To tackle this problem, we use an additional selection step formalized as ILP... we use an additional selection step formalized as ILP."
  - [section 3.4] "However, this constraint can be relaxed to rerank a summary St in order to increase the diversity in the top documents."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: BM25 retrieval with query expansion → Rerank (BoE, QA ASNQ, QA Crisis, ColBERT) → Summarize (ILP selection + MMR scoring)
- Critical path: The retrieval → reranking → summarization pipeline is sequential and each stage depends on the previous one's output
- Design tradeoffs: Using BM25 first trades some precision for computational feasibility; using multiple reranking models provides comparison but increases complexity
- Failure signatures: Low comprehensiveness over time suggests retrieval/ranking stages aren't maintaining diversity; high redundancy indicates MMR/ILP parameters need adjustment
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test baseline performance using only BM25 without reranking to establish retrieval-only baseline
  2. Compare different reranking models (BoE vs QA vs ColBERT) on the same retrieval pool to isolate reranking impact
  3. Evaluate ILP vs simple top-k selection on reranked output to measure diversity improvement

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed approach perform on Facebook data, given that it was excluded from the experiments due to limited access to post contents?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors explicitly state they ignored Facebook data "due to the limited access to the post contents."
- Why unresolved: The Facebook dataset was not evaluated, leaving a gap in understanding how the approach would perform on this data source.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments using Facebook data to measure the performance of the proposed approach across all four data streams (Twitter, Reddit, Web News, and Facebook).

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the temporal diversity challenges on the overall system performance, and how can they be mitigated?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that "comprehensiveness tends to decrease over time" and discusses challenges related to "diversity for each query" and "unsophisticated similarity measures" in the summarization stage.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies temporal diversity challenges, it does not provide a detailed analysis of their impact on overall system performance or specific mitigation strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing and evaluating advanced diversity mechanisms in the retrieval and summarization stages, along with a detailed analysis of their impact on temporal comprehensiveness trends.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of the fine-tuned QA model compare to the pre-trained QA model across different events and reference summaries, and what are the underlying reasons for any performance differences?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that "the fine-tuned QA model decreases the performance in two summarization setups and in terms of comprehensiveness" and hypothesize that "the adapted QA is biased towards the entities of the Crisis-QA dataset."
- Why unresolved: While the authors provide a hypothesis for the performance difference, they do not conduct a detailed comparative analysis across different events and reference summaries to validate their hypothesis.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a detailed comparative analysis of the pre-trained and fine-tuned QA models across different events and reference summaries, including an investigation of the types of queries and facts that are affected by the performance differences.

## Limitations
- The synthetic training data for fine-tuning QA models may introduce bias, as evidenced by decreased performance
- Facebook data was excluded from experiments due to limited access to post contents
- Evaluation relies on manual fact lists that aren't publicly available, limiting reproducibility
- Entity-based concept representation in ILP may be too coarse for certain fact types

## Confidence
- High confidence: The overall pipeline architecture (retrieval → reranking → summarization) is sound and well-established in the literature
- Medium confidence: The specific combination of BM25 with neural rerankers improves performance over BM25 alone, though the magnitude varies by model
- Low confidence: The synthetic domain adaptation approach effectively improves crisis-specific performance without introducing harmful bias

## Next Checks
1. Validate the recall quality of the initial BM25 retrieval stage by measuring coverage of relevant documents before reranking, ensuring sufficient candidates for neural models to work with
2. Conduct ablation studies comparing the three reranking approaches (BoE, QA models, ColBERT) on the same retrieval pool to isolate their individual contributions to the final summary quality
3. Test alternative similarity measures for the ILP stage beyond simple entity matching to capture more nuanced concept relationships and prevent false redundancy penalties
