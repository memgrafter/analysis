# LIQUID: A Framework for List Question Answering Dataset Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01691
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01691
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Improves exact-match F1 scores by 5.0 on MultiSpanQA, 1.9 on Quoref, and 2.8 averaged across three BioASQ benchmarks

## Executive Summary
LIQUID is an automated framework that generates list question answering datasets from unlabeled corpora by combining summarization, named entity recognition, question generation, and iterative filtering/expansion. The framework addresses the challenge of creating list questions where answers are semantically correlated spans rather than contiguous text. By extracting named entities from summarized passages and using a QA model for iterative refinement, LIQUID produces synthetic data that significantly improves the performance of list QA models on multiple benchmarks.

## Method Summary
LIQUID processes unlabeled passages through a pipeline that first summarizes the text, extracts named entities as candidate answers, generates questions using a question generation model, and then applies iterative filtering and answer expansion using a QA model to ensure answer accuracy and completeness. The framework operates on both general domain (Wikipedia) and biomedical (PubMed) corpora, using different NER models for each domain. The iterative process continues until the answer set stabilizes, resulting in synthetic list QA datasets that can be used to train or augment existing QA models.

## Key Results
- Improves exact-match F1 scores by 5.0 on MultiSpanQA benchmark
- Improves exact-match F1 scores by 1.9 on Quoref benchmark
- Improves exact-match F1 scores by 2.8 averaged across three BioASQ benchmarks (7b, 8b, 9b)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Summarization-based answer extraction improves list QA performance by ensuring semantic correlation among candidate answers
- Mechanism: Converting passages to summaries and extracting named entities from these summaries ensures candidate answers are semantically correlated because entities within the same summary are likely to be related to a common topic
- Core assumption: Entities appearing in the same summary are more likely to be semantically related than entities scattered across the entire passage
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We first convert a passage from Wikipedia or PubMed into a summary and extract named entities from the summarized text as candidate answers. This allows us to select answers that are semantically correlated in context..."
  - [section 2.1] "We use summarized text (i.e., ¯c) because a summary is a short snippet that conveys the relevant topics/facts in one or multiple documents; therefore, similar phrases (e.g., named entities of the same type) in the same summary are likely to be semantically related to one another..."
- Break condition: If the summarization model fails to capture core topics/facts or if the NER model incorrectly identifies entities, semantic correlation assumption breaks down

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Iterative filtering with a QA model ensures answer accuracy by eliminating incorrect answers
- Mechanism: After initial question generation, a QA model evaluates confidence scores of candidate answers, filtering out those below a threshold, and regenerating questions based on refined answer sets
- Core assumption: A QA model trained on SQuAD can effectively assess correctness of candidate answers in the context of generated questions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Finally, iterative filtering and answer expansion are performed to ensure the accuracy and completeness of the answers."
  - [section 2.3] "Answers with confidence scores lower than the threshold τ are regarded as incorrect and filtered out to yield a refined answer set..."
- Break condition: If QA model is not well-calibrated or threshold is improperly set, filtering may remove correct answers or retain incorrect ones

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Answer expansion with a QA model ensures answer completeness by identifying additional correct answers
- Mechanism: After iterative filtering, the QA model identifies additional answer spans with confidence scores higher than the lowest score in the filtered set, adding them to the answer set
- Core assumption: The QA model can identify correct but omitted answers not initially extracted by the NER model
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Finally, iterative filtering and answer expansion are performed to ensure the accuracy and completeness of the answers."
  - [section 2.4] "We address this issue by identifying additional answer spans with confidence scores higher than the lowest confidence in the filtered set A′ by using the same QA model as that used for filtering."
- Break condition: If QA model's confidence scores are unreliable or expansion threshold is inappropriate, expansion may add incorrect answers or miss correct ones

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Unlabeled corpus -> Summarization (BART) -> NER (spaCy/BERN2) -> QG (T5) -> QA (RoBERTa/BioBERT) -> Synthetic dataset
- Critical path: Summarization → NER → QG → QA (iterative filtering and expansion)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using summaries vs. full passages: Summaries ensure semantic correlation but may miss some correct answers
  - Iterative filtering vs. single-pass filtering: Iterative filtering improves accuracy but increases computational cost
  - Answer expansion vs. no expansion: Expansion improves completeness but may introduce noise
- Failure signatures:
  - Low F1 scores on benchmark datasets: Indicates issues with any components (summarization, NER, QG, or QA)
  - High proportion of incorrect answers: Suggests problems with iterative filtering or answer expansion
  - Skewed distribution of answer spans: Indicates issues with answer extraction or expansion
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate impact of different summarization models on quality of candidate answers
  2. Compare performance of iterative filtering with different threshold values
  3. Assess effectiveness of answer expansion in improving completeness of candidate answers

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal data size for LIQUID-generated synthetic data across different domains and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that performance tends to initially increase and then decrease as data size increases, indicating the existence of an optimal data size
- Why unresolved: The paper only analyzed the effect of data size on MultiSpanQA and BioASQ 9b datasets, not providing a comprehensive study across all benchmark datasets
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments analyzing the effect of data size on all benchmark datasets (MultiSpanQA, Quoref, BioASQ 7b, 8b, and 9b) would provide a more complete understanding of optimal data size for LIQUID-generated synthetic data

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the answer expansion method in LIQUID be improved to reduce the number of incorrect answers added by the model?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that 4% of errors in generated data can be attributed to incorrect answers added by the answer expansion model
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific methods or techniques to improve the answer expansion method and reduce incorrect answers
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating new techniques or methods to improve the answer expansion process in LIQUID would provide insights into reducing incorrect answers added by the model

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the QG model in LIQUID be improved to generate more diverse and complex questions that require inter-sentence reasoning or domain knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the QG model sometimes generates questions that require domain knowledge, but at a much lower rate than data annotated by human experts. Additionally, there are fewer questions with inter-sentence reasoning in the biomedical domain compared to the general domain
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific methods or techniques to improve the QG model and generate more diverse and complex questions
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating new techniques or methods to improve the QG model in LIQUID would provide insights into generating more diverse and complex questions requiring inter-sentence reasoning or domain knowledge

## Limitations
- The framework's performance heavily depends on the quality of summarization and named entity recognition, with specific model configurations and performance metrics not fully specified
- Iterative filtering and answer expansion processes depend on threshold values requiring manual tuning, which could affect reproducibility
- While significant F1 score improvements are reported, ablation studies showing the individual contribution of each LIQUID component are limited

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the general framework architecture and its core mechanisms (summarization-based answer extraction, iterative filtering, answer expansion)
- **Medium confidence** in the specific F1 score improvements reported, given the lack of detailed ablation studies
- **Low confidence** in the reproducibility of results without knowing the exact threshold values and model configurations
