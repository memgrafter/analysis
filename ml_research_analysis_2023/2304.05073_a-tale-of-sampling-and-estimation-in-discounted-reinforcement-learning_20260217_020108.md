# A Tale of Sampling and Estimation in Discounted Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05073
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05073
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: The all-samples estimator achieves near-minimax optimality for estimating discounted means without requiring careful horizon tuning

## Executive Summary
This paper studies the problem of estimating the mean of a function under the γ-discounted stationary distribution of a Markov chain. The authors provide a minimax lower bound showing that the error scales as Ω(1/√(N(1-βγ))) where N is the number of samples and 1-β is the absolute spectral gap of the chain. They analyze several practical estimators including finite-horizon and adaptive-horizon approaches. The all-samples estimator, which uses all collected samples without truncation, is shown to nearly match the lower bound without requiring careful tuning of the episode horizon, making it the most statistically efficient method among the considered alternatives. Empirical results support these theoretical findings.

## Method Summary
The paper considers estimating the mean of a function under the γ-discounted stationary distribution of a Markov chain. Four estimation algorithms are compared: Fixed-Horizon Non-corrected (FHN), Fixed-Horizon Corrected (FHC), One-Sample (OS), and All-Samples (AS). The methods use a reset policy to generate trajectories and apply different estimators to the collected samples. The analysis provides theoretical guarantees on bias and concentration for each estimator, with particular focus on the all-samples estimator's ability to achieve near-minimax optimality.

## Key Results
- The all-samples estimator nearly matches the minimax lower bound without requiring careful tuning of the episode horizon
- Finite-horizon estimators suffer from bias that doesn't vanish as N→∞ if T is fixed
- The one-sample estimator has large variance due to inefficient use of samples

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The all-samples estimator achieves near-minimax optimality without requiring careful tuning of the episode horizon.
- Mechanism: By using all collected samples without truncation, the estimator leverages the mixing properties of the Markov chain to control statistical dependence within trajectories, while maintaining a large effective sample size.
- Core assumption: The mixing properties of the Markov chain (captured by the spectral gap 1-β) are sufficient to control the dependence between samples within a trajectory.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The all-samples estimator, which uses all collected samples without truncation, is shown to nearly match the lower bound without requiring careful tuning of the episode horizon"
  - [section]: "the AS estimator nearly matches the minimax lower bound of Theorem 4.1"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct citation to all-samples estimator
- Break condition: If the Markov chain has very poor mixing properties (β ≈ 1), the estimator may suffer from increased variance due to strong dependence between samples.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Finite-horizon estimators suffer from bias that does not vanish as N→∞ if T is fixed.
- Mechanism: Truncating trajectories at a fixed horizon T introduces a systematic underestimation of the true discounted mean, as the contribution of future rewards beyond T is ignored.
- Core assumption: The true discounted mean πγf includes contributions from all time steps, not just the first T steps.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "As they truncate each trajectory after T transitions, the FH estimators are affected by a bias, vanishing for large T"
  - [section]: "both estimators are asymptotically unbiased as the horizon T → `8. However, none of them is asymptotically unbiased as the budget N → `8 (provided that T does not depend on N)"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct citation to finite-horizon bias
- Break condition: If T is allowed to grow with N (specifically T ~ log N / log(1/γ)), the bias can be made negligible relative to the estimation error.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The one-sample estimator has large variance due to inefficient use of samples.
- Mechanism: By retaining only one sample per trajectory (the one right before reset), the estimator discards most of the collected information, leading to a smaller effective sample size and higher variance.
- Core assumption: Retaining more samples per trajectory would reduce variance, assuming the dependence between samples can be controlled.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "This estimator has been used in (Thomas, 2014; Metelli et al., 2021), mostly for theoretical reasons, being unbiased"
  - [section]: "the unbiased OS estimator fails to quickly converge to the true mean with high discounting"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct citation to one-sample estimator
- Break condition: If the Markov chain has extremely good mixing properties (β ≈ 0), the variance of the one-sample estimator may become acceptable, though still suboptimal compared to the all-samples estimator.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the FH estimators be made minimax optimal through a better choice of the horizon T that doesn't require knowledge of the spectral gap 1-β?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show that the FH estimators do not match the minimax lower bound. They mention that one could select a value of T depending on the spectral gap 1-β to tighten the confidence bounds, but note that β is usually unknown in practice. They derive that the optimal β-independent choice of T is T*γ = O(log N / log(1/γ)), leading to the rate rO(1/√(N(1-γ))) for the FHN estimator, which is suboptimal when β = 0.
- Why unresolved: The authors are unable to generalize the concentration rate for the FHC estimator for values of β in (0,1), and the optimal choice of T for the FHN estimator does not make it minimax optimal when β = 0.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof showing that the FHC estimator is minimax optimal for all values of β in [0,1] with an appropriate choice of T, or a lower bound showing that no FH estimator can be minimax optimal without knowledge of the spectral gap.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the AS estimator be extended to handle functions that depend on the state history rather than just the current state?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on estimating the mean of a function under the γ-discounted stationary distribution of a Markov chain. The AS estimator uses all collected samples, which introduces some bias due to the dependence between samples within a trajectory. However, this dependence is controlled by the mixing properties of the MC.
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers functions that depend on the current state, and does not address the case of functions that depend on the state history. The concentration inequality for the AS estimator relies on the mixing properties of the MC, which may not hold for functions that depend on the state history.
- What evidence would resolve it: A concentration inequality for the AS estimator that holds for functions depending on the state history, or a counterexample showing that the AS estimator is not consistent for such functions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the OS estimator be modified to achieve a concentration rate that depends on the spectral gap 1-β?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The OS estimator retains only one sample per trajectory, which leads to a concentration rate of rO(1/√(N(1-γ))) that does not depend on the spectral gap. The authors note that this estimator is not minimax optimal.
- Why unresolved: The OS estimator's concentration rate does not depend on the spectral gap because it only uses one sample per trajectory, ignoring the mixing properties of the MC. Modifying the estimator to use more samples while maintaining the independence between retained samples is not straightforward.
- What evidence would resolve it: A modified OS estimator that retains multiple samples per trajectory while maintaining independence between retained samples, along with a concentration inequality showing that the modified estimator's rate depends on the spectral gap.

## Limitations

- Theoretical guarantees for the all-samples estimator rely on assumptions about mixing properties that may not hold for chains with very poor mixing (β close to 1)
- Empirical validation is limited to a simple synthetic Markov chain, with unclear performance on complex real-world MDPs
- The paper focuses on four specific estimators and may not consider other practical alternatives
