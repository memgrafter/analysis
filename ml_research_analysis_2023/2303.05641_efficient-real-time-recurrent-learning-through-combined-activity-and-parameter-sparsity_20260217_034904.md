# Efficient Real Time Recurrent Learning through combined activity and parameter sparsity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05641
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05641
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: RTRL computational and memory requirements can be reduced through combined activity and parameter sparsity without approximations

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates that the computational and memory requirements of Real-Time Recurrent Learning (RTRL) can be significantly reduced by combining activity sparsity (from using Heaviside step activation) with parameter sparsity. Unlike previous approaches that approximate RTRL updates, this method achieves efficiency improvements without compromising the exactness of the learning rule. The approach is particularly effective on hardware supporting unstructured sparsity and opens possibilities for event-based training of recurrent networks on neuromorphic devices.

## Method Summary
The method implements RTRL with an Event-based GRU (EGRU) using Heaviside step activation with surrogate gradient. The influence matrix M(t) is maintained recursively with updates that exploit zeros introduced by activity sparsity (rows of J(t) and M(t) become zero when units are inactive) and parameter sparsity (columns of M̄(t) remain zero when parameters are zero). The combined sparsity reduces computational complexity from O(n²p) to O(β̃²ω²n²p), where β̃ is the activity sparsity level and ω is the parameter sparsity level. Training uses Adam optimizer on a synthetic spiral classification task.

## Key Results
- Activity sparsity from Heaviside activation zeros entire rows in the influence matrix M(t), reducing computation
- Parameter sparsity compounds with activity sparsity to further reduce the computational footprint
- The combined approach achieves significant computational savings without approximating RTRL updates
- On devices with unstructured sparsity support, this enables practical event-based training of RNNs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RTRL updates can be sparsified when network units exhibit activity sparsity.
- Mechanism: In a network with Heaviside step activation, the derivative H' is zero for units whose state lies beyond a threshold ±ϵ. This causes entire rows in the Jacobian J(t) and immediate influence matrix M̄(t) to be zero, which in turn zeros out entire rows in the influence matrix M(t). This row-wise zeroing reduces the matrix multiplications from O(n²p) to O(β̃n²p), where β̃ is the fraction of non-zero rows.
- Core assumption: The Heaviside step function with surrogate gradient is used, and the threshold ϵ is small enough that many units have states outside the active region.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "recurrent networks exhibiting high activity sparsity can reduce the computational cost of RTRL"
  - [section] "each recursive update of the influence matrix can be written as M(t) = J(t)M(t-1) + M̄(t)" and "This implies that at each timestept,β(t)×n rows of the matrixM(t) are fully 0."
- Break condition: If the surrogate gradient width ϵ is too large or the network dynamics fail to induce sparsity, then β̃ → 1 and savings disappear.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Parameter sparsity compounds with activity sparsity to further reduce computation.
- Mechanism: Fixed parameter sparsity sets entire columns in M̄(t) to zero. Since those columns remain zero across timesteps, the influence matrix M(t) also has zero columns. Combined with activity sparsity zeroing rows, the cost becomes O(β̃²ω²n²p), where ω is the parameter non-zero fraction.
- Core assumption: The sparsity pattern is fixed at initialization and does not change during training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "combining activity and parameter sparsity can lead to significant enough savings in computational and memory costs to make RTRL practical"
  - [section] "With a parameter sparsity of ω = 1− ω̃, and activity sparsity, the compute requirement for the influence matrix updates... is O(β̃(t)β̃(t-1)ω̃²n²p)."
- Break condition: If the sparsity pattern is allowed to change during training (e.g., dynamic pruning), the compounding benefit may degrade unless recomputed.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The computational savings are achievable without approximating RTRL.
- Mechanism: The exact recursive RTRL update equations are used, but zeros in J(t) and M̄(t) due to sparsity mean that multiplications with zero entries are skipped, reducing actual compute without changing the update formula.
- Core assumption: The hardware or implementation supports skipping zero multiplications or storing sparse matrices efficiently.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Unlike previous work, this improvement in the efficiency of RTRL can be achieved without using any approximations for the learning process"
  - [section] "This is achieved without the need for approximating the RTRL updates."
- Break condition: If the underlying hardware cannot exploit unstructured sparsity (e.g., dense matrix kernels are used), the theoretical savings are not realized.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - EGRU/EvNN layer: state vector a(t) ∈ ℝⁿ, thresholded by H(v(t))
  - Surrogate gradient module: computes H’(v) for each unit
  - RTRL engine: maintains influence matrix M(t) ∈ ℝⁿˣᵖ, updates via J(t)M(t-1) + M̄(t)
  - Sparsity masks: fixed parameter mask (ω) and dynamic activity mask (β̃)
  - Training loop: Adam optimizer with sparse gradients

- Critical path:
  1. Forward pass: compute v(t) = F(a(t-1), x(t)) - θ, apply H(v(t)) for sparse a(t)
  2. Compute J(t) = ∂a(t)/∂a(t-1) using surrogate gradient
  3. Compute M̄(t) = ∂a(t)/∂w(t) using surrogate gradient
  4. Update M(t) = J(t)M(t-1) + M̄(t) with sparse matrix multiplication
  5. Compute gradient ∂L/∂w via M(t)ᵀc̄(t) and apply Adam step

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed vs. dynamic parameter sparsity: fixed is simpler but may miss later pruning opportunities
  - Surrogate gradient width ϵ: too wide reduces sparsity; too narrow risks vanishing gradients
  - Hardware support: unstructured sparsity acceleration yields real savings; dense fallback kills gains

- Failure signatures:
  - No speedup despite high theoretical sparsity: likely due to dense matrix kernels or poor cache behavior
  - Training divergence: surrogate gradient width ϵ too small, causing near-zero updates
  - Memory blow-up: influence matrix not stored sparsely, losing O(β̃ωnp) savings

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement dense RTRL baseline on synthetic spiral dataset, verify O(n⁴) scaling
  2. Add Heaviside activation with surrogate gradient, measure activity sparsity β̃ and compute reduction
  3. Introduce fixed parameter sparsity mask, confirm O(β̃²ω²n²p) scaling and convergence with minimal accuracy loss

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the theoretical computational savings translate to actual runtime improvements on real hardware platforms?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that benefits can be "realised to the fullest extent on devices with support for unstructured sparsity" but does not provide empirical runtime comparisons on actual hardware.
- Why unresolved: The paper only shows analytical computational savings and convergence on a synthetic dataset, without measuring actual wall-clock time on different hardware platforms (GPUs, TPUs, neuromorphic chips).
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking results showing actual runtime comparisons between sparse RTRL implementations and dense RTRL/BPTT on multiple hardware platforms with varying levels of sparsity.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of surrogate gradient parameters (γ and ϵ) affect the computational savings and learning performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses specific values for γ and ϵ in the surrogate gradient function but does not explore how different parameter choices affect sparsity levels or learning outcomes.
- Why unresolved: The paper assumes fixed surrogate gradient parameters without investigating the trade-off between gradient approximation quality and resulting sparsity levels.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying γ and ϵ parameters to show their effects on activity sparsity levels, computational savings, and final task performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the combined sparsity approach scale effectively to larger networks and more complex tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper only demonstrates results on a synthetic task with 16 hidden units and 17 timesteps, while claiming potential applicability to "real world tasks."
- Why unresolved: The computational savings analysis and empirical results are limited to a small-scale example, without validation on larger networks or more challenging tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing performance and computational efficiency on larger networks (e.g., hundreds of units) and more complex tasks (e.g., sequential MNIST, language modeling) to verify scalability claims.

## Limitations
- The approach requires specialized hardware support for unstructured sparsity to realize theoretical computational savings
- Fixed parameter sparsity patterns may limit model capacity and adaptation during training
- Empirical validation is limited to a synthetic dataset with 16 hidden units and 17 timesteps

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The mathematical framework for combining activity and parameter sparsity in RTRL is sound and the theoretical complexity reductions (O(β̃²ω²n²p)) are well-established.
- Medium Confidence: The empirical validation on the synthetic spiral dataset demonstrates feasibility, but results on more complex tasks are needed to establish general applicability.
- Medium Confidence: The claim that this approach can compete with BPTT requires validation on tasks where BPTT typically excels, such as long-sequence modeling.
