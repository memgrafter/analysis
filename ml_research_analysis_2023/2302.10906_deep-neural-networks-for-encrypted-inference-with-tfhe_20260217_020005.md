# Deep Neural Networks for Encrypted Encrypted Inference with TFHE

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10906
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10906
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: This paper demonstrates how to train deep neural networks for encrypted inference using TFHE with quantization-aware training and L1-norm pruning to control accumulator bit-width while maintaining accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This work presents a method for training deep neural networks that are compatible with TFHE's constraints for fully homomorphic encryption. The key innovation combines quantization-aware training with L1-norm unstructured pruning to control accumulator bit-width while preserving training dynamics. The approach enables the construction of arbitrarily deep neural networks with any activation functions compatible with TFHE constraints. Experimental results on MNIST and CIFAR10 show competitive accuracies in FHE with inference times ranging from 31 seconds for small networks to 180,000 seconds for larger networks.

## Method Summary
The method involves training neural networks using quantization-aware training (QAT) with Brevitas in PyTorch, applying L1-norm unstructured pruning to control accumulator bit-width, and converting the trained models to FHE using the Concrete-ML library and Concrete stack. The TFHE scheme's programmable bootstrapping mechanism allows arbitrary depth computation circuits with exact results, enabling the use of arbitrary activation functions and deep network architectures without multiplicative depth limitations.

## Key Results
- Achieved 98.7% accuracy on MNIST using a LeNet-like architecture in FHE
- Maintained 92.2% accuracy on CIFAR10 with a 6-layer CNN in FHE
- Inference times range from 31 seconds for small networks to 180,000 seconds for larger networks
- Successfully converted arbitrarily deep networks using any activation function to FHE

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: L1-norm unstructured pruning controls accumulator bit-width while maintaining training dynamics.
- Mechanism: Pruning removes neurons from fully-connected layers, reducing the maximum possible sum of products in convolution and fully-connected layers. This directly limits the bit-width needed to represent accumulator values without affecting the remaining active neurons' training dynamics.
- Core assumption: Removing neurons through L1-norm pruning does not significantly degrade model accuracy when compensated by quantization-aware training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "To control the accumulator bit-width while keeping the training dynamics stable, we use L1-norm unstructured pruning. Figure 1 shows the impact of pruning on the accumulator size for two quantization modes: narrow and wide range."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - the corpus neighbors do not specifically discuss pruning strategies for FHE-compatible neural networks.
- Break condition: If pruning removes too many neurons, the model's representational capacity drops below the threshold needed for the target accuracy, making the pruned network unusable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Quantization-aware training enables extreme quantization with less than 4-bit weights and activations while preserving accuracy.
- Mechanism: During training, quantizers are added to network activations and weights, forcing the model to learn parameters that are robust to quantization noise. This allows the network to operate with very low bit-width integers while maintaining competitive accuracy.
- Core assumption: The model can learn to compensate for quantization noise during training, making extreme quantization viable without accuracy loss.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Quantization-aware Training (QAT), used in this work and in [4], is an approach that adds quantizers to network activations and weights during training. QAT enables extreme quantization with less than 4 bit weights and activations."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - while the corpus mentions related work on FHE and neural networks, it doesn't provide specific evidence about QAT effectiveness for extreme quantization in FHE contexts.
- Break condition: If the quantization noise introduced during inference exceeds what the model learned to compensate for during training, accuracy will degrade significantly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: TFHE's programmable bootstrapping mechanism allows arbitrary depth computation circuits with exact results.
- Mechanism: The PBS reduces noise in encrypted accumulators and allows applying lookup tables on ciphertexts. This enables the use of arbitrary activation functions and deep network architectures without the multiplicative depth limitations of leveled schemes.
- Core assumption: The computational cost of PBS is acceptable for the target inference latency requirements.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "In this work we show how to construct Deep Neural Networks (DNN) that are compatible with the constraints of TFHE, an FHE scheme that allows arbitrary depth computation circuits."
  - [section]: "To support arbitrarily deep NNs and any activation function, we make use of the programmable boostrapping mechanism [8] (PBS) of TFHE. PBS reduces the noise in accumulators of ciphertext leveled operations (addition, multiplication with clear constants) but also allows to apply a lookup-table (TLU) on its input ciphertext."
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - the corpus includes related works like "TT-TFHE: a Torus Fully Homomorphic Encryption-Friendly Neural Network Architecture" that discuss TFHE compatibility with neural networks.
- Break condition: If the PBS computational cost becomes prohibitive for the target hardware, making inference times too long for practical use.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: PyTorch model with Brevitas for quantization-aware training -> L1-norm unstructured pruning applied during training -> Concrete-ML for conversion to FHE-compatible format -> Concrete stack for FHE inference execution

- Critical path: 1. Train model with QAT and pruning 2. Convert to FHE using Concrete-ML 3. Optimize cryptographic parameters 4. Execute inference on encrypted data

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. bit-width: Lower bit-width reduces computational cost but may require more aggressive pruning
  - Depth vs. inference time: Deeper networks provide better accuracy but significantly increase PBS operations
  - Pruning level vs. model capacity: More pruning reduces accumulator size but may degrade model performance

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy degradation when moving from clear to encrypted inference (indicates quantization issues)
  - Exception during parameter optimization (indicates circuit bit-width too small for the model)
  - Excessive inference times (indicates need for further pruning or architectural simplification)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a simple 3-layer fully-connected network on MNIST with 2-bit quantization and moderate pruning, measure accuracy and accumulator bit-width
  2. Convert the trained model to FHE and verify that clear and encrypted inference produce identical results (within error tolerance)
  3. Scale to a LeNet-like architecture on MNIST, measuring the impact of increased depth on inference time and accuracy
