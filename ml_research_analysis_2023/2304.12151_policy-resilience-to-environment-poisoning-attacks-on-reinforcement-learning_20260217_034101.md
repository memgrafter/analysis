# Policy Resilience to Environment Poisoning Attacks on Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12151
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12151
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposes a federated meta-learning framework for recovering RL policies poisoned through environment hyper-parameter manipulation, demonstrating effective recovery across model-free and model-based agents.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses environment poisoning attacks on reinforcement learning by introducing a policy-resilience mechanism based on federated meta-learning. The approach enables poisoned agents to efficiently identify their deployment environment dynamics and recover their policies using imagined trajectories. By sharing environment knowledge among independent RL systems during a preparation stage, the mechanism learns a representative dynamics model that captures common environment structures. The method is evaluated on both model-free and model-based RL agents, showing effective recovery of poisoned policies with high deployment performance.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of a three-stage federated framework: (1) Preparation - a server learns a dynamics model using environment information collected from multiple client RL systems through meta-learning, (2) Diagnosis - poisoned agents quickly identify their deployment environment dynamics by fine-tuning the shared knowledge with limited interaction data, and (3) Recovery - agents generate imagined trajectories from the tailored dynamics model to recover their poisoned policies. The approach leverages the assumption that environments share the same Hidden Parameter Markov Decision Process (HiP-MDP) structure with varying hyper-parameters, enabling efficient knowledge extraction and transfer.

## Key Results
- Effective recovery of poisoned policies across both model-free and model-based RL agents
- Efficient identification of deployment environment dynamics using shared knowledge from multiple clients
- High deployment performance achieved through imagined trajectory-based policy recovery

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The federated framework allows poisoned agents to learn environment dynamics more efficiently than training from scratch.
- Mechanism: By sharing local dynamics models among independent RL systems, the server aggregates knowledge to build a representative dynamics model that captures the common structure across environments with varying hyper-parameters.
- Core assumption: All client RL systems operate under the same HiP-MDP structure, differing only in hyper-parameters.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "the proposed mechanism as a federated framework coupled with a meta-learning approach, pursuing an efficient extraction and sharing of the environment knowledge"; [section] "the federated framework consists of one server and a set of client RL systems, each with learnable dynamics models"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Meta-learning enables rapid identification of deployment environment dynamics from limited interactions.
- Mechanism: The server dynamics model is trained using meta-learning so it learns "how to learn" environment dynamics quickly. When a poisoned agent retrieves this model, it can fine-tune to the specific deployment environment with minimal data.
- Core assumption: The meta-learning process successfully captures the underlying structure that generalizes across different hyper-parameter settings.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we design the mechanism as a federated architecture coupled with a meta-learning manner, pursuing an efficient extraction and sharing of the environment knowledge"; [section] "we utilize a meta-learning approach [18] to learn the server dynamics model S based on environment information collected from all the federated RL systems"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Imagined trajectories from the tailored dynamics model enable effective policy recovery.
- Mechanism: Once the poisoned agent identifies the deployment environment dynamics, it can generate imagined trajectories using the learned model. These trajectories are then used to recover the policy through either model-based value expansion (for MF agents) or Model Predictive Control (for MB agents).
- Core assumption: The learned dynamics model accurately represents the deployment environment well enough to generate useful imagined trajectories.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "the agent imagines trajectories based on its accurate understanding of the environment dynamics. Accordingly it recovers the poisoned policy for an optimal deployment performance"; [section] "The agent can predict the future states with consideration of uncertainty which is estimate via the mean and deviation of multiple stochastic forward passes of the models"

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Hidden Parameter Markov Decision Process (HiP-MDP)
  - Why needed here: The framework assumes environments share the same structure but differ in hyper-parameters. HiP-MDP provides the formal framework for this assumption.
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes a HiP-MDP from a standard MDP in terms of how environment dynamics are parameterized?

- Concept: Meta-learning (learning to learn)
  - Why needed here: The preparation stage requires learning how to quickly adapt to new environment configurations, not just learning a single environment.
  - Quick check question: How does the meta-learning objective in equation (1) differ from standard supervised learning objectives?

- Concept: Federated Averaging vs Meta-learning in federated settings
  - Why needed here: The paper contrasts its approach with simply averaging local models, showing meta-learning provides better knowledge extraction.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between using Federated Averaging and the meta-learning approach described for learning the server dynamics model?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Server -> Client RL systems -> Buffer -> Meta-learning coordinator
- Critical path: Preparation (server learns dynamics) → Diagnosis (poisoned agent retrieves and fine-tunes dynamics) → Recovery (agent uses imagined trajectories to recover policy)
- Design tradeoffs: Centralized knowledge sharing vs. privacy preservation, Meta-learning complexity vs. simple averaging, Accuracy of dynamics model vs. computational efficiency
- Failure signatures: Poor policy recovery performance, High variance in recovery across different deployment environments, Sensitivity to poisoning proportion during preparation
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the 3D grid world environment with variable elevation as the hyper-parameter
  2. Build the federated framework with 10 clients, testing with 0%, 50%, and 100% poisoned clients during preparation
  3. Compare meta-learning approach against Federated Averaging baseline for learning the server dynamics model

## Limitations
- Assumes environments share the same HiP-MDP structure with only hyper-parameter differences, limiting applicability to more complex poisoning attacks
- Does not provide theoretical guarantees on convergence or sample complexity of the meta-learning process
- Performance may degrade if deployment environment hyper-parameters fall outside the distribution seen during preparation

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The basic federated framework architecture and its three-stage workflow (preparation → diagnosis → recovery) is clearly specified and internally consistent.
- **Medium confidence**: The meta-learning approach for extracting environment structure knowledge is theoretically sound, but lacks empirical validation across diverse environment types and attack scenarios.
- **Low confidence**: Claims about the efficiency gains from federated meta-learning versus individual training from scratch are not quantified, and the robustness to varying poisoning proportions during preparation remains largely theoretical.

## Next Checks
1. **Distribution shift robustness test**: Evaluate policy recovery performance when deployment environment hyper-parameters fall outside the range seen during meta-learning preparation. Measure degradation in recovery accuracy as the gap between training and deployment distributions increases.

2. **Poisoning proportion sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the proportion of poisoned clients during the preparation stage (0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%) and measure the impact on server dynamics model quality and subsequent policy recovery performance. Identify the threshold beyond which the federated knowledge becomes detrimental.

3. **Baselines comparison on recovery efficiency**: Compare the proposed meta-learning approach against (a) simple federated averaging of local dynamics models, and (b) individual agents learning from scratch in the deployment environment. Measure both final recovery performance and sample efficiency (trajectories needed for adequate recovery).
