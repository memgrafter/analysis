# Attention Boosted Autoencoder for Building Energy Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16097
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16097
- Reference count: 24
- Key outcome: Novel attention mechanism improves anomaly detection in building energy time series by capturing temporal and feature dependencies

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the TiFe Attention model, a novel attention mechanism for building energy anomaly detection in multivariate time series data. The model captures complex feature relationships across time windows using separate mappings for temporal and feature dependencies. When tested on synthetic and real-world datasets, the TiFe Attention model demonstrated improved reconstruction accuracy compared to standard autoencoders, particularly in handling anomalies. The attention maps provided interpretability by revealing how the model weighs different features and time steps during reconstruction.

## Method Summary
The TiFe Attention model processes multivariate time series data through a dual attention mechanism that separately captures temporal and feature dependencies. The model uses separate mappings (f for temporal, g for features) that are combined into a unified representation (h) to guide the reconstruction process. The architecture employs an encoder-decoder structure with window-based processing, using sliding windows of length 168 (1 week) for hourly data. The model is trained to minimize reconstruction error while learning to down-weight anomalous data points through attention mechanisms.

## Key Results
- TiFe Attention model outperforms standard autoencoders in reconstruction accuracy on both synthetic and real-world building energy datasets
- Attention maps successfully reveal learned patterns including daily and weekly periodicity in building energy consumption
- Model correctly identifies anomalies such as unusual power consumption spikes, with improved sensitivity compared to baseline approaches

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: TiFe Attention reduces reconstruction of anomalies by down-weighting input values that don't conform to learned patterns.
- Mechanism: During training, the attention mechanism learns to assign lower weights to data points that deviate from normal patterns, making the autoencoder less likely to reconstruct these anomalies.
- Core assumption: Anomalies can be identified by their deviation from learned normal patterns, and reducing their weight during reconstruction will increase reconstruction error.
