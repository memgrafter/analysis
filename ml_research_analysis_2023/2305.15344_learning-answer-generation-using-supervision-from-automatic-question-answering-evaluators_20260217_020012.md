# Learning Answer Generation using Supervision from Automatic Question Answering Evaluators

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15344
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15344
- Reference count: 18
- This paper proposes using automatic QA evaluation models (GA V A) to supervise the training of generation-based QA (GenQA) models with three strategies showing significant accuracy improvements.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of training generation-based QA models by introducing supervision from automatic QA evaluation models. The authors propose three strategies: augmenting training data with high-scoring generated answers, dynamically augmenting data at each training epoch, and using evaluation scores to weight the training loss. Experiments on academic and industrial datasets demonstrate that these approaches significantly improve answering accuracy over baseline GenQA models, as measured by human evaluation and automatic QA evaluation metrics.

## Method Summary
The paper presents three approaches for training Generation-based QA (GenQA) models using supervision from automatic QA evaluation models (GA V A). First, GA V A-SDA augments training data with high-scoring generated answers by generating multiple candidates and retaining only those above a threshold. Second, GA V A-DDA extends this by performing data augmentation dynamically at every training epoch using the current model's outputs. Third, GA V A-LW weights the training loss using GA V A scores of generated answers. The methods are evaluated on two academic datasets (WQA, MS-MARCO) and one industrial dataset (IQAD), using human accuracy ratings and GA V A-Score as metrics.

## Key Results
- GA V A-SDA, GA V A-DDA, and GA V A-LW significantly improve answering accuracy over baseline GenQA models
- The approaches show consistent improvements across two academic and one industrial dataset
- Human evaluation and automatic QA evaluation metrics both demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GA V A-SDA improves GenQA by augmenting training data with high-quality generated answers selected by GA V A
- Mechanism: The GenQA model generates multiple candidate answers for each training question. GA V A scores these candidates, and only those above a threshold are added as alternate gold answers, expanding the training set with high-quality examples
- Core assumption: GA V A scores reliably correlate with human judgment of answer correctness
- Evidence anchors: Experiments show significant accuracy improvement over baseline; GA V A outperforms A V A in correlation with human annotations
- Break condition: If GA V A scores poorly correlate with human judgments, the augmented data quality degrades and harms training

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GA V A-DDA improves training by dynamically augmenting data at each epoch using the current GenQA model's outputs
- Mechanism: At every training epoch, the current GenQA model generates answers for training questions. GA V A selects top-scoring answers as new targets and context, continuously adapting the training data to the model's improving capabilities
- Core assumption: The GenQA model's generated answers improve in quality over training epochs
- Evidence anchors: Dynamic augmentation shows benefits over static augmentation on MS-MARCO; related work on dynamic data augmentation supports this mechanism
- Break condition: If the GenQA model plateaus early, dynamic augmentation provides no benefit over static augmentation

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GA V A-LW improves training by weighting loss based on GA V A scores of generated answers
- Mechanism: During training, the GenQA model generates answers for each example. The GA V A score of this generated answer weights the cross-entropy loss, making the model focus more on examples where its current output quality is low
- Core assumption: Weighting loss by predicted answer quality improves learning efficiency and generalization
- Evidence anchors: Loss weighting techniques in related domains show improved training dynamics; experiments demonstrate effectiveness
- Break condition: If GA V A scores are noisy or inconsistent, loss weighting may misdirect learning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of GA V A compare to human evaluation in terms of correlation and accuracy across different datasets and tasks?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the performance of GA V A on two Answer Sentence Selection (AS2) datasets (WikiQA and TREC-QA) and compares it to human evaluation in terms of Pearson correlation
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents results for two specific datasets and tasks. It is unclear how GA V A would perform on other datasets or tasks, such as open-domain QA or question generation
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments evaluating GA V A on a wider range of datasets and tasks, comparing its performance to human evaluation in terms of correlation and accuracy

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the limitations of using automatic QA evaluation models like GA V A to supervise the training of GenQA models, and how can these limitations be addressed?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that automatic QA evaluation systems do not achieve perfect correlation with human annotations, which indicates a gap with respect to human evaluation
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the limitations of using automatic QA evaluation models and how these limitations can be addressed
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research investigating the limitations of using automatic QA evaluation models and exploring ways to address these limitations

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the proposed approaches for training GenQA models using GA V A be extended to other natural language generation tasks, such as summarization or dialogue generation?
- Basis in paper: The paper focuses on using GA V A to train GenQA models for answer generation. However, the proposed approaches could potentially be applied to other natural language generation tasks that involve generating text based on input context
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the applicability of the proposed approaches to other natural language generation tasks
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the proposed approaches to other natural language generation tasks, such as summarization or dialogue generation, and evaluating their performance in comparison to existing methods

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of the proposed methods depends on the correlation between GA V A scores and human judgment, which may vary across domains
- The optimal threshold (θ=0.9) for filtering generated answers in SDA and DDA is not empirically justified
- The probabilistic decoding approach for generating multiple answers is mentioned but not detailed

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core hypothesis that GA V A can supervise GenQA training is well-supported by experimental results
- **Medium Confidence**: The assumption that GA V A scores reliably correlate with human judgment may vary across domains
- **Low Confidence**: The optimal threshold for filtering generated answers and the probabilistic decoding approach lack detailed justification
