# Prompt to GPT-3: Step-by-Step Thinking Instructions for Humor Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13195
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13195
- Reference count: 4
- Primary result: Demonstrates method for generating jokes using GPT-3 through structured comedy writing theory and step-by-step thinking instructions

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a method for generating jokes using GPT-3 by modeling human comedy writing theory and leveraging step-by-step thinking instructions. The approach involves creating a topic sentence based on a news article, identifying handles and associations, developing a punchline by combining associations, and connecting the topic and punchline with an angle. The method is demonstrated to be effective in generating configurable and explainable jokes, with examples provided to illustrate the process. The paper also explores the role of cognitive distance in creating humor and suggests future directions for incorporating additional comedy writing theories and techniques.

## Method Summary
The method applies chain-of-thought prompting to sequentially guide GPT-3 through four stages: topic creation, handle/association identification, punchline development, and angle construction. The process begins with a news article (500-800 words) and uses predefined prompts for each stage of joke creation. The approach combines Toplyn's comedy writing theory with structured reasoning to generate jokes that target specific entities with negative emotional framing. The method emphasizes the importance of cognitive distance between associations for creating unexpected and humorous punchlines.

## Key Results
- The step-by-step approach enables configurable and explainable joke generation using GPT-3
- Negative emotional targeting is essential for generating humorous monologue jokes
- Cognitive distance between associations significantly impacts humor effectiveness
- The method successfully generates jokes that connect topic sentences with punchlines through angle construction

- Mechanism: The method applies chain-of-thought prompting to sequentially guide the model through four stages: topic creation, handle/association identification, punchline development, and angle construction
- Core assumption: GPT-3 can maintain context and coherence when processing a series of related prompts that build upon each other
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "explores humor generation using GPT-3 by modeling human comedy writing theory and leveraging step-by-step thinking instructions"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Negative emotional targeting is essential for humor generation
- Mechanism: The prompt explicitly instructs the model to create punchlines that evoke negative emotions toward the first major entity in the topic, which aligns with comedic theory that humor often involves a "target" for ridicule
- Evidence anchors: [section] "it is important to evoke a negative emotion towards the first major entity in the topic for the monologue joke to be humorous"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Cognitive distance between associations enhances humor effectiveness
- Mechanism: Selecting associations with low semantic similarity creates unexpected connections that surprise the audience, which is a key component of humor
- Evidence anchors: [section] "we found that the selection of associations is a critical factor in creating engaging and humorous jokes"

## Limitations
- The method's dependence on negative emotional targeting represents a significant limitation that may produce inappropriate or offensive content
- Claims about humor quality and effectiveness are not supported by evaluation data or quantitative metrics
- The approach may be limited to a specific genre of humor rather than generalizable joke generation

## Confidence
- Medium: The structured prompting approach is technically sound and the chain-of-thought methodology is well-established
- Low: Claims about humor quality and effectiveness are not supported by evaluation data
- Medium: The comedy writing theory framework provides useful structure but may be overly restrictive
