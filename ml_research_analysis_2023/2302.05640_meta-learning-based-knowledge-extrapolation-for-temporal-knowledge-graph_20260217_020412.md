# Meta-Learning Based Knowledge Extrapolation for Temporal Knowledge Graph

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05640
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05640
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: MTKGE achieves up to 70.2% and 70.7% relative increases in MRR and Hits@10 metrics compared to strongest baseline for TKG extrapolation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of temporal knowledge graph (TKG) extrapolation, where new entities and relations emerge over time in incomplete TKGs. The proposed method, MTKGE, leverages meta-learning to learn transferable meta-knowledge between relations, enabling the embedding of unseen components in emerging TKGs. By introducing the Relative Position Pattern Graph (RPPG) and Temporal Sequence Pattern Graph (TSPG) modules, MTKGE captures structural features of relations in terms of space and time, respectively. The model is meta-trained on link prediction tasks sampled from existing TKGs and tested on emerging TKGs with unseen entities and relations, demonstrating consistent superiority over state-of-the-art models for knowledge graph extrapolation.

## Method Summary
The MTKGE model consists of three main modules: the Relative Position Pattern Graph (RPPG), the Temporal Sequence Pattern Graph (TSPG), and a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) module for extrapolation. The RPPG captures structural features of relations in terms of space, while the TSPG learns temporally structural features as relation happening orders. The GCN module is used for the final extrapolation task. The model is meta-trained on link prediction tasks sampled from existing TKGs and tested on emerging TKGs with unseen entities and relations. This approach allows MTKGE to learn transferable meta-knowledge between relations, enabling it to effectively embed unseen components in emerging TKGs.

## Key Results
- MTKGE consistently outperforms state-of-the-art models for knowledge graph extrapolation and specifically adapted KGE and TKGE baselines.
- The model achieves significant improvements in MRR and Hits@10 metrics, with up to 70.2% and 70.7% relative increases compared to the strongest baseline results.
- Experimental results on two TKG extrapolation benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed meta-learning approach for knowledge extrapolation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
MTKGE works by leveraging meta-learning to learn transferable meta-knowledge between relations in temporal knowledge graphs. This meta-knowledge allows the model to effectively embed unseen components in emerging TKGs by capturing structural features of relations in terms of space (via RPPG) and time (via TSPG). By meta-training on link prediction tasks sampled from existing TKGs, MTKGE learns to generalize these patterns to new, unseen entities and relations in emerging TKGs. This approach enables the model to adapt quickly to new graph structures and maintain high performance even when faced with previously unseen components.

## Foundational Learning
- **Temporal Knowledge Graphs**: Represent entities and relations evolving over time. Needed to model real-world scenarios where new information emerges over time.
- **Meta-learning**: Learning to learn, enabling models to quickly adapt to new tasks. Needed to transfer knowledge between relations and generalize to unseen components.
- **Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs)**: Neural networks that operate on graph-structured data. Needed to capture structural features of relations in TKGs.
- **Link Prediction**: Predicting missing edges in a graph. Needed as the primary task for meta-training and evaluation.
- **Relative Position Patterns**: Structural features of relations in terms of space. Needed to capture spatial relationships between entities in TKGs.
- **Temporal Sequence Patterns**: Structural features of relations as happening orders over time. Needed to capture temporal dynamics in TKGs.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map: TKG -> RPPG -> TSPG -> GCN -> Extrapolated Embeddings

Critical Path: The model takes a temporal knowledge graph as input, processes it through the RPPG and TSPG modules to capture spatial and temporal structural features, respectively. These features are then fed into the GCN module, which performs the final extrapolation task to generate embeddings for unseen entities and relations in emerging TKGs.

Design Tradeoffs: The use of meta-learning allows for quick adaptation to new tasks but introduces additional computational overhead during training. The RPPG and TSPG modules capture specific structural features but may not capture all possible patterns in complex TKGs.

Failure Signatures: Poor performance on emerging TKGs with highly complex or novel relational patterns that differ significantly from those seen during meta-training. Degradation in performance when the temporal dynamics of the emerging TKG diverge from the meta-training data.
