# Naver Labs Europe (SPLADE) @ TREC NeuCLIR 2022

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11171
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11171
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: Monolingual strategies showed promise but Adhoc approaches with document back-translation achieved better results for cross-lingual retrieval in Farsi and Russian languages

## Executive Summary
This paper describes Naver Labs Europe's participation in the 2022 TREC NeuCLIR challenge, comparing monolingual and Adhoc strategies for cross-lingual retrieval in Farsi and Russian. The team employed SPLADE and ColBERT as first-stage rankers, with pretraining using MLM+FLOPS and fine-tuning on translated MSMARCO. For Adhoc runs, they tested both query translation and document back-translation approaches. The analysis revealed that while monolingual strategies showed promise, Adhoc approaches achieved better results, particularly with document back-translation. The best performance was observed in Farsi monolingual reranking ensembles, while Russian benefited more from Adhoc document back-translation.

## Method Summary
The approach combined language-specific pretraining with cross-lingual adaptation. Pretraining used 6L DistilBERT with MLM+FLOPS on target language corpora (target documents, translated MSMARCO, and Mr.Tydi), followed by fine-tuning with ColBERT and SPLADE on translated MSMARCO. First-stage retrieval used ensemble methods (SPLADE, ColBERT, BM25), with Rocchio feedback improving SPLADE performance. Reranking employed multiple PLMs (InfoXLM, XLM-Roberta-Large, Electra-Large, Deberta-v3-Large, Deberta-v2-xxLarge, MonoT5-3B) combined via ranx averaging. Evaluation used nDCG@20, mAP@1k, and Recall@1k on filtered HC4 development data.

## Key Results
- Adhoc document back-translation outperformed query translation for cross-lingual retrieval
- Farsi monolingual reranking ensembles achieved the best overall performance
- Russian language benefited more from Adhoc document back-translation than monolingual approaches
- SPLADE+Rocchio consistently improved over SPLADE alone

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Monolingual SPLADE models trained on target language corpora with MLM+FLOPS pretraining outperform direct translation approaches when fine-tuned on translated MSMARCO.
- Mechanism: MLM+FLOPS pretraining builds language-specific contextualized embeddings that preserve semantic nuance lost in translation, while fine-tuning on translated MSMARCO aligns these embeddings with retrieval objectives in the target language.
- Core assumption: Translation quality is imperfect and loses domain-specific semantic mappings that monolingual pretraining can recover.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "For monolingual runs, we start from pretraining models on the target language using MLM+FLOPS and then finetuning using the MSMARCO translated to the language"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Document back-translation to English combined with English PLM reranking outperforms query translation for cross-lingual retrieval.
- Mechanism: Back-translation creates an English corpus that PLMs trained on English MSMARCO can process effectively, while query translation introduces noise that degrades initial ranking quality.
- Core assumption: PLMs are more robust to document translation noise than to query translation noise when ranking.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "While for the Adhoc task we test both query translation (to the target language) and back-translation of the documents (to english). Initial result analysis shows that the monolingual strategy is strong, but that for the moment Adhoc achieved the best results, with back-translating documents being better than translating queries."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensemble of multiple first-stage rankers (SPLADE, ColBERT, BM25) with Rocchio feedback consistently improves retrieval effectiveness over individual models.
- Mechanism: Different models capture complementary retrieval signals - SPLADE provides sparse representations, ColBERT captures late interaction patterns, and BM25 provides traditional term matching. Rocchio expands queries based on top-ranked documents.
- Core assumption: No single retrieval model dominates across all query types and languages.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3] "we had a hierarchy of SPLADE ≥ ColBERT ≥ BM25, but their ensemble seemed more stable than individual runs"

## Limitations
- Effectiveness of MLM+FLOPS pretraining remains partially validated without direct experimental comparison to standard approaches
- Translation quality introduces significant noise, particularly for entity-heavy queries and language-specific terminology
- Results are limited to specific languages (Farsi and Russian) and document collections, limiting generalizability

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Ensemble effectiveness of SPLADE+Rocchio over individual models, and the general superiority of document back-translation over query translation for Adhoc strategies
- **Medium confidence**: Language-specific advantages of monolingual pretraining, as this depends on translation quality assumptions that vary by language pair
- **Low confidence**: Generalizability to other language pairs beyond Farsi and Russian, and the specific impact of MLM+FLOPS pretraining vs. standard approaches
