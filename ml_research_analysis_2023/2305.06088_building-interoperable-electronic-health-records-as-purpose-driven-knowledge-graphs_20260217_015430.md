# Building Interoperable Electronic Health Records as Purpose-Driven Knowledge Graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06088
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06088
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: iTelos methodology enables building interoperable eHealth knowledge graphs by separating data and schema levels and guiding development with purpose-defined competency queries

## Executive Summary
The iTelos methodology addresses the challenge of building interoperable electronic health records by constructing purpose-driven knowledge graphs. The approach separates the development of data and schema levels, allowing independent reuse of heterogeneous medical datasets and reference ontologies. By formalizing functional requirements as competency queries and processing resources through a three-level reusability classification, iTelos enables incremental alignment of data with schemas while maintaining semantic flexibility. The methodology was validated through a large-scale eHealth case study involving integration of medical data from different European countries.

## Method Summary
iTelos implements a middle-out development process consisting of four phases: inception (defining purpose through competency queries), modeling (building entity type graphs from datasets and ontologies), knowledge alignment (aligning ETG with reference ontologies), and data integration (integrating datasets into final entity graphs). The process uses evaluation metrics including coverage, extensiveness, and sparsity at each phase to ensure alignment with purpose and reference ontologies. Key innovations include the separation of data and schema development, a three-level reusability classification system, and iterative evaluation to trigger backtracking when thresholds are not met.

## Key Results
- Successfully integrated local medical data from different European countries into a single multilingual data format
- Discovered and resolved data heterogeneity issues between countries through systematic alignment process
- Demonstrated that separating data and schema levels enables independent reuse of heterogeneous medical datasets and reference ontologies
- Identified human intervention as a scalability bottleneck requiring future automation improvements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Separation of data and schema levels enables independent reuse of heterogeneous medical datasets and reference ontologies
- Mechanism: By modeling datasets as Entity Graphs (EGs) and schemas as Entity Type (etype) Graphs (ETGs), iTelos allows incremental alignment of data with schemas while maintaining semantic flexibility
- Core assumption: Semantic heterogeneity in eHealth data can be managed through parallel development of data and schema layers under a shared purpose
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The key intuition is that the data level and the schema level of an application should be developed independently"
  - [section] "the data level and the schema level of an application should be developed independently, thus allowing for maximum flexibility in the reuse of the prior knowledge"
- Break condition: If the purpose cannot be clearly formalized into competency queries, the separation becomes meaningless and alignment fails

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Three-level reusability classification (common, core, contextual) guides efficient resource selection and reduces integration effort
- Mechanism: iTelos categorizes resources by reusability and processes them in decreasing order, maximizing use of widely available schemas and data while handling domain-specific elements last
- Core assumption: Reusability correlates with availability and ease of integration; common resources are easier to align than contextual ones
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "the availability, and thus the reusability, of resources, and of data in particular, decreases from common to contextual category"
  - [section] "the three types of resources (CQs, ontologies, datasets) handled during the first two phases, into three categories defining how reusable such resources are"
- Break condition: If contextual resources dominate the domain, the classification provides little benefit and may slow progress

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Iterative middle-out process with continuous alignment ensures convergence to a purpose-compliant and shareable KG
- Mechanism: Each phase (Inception, Modeling, Knowledge Alignment, Data Integration) ends with evaluation metrics (coverage, extensiveness, sparsity) that trigger backtracking if thresholds are not met, enforcing alignment
- Core assumption: Continuous evaluation against the purpose and reference ontologies prevents divergence and maintains interoperability
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "each phase ends with an evaluation activity (Eval boxes in Figure 2)"
  - [section] "The evaluation activities implemented in the iTelos process are based on a set of metrics applied to the intermediate outputs"
- Break condition: If evaluation thresholds are too strict, the process may loop indefinitely; if too lax, interoperability is compromised

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Competency Queries (CQs)
  - Why needed here: CQs formalize functional requirements and drive the entire iTelos process by specifying what information the final KG must provide
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between a competency query and a general user requirement in the iTelos context?

- Concept: Entity Graphs vs Entity Type Graphs
  - Why needed here: EGs hold actual data entities; ETGs define the schema. Understanding the distinction is critical for implementing the data-schema separation
  - Quick check question: In iTelos, which graph type would you modify when adding a new medical code standard?

- Concept: Knowledge Graph Alignment Metrics
  - Why needed here: Coverage, extensiveness, and sparsity metrics guide iterative refinement and ensure the KG satisfies both purpose and reusability goals
  - Quick check question: How does the sparsity metric differ from coverage in evaluating ETG-ontology alignment?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Purpose definition → Competency Queries → Dataset & Ontology collection → ETG model → Reference ontology alignment → ETG → Data integration → Final EG
  - Tools: KarmaLinker for mapping, ML-based ETR for schema alignment, evaluation modules for metrics

- Critical path:
  1. Formalize purpose into CQs
  2. Select and align datasets and ontologies
  3. Build and align ETG model
  4. Integrate datasets into final EG

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Separation of data and schema increases flexibility but requires more coordination
  - Reusability classification reduces integration effort but may miss domain-specific optimizations
  - Iterative evaluation ensures quality but adds development overhead

- Failure signatures:
  - Low coverage metrics → incomplete data alignment → backtracking to dataset selection
  - High sparsity → poor ontology reuse → backtracking to ETG model
  - Human intervention bottlenecks → scalability issues in data integration

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a minimal ETG model from a simple CQ and verify coverage against a test dataset
  2. Run the ETR process on a small set of reference ontologies and measure similarity scores
  3. Integrate a single dataset using KarmaLinker and evaluate sparsity between resulting EG and reference ontology

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific metrics can be developed to evaluate the quality of the final entity graph (EG) produced by iTelos, particularly in terms of data integration quality and semantic heterogeneity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that data-level evaluation criteria are crucial but notes that a more precise set of metrics for the final EG's quality is under definition
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed set of metrics for evaluating the final EG's quality, only outlining the need for such metrics
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive set of metrics that can quantitatively assess the EG's quality, including measures for data integration accuracy, semantic consistency, and connectivity

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can iTelos be further automated to reduce human intervention and improve scalability in building knowledge graphs (KGs)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies the need for human supervision as a weakness, limiting the scalability of systematic approaches for KG building
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests that iTelos needs to be improved to provide the highest level of (semi)automation with the least human intervention required, but does not specify how this can be achieved
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed plan or prototype demonstrating increased automation in the iTelos process, showing reduced human intervention and improved scalability

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the reuse of common and core etypes and properties from reference ontologies impact the shareability and reusability of the final entity type (ETG) across different domains and applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the importance of reusing etypes and properties from reference ontologies to enhance the shareability of the ETG
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide empirical evidence or case studies showing the impact of reusing common and core etypes on the ETG's shareability and reusability
- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies or empirical data demonstrating the benefits of reusing common and core etypes in terms of ETG shareability and reusability across multiple domains and applications

## Limitations
- Human intervention required in data integration and schema alignment phases limits scalability of systematic KG building approaches
- Effectiveness of three-level reusability classification depends heavily on availability of well-defined reference ontologies and standardized datasets
- Evaluation based primarily on single large-scale eHealth case study with limited quantitative validation across diverse domains

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1: Medium - Strong theoretical foundation but limited empirical validation of separation strategy
- Mechanism 2: Medium - Classification concept is clear but benefits depend on domain-specific resource availability
- Mechanism 3: Medium - Iterative evaluation process is well-defined but threshold sensitivity not characterized
