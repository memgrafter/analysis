# Gradient Remedy for Multi-Task Learning in End-to-End Noise-Robust Speech Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11362
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11362
- Reference count: 0
- Relative WER reductions of 9.3% and 11.1% on RATS and CHiME-4 datasets, respectively

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a gradient remedy (GR) approach to address interference between task gradients in multi-task learning for noise-robust speech recognition. The method projects the speech enhancement (SE) task's gradient onto a dynamic surface at an acute angle to the automatic speech recognition (ASR) gradient, resolving conflicts while maintaining beneficial contributions. An adaptive rescaling strategy is employed to prevent the dominant ASR task from being misled by SE gradients. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements in ASR performance on both RATS and CHiME-4 datasets.

## Method Summary
The proposed approach tackles gradient interference in multi-task learning by projecting the SE gradient onto a dynamic surface that maintains an acute angle with the ASR gradient. This projection removes conflicts between the two gradients while preserving beneficial contributions from the SE task. Additionally, an adaptive rescaling strategy is implemented to dynamically adjust the influence of the SE gradient on the ASR task, preventing the dominant ASR task from being negatively affected. The method is evaluated on noise-robust speech recognition tasks using the RATS and CHiME-4 datasets, showing substantial improvements in word error rate (WER).

## Key Results
- Achieved relative WER reductions of 9.3% on the RATS dataset
- Achieved relative WER reductions of 11.1% on the CHiME-4 dataset
- Demonstrated effectiveness of gradient projection and adaptive rescaling in resolving task interference

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The gradient remedy approach works by addressing the fundamental issue of conflicting gradients in multi-task learning. By projecting the SE gradient onto a dynamic surface that maintains an acute angle with the ASR gradient, the method resolves interference while preserving the beneficial contributions of the SE task. The adaptive rescaling strategy dynamically adjusts the influence of the SE gradient, ensuring that the dominant ASR task is not misled by potentially conflicting gradients from the SE task.
