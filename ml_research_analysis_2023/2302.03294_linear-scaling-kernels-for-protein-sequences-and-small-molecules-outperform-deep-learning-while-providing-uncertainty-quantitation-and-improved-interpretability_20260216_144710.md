# Linear-scaling kernels for protein sequences and small molecules outperform deep learning while providing uncertainty quantitation and improved interpretability

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03294
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03294
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: xGPR achieves competitive or superior performance to deep learning on 20 benchmarks while providing uncertainty quantitation and interpretability

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the computational limitations of Gaussian processes by developing xGPR with fast approximations and novel kernels for protein sequences and small molecules. The method achieves linear scaling with data size through preconditioned conjugate gradients and efficient convolution kernels, enabling training on large datasets in minutes rather than hours. Applied to 20 benchmarks, xGPR matches or exceeds deep learning performance while providing uncertainty estimates and interpretable data representations for clustering and visualization.

## Method Summary
xGPR combines scalable Gaussian process regression with novel kernels for sequences and graphs, achieving O(N) scaling through random feature approximations and preconditioned conjugate gradients. The approach uses fast Hadamard transform-based convolution kernels (FHTConv-1d for sequences, GraphConv-1d for molecules) with 8,192-16,384 random features. A randomized Nystrom preconditioner accelerates matrix inversion, reducing training time by up to two orders of magnitude. The method is implemented in an open-source Python library with Bayesian hyperparameter optimization using marginal likelihood maximization.

## Key Results
- Outperforms deep learning on 20 benchmarks including protein sequence and small molecule property prediction
- Reduces training times from hours to minutes (e.g., protein sequence models in under 8 minutes)
- Provides well-calibrated uncertainty estimates that identify distribution shifts
- Generates interpretable data representations useful for clustering and visualization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fast convolution kernels and efficient matrix inversion achieve competitive performance to deep learning
- Mechanism: Randomized Nystrom preconditioner with conjugate gradients reduces O(N³) to O(N) complexity; convolution kernels scale linearly with data size
- Core assumption: Random feature approximations preserve accuracy while enabling fast computation
- Evidence anchors: "fast convolution kernels which scale linearly with graph or sequence size"; "PCG strategy reduces iterations by two orders of magnitude"
- Break Condition: Poor eigengap or high random feature approximation error degrades convergence and accuracy

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Provides well-calibrated uncertainty estimates lacking in deep learning
- Mechanism: GP posterior distributions naturally quantify prediction reliability; high variance indicates inputs far from training data
- Core assumption: Kernel captures relevant similarity structure for meaningful uncertainty
- Evidence anchors: "xGPR provides uncertainty information not available from typical deep learning models"; high uncertainty predictions are usually incorrect
- Break Condition: Kernel misspecification or poor random feature approximation yields overconfident or uninformative uncertainty

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Generates interpretable data representations for clustering and visualization
- Mechanism: Random feature transformation approximates kernel PCA embedding for downstream analysis
- Core assumption: Random feature space preserves meaningful structure from original input space
- Evidence anchors: "xGPR provides a representation of input data for clustering and visualization"; demonstrated on QM9 dataset
- Break Condition: Insufficient random features or poor tuning loses important information

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data → Kernel selection → Random feature approximation → Preconditioner construction → Conjugate gradients fitting → Hyperparameter tuning → Prediction/Uncertainty
- Critical path: Data → Kernel selection → Random feature approximation → Preconditioner construction → Conjugate gradients fitting → Hyperparameter tuning → Prediction/Uncertainty
- Design tradeoffs: Accuracy vs speed (more features = better accuracy but slower); memory vs computation (preconditioner rank affects convergence and memory)
- Failure signatures: Poor CG convergence (bad preconditioner), high prediction error (inadequate features), overconfident uncertainty (kernel misspecification)
- First 3 experiments: 1) Fit RBF kernel on small tabular dataset and compare with exact GP; 2) Compare FHTConv-1d vs FastConv-1d on protein sequence benchmark; 3) Generate embeddings and perform clustering on QM9 dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Open Question 1: What specific types of protein engineering and small molecule property prediction problems are most suitable for Gaussian processes compared to deep learning?
  - Basis in paper: GPs are most useful when existing similarity measures exist for a problem
  - Why unresolved: General hypothesis provided but not systematically tested across diverse tasks
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of GP vs deep learning across broad range of protein and small molecule tasks

- Open Question 2: How can the computational efficiency of xGPR be further improved for extremely large datasets?
  - Basis in paper: Achieves linear scaling but notes matrix decomposition remains cubic in random features
  - Why unresolved: Efficiency gains demonstrated but limits and further optimizations unexplored
  - What evidence would resolve it: Testing on millions of datapoints with different chunking strategies and optimizations

- Open Question 3: How can the uncertainty quantification provided by xGPR be best utilized in practical applications like drug discovery?
  - Basis in paper: Demonstrates uncertainty can detect distribution shifts but doesn't explore practical applications
  - Why unresolved: Shows capability but lacks real-world application demonstrations
  - What evidence would resolve it: Case studies showing how uncertainty guides experimental design or resource allocation in drug discovery

## Limitations

- Performance claims rely on benchmarks not fully described in main text
- Limited hyperparameter tuning details and random seed variability analysis
- Interpretability claims based on qualitative visualizations rather than quantitative metrics

## Confidence

- Performance claims: Medium - strong results but limited hyperparameter details
- Uncertainty quantification: High - core GP property, well-established in theory
- Interpretability claims: Low - primarily based on visualizations without quantitative validation
