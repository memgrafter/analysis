# COMBO: A Complete Benchmark for Open KG Canonicalization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03905
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03905
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces COMBO, a comprehensive benchmark for open knowledge graph canonicalization

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces COMBO, a comprehensive benchmark for open knowledge graph canonicalization. It extends previous work by providing annotations for relation phrase canonicalization (RPC) and ontology-level noun phrase canonicalization (NPC-O) in addition to entity-level NPC-E. The benchmark includes 18K triples extracted from Wiki20 and Wikidata, along with source sentences. Evaluation metrics are proposed for each task. Experiments compare existing methods with new PLM-based baselines, showing that encoding triples with pretrained language models improves performance, especially on RPC and NPC-O. Triple-level pretraining further boosts results. COMBO enables the first comprehensive evaluation of open KG canonicalization methods across all three tasks.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces COMBO, a benchmark for open knowledge graph canonicalization that includes three tasks: entity-level NPC-E, relation phrase RPC, and ontology-level NPC-O. The dataset contains 18K triples from Wiki20 and Wikidata with source sentences and human annotations. PLM-based baselines are developed using BERT, RoBERTa, SpanBERT, and ERNIE2.0, encoding triples in various forms (sentence, triple, triple-sep, sep) with different span representations (mean, max, diffsum). HAC clustering with complete linkage is used for grouping similar phrases. Triple-level pretraining is introduced to adapt PLMs to knowledge graph structure. Evaluation uses macro/micro/pairwise F1 for NPC-E and RPC, and modified micro/Jaccard-based metrics for NPC-O.

## Key Results
- PLM-based encoding significantly improves RPC and NPC-O performance compared to static embeddings
- Triple-level pretraining provides additional gains, especially for RPC (+9.73 F1 for RoBERTa)
- Different PLM layers show varying effectiveness: lower layers for NPC-E, upper layers for RPC and NPC-O
- Mean pooling of span representations performs best across tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Contextualized phrase embeddings from PLMs improve RPC and NPC-O by resolving ambiguity that static embeddings cannot capture
- Mechanism: PLMs encode each phrase in context of its sentence, allowing disambiguation based on surrounding text and other entities in the triple. This is crucial for RPC where surface forms are ambiguous (e.g., "in" can mean location or part-of)
- Core assumption: Ambiguity in relation phrases and ontological NPs requires context to disambiguate, unlike entity-level NP canonicalization where surface forms are often similar
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that properly encoding the phrases in a triple using pretrained language models results in better relation canonicalization and ontology-level canonicalization of the noun phrase."
  - [section 5.1] "Previous methods encode NPs and RPs using either token frequency features or static word embedding. Although CESI and CUV A learn graph embedding on open KG triples, they assign the same representation to phrases with the same surface form and therefore cannot deal with ambiguity."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Triple-level pretraining on masked phrases improves performance by adapting PLMs to the specific structure of knowledge graph triples
- Mechanism: By masking phrases in triples and training PLMs to predict them, the model learns to better represent the semantic relationships between head NP, relation, and tail NP, which are crucial for canonicalization
- Core assumption: The structure of knowledge graph triples contains information that general PLM pretraining doesn't capture, and this structure is relevant to canonicalization tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2.2] "Inspired by the HolE algorithm used in previous works (Vashishth et al., 2018; Dash et al., 2021), we may perform an optional triple-level continuous pretraining step before encoding to mimic the link prediction objectives in knowledge graph embedding learning."
  - [section 5.4] "We also found the triple-level pretraining effective, having a positive influence in most cases, especially on RPC (e.g., +9.73 for RoBERTa)."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Different PLM layers contain complementary information for the three canonicalization tasks, with lower layers better for NPC-E and upper layers better for RPC and NPC-O
- Mechanism: The model architecture of PLMs means that lower layers capture more surface-level features while upper layers capture more context-dependent semantic information. This matches the varying context needs of the tasks
- Core assumption: The layer-wise specialization of PLMs reflects the different information requirements of the canonicalization tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.4] "We empirically find that lower layers [1,2,3] perform well for NPC-E, upper layers [10,11,12] perform best for RPC and NPC-O (subj), while middle layers [3,4,5,6,7] perform relatively better on NPC-O (obj)."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Wiki20 + Wikidata → OpenIE triples → human revision → ontology extraction → PLM encoding → HAC clustering → evaluation
- Critical path: Data → Encoding → Clustering → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using PLMs vs static embeddings: PLMs provide context but are slower and require more memory
  - Sentence vs triple encoding: Sentence provides more context but may include irrelevant information
  - Mean vs max vs diff-sum pooling: Mean pooling performs best but may lose some information
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor RPC performance: Likely due to insufficient context or ambiguous relation phrases
  - Poor NPC-O performance: Could indicate issues with ontology extraction or overlapping cluster handling
  - High variance between runs: May suggest sensitivity to initialization or data ordering
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare PLM-based encoding vs static embeddings on RPC task only
  2. Test different input forms (sentence, triple, triple-sep, sep) on NPC-E
  3. Evaluate the impact of triple-level pretraining on all three tasks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of triple-level pretraining vary across different PLM architectures and pretraining objectives?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares triple-level pretraining with causal subword-level pretraining and finds varying results across different PLMs and tasks
- Why unresolved: The study only examines a limited set of PLMs (BERT, RoBERTa, SpanBERT, ERNIE2.0) and pretraining objectives. Different PLM architectures or pretraining strategies might yield different results
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments comparing triple-level pretraining across a wider range of PLM architectures (e.g., GPT, T5, ELECTRA) and pretraining objectives (e.g., MLM, SOP, denoising) on COMBO and other canonicalization benchmarks

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can joint canonicalization of NPs and RPs improve overall performance compared to the current task-specific approach?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that NPC-E, NPC-O, and RPC are closely correlated (e.g., NPC-O clusters of NPs reveal the domain and range of the relation given by RPC), but currently treats them as separate tasks
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore joint canonicalization methods that could leverage the interdependencies between these tasks
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of joint canonicalization models that simultaneously cluster NPs and RPs, with comparisons to the current task-specific approach on COMBO and other benchmarks

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of COMBO scale with dataset size, and what is the minimum dataset size required for effective canonicalization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that COMBO (18K triples) is relatively small compared to previous datasets and suggests it could be more challenging for methods that do not use contextualized embedding
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate how performance scales with dataset size or determine the minimum size needed for effective canonicalization
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments training and evaluating canonicalization models on progressively larger subsets of COMBO and other datasets to determine the relationship between dataset size and performance, identifying the minimum effective size

## Limitations
- COMBO benchmark covers only English text from Wikipedia and Wikidata, limiting generalizability
- Evaluation relies on human-annotated gold clusters with limited detail on annotation process and inter-annotator agreement
- PLM-based baselines use relatively standard architectures without exploring more sophisticated approaches
- Paper does not address computational efficiency concerns of resource-intensive PLM methods

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The observation that PLM-based encoding improves performance over static embeddings
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that triple-level pretraining is effective
- **Medium confidence**: The layer-wise findings for different tasks
