# On the State of German (Abstractive) Text Summarization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.07095
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07095
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Over 50% of samples in popular German summarization datasets are invalid (mostly extractive summaries), leading to inflated model performance and poor real-world generalization.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the state of German abstractive text summarization, revealing widespread data quality issues that undermine model training and evaluation. Popular datasets, particularly MLSUM, are found to contain over 50% invalid samples—mostly extractive summaries that do not reflect the intended abstractive task. Retraining or re-evaluating models on cleaned data leads to dramatic drops in ROUGE-1 scores (over 20 points), exposing significant performance inflation due to data biases. The study also shows that publicly available German summarization systems often fail to outperform simple extractive baselines and frequently copy text from inputs. The authors call for more rigorous data quality checks, diverse evaluation protocols, and improved factual consistency in German summarization models.

## Method Summary
The authors analyze German abstractive summarization datasets, identifying invalid samples (mainly extractive summaries) using filtering heuristics and manual inspection. They retrain or re-evaluate popular models on cleaned datasets and benchmark publicly available systems against simple extractive baselines. Automated metrics (ROUGE) are used for evaluation, but the study acknowledges the absence of human evaluation and factual consistency checks.

## Key Results
- Over 50% of samples in MLSUM and similar datasets are invalid, mostly extractive summaries.
- Retraining or re-evaluating models on cleaned data reduces ROUGE-1 scores by more than 20 points.
- Publicly available German summarization systems frequently copy input text and fail to outperform simple extractive baselines.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that data contamination—specifically, the presence of extractive summaries in supposedly abstractive datasets—biases model training and evaluation, leading to inflated performance metrics. By cleaning datasets and reassessing models, the true capabilities (and limitations) of German summarization systems are revealed.

## Foundational Learning

- **ROUGE metrics:** Used to evaluate summarization quality by comparing system outputs to reference summaries. *Why needed:* Provides a standardized, automated way to measure overlap. *Quick check:* Compute ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L on sample summaries.
- **Extractive vs. abstractive summarization:** Extractive selects and concatenates input sentences; abstractive generates novel sentences. *Why needed:* Task definitions guide dataset construction and model design. *Quick check:* Compare summary content to input for direct copying.
- **Data contamination:** When datasets contain samples not representative of the target task (e.g., extractive summaries in abstractive datasets). *Why needed:* Contamination biases model learning and evaluation. *Quick check:* Inspect a random sample of summaries for overlap with input.
- **Filtering heuristics:** Automated rules to remove invalid samples (e.g., high n-gram overlap with input). *Why needed:* Enables scalable dataset cleaning. *Quick check:* Apply filters and measure reduction in dataset size.
- **Public system benchmarking:** Evaluating publicly available models against baselines. *Why needed:* Assesses real-world system performance. *Quick check:* Run multiple systems on the same inputs and compare outputs.
- **Factual consistency:** Ensuring generated summaries do not introduce factual errors. *Why needed:* Critical for reliable summarization, especially in abstractive settings. *Quick check:* Manually verify key facts in generated summaries.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map:** Data collection -> Dataset filtering -> Model training -> Evaluation (ROUGE) -> Public system benchmarking

**Critical path:** Dataset quality → Model performance → Evaluation validity

**Design tradeoffs:** Using automated filters vs. manual inspection for dataset cleaning; relying on ROUGE vs. human evaluation for assessment.

**Failure signatures:** High n-gram overlap between summaries and inputs; large performance drops after dataset cleaning; system outputs that are near-duplicates of input text.

**First experiments:**
1. Apply the proposed filtering heuristics to a German summarization dataset and measure contamination rate.
2. Retrain a standard model on the cleaned dataset and compare ROUGE scores to the original.
3. Benchmark a publicly available German summarization system on both original and cleaned datasets.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How to best ensure factual consistency and prevent hallucination in German abstractive summarization?
- What are the most effective methods for diversifying evaluation domains in German summarization?
- How can human evaluation be integrated into the assessment of German summarization models?

## Limitations
- Contamination rate estimates are based on heuristics and manual inspection of a subset, so may not generalize.
- Performance drops are demonstrated only for specific models and datasets tested.
- No assessment of factual consistency or hallucination in generated summaries.
- Reliance on automated metrics alone, without human evaluation.

## Confidence

- Dataset contamination and its impact on model performance: **High**
- Evaluation scores are inflated due to data biases: **Medium**
- Publicly available systems are extractive and underperform: **Medium**
- Need for better data quality and evaluation protocols: **High**
