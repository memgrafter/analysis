# The Role of Baselines in Policy Gradient Optimization

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.06276
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06276
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Value baselines in stochastic NPG achieve O(1/t) almost sure convergence by reducing update aggressiveness rather than variance

## Executive Summary
This paper resolves a long-standing theoretical gap by rigorously proving why value baselines improve convergence in stochastic policy gradient optimization. While baselines are widely used in practice to reduce variance, the authors demonstrate that in the on-policy stochastic setting with softmax policies, the primary benefit comes from controlling update aggressiveness. The analysis shows that value baselines prevent the optimal action's probability from vanishing, ensuring sufficient exploration. The key insight is that without baselines, repeated sampling of sub-optimal actions causes their parameters to grow excessively, leading to premature convergence to deterministic sub-optimal policies.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes stochastic natural policy gradient (NPG) optimization with on-policy importance sampling and state value baselines for softmax policies. The method uses a learning rate schedule that adapts to the current policy and reward estimates, combined with subtracting the state value baseline from policy gradient updates. The theoretical analysis focuses on one-state MDPs and extends to general MDPs with finite state and action spaces. The approach relies on establishing a stochastic version of the Non-uniform Łojasiewicz inequality and proving that the baseline prevents optimal action probability from vanishing.

## Key Results
- Value baselines achieve O(1/t) almost sure global convergence rate for stochastic NPG with softmax policies
- The variance reduction effect of baselines is insufficient to explain their utility in this setting
- The primary mechanism is reducing update aggressiveness to maintain exploration
- Without baselines, optimal action probability decays exponentially to zero due to over-committal behavior

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The state value baseline prevents the optimal action's probability from vanishing, ensuring sufficient exploration
- Mechanism: By subtracting the state value baseline from the policy gradient estimate, the update aggressiveness is reduced. This causes the optimal action's probability to decay no faster than O(1/t), preventing premature convergence to sub-optimal deterministic policies
- Core assumption: The optimal action's probability must remain bounded away from zero for convergence to occur
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the state value baseline prevents the optimal action's probability from vanishing, thus ensuring sufficient exploration"
  - [section]: "Using Update 2 with conditions in Lemma 1 and ˆrt from Definition 1, for an arbitrary initializationθ1 ∈ RK, we have, c := inf t≥1 πθt(a∗) > 0, almost surely (a.s.)"
- Break condition: If the learning rate becomes too large relative to the reward gap, the baseline effect may be overwhelmed

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Variance reduction cannot explain the utility of baselines in this setting
- Mechanism: The policy gradient estimator variance remains unbounded with or without a baseline when the policy approaches deterministic behavior. Therefore, variance reduction alone cannot account for the convergence improvement
- Core assumption: The variance of the gradient estimator must be bounded for variance reduction arguments to apply
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "the variance of natural policy gradient estimates remains unbounded with or without a baseline"
  - [section]: "by showing that the variance of natural policy gradient estimates remains unbounded with or without a baseline, we find that variance reduction cannot explain their utility in this setting"
- Break condition: If the policy parameterization changes to something other than softmax

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The baseline reduces update aggressiveness to achieve a particular effect on long-term sampling behavior
- Mechanism: Without a baseline, repeated sampling of sub-optimal actions causes their parameters to grow as Θ(t), leading to exponential decay of their probabilities (1-πθt(a) ∈ O(e^(-c·t))). The baseline dampens this growth, limiting it to O(log t) and ensuring 1-πθt(a) ∈ Ω(1/t)
- Core assumption: The update aggressiveness must be controlled to maintain exploration
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "we find that the key role the baseline plays in ensuring global convergence is to reduce the aggressiveness of the updates"
  - [section]: "by increasing value baselines to be larger than the dominating sub-optimal action's true mean reward, such that the dominating sub-optimal action will start losing probabilities"
- Break condition: If the reward distribution changes such that the baseline becomes ineffective

  2. Estimate reward with IS
  3. Compute value baseline
  4. Apply NPG update with baseline subtraction
  5. Adjust learning rate based on current policy

  - Fixed learning rate vs adaptive learning rate
  - Computational cost of value estimation vs convergence speed

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the exact role of variance reduction in stochastic policy gradient optimization with baselines?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that variance reduction "cannot explain their utility in this setting" and shows that the variance of natural policy gradient estimates remains unbounded with or without a baseline.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that variance reduction is not the primary mechanism by which baselines improve convergence, but it does not provide a complete alternative explanation beyond the reduction of update aggressiveness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing convergence rates and final policy quality across different baseline techniques (value, state-action advantage, or learned baselines) while controlling for update aggressiveness could help clarify the relative contributions of variance reduction versus other factors.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the O(1/t) convergence rate be improved to O(e^{-ct}) for stochastic policy gradient methods with baselines?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that in the exact gradient setting, techniques like entropy regularization or normalization can achieve O(e^{-ct}) rates, but these techniques fail in the stochastic setting due to "over-committal behavior."
- Why unresolved: The paper establishes that O(1/t) is achievable with value baselines in the stochastic setting, but does not explore whether the additional techniques that work in the exact setting could be modified to work in the stochastic setting.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and analyzing new techniques that combine variance reduction, entropy regularization, or other methods with careful control of update aggressiveness in the stochastic setting could potentially achieve exponential convergence rates.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of learned value function baselines compare to using the true state values as baselines in stochastic policy gradient optimization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses true state values as baselines in its analysis and experimental results, but notes that "it would be interesting to consider the effect of estimating the value baseline."
- Why unresolved: The theoretical analysis relies on using true values, which are not available in practice, making the practical applicability of the results dependent on the quality of value function approximation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing convergence rates and final policy quality when using learned value function baselines (with different function approximation methods) versus true values would reveal the practical impact of this approximation.

## Limitations
- Theoretical analysis is limited to softmax policy parameterizations with specific reward structures
- Proof relies on stochastic Non-uniform Łojasiewicz inequality that may not extend to more complex policy classes
- Practical significance demonstrated primarily on small MDPs, raising scalability questions

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The claim that baselines reduce update aggressiveness rather than variance (supported by rigorous mathematical proof in the supplementary material)
- **Medium Confidence**: The O(1/t) convergence rate (proven theoretically but with assumptions that may be difficult to verify in practice)
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism preventing optimal action probability vanishing (theoretically established but requires careful implementation)
