# Recon: Reducing Conflicting Gradients from the Root for Multi-Task Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2302.11289
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11289
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Key outcome:** Recon reduces conflicting gradients in MTL by converting the most conflicting shared layers to task-specific ones, improving performance across multiple datasets with only slight parameter increase.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental challenge of conflicting gradients in multi-task learning, which leads to optimization difficulties and negative transfer. While existing methods attempt to manipulate gradients to mitigate conflicts, the authors discover these approaches don't effectively reduce the occurrence of conflicting gradients. Instead, they propose Recon, a simple yet effective method that identifies and converts the most conflicting shared layers into task-specific ones. By analyzing layer-wise conflict scores, Recon significantly reduces conflicting gradients and improves performance across multiple datasets and state-of-the-art methods, with only a slight increase in model parameters in many cases.

## Method Summary
Recon tackles conflicting gradients in multi-task learning by identifying shared layers where task gradients point in opposing directions and converting these layers to task-specific parameters. The method operates in two phases: first, joint training is performed to collect gradient statistics across shared layers; second, layer-wise conflict scores are computed using cosine similarity between task gradients, and the top-K most conflicting layers are converted to task-specific parameters. The modified network is then retrained from scratch. This approach differs from existing gradient manipulation methods by addressing conflicts at their source rather than attempting to fix them post-hoc.

## Key Results
- Recon significantly reduces the occurrence of conflicting gradients in shared layers across multiple datasets including Multi-Fashion+MNIST, CityScapes, NYUv2, PASCAL-Context, and CelebA
- The method achieves better multi-task performance than joint-training baselines while adding only a slight increase in model parameters
- Recon can be applied to improve various gradient manipulation methods and architecture search methods, demonstrating broad applicability
- An interesting observation is that network architecture appears to be the primary factor determining conflict layers, enabling transferability across datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Layer-wise conflicting gradients are the root cause of optimization inefficiency in MTL.
- **Mechanism:** By identifying shared layers where task gradients point in opposing directions (negative cosine similarity), and converting these into task-specific layers, the model avoids gradient conflicts at the source rather than trying to fix them post-hoc.
- **Core assumption:** Task conflicts manifest primarily at specific shared layers, not uniformly across all shared parameters.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "We investigate the task gradients w.r.t. each shared network layer, select the layers with high conflict scores, and turn them to task-specific layers."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Reducing conflicting gradients at the layer level improves optimization stability without significant model size increase.
- **Mechanism:** By selectively converting only the most conflicting shared layers to task-specific ones (rather than entire network), the method preserves parameter efficiency while resolving critical conflicts.
- **Core assumption:** A small subset of layers contributes disproportionately to gradient conflicts.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "such a simple approach can greatly reduce the occurrence of conflicting gradients in the remaining shared layers and achieve better performance, with only a slight increase in model parameters in many cases."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Once conflict layers are identified for a network architecture, they remain consistent across different MTL methods and datasets.
- **Mechanism:** The structural properties causing conflicts are architecture-dependent rather than dataset or method-dependent, enabling one-time layer identification for broad applicability.
- **Core assumption:** Network architecture determines gradient conflict patterns more than training data or optimization method.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] "An interesting observation from our experiments is that network architecture seems to be the deciding factor for the conflict layers found by Recon."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Conflict layer identifier -> Network modifier -> Training orchestrator
- **Critical path:** 1) Joint training for I iterations to collect gradient statistics, 2) Compute conflict scores for all shared layers, 3) Select top-K conflicting layers and convert to task-specific, 4) Retrain from scratch with modified architecture
- **Design tradeoffs:** More layers converted → better conflict resolution but higher parameter count; fewer iterations for conflict detection → faster but potentially noisier identification; lower S threshold → more conservative (fewer layers converted) but may miss subtle conflicts
- **Failure signatures:** No performance improvement (likely selected wrong layers or too few layers); performance degradation (may have converted critical shared layers or introduced too many parameters); slow convergence (conflict identification phase may have been too short or S threshold too high)
- **First 3 experiments:** 1) Run Recon on a simple two-task MNIST/FashionMNIST setup with ResNet18, starting with K=5 and S=-0.1, 2) Compare performance with baseline joint-training and with random layer selection, 3) Test transferability by using conflict layers found on MNIST to improve performance on a different dataset with same architecture

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** How does the severity threshold S for conflict scores interact with different network architectures and task complexities?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper mentions that S is sensitive to hyperparameters and different values lead to performance degradation, but doesn't provide a systematic analysis of this interaction.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only shows sensitivity analysis on one dataset (Multi-Fashion+MNIST) with one backbone architecture (ResNet18), leaving open how S interacts with different architectures and task complexities.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive ablation study across multiple network architectures (ResNet, SegNet, MobileNet) and task complexities showing how optimal S values vary with these factors.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** What is the relationship between the number of conflict layers K and the convergence speed of the modified network?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper discusses performance impact of K but doesn't analyze how K affects convergence speed or training dynamics.
- **Why unresolved:** While the paper shows performance changes with different K values, it doesn't measure or analyze how the choice of K affects the number of training iterations needed to converge or the stability of training.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Experiments comparing convergence curves (loss vs. iterations) for different K values on the same dataset, showing both final performance and convergence speed trade-offs.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** How transferable are the conflict layers between datasets with different domain characteristics?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper shows that conflict layers found on one dataset can be used on another dataset for some architectures, but the analysis is limited.
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only demonstrates transferability for specific architecture-dataset pairs (e.g., SegNet between CityScapes and NYUv2) without exploring the limits of this transferability or what characteristics make datasets more/less compatible.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments testing transferability across diverse dataset pairs (different domains, tasks, sizes) with quantitative measures of performance degradation/gain when using conflict layers from other datasets.

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of Recon depends heavily on correctly identifying conflict layers, but the selection criteria (K and S threshold) are not fully specified for all datasets and architectures
- The transferability claim—that conflict layers found on one dataset can improve performance on another—needs more validation across diverse architecture-task combinations
- The computational overhead of the conflict detection phase and its impact on overall training efficiency is not thoroughly discussed

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of layer-wise conflict detection and conversion is well-founded and the experimental results across multiple datasets are convincing
- **Medium confidence**: The claim about architectural determinants of conflict layers is supported by experiments but needs broader validation
- **Medium confidence**: The performance improvements over baseline methods are substantial, though the extent varies by dataset and task combination
