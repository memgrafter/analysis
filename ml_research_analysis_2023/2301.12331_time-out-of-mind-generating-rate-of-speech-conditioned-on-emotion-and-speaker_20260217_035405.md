# Time out of Mind: Generating Rate of Speech conditioned on emotion and speaker

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12331
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12331
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: GAN model conditioned on emotion and speaker generates more accurate word lengths than baseline TTS for neutral and happy speech

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of generating emotionally conditioned word-level speech rates for text-to-speech synthesis. The authors propose a GAN model conditioned on both emotion and speaker identity to generate relative word lengths for input text, which can be integrated into TTS systems via SSML. The model shows improved performance over baseline TTS on objective measures for neutral and happy speech, though it struggles with angry speech generation.

## Method Summary
The method involves training a GAN to generate relative word lengths conditioned on emotion and speaker identity. The approach uses the CREMA-D dataset with 6 emotions, 91 speakers, and 12 sentences, processing text with spaCy for POS tags and dependency parsing. Word durations are extracted using Gentle Aligner relative to neutral speech. The GAN architecture includes text, emotion, and speaker encoders feeding into an LSTM-based decoder, trained with Wasserstein loss and reconstruction loss. An IMLE variant is also explored for potentially more diverse outputs.

## Key Results
- GAN model outperforms baseline TTS on PCC and RMSE for neutral speech
- Better time alignment achieved for happy speech compared to baseline
- Model struggles with accurate word length generation for angry speech
- IMLE produces more diverse outputs but with higher error rates

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GAN conditioning on both emotion and speaker improves word length generation accuracy.
- Mechanism: Adding speaker identity to the conditioning space allows the model to learn speaker-specific prosodic patterns alongside emotion-driven variations.
- Core assumption: Speaker identity is a significant source of variance in word duration that is independent of emotion.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We were able to achieve better performances on objective measures for neutral speech, and better time alignment for happy speech when compared to an out of box model."
  - [section] "As shown in Fig. 8, there a significant variance in speech rates of different speakers and this information is missed when the model is conditioned on only emotion."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations, only empirical observations in paper.
- Break condition: If speaker-specific variance is negligible or fully explained by emotion and text features, adding speaker conditioning may not improve performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation (IMLE) generates more diverse word lengths than GAN.
- Mechanism: IMLE pulls generated samples toward real data distribution more evenly across modes, reducing mode collapse.
- Core assumption: GANs tend to overfit to dominant modes in the training distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "After 4000 epochs, the generated data from IMLE model still appears more diverse than GAN results."
  - [section] "the IMLE model has tried to learn the entire distribution, introducing more noise for all the data points"
  - [corpus] Weak - only indirect comparison within paper.
- Break condition: If training data is highly multimodal with well-separated clusters, GAN's mode specialization might be preferable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Relative word lengths to neutral speech enable better SSML integration.
- Mechanism: Encoding duration as percentage changes relative to neutral speech allows dynamic rate adjustments without requiring absolute timing calibration.
- Core assumption: Neutral speech provides a stable reference point across speakers and emotions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Using these start and end times we were able to calculate the duration of each word. To ensure that duration is independent of word length we calculated each of the word lengths relative to the neutral word in the given phrase for each of the emotions."
  - [abstract] "These word lengths are relative to neutral speech and can be provided, through speech synthesis markup language (SSML) to a text to speech (TTS) system"
  - [corpus] Weak - no external validation cited.
- Break condition: If neutral speech varies significantly across speakers or recording conditions, relative encoding loses stability.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Text encoder: 1-hot encoding → linear → bidirectional LSTM → linear → text latent code
  Emotion encoder: 1-hot → linear → emotion latent code
  Speaker encoder: 1-hot → linear → speaker latent code
  Noise encoder: Normal distribution → linear → noise latent code
  Generator decoder: Concatenate latents → LSTM → linear → word lengths
  Discriminator: LSTM → linear → real/fake classification
  Emotion prediction head: Linear → emotion classification

- Critical path:
  Text/Emotion/Speaker → Text encoder → Emotion encoder → (Speaker encoder) → Noise encoder → Concatenate → Generator decoder → Word lengths → SSML → TTS

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Relative vs absolute word durations: Relative encoding is more robust to speaker variation but requires stable neutral reference
  - GAN vs IMLE: GAN achieves lower MSE but may suffer mode collapse; IMLE produces more diverse outputs but higher error
  - Conditioning choices: Adding speaker conditioning improves accuracy but increases model complexity

- Failure signatures:
  - High MSE but good PCC: Model captures average trends but struggles with individual word timing
  - Low MSE but poor subjective quality: Overfitting to training distribution, lack of diversity
  - Mode collapse in IMLE: Insufficient noise variance or too aggressive L2 loss

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train baseline GAN without speaker conditioning on neutral speech only; measure MSE vs baseline TTS
  2. Add reconstruction loss and dynamic length handling; compare convergence speed and final MSE
  3. Switch to IMLE with pretrained encoders; measure diversity metrics and compare to GAN outputs

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How does the model's performance generalize to more diverse text and larger datasets with varying linguistic complexity?
- What is the perceptual quality difference between the GAN-generated speech and baseline TTS for each emotion?
- How do the various confounding factors (speaker, intensity, phrase) individually and interactively affect the model's ability to generate accurate word lengths?

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies solely on objective metrics without subjective listening tests to validate perceptual quality
- CREMA-D dataset is relatively small for training complex generative models, with no discussion of overfitting or data augmentation
- Model struggles specifically with angry speech generation, but the underlying cause is not investigated

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Technical implementation details of GAN and IMLE architectures are well-specified and reproducible
- **Medium Confidence**: Objective evaluation results are reported transparently, but interpretation of practical significance is limited
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about practical utility and deployment readiness are not supported by presented evidence

## Next Checks
1. Design and conduct a MUSHRA-style listening test comparing GAN-generated speech with baseline TTS across all three emotions to validate whether objective improvements translate to perceptual quality gains
2. Test trained models on an independent emotional speech dataset (such as MSP-Podcast or IEMOCAP) to assess generalization beyond CREMA-D
3. Train and evaluate models with and without speaker identity conditioning on a subset of speakers to quantify the actual contribution of speaker information versus emotion and text features alone
