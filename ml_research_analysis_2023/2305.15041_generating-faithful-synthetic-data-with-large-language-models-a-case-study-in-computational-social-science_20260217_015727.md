# Generating Faithful Synthetic Data with Large Language Models: A Case Study in Computational Social Science

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15041
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15041
- Reference count: 6
- Primary result: Grounding consistently improves classifier performance on synthetic data for sarcasm detection

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of generating faithful synthetic data using large language models (LLMs) for computational social science applications. The authors propose three strategies—grounding, filtering, and taxonomy-based generation—to improve the quality and faithfulness of synthetic data. Through experiments on sarcasm detection, they demonstrate that grounding (providing real-world examples in prompts) consistently improves downstream classifier performance, while filtering proves ineffective due to poor discriminator performance. The study highlights the importance of grounding for generating faithful synthetic data and suggests potential for cost-effective data augmentation in low-resource tasks, though even the best synthetic data underperforms compared to zero-shot ChatGPT annotation and real data.

## Method Summary
The study employs a three-strategy approach to generate faithful synthetic data using LLMs. Grounding involves providing real-world examples from training data in LLM prompts to improve topical diversity and stylistic alignment. Filtering uses a discriminator model trained to distinguish real from synthetic data to remove unfaithful samples. Taxonomy-based generation includes a taxonomy in prompts to encourage diversity by breaking down the construct into sub-types. The methods are evaluated on the SemEval-2022 Task 6 sarcasm detection dataset, with synthetic data used to fine-tune an E5-base classifier and performance measured using macro-F1 scores.

## Key Results
- Grounding consistently improved classifier performance, with grounding and rewrite achieving the highest macro-F1 score of 0.55
- Filtering was ineffective, likely due to poor discriminator performance
- Even the best synthetic data underperformed compared to zero-shot ChatGPT annotation (macro-F1 0.60) and real data
- Grounding is crucial for generating faithful synthetic data in low-resource tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Grounding improves topical alignment by injecting real-world examples
- Claim: Grounding prompts that include real-world examples from training data improve the topical diversity and stylistic alignment of generated synthetic data, leading to better downstream classifier performance.
- Mechanism: By conditioning the LLM generation on real text examples, the model is steered toward generating data that reflects the real-world distribution of topics and language styles, reducing the "synthetic" bias present in unconstrained generation.
- Core assumption: Real-world examples provided in the prompt act as anchors that bias the LLM toward producing more topically and stylistically diverse outputs.
- Break condition: If the real-world examples themselves are not representative of the target distribution, grounding may inject bias instead of reducing it.

### Mechanism 2: Taxonomy-based generation increases construct diversity
- Claim: Including a taxonomy in the prompt encourages the LLM to generate data that covers a wider range of ways a linguistic construct (e.g., sarcasm) can manifest.
- Mechanism: The taxonomy provides a structured breakdown of the construct into sub-types, and the LLM is prompted to generate examples for each, improving coverage of the construct space.
- Core assumption: The taxonomy accurately represents the diversity of the construct and that uniform sampling over its sub-types yields representative synthetic data.
- Break condition: If the taxonomy is incomplete or biased, the generated data may miss important variants of the construct.

### Mechanism 3: Filtering removes artifacts but is ineffective with weak discriminators
- Claim: Using a discriminator to filter out synthetic data that the model classifies as synthetic should improve data faithfulness, but this fails when the discriminator itself is not accurate.
- Mechanism: A classifier trained to distinguish real from synthetic data is used to remove synthetic samples that it deems unlikely to be real, ideally leaving only more faithful samples.
- Core assumption: The discriminator is sufficiently accurate to reliably identify and remove unfaithful synthetic data.
- Break condition: If the discriminator is not well-trained or the decision boundary is too strict/loose, filtering may remove good samples or leave poor ones.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Real data → Prompt construction → LLM generation → Optional discriminator filtering → Synthetic dataset → E5-base fine-tuning → Macro-F1 evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Load real data → extract examples
  2. Construct prompts (simple, grounding, taxonomy)
  3. Generate synthetic data via LLM
  4. Optionally filter with discriminator
  5. Train E5-base on synthetic data
  6. Evaluate on real test set using macro-F1

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using ChatGPT vs. open models: trade-off between generation quality and reproducibility
  - Number of rewrites vs. diversity: more rewrites may capture more variants but increase cost
  - Filtering strictness: aggressive filtering may reduce dataset size but improve quality

- Failure signatures:
  - Low believability scores indicate synthetic artifacts remain
  - Macro-F1 much lower than zero-shot baseline suggests poor data alignment
  - High variance in macro-F1 across runs may indicate unstable generation

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate synthetic data using only simple prompts; measure macro-F1 and believability.
  2. Repeat with grounding-only prompts; compare macro-F1 and believability to simple.
  3. Add taxonomy to grounding prompts; measure impact on macro-F1 and believability.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal number of synthetic examples to generate per real sample to maximize model performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that "it is necessary to generate several sentences for each individual real sample" and that "typically, the later generations capture more interesting forms of sarcasm than the initial generation and cover a broader range of topics." However, they do not specify what number is optimal.
- Why unresolved: The paper only mentions that "several" generations are needed but doesn't quantify this. The relationship between the number of generations and task complexity remains unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies varying the number of generations per real sample across different tasks and measuring resulting model performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would fine-tuning larger language models (100M+ parameters) on synthetic data yield better performance than the E5-base model used in this study?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note "A small capacity model like E5 may not be capable of capturing complex linguistic features like sarcasm" and "It may be a worthwhile effort to fine-tune on a larger model like Flan-T5."
- Why unresolved: The study only tested fine-tuning with the E5-base model. They acknowledge that sarcasm is complex and may require larger models to capture effectively.
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison of sarcasm detection performance using synthetic data with models of varying sizes (E5-base vs. Flan-T5 vs. other larger models).

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the effectiveness of grounding strategies vary across different NLP tasks and domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors found grounding to be the most effective strategy for sarcasm detection, but note their strategies are "general and can be applied for other NLP tasks."
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated these strategies on sarcasm detection. While grounding showed strong results in this domain, its effectiveness across diverse tasks remains untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of grounding and other strategies across multiple NLP tasks (sentiment analysis, named entity recognition, relation extraction, etc.) with comparison of relative performance gains.

## Limitations

- Limited to single task and dataset, reducing generalizability across domains
- Potential data leakage from using same dataset for grounding examples and evaluation
- Taxonomy not validated for completeness or accuracy
- Filtering approach failed due to poor discriminator performance with no alternative strategies explored

## Confidence

**High Confidence:** The observation that grounding consistently improves classifier performance over simple prompting is well-supported by the experimental results. The mechanism by which real-world examples in prompts improve topical alignment is logically sound and aligns with established prompt engineering principles.

**Medium Confidence:** The claim that taxonomy-based generation increases construct diversity has theoretical merit but limited empirical support in this study. While the approach is promising, the paper doesn't provide strong evidence that the generated data actually covers more diverse manifestations of sarcasm.

**Low Confidence:** The effectiveness of the filtering strategy is questionable given its poor performance in the experiments. The study doesn't adequately explain why the discriminator failed or explore alternative filtering approaches that might work better.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-dataset validation:** Test the grounding, taxonomy, and filtering strategies on multiple NLP tasks and datasets to assess generalizability and identify domain-specific limitations.

2. **Taxonomy validation:** Conduct a systematic evaluation of the taxonomy's coverage and accuracy by having human experts assess whether the generated examples truly span the intended construct space.

3. **Discriminator improvement study:** Investigate alternative discriminator architectures, training strategies, and filtering thresholds to determine if the filtering approach can be made effective with better model selection and tuning.
