# Dissecting the Effects of SGD Noise in Distinct Regimes of Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13703
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13703
- Reference count: 24
- One-line primary result: SGD noise effects on deep learning depend on training regime, with scaling laws relating temperature, dataset size, and performance

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes how stochastic gradient descent (SGD) noise affects deep neural network training in different regimes. The authors study the effects of noise magnitude T, training set size P, and initialization scale α on model performance. They find that SGD noise can be either detrimental or beneficial depending on whether the network is in the lazy training regime (large α) or feature learning regime (small α). The characteristic temperature Tc where SGD starts affecting performance scales as Tc ~ P^(-a), and several observables follow power-law scaling relationships with respect to T and P.

## Method Summary
The study uses binary classification on MNIST (even vs odd digits) and CIFAR10 (animals vs rest) with 5-layer fully-connected networks and 9-layer convolutional networks (MNAS architecture). Training uses hinge loss with margin 1/α, constant learning rate, and batch sizes chosen to be in the "noise dominated" regime. The perceptron model is used for theoretical analysis with synthetic data. Key metrics tracked include test error, training time, and weight variations across different temperature (learning rate/batch size) and initialization scale combinations.

## Key Results
- SGD noise increases weights in non-informative directions, requiring larger informative weights to fit data
- The characteristic temperature Tc where SGD affects performance scales as Tc ~ P^(-0.5) with training set size
- SGD effects differ between lazy training regime (large α) and feature learning regime (small α)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SGD noise can be detrimental or beneficial depending on the training regime.
- Mechanism: In the lazy regime (large α), SGD noise increases weights in non-informative directions, requiring larger informative weights to fit data. This delays convergence and increases total weight variation. In the feature regime (small α), noise can help escape the lazy regime by inducing feature learning.
- Core assumption: The decision boundary alignment between the model and true boundary is critical for performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SGD noise can be detrimental or instead useful depending on the training regime"
  - [section] "SGD noise increases weights in directions irrelevant to the task, implying that the correct weights must grow much larger to fit the data"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct mention of regime-dependent effects

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The characteristic temperature Tc where SGD affects performance scales as Tc ~ P^(-a) with training set size P.
- Mechanism: Larger training sets require better alignment of the model decision boundary with the true boundary. SGD noise in non-informative directions necessitates larger informative weights to fit all data points, especially those near the boundary. This increases both weight variation and training time.
- Core assumption: The hardest data points to fit are those closest to the decision boundary, and their density scales with P.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the characteristic temperature Tc where the noise of SGD starts affecting the trained model...is a power law of P"
  - [section] "both T and P increase the gradients magnitude ||∂xF (x∗)||, but only increasing P gives a better boundary alignment"
  - [corpus] Missing - no direct evidence about Tc scaling with P

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The exponents characterizing SGD effects depend on the data distribution near the decision boundary.
- Mechanism: The perceptron model shows that the asymptotic behavior of weight variation and training time with respect to P depends on the parameter χ controlling data distribution. Specifically, γ = 1/(1+χ) where χ determines how data density varies near the boundary.
- Core assumption: The statistics of data points near the decision boundary control the scaling exponents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "exponents γ and b of P for the perceptron depend on the parameter χ of the data distribution"
  - [section] "γ = 1/(1+χ), in excellent agreement with data"

## Limitations
- The analysis relies heavily on the perceptron model's ability to capture the asymptotic behavior of deep networks
- The scaling relationships are derived in the limit of large P, and their applicability to moderate dataset sizes remains uncertain
- The claim that SGD noise increases weights in irrelevant directions is primarily demonstrated through the perceptron model rather than direct observation in neural networks

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The empirical observation that SGD noise affects performance differently in lazy vs feature regimes, and that Tc scales as P^(-0.5)
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanistic explanation that SGD noise increases weights in non-informative directions based on perceptron analysis
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that these effects are universal across different network architectures and datasets
