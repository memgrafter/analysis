# Continual Causal Inference with Incremental Observational Data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01775
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01775
- Reference count: 35
- Key outcome: Novel continual causal inference method achieves comparable performance to ideal strategies with limited memory usage

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel problem of continual causal effect estimation with incrementally available observational data, which is common in industrial applications like Alipay. The authors propose three evaluation criteria: extensibility, adaptability, and accessibility. They present a Continual Causal Effect Representation Learning (CERL) method that stores limited feature representations from previous data and combines selective and balanced representation learning, feature representation distillation, and feature transformation. CERL achieves continual causal effect estimation for new data without compromising performance on original data. Experiments on News, BlogCatalog, and synthetic datasets demonstrate CERL's effectiveness, showing comparable performance to ideal strategies that have access to all previous data, while being more memory-efficient. The method remains stable against domain shifts and outperforms straightforward adaptation strategies.

## Method Summary
The CERL method addresses continual causal effect estimation by storing a limited subset of feature representations from previous datasets rather than raw data. For each new dataset, the method learns new feature representations, distills knowledge from previous models through similarity regularization, transforms old representations to align with new feature space, and balances treatment/control distributions across all domains using Wasserstein distance. The approach uses elastic net regularization for selective feature learning, cosine normalization to control variance across domains, and a herding algorithm to select representative feature representations for storage. The final model estimates individual treatment effects (ITE) and average treatment effects (ATE) by combining transformed old representations with new representations in a globally balanced space.

## Key Results
- CERL achieves comparable performance to ideal strategies (CFR-C) that have access to all previous data
- The method remains stable against domain shifts while being more memory-efficient than baseline approaches
- CERL outperforms straightforward adaptation strategies (CFR-A and CFR-B) on News, BlogCatalog, and synthetic datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Selective and balanced representation learning preserves domain-invariant causal features while eliminating selection bias between treatment and control groups.
- Mechanism: The method learns lower-dimensional feature representations using elastic net regularization for variable selection, then applies cosine normalization in the final layer to bound activation magnitudes and control variance across domains. IPM with Wasserstein distance ensures representation distributions are balanced between treatment and control groups.
- Core assumption: Feature representations can be made domain-invariant through selective learning and normalization while preserving causal information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Combining selective and balanced representation learning, feature representation distillation, and feature transformation"
  - [section III.A.1] "We adopt the IPM defined in the family of 1-Lipschitz functions, which leads to IPM being the Wasserstein distance"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found - this is the paper's novel contribution
- Break condition: When domain shifts introduce fundamentally different causal mechanisms that cannot be captured by feature transformations, or when the elastic net regularization removes causally relevant features.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Feature representation distillation transfers knowledge from previous models while preventing catastrophic forgetting.
- Mechanism: The method encourages similarity between old and new model representations using Euclidean distance (equivalently cosine similarity after normalization), allowing the new model to inherit knowledge without requiring access to original data.
- Core assumption: Representations learned from previous data remain informative for new data distributions when encouraged to be similar.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III.A.3] "we propose feature representation distillation to encourage the representation vector {gw1(x)|x ∈ D2} based on baseline model to be similar to the representation vector{gw2(x)|x ∈ D2}"
  - [abstract] "Combining selective and balanced representation learning, feature representation distillation, and feature transformation"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found - this is the paper's novel contribution
- Break condition: When domain shifts are so large that forcing similarity between old and new representations actively harms performance by propagating outdated information.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Feature transformation maps old representations into new representation space, enabling balanced global representation learning across domains.
- Mechanism: A transformation function φ1→2 maps stored old feature representations into compatibility with new feature space, then IPM balances treatment/control distributions across all domains in the transformed space.
- Core assumption: A transformation function can align representations from different domains sufficiently well to enable joint balancing and causal effect estimation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III.A.3] "we define a feature transformation function as φ1→2 :R1→ ˜R1" and "To keep the transformed space compatible with the new feature representation space, we train the transformation function φ1→2 by making theφ1→2(gw1(x)) andgw2(x) similar"
  - [abstract] "Instead of having access to all seen observational data, our method only stores a limited subset of feature representations learned from previous data"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found - this is the paper's novel contribution
- Break condition: When transformation function cannot adequately align representations due to large domain shifts, or when the transformed space loses discriminative information necessary for causal effect estimation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Baseline causal effect learning model (for first dataset) → Feature representation storage with herding algorithm → Continual causal effect learning model (for subsequent datasets) with feature distillation and transformation → Global representation balancing
- Critical path: Train baseline model → Store feature representations → For each new dataset: learn new representations, distill knowledge from old, transform old representations, balance global space, update memory
- Design tradeoffs: Memory efficiency vs. representation fidelity (limited stored representations may lose information), domain adaptation vs. stability (forcing similarity may hurt when domains differ greatly), computational complexity vs. performance (transformation and distillation add overhead)
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on new data indicates inadequate domain adaptation; poor performance on old data indicates catastrophic forgetting; unstable performance suggests hyperparameter issues with balancing terms

  2. Add feature distillation to second dataset and verify performance doesn't degrade on first dataset
  3. Add feature transformation and herding memory selection, then verify stable performance across both datasets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of CERL compare to other continual learning methods specifically designed for causal inference when applied to multiple data sources with varying degrees of domain shift?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares CERL to three adaptation strategies (CFR-A, CFR-B, CFR-C) based on the counterfactual regression model (CFR) [28]. The authors state that CERL achieves comparable performance to the ideal strategy (CFR-C) while being more memory-efficient and stable against domain shifts.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares CERL to CFR-based methods and does not explore other continual learning approaches for causal inference.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing CERL to other continual learning methods for causal inference on multiple data sources with varying degrees of domain shift.

### Open Question 2

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experiments with a large number of data sources or units per source to assess scalability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing CERL's performance as the number of data sources and units per source increases.

- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experiments varying the memory constraint to assess its impact on CERL's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing CERL's performance on new data sources with varying degrees of domain shift under different memory constraints.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation on real-world industrial datasets, with experiments conducted primarily on synthetic and semi-synthetic data
- Missing ablation studies to quantify the individual contributions of distillation, transformation, and herding components
- Unclear how the method performs when domain shifts exceed the transformation function's capacity to align representations

## Confidence

- **High**: The overall problem formulation (continual causal inference with incremental data) is novel and well-motivated by industrial applications
- **Medium**: The theoretical framework for combining selective learning, distillation, and transformation is sound
- **Low**: Empirical validation is limited to synthetic and semi-synthetic datasets without real-world industrial data, and ablation studies for individual components are absent
