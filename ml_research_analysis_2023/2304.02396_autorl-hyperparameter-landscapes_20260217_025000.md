# AutoRL Hyperparameter Landscapes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02396
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02396
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Dynamic hyperparameter landscapes in RL show significant temporal variation, with only a small fraction of configurations producing stable unimodal return distributions.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a pipeline for analyzing hyperparameter landscapes in AutoRL settings by collecting performance data at multiple training phases. The approach models landscapes using both interpolated models and Gaussian processes, then analyzes them for structure, stability, and modality. Applied to DQN on Cartpole and SAC on Hopper, the study finds that hyperparameter landscapes change significantly over time, with performance peaks shifting across hyperparameters and training phases. Only a small fraction of hyperparameter configurations produce unimodal return distributions, contrasting with benign landscapes observed in static AutoML settings.

## Method Summary
The paper develops a pipeline for studying hyperparameter landscapes in dynamic AutoRL settings. It uses Sobol sequence sampling to generate hyperparameter configurations, trains RL agents while collecting performance snapshots at multiple phases, and models landscapes using both interpolated models and Gaussian processes. The analysis includes ICE curves, modality assessment via folding tests, and IQM aggregation. The approach is applied to DQN on Cartpole and SAC on Hopper environments with 400 sampled configurations and 5 seeds each.

## Key Results
- Hyperparameter landscapes change significantly over time across training phases
- Performance peaks shift across hyperparameters and phases, indicating non-stationary optimal configurations
- Only a small fraction of hyperparameter configurations produce unimodal return distributions
- Gaussian process regressors reveal smoother landscapes showing more global patterns than interpolated models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The dynamic nature of hyperparameter landscapes in RL is caused by the non-stationary interaction between the agent and environment. As the agent explores the environment, it changes the data distribution it learns from, which in turn affects how hyperparameters influence learning dynamics. This creates phase-dependent optimal configurations. The core assumption is that non-stationarity in RL learning is significant enough to shift optimal hyperparameter values during training.

### Mechanism 2
Hyperparameter modality (unimodal vs multimodal distributions) affects the stability and effectiveness of RL learning. Configurations producing unimodal return distributions lead to more stable learning outcomes, while multimodal distributions create instability but potentially better exploration of the solution space. The modality of the return distribution for a given hyperparameter configuration is a meaningful indicator of learning stability.

### Mechanism 3
Gaussian Process regression with independent modeling of surfaces captures global patterns in hyperparameter landscapes better than interpolated models. Unlike RBF interpolation which passes through every data point, GP regression generalizes over samples and reveals smoother landscapes that show broader optimization trends. The underlying hyperparameter landscape is smooth enough that GP generalization reveals meaningful patterns rather than overfitting to noise.

## Foundational Learning

  - Why needed here: The entire RL framework is built on MDP theory, and understanding how hyperparameters interact with MDP components is crucial for landscape analysis
  - Quick check question: What are the five components of an MDP and how do they relate to the learning process?

- **Concept: Temporal Difference (TD) Learning vs Policy Gradient methods**
  - Why needed here: Different RL algorithms (DQN uses TD-learning, SAC uses policy gradient) have fundamentally different optimization dynamics that affect how hyperparameters influence learning
  - Quick check question: How do the optimization objectives differ between TD-learning and policy gradient approaches?

- **Concept: Sobol sequence sampling**
  - Why needed here: The paper uses scrambled Sobol sampling to efficiently cover the hyperparameter search space while avoiding the inefficiencies of grid search
  - Quick check question: Why is Sobol sampling preferred over random sampling for hyperparameter optimization?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Sobol sampler → RL algorithm training → return evaluation → snapshot management → Interpolated models (ILM) and Gaussian Process regressors (IGPR) → Analysis (ICE curves, modality analysis, IQM aggregation)

- **Critical path:** Sampling → Training → Evaluation → Landscape modeling → Analysis
- **Design tradeoffs:** Computational cost vs. landscape resolution (more phases = better temporal resolution but higher cost)
- **Failure signatures:** 
  - Noisy landscapes indicating insufficient samples or seeds
  - Unstable best configurations across phases
  - Poor correlation between landscape predictions and actual performance
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run the pipeline on a simple environment (Cartpole) with DQN to verify data collection and basic landscape generation
  2. Compare ILM vs IGPR models on the same data to understand modeling differences
  3. Apply modality analysis to identify stable vs unstable configurations in phase 1

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do the hyperparameter landscapes change across different RL algorithms beyond DQN and SAC? The paper only examines DQN on Cartpole and SAC on Hopper environments, leaving open whether findings generalize to other RL algorithms.

### Open Question 2
Can we develop predictive models that forecast how hyperparameter landscapes evolve throughout training? The paper observes that landscapes change significantly over time but doesn't attempt to model this temporal evolution.

### Open Question 3
How do hyperparameter modality and landscape characteristics relate to algorithm convergence stability? The paper finds that only a small fraction of configurations produce unimodal return distributions but doesn't explore implications for learning stability or convergence.

## Limitations

- The study focuses on only two environments (Cartpole, Hopper) and two algorithms (DQN, SAC), limiting generalizability
- Computational constraints limited sampling to 400 configurations with 5 seeds each, potentially missing fine-grained structure
- The modality analysis relies on the folding test of unimodality, which has statistical assumptions that may not hold for all RL return distributions

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The observation that hyperparameter landscapes change over training time is robustly supported by empirical evidence across multiple phases and algorithms.
- **Medium confidence**: The claim about modality analysis being a meaningful indicator of stability requires further validation.
- **Medium confidence**: The superiority of dynamic hyperparameter schedules over static ones is suggested but not conclusively proven.

## Next Checks

1. Replicate the landscape analysis on additional environments (e.g., HalfCheetah, Walker2d) and algorithms (e.g., PPO, TD3) to test generalizability of observed temporal dynamics.
2. Conduct ablation studies varying the number of samples and seeds to quantify the robustness of landscape features to sampling density.
3. Implement and test a simple dynamic hyperparameter scheduling approach based on landscape predictions to empirically validate whether dynamic schedules outperform static configurations in practice.
