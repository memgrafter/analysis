# GersteinLab at MEDIQA-Chat 2023: Clinical Note Summarization from Doctor-Patient Conversations through Fine-tuning and In-context Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05001
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05001
- Reference count: 15
- The team ranked fourth among all submissions in the MEDIQA-Chat 2023 Dialogue2Note shared task

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the GersteinLab's approach to the MEDIQA-Chat 2023 Dialogue2Note shared task, which focuses on generating clinical notes from doctor-patient conversations. The team employed two main strategies: fine-tuning pre-trained models (CONFIT, RoBERTa, SciBERT) and few-shot in-context learning using GPT-4. Their approach involved section classification using RoBERTa and SciBERT models, followed by clinical note generation using fine-tuned CONFIT or GPT-4 models. Despite scoring slightly lower on automated metrics, the GPT-4 in-context learning approach received higher scores in human evaluations, highlighting the limitations of automated metrics for evaluating zero-shot/few-shot LLM outputs.

## Method Summary
The GersteinLab team tackled the MEDIQA-Chat 2023 Dialogue2Note shared task using a two-pronged approach. For subtask A, they fine-tuned the CONFIT model for clinical note summarization and used RoBERTa and SciBERT for section classification. They also explored dynamic max length adjustment during generation. For subtask B, they experimented with fine-tuning OpenAI's Davinci model and employed few-shot in-context learning with GPT-4. The team used expert annotations to validate their results, finding that GPT-4's in-context learning approach outperformed other baselines in human evaluations despite lower automated metric scores.

## Key Results
- Achieved ROUGE-1 F1 score of 0.4011, BERTScore F1 of 0.7058, and BLEURT of 0.5421
- Dynamic max length approach slightly improved model performance in the summarization task
- GPT-4 in-context learning received higher human evaluation scores than fine-tuned models
- Ranked fourth among all submissions in the MEDIQA-Chat 2023 Dialogue2Note shared task

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Few-shot in-context learning with GPT-4 outperforms fine-tuned models on human evaluation despite lower automated metrics.
- Mechanism: GPT-4 uses provided examples to infer desired structure, style, and length, leveraging its larger input/output capacity to handle long conversations better than traditional fine-tuned models.
- Core assumption: Human evaluators prioritize coherence, structure, and clinical relevance over exact string overlap metrics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Additionally, we utilize expert annotations to demonstrate that the notes generated through the ICL GPT-4 are better than all other baselines."
  - [section] "Although the GPT-based model scored slightly lower on automated metrics, it received high scores in human evaluations."
- Break condition: When input/output length exceeds GPT-4's limits, or when expert evaluation standards shift to favor exact metric alignment.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Dynamic max length adjustment during generation improves summary quality compared to fixed max length.
- Mechanism: The model calculates output length as a function of input dialogue length (0.55 * dialoglen + 18), ensuring summaries scale appropriately with conversation complexity.
- Core assumption: Longer conversations require proportionally longer summaries to capture essential information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "For the CONFIT model and the CONFIT model with dynamic max length, we obtained aggregate scores of 0.5392 and 0.5497, respectively."
  - [section] "The results demonstrate that the dynamic max length approach slightly improves the overall performance of the model in the summarization task."
- Break condition: When the linear scaling formula fails to account for conversation complexity that doesn't correlate with length.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using domain-specific embeddings (SciBERT) improves section classification accuracy compared to general embeddings (RoBERTa).
- Mechanism: SciBERT's pretraining on scientific literature provides better representation of medical terminology and section categories.
- Core assumption: Medical domain knowledge improves classification of clinical note sections.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In addition to the RoBERTa classifier, we also utilized SciBERT to improve the initial classification performance."
  - [section] "By incorporating the domain-specific knowledge embedded in SciBERT, we were able to enhance the accuracy of our section classification task."
- Break condition: When classification categories become too domain-specific for general scientific pretraining to help.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input pipeline: Dialogue preprocessing → Tokenization (max 1024 tokens)
  - Classification module: RoBERTa/SciBERT → Section header prediction
  - Summarization module: CONFIT (fine-tuned) or GPT-4 (ICL) → Clinical note generation
  - Evaluation pipeline: ROUGE/BERTScore/BLEURT + human evaluation

- Critical path: Input → Section classification → Section-specific summarization → Output assembly
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fine-tuning vs. ICL: Fine-tuning offers better control but requires more data; ICL is faster but less predictable
  - Model capacity vs. inference cost: GPT-4 handles longer texts better but is more expensive than CONFIT
  - Automated vs. human evaluation: Metrics are faster but may not capture clinical quality

- Failure signatures:
  - Low classification accuracy → Incorrect section headers
  - Excessive verbosity → Model doesn't respect length constraints
  - Factual inconsistencies → Model hallucinates medical information
  - Poor human scores despite good metrics → Metrics don't capture clinical quality

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare classification accuracy between RoBERTa and SciBERT on a held-out validation set
  2. Test dynamic vs. fixed max length on a small subset to measure quality impact
  3. Run human evaluation on GPT-4 ICL vs. CONFIT outputs to validate automated metric limitations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How effective are current automated metrics for evaluating zero-shot/few-shot LLM summarization outputs in clinical note generation tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that automated metrics often do not have a strong correlation with summary quality for zero-shot models and that human evaluation becomes essential for accurate assessment.
- Why unresolved: Existing metrics were designed for supervised learning scenarios and may not capture nuances important for LLM-generated summaries in specialized domains like clinical notes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of new evaluation metrics specifically designed for zero-shot/few-shot LLM outputs, benchmarked against human expert evaluations in clinical note generation tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What prompting strategies (e.g., demonstration example selection methods like SemScore, LMScore, TLength) would optimize LLM performance for clinical note generation from doctor-patient conversations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge they did not explore different selection strategies for demonstration examples, which could potentially improve results.
- Why unresolved: The impact of different prompting strategies on LLM performance in this specific domain has not been systematically investigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of different prompting strategies and their effects on LLM-generated clinical notes, measured by both automated and human evaluation metrics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can factual inconsistency in LLM-generated clinical notes be effectively detected and mitigated?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that they observed several factual errors in summaries and reference a previous study showing LLMs exhibit attribute errors and misinterpretation errors.
- Why unresolved: Current approaches do not specifically address factual inconsistency detection or mitigation in LLM-generated clinical documentation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of methods to detect factual inconsistencies in LLM-generated clinical notes, along with techniques to improve factual accuracy through fine-tuning or post-processing.

## Limitations
- The paper lacks detailed hyperparameter specifications for fine-tuning procedures, particularly for the CONFIT model and OpenAI Davinci fine-tuning
- The exact in-context learning prompts and few-shot examples used with GPT-4 are unspecified, significantly impacting reproducibility
- The study's findings are constrained by the MEDIQA-Chat 2023 dataset size (1,201 training pairs for subtask A, 67 for subtask B), which may not capture the full complexity of real-world clinical documentation

## Confidence
- High confidence: The automated evaluation metrics (ROUGE-1 F1: 0.4011, BERTScore F1: 0.7058, BLEURT: 0.5421) and the ranking of fourth place among submissions are well-documented and verifiable.
- Medium confidence: The claim that GPT-4 ICL outperforms fine-tuned models in human evaluation is supported by the paper but lacks detailed methodology for the expert annotations, making independent verification difficult.
- Low confidence: The specific mechanisms for why dynamic max length adjustment improves performance and why SciBERT improves classification accuracy are asserted but not empirically validated against alternative approaches.

## Next Checks
