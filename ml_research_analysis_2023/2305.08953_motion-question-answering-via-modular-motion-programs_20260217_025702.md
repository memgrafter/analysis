# Motion Question Answering via Modular Motion Programs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08953
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08953
- Reference count: 40
- We propose the HumanMotionQA task to evaluate complex, multi-step reasoning abilities of models on long-form human motion sequences. We generate a dataset of question-answer pairs that require detecting motor cues in small portions of motion sequences, reasoning temporally about when events occur, and querying specific motion attributes. In addition, we propose NSPose, a neuro-symbolic method for this task that uses symbolic reasoning and a modular design to ground motion through learning motion concepts, attribute neural operators, and temporal relations. We demonstrate the suitability of NSPose for the HumanMotionQA task, outperforming all baseline methods.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HumanMotionQA, a novel task for evaluating complex reasoning on long-form human motion sequences. The authors propose NSPose, a neuro-symbolic approach that combines symbolic reasoning with modular design to ground motion understanding through learned concepts, attribute neural operators, and temporal relations. NSPose outperforms all baseline methods on the generated HumanMotionQA dataset derived from the BABEL motion capture dataset.

## Method Summary
NSPose employs a neuro-symbolic framework that integrates symbolic reasoning with neural components to answer questions about human motion sequences. The system learns motion concepts from the BABEL dataset, applies attribute neural operators to extract relevant features, and uses temporal relations to reason about event sequences. The modular design allows for interpretable reasoning while maintaining flexibility in handling various motion question types.

## Key Results
- NSPose achieves state-of-the-art performance on the HumanMotionQA task
- Outperforms all baseline methods including 2s-AGCN-MLP and 2s-AGCN-RNN
- Demonstrates effectiveness in detecting motor cues, temporal reasoning, and querying motion attributes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The neuro-symbolic approach works by combining the interpretability of symbolic reasoning with the pattern recognition capabilities of neural networks. NSPose grounds motion understanding through learned concepts that capture meaningful motion primitives, while temporal relation modules enable reasoning about event sequences. The modular design allows each component to specialize in specific aspects of the reasoning process, leading to more accurate and interpretable answers.

## Foundational Learning

**Motion Capture Data**: Human motion sequences recorded via motion capture systems
- Why needed: Provides ground truth for training and evaluating motion understanding models
- Quick check: Verify dataset contains diverse motion types and realistic temporal patterns

**Temporal Reasoning**: Understanding how events unfold over time in motion sequences
- Why needed: Many questions require identifying when specific actions occur
- Quick check: Test model on sequences with varying action durations and transitions

**Symbolic vs Neural Integration**: Combining rule-based reasoning with learned representations
- Why needed: Balances interpretability with flexibility in handling complex motion patterns
- Quick check: Evaluate performance with different ratios of symbolic vs neural components

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input Motion Sequence -> Feature Extractor -> Motion Concept Learner -> Attribute Neural Operators -> Temporal Relations Module -> Answer Generator

Critical path: Motion Feature Extraction → Concept Learning → Temporal Reasoning → Answer Generation

Design tradeoffs: The modular design provides interpretability but may limit end-to-end optimization compared to fully neural approaches. The choice between simple linear temporal relations and 1D convolutional layers represents a complexity-accuracy tradeoff.

Failure signatures: Performance degradation on sequences with complex action compositions, incorrect action boundary identification, or inability to generalize to motion patterns not present in training data.

First experiments: 1) Test on sequences with known ground truth to verify concept learning accuracy, 2) Evaluate temporal reasoning on sequences with varying action durations, 3) Perform ablation studies on modular components to quantify individual contributions.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well would NSPose generalize to real-world human motion data outside of the BABEL dataset, which may have more complex and varied compositions of actions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that BABEL contains "real-world human motion capture of many types of movements" but also notes that certain concepts often co-occur in the dataset, causing data bias. NSPose's performance on the test set suggests it can handle the complexity within BABEL, but it's unclear how it would fare with motion sequences that have significantly different characteristics or compositions.
- Why unresolved: The experiments were conducted on the BABEL-QA dataset derived from BABEL. While BABEL is described as containing real-world motion capture data, the extent to which it represents the full diversity of real-world human motion is not clear. The paper doesn't provide evidence of NSPose's performance on external datasets or more diverse motion capture data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing NSPose on a variety of external datasets containing different types of human motion, such as motion capture data from different sources, real-world videos, or even motion data from different cultures or activities not represented in BABEL. Comparing its performance on these external datasets to its performance on BABEL-QA would indicate its generalization capabilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different temporal relation functions (e.g., simple linear vs. 1D convolutional layers with dilation) on NSPose's ability to accurately identify action boundaries and perform multi-step reasoning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions two approaches for learning temporal relations: a simple linear model and a convolutional neural network with 1D convolutional layers and dilation. The results show similar overall accuracy for both methods, but the paper doesn't delve into their specific strengths and weaknesses in different scenarios or their impact on the quality of action boundary identification.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides a comparison of overall accuracy, it doesn't explore how these temporal relation functions perform in different situations, such as when dealing with complex motion sequences with many transitions or when the actions have varying durations. The paper also doesn't discuss the interpretability of the learned temporal relations or their impact on the model's ability to explain its reasoning process.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a detailed analysis of the temporal relation functions' performance on various types of motion sequences, including those with complex compositions and varying action durations. Evaluating their ability to accurately identify action boundaries and their impact on the model's reasoning process through visualizations or ablation studies. Additionally, comparing the learned temporal relations to ground truth action boundaries (if available) would provide insights into their effectiveness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does NSPose's performance compare to end-to-end methods that utilize more advanced motion feature extractors or architectures, such as transformers or other state-of-the-art models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper compares NSPose to several baseline methods, including 2s-AGCN-MLP and 2s-AGCN-RNN, which use the same motion feature extractor as NSPose. However, it doesn't explore the potential benefits of using more advanced feature extractors or architectures, such as transformers, which have shown success in various sequence modeling tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating the effectiveness of NSPose's neuro-symbolic approach and its ability to learn modular programs. While it compares NSPose to some baseline methods, it doesn't investigate the potential improvements that could be achieved by combining NSPose's neuro-symbolic framework with more advanced motion feature extractors or architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimenting with different motion feature extractors or architectures, such as transformers or other state-of-the-art models, in conjunction with NSPose's neuro-symbolic framework. Comparing the performance of these combinations to NSPose with the 2s-AGCN feature extractor would indicate the potential benefits of using more advanced models and the impact on the overall performance of the system.

## Limitations
- Dataset generation relies on predefined motion attributes and temporal relations, potentially limiting real-world diversity
- Evaluation focuses on specific question types that may not capture full breadth of human motion understanding
- Modular design's scalability to more complex motion sequences remains uncertain

## Confidence
- NSPose outperforms baselines: Medium
- Dataset-specific comparisons introduce uncertainty about broader applicability
- Modular design benefits are demonstrated but not fully quantified
