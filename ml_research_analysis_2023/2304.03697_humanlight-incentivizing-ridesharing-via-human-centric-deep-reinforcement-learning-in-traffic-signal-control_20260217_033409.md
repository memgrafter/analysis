# HumanLight: Incentivizing Ridesharing via Human-centric Deep Reinforcement Learning in Traffic Signal Control

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.03697
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03697
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HumanLight achieves 15-55% improvements in person delays and queues compared to vehicle-level controllers

## Executive Summary
HumanLight introduces a decentralized adaptive traffic signal control algorithm that optimizes people throughput at intersections using reinforcement learning with a person-level pressure-based reward function. The algorithm incorporates active vehicles—those within range of the intersection within the action interval window—to better handle multimodal traffic with varying occupancies. By rewarding high-occupancy vehicles (HOVs) with travel time savings, HumanLight achieves socially equitable green time allocation. Experiments across various network configurations and HOV adoption scenarios demonstrate significant improvements in person delays and queues compared to vehicle-level state-of-the-art controllers.

## Method Summary
HumanLight employs a deep reinforcement learning approach to traffic signal control, focusing on optimizing person throughput rather than vehicle throughput. The algorithm uses a decentralized architecture where each intersection makes independent decisions based on local observations. It introduces a person-level pressure-based reward function that accounts for vehicle occupancy, incentivizing high-occupancy vehicles (HOVs) and ridesharing. The system considers active vehicles—those within a defined range of the intersection within the action interval window—to better handle multimodal traffic. This approach allows for more efficient allocation of green time based on the number of people being served rather than just the number of vehicles.

## Key Results
- Achieves 15-55% improvements in person delays and queues compared to vehicle-level state-of-the-art controllers
- Enables regulation of HOV prioritization aggressiveness
- Achieves comparable or better vehicle-level metrics due to active vehicle consideration

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
HumanLight works by shifting the optimization objective from vehicle throughput to person throughput in traffic signal control. By incorporating vehicle occupancy into the reward function, the algorithm naturally incentivizes high-occupancy vehicles, leading to more efficient use of intersection capacity. The consideration of active vehicles within a defined range allows for better anticipation of incoming traffic and more effective green time allocation. This person-centric approach aligns traffic signal priorities with social equity goals, encouraging ridesharing and public transit use while maintaining or improving overall traffic flow.

## Foundational Learning
- **Person-level pressure-based reward function**: Rewards based on the number of people waiting at intersections rather than vehicles, needed to incentivize ridesharing and public transit; quick check: verify that HOVs receive preferential treatment in green time allocation.
- **Active vehicle consideration**: Includes vehicles within a defined range of the intersection in decision-making, needed to better anticipate and handle incoming traffic; quick check: ensure that vehicles approaching the intersection are factored into green time allocation decisions.
- **Decentralized control architecture**: Each intersection makes independent decisions based on local observations, needed for scalability and adaptability to local conditions; quick check: verify that intersections can adapt to local traffic patterns without centralized coordination.
- **Occupancy prediction**: Estimates vehicle occupancy for active vehicles, needed to accurately calculate person-level metrics; quick check: assess the accuracy of occupancy predictions and their impact on algorithm performance.
- **Reinforcement learning optimization**: Uses deep RL to learn optimal signal control policies, needed to adapt to complex, dynamic traffic patterns; quick check: evaluate the convergence and stability of the learned policies over time.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Traffic sensors -> Occupancy prediction -> Active vehicle identification -> Person-level pressure calculation -> RL agent -> Signal control output
- **Critical path**: Sensor data collection → Occupancy prediction → Active vehicle identification → Person-level pressure calculation → RL decision-making → Signal control
- **Design tradeoffs**: Balances between computational complexity (considering active vehicles) and improved traffic flow prediction accuracy
- **Failure signatures**: Inaccurate occupancy predictions leading to suboptimal green time allocation; sensor failures causing incomplete active vehicle identification
- **First experiments**: 1) Test person-level pressure calculation with varying occupancy rates, 2) Evaluate active vehicle consideration impact on green time allocation, 3) Assess RL agent performance in single intersection scenarios

## Limitations
- Performance evaluation relies on simulation environments, which may not fully capture real-world complexities
- Does not address potential scalability challenges when implementing across an entire city with heterogeneous traffic patterns
- Model's dependency on accurate occupancy predictions for active vehicles introduces a potential source of error

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The algorithmic framework and technical implementation are sound, with clear methodology and reproducible results within the simulation environment
- **Medium confidence**: The claimed performance improvements (15-55% reduction in person delays and queues) are well-supported by the experimental results, though transferability to real-world conditions remains uncertain
- **Medium confidence**: The person-level pressure-based reward function and its ability to incentivize ridesharing through traffic signal prioritization shows promise but requires further validation beyond the controlled simulation scenarios
