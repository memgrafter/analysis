# Can ChatGPT-like Generative Models Guarantee Factual Accuracy? On the Mistakes of New Generation Search Engines

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.11076
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11076
- **Reference count**: 2
- **Primary result**: Large conversational AI models like ChatGPT exhibit significant factual inaccuracies in search engine applications, with issues including fabricated numbers, incorrect personal details, and missing source citations.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates factual accuracy in large conversational AI models when deployed in search engines, specifically analyzing public demonstrations from Microsoft's new Bing and Google's Bard. The authors systematically examined numerous factual claims made by these systems across various domains including financial reporting, personal information, and business details. Their analysis revealed three main categories of factual issues: claims conflicting with reference sources, claims not existing in reference sources, and claims without any reference sources. The study demonstrates that despite the advanced capabilities of these generative models, ensuring factual grounding and transparency remains a significant challenge that requires further research and development.

## Method Summary
The researchers collected public demonstration materials from Microsoft's new Bing and Google's Bard, including video demos and social media posts containing factual claims. They systematically cross-referenced each claim with its reference source (when provided) and external web sources to identify factual discrepancies. Claims were categorized into three types: those conflicting with reference sources, those not existing in reference sources, and those without any source citations. The analysis focused on specific examples involving financial summaries, personal details about public figures, and business information to illustrate the patterns of factual errors.

## Key Results
- Analysis of Bing and Bard demonstrations revealed fabricated numbers in financial summaries that don't exist in original source documents
- Incorrect personal information was provided about public figures, including inaccurate details about Timnit Gebru's career timeline
- Business information contained errors, such as incorrect founding years and employee counts for companies like Duolingo
- Bing demonstrated better transparency by providing source citations in most cases, enabling user fact-checking

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Factual hallucinations arise because generative models produce text probabilistically rather than retrieving exact facts
- Mechanism: Models use next-token prediction from a fixed vocabulary, which can generate plausible-sounding but incorrect information
- Core assumption: Training objective is language modeling rather than fact verification or retrieval
- Evidence anchors: Abstract statement about word-picking vs. fact-copying, difficulty controlling generative outputs

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Factual errors occur when AI models fail to ground responses in provided reference sources
- Mechanism: Models may generate content that doesn't exist in reference documents or conflicts with them
- Core assumption: Models have access to sources but don't properly constrain generation to match them
- Evidence anchors: Examples of fabricated financial numbers not found in original documents

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Transparency through source citations improves trustworthiness of AI-generated answers
- Mechanism: Providing reference links allows users to independently verify claims
- Core assumption: Users will check citations and cited sources are reliable
- Evidence anchors: Observation that Bing provides reference links in most cases

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Information retrieval system -> Large language model -> Response generator -> Source citation module
- **Critical path**: Query processing -> Document retrieval -> Context formation -> Generation with grounding constraints -> Response + citations
- **Design tradeoffs**: Balance between generation quality and factual accuracy, latency vs. thorough verification, user experience vs. transparency
- **Failure signatures**: Fabricated numbers/figures, incorrect personal details, missing citations, confident but wrong statements
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement retrieval-augmented generation that constrains generation to match retrieved content
  2. Add fact-checking layer that verifies claims against source documents before presentation
  3. Create confidence scoring system that flags low-confidence responses and provides more citations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: Can we develop verifiable metrics to quantify factual accuracy improvements in conversational AI search engines? This remains unresolved because current evaluation methods are largely qualitative and based on cherry-picked examples rather than systematic, reproducible metrics.

- **Open Question 2**: How can we design AI systems that better recognize and communicate uncertainty in their responses? This is unresolved because current systems often present fabricated information with high confidence, and uncertainty quantification remains an open challenge.

- **Open Question 3**: What architectural modifications would enable conversational AI to maintain factual consistency while preserving creative generation capabilities? This remains unresolved because current models struggle to balance factual accuracy with creative generation.

## Limitations

- Analysis relies on publicly available demonstration materials rather than controlled experiments with actual AI systems
- Researchers couldn't systematically test all possible queries or verify representativeness of demonstrated responses
- Limited ability to access original reference sources if they are behind paywalls or no longer available
