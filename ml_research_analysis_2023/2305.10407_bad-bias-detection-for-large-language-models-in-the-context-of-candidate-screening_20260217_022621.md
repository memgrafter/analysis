# BAD: BiAs Detection for Large Language Models in the context of candidate screening

## Executive Summary
This study investigates social bias in large language models (LLMs) when used for candidate screening. The authors generate a dataset of 240 synthetic resumes using ChatGPT, controlling for name-based gender-ethnicity pairings while keeping resume content identical. They find significant bias in job title and area assignments, with specific patterns such as API males being over-associated with Software Engineering and white females with Marketing. The Context Awareness Test (CAT) further confirms these biases, revealing the model's tendency to make stereotypical associations when presented with limited choice options.

## Method Summary
The researchers created a dataset of 240 synthetic resumes by prompting ChatGPT to generate resumes for names correlated with specific gender-ethnicity pairings. They then analyzed the job titles and areas assigned to identify bias patterns. A Context Awareness Test (CAT) was devised to evaluate the model's level of bias by forcing binary choices between stereotypical and anti-stereotypical associations. Statistical analysis using chi-squared tests and p-values was performed to determine the significance of observed associations. Various metrics including representation scores, stereotype scores, neutral selection scores, and ICAT scores were computed to quantify the bias present in the LLMs.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT showed significant bias in job assignments, with API males over-associated with Software Engineering and white females with Marketing
- The Context Awareness Test confirmed these biases, with the model consistently making stereotypical associations when presented with limited choice options
- Statistical analysis revealed significant chi-squared values (72.3) and p-values (0.00031) for job title-ethnicity pairings, indicating non-random associations

- Mechanism: By generating resumes with identical content except for first and last names correlated with specific gender-ethnicity pairings, the study creates a controlled environment where differences in job assignments can be attributed to the names themselves.
- Core assumption: ChatGPT's resume generation is consistent across multiple prompts for the same name, and that the model's outputs are solely influenced by the provided names without other implicit biases from prompt variations.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The Context Awareness Test (CAT) reveals stereotypical associations by forcing binary choices between stereotype, anti-stereotype, and neutral options.
- Mechanism: By presenting the model with scenarios where it must choose between stereotypical and anti-stereotypical associations (with a neutral option), the test exposes the model's tendency to favor certain stereotypes when given limited choice options.
- Core assumption: The model's choice in a constrained selection reveals its underlying biases, and that the inclusion of a neutral option allows the model to demonstrate awareness of stereotypes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.3] "Inspired by [12], we devise our own version of the context awareness test (CAT), focusing primarily on the interference examples."

  - [corpus] The CAT methodology draws from established bias measurement frameworks in NLP research

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data collection pipeline: Name generation → Resume generation via ChatGPT → Data extraction
  - Analysis pipeline: Statistical testing → Context Awareness Test → Result interpretation
  - Output: Dataset creation, bias quantification metrics, ICAT scores

- Critical path: The most time-consuming and critical step is the manual extraction of data from ChatGPT-generated resumes, as each resume requires iterative prompting to obtain all necessary fields.

- Design tradeoffs: Using synthetic resumes provides controlled testing conditions but may not capture the full complexity of real-world resumes, potentially limiting the generalizability of findings.

- Failure signatures: If ChatGPT refuses to generate certain types of resumes or consistently generates placeholder values, the dataset creation process would fail. Similarly, if the model consistently refuses to answer CAT questions, the bias measurement would be incomplete.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate a small sample (10-20) of resumes for a single name pairing to test the prompting strategy and data extraction process
  2. Run the CAT test with a reduced question set (4-5 questions) on a single model to verify the scoring methodology
  3. Perform statistical tests on a subset of the data to confirm the analysis pipeline works before scaling up

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies significant bias in ChatGPT's resume generation but doesn't explore the real-world impact on hiring decisions.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on identifying and quantifying bias in generated resumes, but does not investigate how these biases affect actual hiring processes or outcomes.

## Limitations

- Dataset Representativeness: The study relies on synthetically generated resumes, which may not fully capture the complexity and diversity of real-world resumes.
- Context Awareness Test (CAT) Effectiveness: The test's effectiveness depends on the model's willingness to respond to prompts and may not capture more subtle or nuanced forms of bias.
- Statistical Significance Interpretation: While statistically significant associations are reported, the practical significance and real-world impact of these findings are not fully explored.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The methodology for generating synthetic resumes with controlled name variables is clearly specified and aligns with established bias detection approaches in NLP. The statistical analysis pipeline (chi-squared tests, p-value computation) is standard and well-documented.

**Medium Confidence**: The identification of specific bias patterns (e.g., API males associated with Software Engineering, white females with Marketing) is supported by the data analysis, but the practical significance and generalizability to real-world hiring scenarios remain uncertain due to the synthetic nature of the dataset.

**Low Confidence**: The interpretation of CAT scores as definitive measures of model bias should be viewed cautiously, as the test's design and the model's response patterns could introduce additional uncertainties not fully accounted for in the analysis.

## Next Checks

1. **Replicate with Real Resumes**: Validate the findings by analyzing a dataset of real resumes with identifiable names, controlling for other demographic information where possible. This would test whether the synthetic dataset accurately reflects real-world bias patterns.

2. **Cross-Model Comparison**: Repeat the analysis with multiple LLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, LLaMA) to determine whether the observed biases are model-specific or represent a broader trend in language model outputs for resume screening tasks.

3. **Bias Magnitude Assessment**: Calculate effect sizes (e.g., Cohen's d, odds ratios) alongside p-values to quantify the practical significance of the observed bias patterns, providing a more complete picture of their real-world impact on hiring decisions.
