# Dropout Reduces Underfitting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01500
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01500
- Reference count: 26
- Key outcome: Early dropout reduces underfitting by decreasing gradient variance across mini-batches and aligning them with full dataset gradients

## Executive Summary
This study reveals a novel application of dropout regularization, demonstrating that it can effectively mitigate underfitting when applied during the initial training phases. Contrary to dropout's traditional use for preventing overfitting, the authors show that early dropout reduces gradient variance across mini-batches and aligns them more closely with the full dataset's gradient, counteracting the stochasticity inherent in SGD. The paper introduces "early dropout" (dropout used only in initial training phases) to improve underfitting models, and "late dropout" (dropout activated later) to regularize overfitting models.

The experimental results across ImageNet and various vision tasks show that early dropout consistently improves test accuracy and lowers training loss compared to no dropout or standard dropout, while late dropout improves generalization for large models. The methods demonstrate robustness across model sizes, scheduling strategies, and downstream tasks including object detection, segmentation, and classification. This work challenges the conventional wisdom about dropout's role in training and provides practical scheduling strategies for optimizing model performance.

## Method Summary
The authors propose two novel dropout scheduling strategies: early dropout and late dropout. Early dropout applies dropout only during the initial training phases to reduce underfitting, while late dropout activates dropout later in training to regularize overfitting models. The mechanism is based on the observation that early dropout reduces gradient variance across mini-batches and aligns them with the full dataset's gradient, counteracting SGD stochasticity. The experimental validation involves training various vision models on ImageNet and related benchmarks with different dropout schedules, comparing performance against standard dropout and no dropout baselines. The study systematically evaluates the impact of these scheduling strategies across different model sizes and vision tasks.

## Key Results
- Early dropout consistently improves test accuracy and lowers training loss compared to no dropout or standard dropout schedules
- Early dropout reduces gradient variance across mini-batches and aligns them with full dataset gradients
- Late dropout improves generalization for large models when activated after initial training phases
- Methods show robustness across model sizes, scheduling strategies, and downstream tasks including object detection and segmentation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The proposed mechanism centers on gradient variance reduction. When dropout is applied early in training, it reduces the variance of gradients across different mini-batches, making them more consistent with the gradient computed from the full dataset. This alignment counteracts the stochasticity inherent in SGD, which can cause underfitting when gradients from different mini-batches point in divergent directions. By reducing this variance early, the model can converge more effectively toward better solutions. The late dropout strategy works by activating regularization after the model has already found a good solution, preventing overfitting that might occur in later training stages.

## Limitations
- Experimental scope primarily limited to vision tasks on ImageNet and related benchmarks, with unverified generalizability to other domains like NLP or reinforcement learning
- Mechanism explanation relies on empirical observations of gradient variance reduction rather than theoretical guarantees
- Computational overhead of early dropout during initial training phases is not discussed, which could be significant for large-scale models

## Confidence
High confidence: Empirical observation that early dropout improves underfitting models is well-supported across multiple vision tasks
Medium confidence: Gradient variance reduction mechanism is plausible but not definitively proven as the primary explanation
Low confidence: Generalization claims for late dropout in large models and robustness across all scheduling strategies require more extensive validation
