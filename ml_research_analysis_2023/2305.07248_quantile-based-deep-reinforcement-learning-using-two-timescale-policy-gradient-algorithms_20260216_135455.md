# Quantile-Based Deep Reinforcement Learning using Two-Timescale Policy Gradient Algorithms

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.07248
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07248
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces QPO and QPPO algorithms for quantile-based RL with theoretical convergence guarantees and experimental validation on financial and inventory tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the limitations of traditional mean-based reinforcement learning by developing algorithms that optimize quantiles of cumulative rewards. The authors introduce QPO, which uses a two-timescale stochastic approximation framework to simultaneously estimate quantiles and update policy parameters, and QPPO, which improves data efficiency through multiple parameter updates per episode. The algorithms are theoretically grounded with finite-time performance bounds and are validated on financial investment and inventory management tasks, where QPPO demonstrates superior performance in capturing tail behavior and achieving more stable policies compared to baseline methods like PPO.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two algorithms for quantile-based reinforcement learning. QPO employs a two-timescale stochastic approximation approach where quantile estimates are updated at a faster timescale than policy parameters, ensuring stable simultaneous learning. QPPO extends this by incorporating importance sampling for off-policy updates and a clipped surrogate objective adapted from PPO, enabling multiple policy updates per episode. Both algorithms optimize a specific quantile (e.g., 0.1-quantile) of cumulative rewards rather than the mean, making them suitable for risk-sensitive applications where tail performance matters.

## Key Results
- QPPO achieves more concentrated reward distributions compared to PPO in financial investment tasks
- QPPO demonstrates significantly more stable policies in inventory management scenarios
- Theoretical convergence guarantees are established for both QPO and QPPO under appropriate step-size conditions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The two-timescale stochastic approximation enables stable simultaneous learning of quantile values and policy parameters.
- Mechanism: QPO uses a faster timescale for updating quantile estimates and a slower timescale for updating policy parameters. This separation ensures that quantile estimates converge to true quantiles before policy updates significantly alter them.
- Core assumption: The step sizes satisfy βk = o(γk) so that quantile estimation dominates parameter updates temporally.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "QPO uses two coupled iterations running at different timescales for simultaneously updating quantiles and policy parameters"
  - [section] "When θk is fixed, it can be seen that (5) is essentially an SA iteration for solving the root-finding problem FR(q; θk) = α"
- Break condition: If step sizes violate βk = o(γk), the coupling becomes unstable and both recursions may diverge.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Importance sampling enables efficient off-policy updates while maintaining convergence guarantees.
- Mechanism: QPPO transforms on-policy updates into off-policy updates using importance ratios ρ(τ; θ, θ̂), allowing reuse of trajectories generated by a fixed behavior policy.
- Core assumption: The importance ratios remain bounded and the behavior policy sufficiently covers the target policy.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "the importance sampling ratio is defined as ρ(τ; θ, θ̂) = Π(τ; θ) / Π(τ; θ̂)"
  - [corpus] Weak/no direct evidence for boundedness conditions in financial/inventory examples.
- Break condition: When the behavior policy deviates significantly from the target policy, importance ratios explode and variance becomes unmanageable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Clipped surrogate objective reduces variance of quantile gradient estimates while preserving convergence.
- Mechanism: QPPO adapts the PPO clipped surrogate technique to quantile objectives, constraining the ratio term to prevent large policy updates that would increase variance.
- Core assumption: The clipped ratio maintains sufficient gradient information while bounding the variance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we adapt the technique used in PPO to our quantile-based algorithm" with clipped surrogate objective (22)
  - [abstract] "QPPO improves data efficiency by allowing multiple parameter updates per episode"
- Break condition: If clipping threshold ε is too aggressive, the algorithm may fail to make meaningful progress.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the convergence behavior of QPO change when the reward distribution is heavy-tailed or has infinite variance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper analyzes convergence under assumptions of Lipschitz continuity and bounded gradients, but does not address heavy-tailed reward distributions where these assumptions may fail.
- Why unresolved: Heavy-tailed distributions are common in real-world applications like finance and operations management, yet the theoretical analysis does not extend to this case.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis or empirical results showing convergence (or lack thereof) of QPO under heavy-tailed reward distributions.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of trajectory truncation length T0 on the finite-sample performance of QPPO in practice?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Theorem 7 and related analysis show that the error from truncation diminishes as T0 increases, but the practical trade-off between computational cost and accuracy is not explored.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides asymptotic guarantees but does not empirically investigate the optimal choice of T0 for different problem settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Numerical experiments varying T0 across different environments to identify practical guidelines for its selection.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the quantile-based objective affect the exploration-exploitation trade-off compared to mean-based RL?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows QPPO achieves better tail performance, but does not analyze how the quantile objective influences the agent's exploration strategy during learning.
- Why unresolved: Understanding the exploration dynamics under quantile optimization could provide insights into algorithm design and hyperparameter tuning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of state visitation frequencies or entropy of policies during training to characterize exploration patterns under different objectives.

## Limitations

- The experimental validation is limited to two specific domains (financial investment and inventory management) without testing on more complex benchmark environments
- The theoretical bounds provided are asymptotic, with limited demonstration of finite-time performance in practical settings
- The importance sampling approach may face practical challenges in high-dimensional state spaces where the behavior policy coverage assumption could be violated

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The theoretical framework for two-timescale stochastic approximation and its application to quantile estimation is well-established in the literature.

**Medium Confidence**: The adaptation of PPO's clipped surrogate objective to the quantile setting is conceptually sound, but the paper provides limited analysis of how this affects the variance-variance tradeoff compared to the original PPO algorithm.

**Low Confidence**: The importance sampling approach in QPPO, while theoretically justified, may face practical challenges in high-dimensional state spaces where the behavior policy coverage assumption could be violated.
