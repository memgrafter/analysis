# Stochastic Bridges as Effective Regularizers for Parameter-Efficient Tuning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17670
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17670
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Regularizing intermediate states of PETs using stochastic bridges improves performance across multiple tasks and methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel regularization approach for parameter-efficient tuning (PET) methods by leveraging stochastic bridges derived from diffusion processes. The method regularizes intermediate hidden states during fine-tuning by projecting them into a learned latent space and matching them to diffusion bridge dynamics. Two fitting techniques are proposed: one approximates transition probability density functions for efficiency, and the other fits stochastic differential equations for effectiveness. Experiments on GLUE tasks demonstrate consistent improvements across different PETs (Prompt tuning, LoRA, BitFit, Adapter), PLMs (BERT-large, Deberta-xlarge), and bridge types (Brownian, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck).

## Method Summary
The approach learns a mapping function gγ from hidden states to a latent space using pre-training corpus, then applies this mapping during PET training to compute a regularization term based on the goodness of approximation to latent stochastic bridges. Two fitting methods are proposed: PDF fitting directly approximates the transition probability density for computational efficiency, while SDE fitting approximates the underlying stochastic differential equation for stronger regularization. The regularization term is added to the PET objective as a running cost, encouraging intermediate states to follow pre-training-like dynamics during fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- PDF regularizer achieves +0.6 GLUE score improvement on average across PETs and tasks
- SDE regularizer provides stronger gains (+0.8 average improvement) but with higher computational cost
- Regularization shows particular effectiveness in few-shot settings (e.g., +1.3 on CoLA with 16-shot)
- Better-performing PETs naturally exhibit bridge-like latent dynamics without explicit regularization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Stochastic bridges act as effective regularizers by enforcing latent dynamics similar to pre-training.
- Mechanism: By projecting intermediate hidden states into a learned latent space and matching them to diffusion bridges, the method encourages PETs to follow similar state trajectories as during pre-training, providing implicit regularization on intermediate states.
- Core assumption: The intermediate hidden states for generating the same token have similar dynamics across pre-training and fine-tuning, and these dynamics can be modeled by diffusion bridges.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose to use latent stochastic bridges to regularize the intermediate states and use the regularization as the running cost of PETs"
  - [section] "the latent dynamics of intermediate hidden states for generating the same token y should be similar in both the pre-training and downstream tasks"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence; based on theoretical assumption about state dynamics
- Break condition: If the assumption about similar dynamics across tasks is violated, or if the mapping from hidden to latent space fails to capture relevant dynamics, the regularization would lose effectiveness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Two fitting methods (PDF vs SDE) offer a performance-efficiency tradeoff.
- Mechanism: The PDF fitting method directly approximates the transition probability density, which is computationally efficient but less accurate. The SDE fitting method approximates the underlying stochastic differential equation, which is more accurate but computationally heavier.
- Core assumption: The Brownian/OU bridge properties (Markov, SDE solution) can be exploited to create two distinct fitting approaches with different computational profiles.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we correspondingly propose two methods to learn the mapping: (1) fitting the Markov transition probability density function (PDF) and (2) fitting the SDE directly"
  - [section] "The two methods act as a trade-off between efficiency and effectiveness: the first method incurs only negligible computational cost and has satisfactory results, while the second one is slower but yields better regularizers"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence; based on mathematical properties of diffusion bridges
- Break condition: If the computational overhead of SDE fitting outweighs its performance gains, or if PDF fitting proves insufficient for capturing necessary dynamics, the tradeoff may not be worthwhile.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Better-performing PETs naturally exhibit bridge-like latent dynamics.
- Mechanism: When projecting the intermediate hidden states of well-trained PETs into the latent space, these states spontaneously approach the learned diffusion bridges, indicating that effective PETs inherently follow similar dynamics to the pre-training phase.
- Core assumption: There exists an intrinsic connection between PET performance and adherence to pre-training-like latent dynamics in the intermediate states.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that well-trained PETs without any regularization spontaneously exhibit stochastic-bridge-like latent dynamics"
  - [section] "the better the PETs perform, the closer the latent states are to our latent bridges"
  - [corpus] Weak - no corpus evidence; based on experimental observation of hidden state projections
- Break condition: If the correlation between PET performance and bridge proximity doesn't hold across different architectures or tasks, or if the phenomenon is specific to certain model families, the mechanism would be limited.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Pre-training corpus -> Mapping learning (gγ) -> Regularized PET training
  - gγ: 3-layer MLP mapping from hidden states to latent space
  - Regularizers: PDF-based (transition PDF approximation) and SDE-based (drift approximation)
  - PLMs: BERT-large and DeBERTa-xlarge
  - PETs: Prompt tuning, LoRA, BitFit, Adapter

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-train gγ on pre-training corpus by maximizing goodness of approximation to latent bridges
  2. Freeze gγ parameters
  3. Train PETs with added regularization term based on gγ output
  4. Evaluate on downstream tasks

- Design tradeoffs:
  - PDF vs SDE fitting: computational efficiency vs regularization strength
  - Bridge type: Brownian vs Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (minimal performance difference observed)
  - Regularization intensity α: stronger regularization can improve performance but may cause overfitting on small datasets

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance despite regularization: likely indicates gγ not properly capturing dynamics or α too high/low
  - Excessive training time with SDE: suggests computational cost outweighs benefits for the task
  - No performance difference from vanilla PET: may indicate the latent dynamics are already optimal or regularization is ineffective

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train gγ on pre-training corpus with PDF fitting method, then apply to BERT-large with LoRA on MNLI task
  2. Compare PDF vs SDE fitting methods on the same setup to observe performance-efficiency tradeoff
  3. Test few-shot performance (e.g., 16-shot) on CoLA to verify regularization benefits on small datasets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: Can more sophisticated underlying stochastic bridges (beyond Brownian and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck) yield significantly better regularizers for PETs?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note "One can expect a better performance by leveraging more sophisticated underlying stochastic bridges" and suggest this as future work.
  - Why unresolved: The paper only experiments with Brownian and OU bridges, which showed similar performance without significant differences.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of PET performance using alternative bridge types (e.g., geometric Brownian motion, fractional Brownian motion) or learned bridge dynamics would demonstrate whether more complex bridges provide measurable improvements.

- Question: How sensitive are the regularizers to the choice of endpoints for the diffusion bridges?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge they use fixed endpoints determined by PCA embeddings "for simplicity" and note that "optimizing endpoints and gγ jointly may admit a trivial solution," suggesting this is an open design choice.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative endpoint determination strategies beyond fixed PCA-based embeddings, leaving the impact of endpoint selection unexplored.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying endpoint selection methods (learned endpoints, data-driven endpoints, or learned constraints) and measuring their effect on PET performance would clarify the sensitivity.

- Question: Can the proposed regularizers be effectively extended to non-text modalities or tasks beyond GLUE?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors demonstrate effectiveness across different PETs, PLMs, and GLUE tasks, but do not explore other modalities or task types.
  - Why unresolved: The experiments are limited to text classification tasks on BERT and DeBERTa, leaving applicability to other domains unverified.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Application and evaluation of the regularizers on vision, speech, or multimodal tasks with appropriate adaptations to the hidden state dynamics would establish cross-modal generalizability.

- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the learned latent bridge dynamics and the intrinsic optimization dynamics of PETs?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that better-performing PETs spontaneously exhibit bridge-like latent dynamics, suggesting an intrinsic connection, but do not provide theoretical justification.
  - Why unresolved: While empirical evidence shows spontaneous alignment, the paper does not derive why or how the PET optimization naturally converges to bridge-like trajectories.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis linking the optimal control perspective of PETs to the properties of diffusion bridges, possibly through convergence guarantees or Lyapunov-style arguments, would explain the observed phenomenon.

## Limitations

- Limited Scope of Validation: Evaluation focuses exclusively on GLUE benchmark tasks and a small set of PET methods, with no exploration of other NLP tasks or larger PLMs beyond BERT-large and DeBERTa-xlarge.

- Computational Overhead: While PDF regularizer is computationally efficient, SDE-based method introduces significant overhead without detailed cost-benefit analysis of whether performance gains justify additional computational expense.

- Theoretical Assumptions: The method relies on assumption that intermediate hidden states during pre-training and fine-tuning follow similar dynamics that can be modeled by diffusion bridges, which is not empirically validated across diverse tasks or model architectures.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The experimental results on GLUE tasks are consistent and show clear improvements across multiple PET methods, PLMs, and bridge types. The mechanism of using latent stochastic bridges as regularizers is theoretically grounded and the empirical evidence supports the core claims.

- Medium Confidence: The performance-efficiency tradeoff between PDF and SDE fitting methods is demonstrated, but the long-term stability and scalability of these methods are not explored. The paper does not address potential issues such as overfitting in extreme cases or the behavior of the regularizers in very large-scale settings.

- Low Confidence: The claim that better-performing PETs naturally exhibit bridge-like latent dynamics is based on observed correlations rather than causal evidence. The paper does not rule out alternative explanations for this phenomenon, such as the possibility that certain PETs inherently produce smoother latent trajectories regardless of regularization.

## Next Checks
