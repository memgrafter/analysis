# GreekBART: The First Pretrained Greek Sequence-to-Sequence Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.00869
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00869
- Reference count: 15
- Primary result: GreekBART is the first monolingual sequence-to-sequence transformer model for Greek, achieving state-of-the-art performance on three out of four discriminative tasks and competitive results on summarization tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GreekBART, the first monolingual sequence-to-sequence transformer model for the Greek language. Based on the BART-base architecture and pretrained on a large-scale Greek corpus of 87.6GB, the model is evaluated on four discriminative tasks (news category classification, sentiment analysis, and natural language inference) and two generative summarization tasks using the newly created GreekSUM dataset. GreekBART achieves state-of-the-art performance on three of the four discriminative tasks and shows competitive performance on summarization tasks, even outperforming larger multilingual models in some cases. The work also demonstrates the potential of zero-shot learning with multilingual models on Greek tasks. The model, code, and dataset are made publicly available.

## Method Summary
GreekBART is based on the BART-base architecture with 6 encoder and decoder layers, 768 hidden dimensions, and 12 attention heads. It was pretrained on a 87.6GB Greek corpus consisting of Wikipedia, EuroParl, OSCAR, and Greek Web Corpus using text infilling (30% mask) and sentence permutation for 20 epochs. Fine-tuning was performed on four discriminative tasks (news category classification, Makedonia classification, sentiment analysis, and XNLI) and two summarization tasks from the GreekSUM dataset using Adam optimizer with learning rates ranging from 1e-4 to 5e-5. The model was trained using Fairseq on 2 NVIDIA V100 GPUs with 768k tokens per GPU.

## Key Results
- GreekBART achieved state-of-the-art performance on three out of four discriminative tasks (news category classification, sentiment analysis, and natural language inference)
- The model showed competitive performance on summarization tasks, outperforming BART-large models in some cases
- Zero-shot learning capabilities were demonstrated with multilingual models like mBART50 on Greek summarization tasks despite not being pretrained on modern Greek
- GreekBART exceeded the performance of Greek-BERT, which was focused on generative tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
GreekBART achieves state-of-the-art performance on three out of four discriminative tasks due to its monolingual pretraining on a large Greek corpus. The extensive pretraining data (87.6GB) from diverse sources allows the model to learn a robust Greek language model, outperforming multilingual models like XLM-R and other monolingual models like Greek-BERT on discriminative tasks.

### Mechanism 2
GreekBART shows competitive performance on summarization tasks, even outperforming larger multilingual models in some cases, due to its ability to generate high-quality sequences of text. Based on the BART architecture and fine-tuned on the GreekSUM dataset, the model effectively generates abstractive summaries.

### Mechanism 3
The potential of zero-shot learning with multilingual models on Greek tasks is demonstrated, even though mBART models are not pretrained on modern Greek. The shared vocabulary and learned representations from pretraining on 25-50 languages allow mBART models to achieve good performance on Greek summarization tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Encoder (6 layers, 768 hidden dimensions, 12 attention heads) -> Decoder (6 layers, 768 hidden dimensions, 12 attention heads) -> Normalization layers on top of both encoder and decoder -> 50K sub-words vocabulary (SentencePiece) -> Total parameters: ~181M

- Critical path: 1. Pretraining on large-scale Greek corpus, 2. Fine-tuning on discriminative tasks (classification, sentiment analysis, NLI), 3. Fine-tuning on generative tasks (text summarization)

- Design tradeoffs: Using BART-base architecture instead of BART-large to reduce computational cost while maintaining good performance; choosing a monolingual model over a multilingual model for better performance on Greek tasks; using a large and diverse pretraining corpus to improve the model's understanding of the Greek language

- Failure signatures: Poor performance on discriminative tasks (model may not have learned a robust Greek language model during pretraining, or fine-tuning may not have been effective); poor performance on generative tasks (GreekSUM dataset may not be representative, or model may not have been fine-tuned properly for summarization tasks)

- First 3 experiments: 1. Evaluate GreekBART on a held-out validation set of the GreekSUM dataset to check its performance on text summarization tasks; 2. Compare GreekBART's performance on discriminative tasks with other models (e.g., Greek-BERT, XLM-R) to validate its effectiveness; 3. Test GreekBART's ability to generate summaries for unseen documents to assess its generalization capabilities

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does GreekBART's performance on Greek language tasks compare to multilingual models when the multilingual models are fine-tuned on Greek data rather than using zero-shot learning? The study only evaluates zero-shot learning capabilities of multilingual models on Greek tasks, leaving open the question of whether fine-tuning these models on Greek data would close the performance gap with monolingual models like GreekBART.

### Open Question 2
What specific linguistic features of the Greek language (e.g., morphology, syntax) contribute to GreekBART's superior performance on discriminative tasks compared to multilingual models? While the paper demonstrates superior performance, it does not analyze which specific aspects of Greek language structure GreekBART captures better than multilingual models.

### Open Question 3
Would adding an adaptive pretraining phase on Greek data significantly improve the performance of multilingual models like mBART50 on Greek summarization tasks? This remains a theoretical suggestion without experimental validation in the current study.

## Limitations

- The Greek Web Corpus preprocessing steps are vaguely described as "manual" detection of noisy sentences without clear criteria, introducing potential variability in corpus quality
- The GreekSUM dataset, while novel, has not been independently validated for quality and representativeness, which could affect the reliability of the summarization results
- The comparison with multilingual models like mBART50 on summarization tasks is complicated by the fact that mBART50 was not pretrained on modern Greek, suggesting the evaluation conditions may not be entirely fair or comparable

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: GreekBART achieves state-of-the-art performance on three out of four discriminative tasks (news category classification, sentiment analysis, and natural language inference). The controlled comparison with established models like Greek-BERT and XLM-R strengthens this claim.

**Medium Confidence**: GreekBART shows competitive performance on summarization tasks, even outperforming larger multilingual models in some cases. While ROUGE and BERTScore metrics are provided, the novelty of the GreekSUM dataset means there's limited external validation of the evaluation quality.

**Medium Confidence**: The demonstration of zero-shot learning capabilities with multilingual models on Greek tasks is supported by the experimental results but is somewhat surprising given that mBART models were not pretrained on modern Greek.

## Next Checks

1. Conduct an independent audit of the Greek pretraining corpus quality by having multiple native Greek speakers manually sample and evaluate random subsets from each source (Wikipedia, EuroParl, OSCAR, Greek Web Corpus) for noise, relevance, and domain coverage.

2. Evaluate GreekBART on a completely held-out Greek corpus from a domain not represented in the pretraining data (e.g., scientific literature or social media Greek) to assess whether the model's strong performance on the evaluation tasks generalizes beyond the specific domains in the training corpus.

3. Repeat the summarization task comparison using mBART50 but with a controlled fine-tuning setup on GreekSUM (rather than zero-shot), and compare this to GreekBART's performance to determine whether the competitive results are due to the pretraining approach or simply the amount of fine-tuning data available.
