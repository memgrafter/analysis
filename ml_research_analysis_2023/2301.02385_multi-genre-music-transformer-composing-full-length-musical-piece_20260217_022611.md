# Multi-Genre Music Transformer -- Composing Full Length Musical Piece

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.02385
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02385
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 13
- This work implements a Multi-Genre Transformer that learns to produce music pieces through a more adaptive learning process. It uses compound word representation of musical events and builds a multi-genre compound word dataset. The linear transformer is trained on this dataset to generate full-length new musical pieces that are diverse and comparable to original tracks. The model trains 2-5 times faster than other models discussed. The Multi-Genre Transformer demonstrates that incorporating genre information allows it to generate more diverse and expressive music compared to models trained on a single genre.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Multi-Genre Transformer, a novel approach to music generation that leverages compound word representation and multi-genre training. By grouping musical tokens into compound words and training on a dataset spanning EDM, Indie, and Hip-Hop genres, the model learns to generate full-length musical pieces that are diverse and expressive. The implementation uses a linear transformer architecture with independent feed-forward heads per token type, enabling faster training while maintaining generation quality. The model demonstrates that incorporating genre information as a token type significantly enhances the diversity and expressiveness of generated music compared to single-genre approaches.

## Method Summary
The Multi-Genre Transformer uses compound word representation to model musical events as sequences, reducing sequence length and improving expressiveness. It implements a linear transformer with independent feed-forward heads for each token type (pitch, duration, chord, bar, velocity, placement, genre), trained on a multi-genre dataset of 150 musical pieces. The model uses nucleus sampling with token-specific temperature and threshold parameters for inference. Training occurs over 180 epochs with 12 self-attention layers, 8 feed-forward heads, and 512 model dimension, achieving 2-5x speedup compared to standard transformers.

## Key Results
- Multi-Genre Transformer generates full-length musical pieces diverse and comparable to original tracks
- Model trains 2-5 times faster than other models discussed
- Incorporating genre information enables generation of more diverse and expressive music compared to single-genre models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Compound word representation enables modeling of musical events as sequences, improving expressive power compared to treating tokens equally.
- Mechanism: By grouping multiple tokens (e.g., pitch, duration, chord, bar) into compound words, the model captures co-occurrence relationships and preserves musical context better than treating tokens as independent. This grouping reduces sequence length and allows attention to focus on meaningful musical events.
- Core assumption: Musical events can be meaningfully decomposed into atomic tokens that, when grouped, form coherent compound words representing actual musical progressions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Compound Word Transformer (Hsiao et al., 2021) was able to represent music generation task as a sequence generation challenge involving musical events defined by compound words. These musical events give a more accurate description of notes progression, chord change, harmony and the art factor."
  - [section 1.3]: "A musical note can be described by note’s pitch, chord, bar, duration, velocity (dynamics), placement (onset time). If we consider these as tokens we can then define music as sequence of tokens and these tokens are a part of pre-defined vocabulary. As music is multi-faceted a particular type of token can capture only a certain feature like melody, rhythm, harmony. All the neural networks until now treated these tokens as equal and thus lacked heterogeneity."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no corpus paper directly addresses compound word representation for music.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Independent feed-forward heads per token type allow scalable learning of different musical aspects (melody, harmony, genre).
- Mechanism: Each token type (e.g., note, chord, genre) has its own feed-forward head in the transformer decoder, enabling the model to learn genre-specific patterns or harmonic structures without interference from other token types. This modularity supports adding new token types (e.g., form, chord progression) easily.
- Core assumption: Different musical aspects (melody, harmony, genre) can be learned independently without significant cross-token interference, and their losses can be optimized separately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We implement a linear transformer which was trained on this dataset. We call this Multi-Genre Transformer, which was able to generate full length new musical pieces which is diverse and comparable to original tracks."
  - [section 2.2.2]: "This allows the model to scale for additional perspective (like genre, form or involving a particular chord progression) in the music by adding an additional token type. We implement our transformer model in a generic way which allows user to define its own token sampling model, token embedding model and these can be scalable for any number of token types."
  - [section 2.2]: "Having an independent feed-forward head in the Transformer Decoder allows to improve the loss of independent tokens."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no corpus paper explicitly describes independent feed-forward heads per token type for music transformers.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Linear transformer with linear attention reduces computational complexity, enabling faster training and inference while maintaining sequence modeling quality.
- Mechanism: Replacing standard quadratic attention with linear attention (via kernelized attention) reduces time and memory complexity from O(n²) to O(n), allowing the model to handle longer sequences or train faster without sacrificing the ability to model long-range dependencies.
- Core assumption: Linear attention can approximate the behavior of standard self-attention sufficiently well for music generation tasks, preserving the quality of sequence modeling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The model trains 2-5 times faster than other models discussed."
  - [section 2.2]: "We implement a linear transformer to address long sequence dependency which is a very relevant factor in music generation due to the presence of a context or a rhythm in the entire musical piece."
  - [section 2.2.2]: "Generating a new musical event or a group of tokens to be combined as a compound word at each time step is the backbone of this model, but it relies on assuming that no two musical events can occur together."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no corpus paper directly compares linear transformers to standard transformers for music generation.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Compound word representation for music
  - Why needed here: Music involves multiple simultaneous features (pitch, duration, chord, bar, dynamics). Treating each as an independent token loses the co-occurrence relationships that define musical events. Compound words preserve these relationships.
  - Quick check question: How does grouping tokens into compound words reduce sequence length and improve modeling of musical events compared to treating tokens independently?

- Concept: Transformer self-attention and its computational complexity
  - Why needed here: Music sequences are long and require modeling of temporal dependencies. Standard self-attention is O(n²), which is prohibitive for long sequences. Linear transformers reduce this to O(n).
  - Quick check question: What is the computational complexity of standard self-attention, and how does linear attention reduce it for long music sequences?

- Concept: Multi-genre dataset construction and its impact on model generalization
  - Why needed here: Training on a single genre (e.g., pop) limits the model's expressive power. A multi-genre dataset exposes the model to diverse musical structures, improving its ability to generate varied and expressive music.
  - Quick check question: How does training on a multi-genre dataset improve the model's ability to generate diverse and expressive music compared to single-genre training?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Dataset pipeline: MIDI/audio → piano roll → compound word dictionary (multi-genre)
  - Token embedding: Independent embedding per token type → concatenation → positional encoding
  - Transformer: Linear attention layers (N) → independent feed-forward heads per token type
  - Sampling: Nucleus sampling per token type with token-specific temperature and threshold
  - Loss: Independent loss per token type → averaged total loss

- Critical path:
  1. Dataset preparation (compound word conversion)
  2. Token embedding and positional encoding
  3. Linear attention computation
  4. Independent feed-forward prediction per token type
  5. Nucleus sampling for inference
  6. Loss computation and backpropagation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Linear vs. standard attention: Faster training/inference vs. potential loss of modeling quality for very long sequences
  - Independent vs. shared feed-forward heads: Modularity and scalability vs. potential loss of cross-token interactions
  - Multi-genre vs. single-genre training: Broader expressive power vs. potential dilution of genre-specific patterns

- Failure signatures:
  - Training instability: Gradient explosion or vanishing in linear attention or independent heads
  - Poor generation quality: Lack of musical coherence, repetitive patterns, or failure to capture genre/style
  - Sampling collapse: Degenerate outputs due to inappropriate temperature or threshold settings

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train on a small single-genre dataset with standard attention to verify basic functionality and generation quality.
  2. Switch to linear attention and measure speedup and any degradation in generation quality.
  3. Add a genre token with independent feed-forward head and train on multi-genre data to verify genre-aware generation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the model handle simultaneous musical events that occur at the same time step, given that it marks other tokens as [ignore] once an event of a particular token type is detected?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "But it relies on assuming that no two musical events can occur together. The new hyperedge generated by the Transformer decoder marks other tokens as [ignore] once an event of a particular token type is detected."
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this limitation but does not provide evidence or analysis of how this restriction affects the quality or diversity of generated music.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of generated music quality with and without this restriction, or demonstration of how the model could be modified to handle simultaneous events.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the addition of genre information as a token type specifically improve the expressiveness and diversity of generated music compared to single-genre models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "The Multi-Genre Transformer demonstrates that incorporating genre information allows it to generate more diverse and expressive music compared to models trained on a single genre."
- Why unresolved: While the paper claims improved diversity, it does not provide quantitative metrics or detailed analysis of how genre information influences the generated music's characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of generated music from multi-genre and single-genre models using quantitative metrics for diversity and expressiveness, or detailed qualitative analysis of genre-specific features in the output.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal threshold p and temperature parameter τ for nucleus sampling to balance between degeneration and diversity in music generation?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] "We also had each token to have a separate sampling policy by defining different threshold p and different temperature parameter τ for reshaping the probability before sampling."
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions using different thresholds and temperatures but does not provide analysis of how these parameters affect the quality or diversity of generated music.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic exploration of parameter space with corresponding evaluation of generated music quality, or ablation studies showing the impact of different sampling configurations on music characteristics.

## Limitations
- Evaluation methodology relies heavily on qualitative assessment rather than quantitative metrics
- Claims about generation quality comparable to original tracks lack systematic validation
- Linear transformer's effectiveness for music generation not rigorously validated against standard attention

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Architectural design of compound word representation and independent feed-forward heads is clearly specified and mechanistically sound
- **Medium confidence**: Multi-genre training improves diversity and expressiveness is plausible but under-supported
- **Low confidence**: Generated music is "comparable to original tracks" lacks systematic validation

## Next Checks
1. **Quantitative quality comparison**: Implement controlled experiment comparing generation quality between multi-genre transformer and single-genre baselines using metrics like BLEU, ROUGE, or music-specific metrics like groove or harmony scores.

2. **Linear attention quality retention**: Systematically evaluate impact of linear attention on generation quality by comparing multi-genre transformer with standard self-attention transformers of equivalent size and training duration.

3. **Human preference study**: Conduct blind listening test with musicians or music enthusiasts to rate generated pieces from multi-genre transformer against human-composed music and single-genre model outputs.
