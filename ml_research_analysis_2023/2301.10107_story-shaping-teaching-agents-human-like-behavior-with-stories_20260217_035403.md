# Story Shaping: Teaching Agents Human-like Behavior with Stories

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10107
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10107
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 17
- Key outcome: Story Shaping enables RL agents to learn human-like behavior from exemplar stories, improving commonsense expression while maintaining task completion.

## Executive Summary
Story Shaping is a novel technique that enables reinforcement learning agents to learn human-like behavior by using exemplar stories as guides. The approach constructs knowledge graphs from both stories and the agent's observations, generating intrinsic rewards based on their similarity. This allows agents to align their behavior with the implicit knowledge contained in stories, even when those stories reference different environments than the agent inhabits. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements in agents' expression of commonsense and social norms while maintaining or improving task completion rates.

## Method Summary
The method involves constructing knowledge graphs from exemplar stories (Story KG) using Semantic Role Labeling with VerbAtlas, and from observations (World KG) using ALBERT-QA. These graphs represent entities, relations, and actions in both the story and current environment. Intrinsic rewards are computed based on the similarity between World KG and Story KG, guiding the agent toward story-aligned behavior. The RL agent (KG-A2C) is trained using A2C with combined extrinsic rewards from task completion and intrinsic rewards from story alignment. The approach is tested across multiple text-based game environments with both human-written and automatically generated stories.

## Key Results
- Story Shaping significantly improves agents' expression of commonsense and social norm knowledge while maintaining task completion rates
- The technique enables effective role-playing of different personas using exemplar stories
- Story Shaping demonstrates robustness to automatically generated stories with missing or mismatched details

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Story Shaping allows RL agents to learn implicit procedural knowledge from exemplar stories by constructing a knowledge graph representation of both the story and the agent's current world state.
- Mechanism: The agent constructs a Story KG from the exemplar story and a World KG from its observations. It then generates intrinsic rewards based on the similarity between these two graphs, guiding the agent to align its behavior with the story's implicit world.
- Core assumption: The knowledge graph representation can effectively capture the essential objects, locations, and actions described in the story, even if the story is incomplete or references a different environment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Story Shaping infers a knowledge graph representation of the world state from observations, and also infers a knowledge graph from the exemplar story. An intrinsic reward is generated based on the similarity between the agent's inferred world state graph and the inferred story world graph."
  - [section]: "The agent rewards itself for how similar its current world state representation is from the desired representation derived from the exemplar story."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Story Shaping enables RL agents to role-play different personas by using exemplar stories that describe the desired behavior of the persona.
- Mechanism: The agent constructs a Story KG from the exemplar story that describes the persona's behavior. It then uses this Story KG to generate intrinsic rewards that guide the agent to act in accordance with the persona's characteristics.
- Core assumption: The exemplar story can effectively convey the key characteristics and behaviors of the persona, even if the story does not reference the exact environment the agent inhabits.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Experiments in text-based games show that Story Shaping significantly improves agents' expression of commonsense and social norm knowledge while maintaining task completion rates. The technique also enables agents to role-play different personas and is robust to automatically generated stories with missing or mismatched details."
  - [section]: "We train agents to play an open-ended role-playing game, using different exemplar quests to create different personas."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Story Shaping is robust to automatically generated stories with missing or mismatched details.
- Mechanism: The agent constructs a Story KG from the automatically generated story and a World KG from its observations. It then uses these graphs to generate intrinsic rewards that guide the agent's behavior, even if the story contains elements that do not exist in the agent's environment.
- Core assumption: The agent can effectively use the elements of the story that are relevant to its environment, even if other elements are missing or mismatched.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Experiments in text-based games show that Story Shaping significantly improves agents' expression of commonsense and social norm knowledge while maintaining task completion rates. The technique also enables agents to role-play different personas and is robust to automatically generated stories with missing or mismatched details."
  - [section]: "Instead, the story KG provides the key elements that the agent should encounter, and the agent receives more rewards for encountering and doing as many of them as possible."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Exemplar story provider -> Semantic Role Labeling model -> Knowledge Graph construction module -> RL agent -> Intrinsic reward calculation module -> Game environment
- Critical path:
  1. Exemplar story is provided
  2. Story KG is constructed from the exemplar story
  3. RL agent explores the game environment
  4. World KG is constructed from the agent's observations
  5. Intrinsic rewards are calculated based on the similarity between the Story KG and World KG
  6. RL agent updates its policy based on the intrinsic rewards
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a knowledge graph representation allows for flexibility in the agent's environment, but may miss some nuanced details of the story.
  - Automatically generating exemplar stories can save time and resources, but may introduce missing or mismatched details.
- Failure signatures:
  - If the agent's behavior does not align with the exemplar story, it may indicate a problem with the knowledge graph representation or the similarity metric.
  - If the agent's performance in the game environment does not improve, it may indicate a problem with the intrinsic reward calculation or the RL agent's ability to learn from the rewards.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the RL agent with Story Shaping on a simple text-based game with a human-written exemplar story.
  2. Compare the agent's performance and behavior with a baseline RL agent without Story Shaping.
  3. Test the agent's robustness by using an automatically generated exemplar story with missing or mismatched details.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of Story Shaping scale with the complexity and length of the exemplar story? Is there a point where longer or more detailed stories might hinder rather than help the agent's learning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that stories are "high-level abstractions" that leave out details, but doesn't explore the impact of story complexity on performance.
- Why unresolved: The experiments use relatively simple stories, and the paper doesn't systematically vary story length or detail to study its effect on agent performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying story length and complexity while measuring agent performance and learning efficiency would clarify this relationship.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does Story Shaping perform when the exemplar story contains contradictions or inconsistencies with the actual game environment?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that Story Shaping is robust to automatically generated stories with "missing or mismatched details," but doesn't explore contradictions.
- Why unresolved: The experiments only test for missing or mismatched details, not for direct contradictions between the story and game environment.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments introducing deliberate contradictions between exemplar stories and game environments, measuring agent performance and behavior adaptation.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of Story Shaping on the agent's exploration strategy and how does it affect the efficiency of finding optimal solutions in complex environments?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions an "exploration intrinsic reward" but doesn't deeply analyze how Story Shaping affects exploration strategies.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions exploration rewards, it doesn't provide detailed analysis of how Story Shaping influences exploration patterns or solution efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of exploration patterns with and without Story Shaping, measuring solution efficiency and path optimality in complex environments.

## Limitations
- Knowledge graph representation may miss nuanced contextual information present in natural language stories
- Results are limited to text-based games, raising questions about generalization to other domains
- Reliance on ALBERT-QA and VerbAtlas for knowledge extraction introduces potential errors in entity and relationship capture

## Confidence
**High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using knowledge graph similarity as an intrinsic reward to guide RL agents toward story-aligned behavior is technically sound and well-supported by the experimental results across multiple game environments.

**Medium Confidence**: Claims about robustness to automatically generated stories with missing or mismatched details are supported but could benefit from more systematic analysis of how different types of story imperfections affect performance.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that Story Shaping enables effective role-playing of different personas is demonstrated but relies heavily on human evaluation, which introduces subjectivity and lacks detailed methodology description.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation on knowledge graph components**: Systematically remove different elements of the knowledge graph (entities, relations, or both) to determine which components are most critical for the observed improvements, helping quantify the contribution of each aspect of the approach.

2. **Cross-domain transfer evaluation**: Test whether agents trained with Story Shaping in one text-based game can effectively apply learned behaviors to novel environments or whether the knowledge graphs successfully transfer beyond the specific training context.

3. **Detailed analysis of story imperfection tolerance**: Quantify exactly how much missing information, incorrect details, or environmental mismatches the system can tolerate before performance degrades significantly, providing clearer boundaries for practical deployment.
