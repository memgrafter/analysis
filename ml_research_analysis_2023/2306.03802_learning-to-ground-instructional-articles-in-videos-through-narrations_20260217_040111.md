# Learning to Ground Instructional Articles in Videos through Narrations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03802
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03802
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art step grounding (37.4% R@1) and narration alignment (66.5% R@1) without manual supervision

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for grounding instructional steps in narrated how-to videos without manual supervision. It leverages wikiHow articles as a source of step descriptions and uses video narrations to align steps to frames. The approach fuses two pathways: direct step-to-video alignment and indirect step-to-narration-to-video alignment. A teacher-student framework with iterative pseudo-label refinement is employed for training. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on step grounding and narration alignment tasks, and demonstrates strong zero-shot performance on CrossTask dataset.

## Method Summary
The method uses wikiHow articles to provide step descriptions and HowTo100M videos with ASR narrations. A multimodal model (VINA) encodes video frames using S3D, narrations and steps using Word2Vec, and processes them through a multimodal transformer. Two alignment pathways are used: direct step-to-video and indirect step-to-narration-to-video. The model is trained with InfoNCE loss using pseudo-labels iteratively refined through a teacher-student framework with aggressive filtering. Task-video associations are determined via narration-to-title similarity or metadata.

## Key Results
- Achieves 37.4% R@1 on HT-Step for step grounding
- Achieves 66.5% R@1 on HTM-Align for narration alignment
- Demonstrates 44.8% R@1 zero-shot performance on CrossTask dataset

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The indirect pathway (steps-to-narrations-to-video) complements the direct pathway by providing loose temporal priors for steps with matching narrations.
- Mechanism: Narrations are matched to steps using text similarity, then those narrations are aligned to video frames, providing an alternative route for grounding steps.
- Core assumption: A significant portion of steps have corresponding narrations that can be reliably matched using text similarity.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] narrations matched to steps to localize them; [section 3.2] combining steps-to-narrations and narrations-to-video alignment matrices.
- Break condition: If narrations are absent or poorly matched to steps, the indirect pathway provides no useful information.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The teacher-student framework with iterative pseudo-label refinement improves grounding accuracy by progressively filtering out unreliable pseudo-labels.
- Mechanism: Initial pseudo-labels are generated using a pre-trained model, filtered based on confidence thresholds, and used to train a student model with periodically updated teacher.
- Core assumption: Pseudo-labels contain enough reliable labels to bootstrap training, and iterative refinement can progressively improve label quality.
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.3.3] filtering out pseudo-labels with low confidence; [section 4.3.2] aggressive filtering is key to observing gains.
- Break condition: If initial pseudo-labels are too noisy or filtering threshold is too high, training may not converge.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-task training on both narration grounding and step grounding improves the quality of learned multimodal embeddings.
- Mechanism: The model is trained to align both narrations to video and steps to video simultaneously, encouraging a shared embedding space.
- Core assumption: There is significant overlap between information conveyed by narrations and steps, and joint training will improve the shared embedding space.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] fusing information from two distinct pathways; [section 3.3.1] meaningful alignments emerge during joint training.
- Break condition: If narrations and steps convey very different information, joint training may not provide benefits.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Contrastive learning with InfoNCE loss
  - Why needed here: To learn a shared embedding space where corresponding video frames, narrations, and steps are close together while non-corresponding pairs are far apart.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between the InfoNCE loss and the standard contrastive loss?

- Concept: Pseudo-labeling in semi-supervised learning
  - Why needed here: To generate training targets for steps without manual annotations by using the model's own predictions, filtered for reliability.
  - Quick check question: How does aggressive filtering of pseudo-labels help prevent error accumulation in iterative refinement?

- Concept: Multimodal transformers with self-attention
  - Why needed here: To capture interactions within and across modalities (video frames, narrations, steps) and learn contextualized representations.
  - Quick check question: How does the self-attention mechanism in the multimodal transformer help the model learn correspondences between different modalities?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Video frames → S3D encoder → Multimodal encoder → Alignment matrices → Step grounding; Narrations/steps → Word2Vec encoders → Multimodal encoder → Alignment matrices → Fused alignment
- Critical path: Video/narration/step features → Unimodal Encoders → Multimodal Encoder → Alignment Matrices → (Direct/Indirect) Step Grounding → Fused Alignment → Pseudo-Label Generation → Teacher-Student Training Loop
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using pre-trained S3D vs training from scratch: Pre-trained S3D provides better visual features but limits domain adaptation
  - Aggressive vs lenient pseudo-label filtering: Aggressive filtering improves label quality but may discard too many labels and slow training
  - Direct vs indirect pathway fusion: Fusion improves performance but adds complexity and may not always be beneficial
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on HT-Step: Could indicate issues with unimodal encoders, multimodal transformer, or pseudo-label generation
  - High variance in results: Could indicate sensitivity to hyperparameters or instability in teacher-student training loop
  - Failure to improve with narrations: Could indicate issues with indirect pathway or fusion mechanism
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate direct steps-to-video alignment alone to assess quality of unimodal encoders and direct alignment
  2. Evaluate indirect steps-to-narrations-to-video alignment alone to assess quality of text matching and indirect alignment
  3. Evaluate fused alignment with and without narrations during inference to assess fusion mechanism and importance of narrations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the model performance change when using a different video encoder (e.g., not S3D)?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses S3D [60] as the video encoder but does not explore other options.
  - Why unresolved: The authors only experiment with S3D and do not compare against other encoders like ViT or TimeSformer.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Running the same experiments with different video encoders and comparing step grounding and narration alignment performance.

- Question: What is the impact of using different language models for step-to-narration alignment?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that substituting the learned steps-to-narrations alignment with one computed using MPNet significantly degrades performance.
  - Why unresolved: The authors only test MPNet and do not explore other language models like BERT or RoBERTa.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Comparing the performance of the model using different language models for step-to-narration alignment.

- Question: How does the model perform on instructional videos from domains other than cooking and DIY?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on cooking and DIY tasks but does not evaluate the model on other domains like fitness or gardening.
  - Why unresolved: The authors only test the model on the Food & Entertainment subset of HowTo100M and CrossTask, which are limited to cooking and DIY tasks.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the model on instructional videos from other domains and comparing the performance.

## Limitations
- The method heavily relies on the presence and quality of video narrations for step grounding, with no quantitative analysis of narration coverage or quality across the dataset
- The pseudo-label generation and filtering process lacks specific hyperparameter values, which could significantly impact model performance
- The aggressive filtering strategy raises questions about potential information loss and its effect on model convergence

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using wikiHow articles as step descriptions and leveraging narrations for alignment is well-supported by results (37.4% R@1 on HT-Step, 66.5% R@1 on HTM-Align).

**Medium Confidence**: The teacher-student framework with iterative pseudo-label refinement is theoretically sound and shows promising results, but specific implementation details (exact filtering thresholds, teacher update frequency) are not fully specified.

**Low Confidence**: The generalization of the method to domains without narrations is questionable, as zero-shot performance (44.8% R@1 on CrossTask) is still on instructional videos and may not represent full range of applicable scenarios.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation on Pseudo-Label Quality**: Conduct experiments to quantify impact of pseudo-label filtering aggressiveness on model performance, measuring number of pseudo-labels retained at different thresholds and correlating with final R@1 scores on HT-Step.

2. **Narration Dependency Analysis**: Evaluate model performance on a subset of HT-Step where narrations are removed or significantly degraded to quantify model's reliance on narrations and identify potential failure modes in their absence.

3. **Cross-Domain Generalization**: Test the model on a dataset from a different domain (e.g., non-instructional videos or videos without narrations) to assess its ability to generalize beyond the instructional video domain and validate zero-shot performance claims.
