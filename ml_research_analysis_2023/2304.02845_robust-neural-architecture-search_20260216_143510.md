# Robust Neural Architecture Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02845
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02845
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Combines neural architecture search with adversarial training, achieving state-of-the-art performance with 2.65% test error and 53.67% robust accuracy under FGSM attack

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Robust Neural Architecture Search (RNAS), a novel method that integrates adversarial training into neural architecture search to produce models that balance accuracy and robustness. RNAS introduces a regularization term that maximizes the correlation between model outputs on natural and adversarial examples, effectively trading off accuracy for robustness in a controlled manner. The method includes two variants: RNAS-max using adversarial examples during search, and RNAS-uniform using noise examples to significantly reduce search cost while maintaining performance. Experiments on CIFAR-10 demonstrate that RNAS achieves state-of-the-art results, outperforming existing methods in both clean accuracy and adversarial robustness.

## Method Summary
RNAS extends the DARTS framework by incorporating adversarial training into the architecture search process. The method uses a regularization term that encourages similarity between outputs on clean and adversarial examples, thereby promoting robustness during architecture optimization. Two variants are proposed: RNAS-max, which uses true adversarial examples during search, and RNAS-uniform, which uses noise examples as a computationally efficient approximation. Both methods maintain the standard DARTS search space and strategy while adding the robustness-promoting regularization. The search process optimizes architecture parameters using Adam while network weights are trained using SGD, with PGD attacks generated for adversarial training. The key innovation is the regularization term that balances the accuracy-robustness trade-off by maximizing output correlation between natural and perturbed inputs.

## Key Results
- RNAS-max achieves 2.65% test error and 53.67% robust accuracy under FGSM attack on CIFAR-10
- RNAS-uniform achieves 2.60% test error with significantly reduced search time compared to RNAS-max
- Both methods outperform state-of-the-art approaches in balancing accuracy and robustness
- RNAS successfully demonstrates that architecture search can be effectively combined with adversarial training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
RNAS works by embedding robustness directly into the architecture search process rather than training a robust model after architecture selection. The regularization term explicitly encourages architectures that produce similar outputs on both clean and adversarial examples, which promotes both accuracy (on clean data) and robustness (against perturbations). By optimizing architectures with this constraint from the beginning, RNAS finds network structures inherently better suited to handling adversarial examples. The use of noise examples in RNAS-uniform approximates the effect of adversarial examples while dramatically reducing computational cost, making the approach practical for real-world deployment.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: DARTS search space -> Regularization term -> Architecture optimization -> Network training -> Adversarial evaluation

Critical path: Architecture search (with regularization) → Architecture selection → Network training (with adversarial examples) → Robustness evaluation

Design tradeoffs: Accuracy vs. robustness (controlled by λ), computational cost vs. robustness (RNAS-uniform vs RNAS-max), search stability vs. performance

Failure signatures: 
- High clean accuracy but low robust accuracy indicates insufficient regularization
- Poor convergence during search suggests incorrect learning rate or regularization strength
- High computational cost suggests need for RNAS-uniform variant
- Architecture collapse indicates instability in gradient-based search

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does RNAS performance compare to other NAS methods when evaluated on different datasets or tasks beyond CIFAR-10?
- Basis in paper: The paper primarily focuses on CIFAR-10 experiments and mentions achieving state-of-the-art performance on this dataset, but does not provide comprehensive evaluation across multiple datasets
- Why unresolved: The paper only presents results on CIFAR-10, limiting understanding of RNAS's generalization capabilities to other image classification tasks or domains
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments on additional datasets like ImageNet, CIFAR-100, or specialized domains (medical imaging, satellite imagery) would demonstrate RNAS's broader applicability and robustness across different tasks

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal value for the regularization coefficient λ that balances accuracy and robustness across different attack types and magnitudes?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions λ as a regularization coefficient controlling the tradeoff between robustness and accuracy, setting it to 1.0, but does not explore how different values affect performance or whether a single optimal value exists
- Why unresolved: The paper fixes λ at 1.0 without exploring its sensitivity or providing guidelines for tuning it based on specific requirements or threat models
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying λ across a range of values and testing against multiple attack types (FGSM, PGD with different steps, Carlini-Wagner) would identify optimal configurations for different security requirements

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does RNAS perform when evaluated against adaptive attacks specifically designed to exploit its search methodology or architecture characteristics?
- Basis in paper: The paper evaluates against standard attacks (FGSM, PGD-20) but does not address whether an attacker aware of RNAS's design could craft more effective attacks
- Why unresolved: The evaluation uses conventional attack methods that may not probe the specific vulnerabilities introduced by RNAS's architecture search process or its robustness-accuracy balancing mechanism
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with white-box attacks that exploit knowledge of RNAS's architecture structure, transfer attacks between RNAS-searched models, or attacks specifically targeting the correlation-based regularization would reveal potential weaknesses in the approach

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation to CIFAR-10 dataset without testing generalization to other domains or larger-scale datasets
- Fixed regularization coefficient λ=1.0 without exploring sensitivity or providing tuning guidelines
- Standard attack evaluation without adaptive attacks specifically targeting RNAS's unique architecture characteristics

## Confidence

High confidence in core claims about achieving state-of-the-art performance in balancing accuracy and robustness through RNAS method. Medium confidence regarding exact implementation details of regularization term and hyperparameter settings for noise examples. Low confidence in generalizability across different datasets and threat models due to limited evaluation scope.
