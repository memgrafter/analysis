# Active Semi-Supervised Learning by Exploring Per-Sample Uncertainty and Consistency

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.08978
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08978
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: ASSL achieves 5.3× computational efficiency compared to SSL while maintaining performance and improves over state-of-the-art AL methods by 8.11% in average accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the high labeling cost in deep learning by combining active learning (AL) and semi-supervised learning (SSL) into active semi-supervised learning (ASSL). The core challenge is that temporal instability during SSL training makes it difficult to accurately measure sample uncertainty, leading to poor performance compared to random sampling. To address this, the authors propose using exponential moving average (EMA) and upper confidence bound (UCB) techniques to stabilize uncertainty measurements, and they also measure data-inconsistency between weak and strong augmentation pairs to identify samples that introduce noise into the consistency regularization process. The acquisition function combines both uncertainty and data-inconsistency scores.

## Method Summary
The proposed ASSL method stabilizes uncertainty measurements during SSL training by applying EMA and UCB techniques to track uncertainty trajectories over time. It also introduces data-inconsistency scoring using KL divergence between weak and strong augmentations to identify samples that produce inconsistent supervisory signals. The final acquisition function combines these two metrics multiplicatively, selecting samples that are both informative for supervised learning and beneficial for consistency regularization. The method is evaluated on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and MiniImageNet using FixMatch framework with ResNet-18 or ViT-small architectures.

## Key Results
- ASSL achieves approximately 5.3× higher computational efficiency than SSL while maintaining the same performance
- ASSL outperforms state-of-the-art AL methods with an 8.11% improvement in average accuracy
- The method shows consistent improvements across multiple datasets including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and MiniImageNet

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Temporal instability in SSL causes prediction variance for unlabeled data that correlates with uncertainty scores
- Mechanism: During SSL training, unlabeled data predictions change over time due to dynamic weight updates, making single-time uncertainty measurements unreliable. EMA and UCB stabilize these measurements by tracking uncertainty trajectories and selecting samples with high expected value
- Core assumption: Uncertainty scores for unlabeled data follow temporal patterns that can be modeled with moving averages
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "temporal instability of the predicted probabilities of the unlabeled data"; [section 3.3] "uncertainties of consecutive time-stamps showed almost no correlation" and strong positive correlation between uncertainty and temporal instability
- Break condition: If unlabeled data predictions become stable (e.g., very few class changes), EMA/UCB provides no benefit over single measurements

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Data-inconsistency between weak and strong augmentations identifies samples that introduce label noise into unsupervised loss
- Mechanism: Samples with high KL divergence between weak/strong augmentations are overconfident but produce inconsistent supervisory signals. Selecting samples with both high uncertainty and high data-inconsistency improves both supervised and unsupervised loss quality
- Core assumption: High data-inconsistency correlates with samples that hurt consistency regularization
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we analyzed the effect of label noise on unsupervised learning by using weak and strong augmentation pairs to address data-inconsistency"; [section 3.5] "samples with high data-inconsistency are overly confident but the unsupervised loss based on these samples are hard to minimize"
- Break condition: If augmentation strength is too similar or data-inconsistency doesn't correlate with training difficulty, this signal becomes uninformative

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining uncertainty and data-inconsistency through multiplicative scoring captures both supervised and unsupervised learning benefits
- Mechanism: Final score = UCB(uncertainty) × UCB(data-inconsistency) ensures selected samples are both informative for supervised learning and beneficial for consistency regularization
- Core assumption: Uncertainty and data-inconsistency capture complementary aspects of sample quality
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "By considering both uncertainty and data-inconsistency, we acquired data samples that were used in the proposed ASSL method"; [section 3.5] "we define the ultimate AL score by considering both uncertainty and inconsistency"
- Break condition: If either component becomes saturated or uninformative, multiplicative combination may reduce to single-component selection

  - Why needed here: Understanding why single-time uncertainty measurements fail in ASSL context

- Concept: Exponential moving average and upper confidence bound
  - Why needed here: Core mathematical tools for stabilizing uncertainty measurements over time
  - Quick check question: How does UCB help select samples when uncertainty measurements are unstable?

- Concept: Data-inconsistency measurement using KL divergence
  - Why needed here: Quantifying how augmentation affects prediction consistency for unlabeled samples
  - Quick check question: What does high KL divergence between weak and strong augmentations indicate about a sample?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: SSL training loop with weak/strong augmentation pairs -> Uncertainty tracking (EMA + UCB) for unlabeled mini-batches -> Data-inconsistency tracking (KL divergence + EMA + UCB) -> Acquisition function combining both scores -> Labeled data update mechanism

- Critical path: 1. SSL training with consistency regularization 2. Uncertainty and data-inconsistency measurement during training 3. Score calculation using EMA and UCB 4. Sample acquisition based on combined score 5. Model update with newly labeled data

- Design tradeoffs: Training steps: Fewer steps (1% of typical SSL) vs. convergence quality; EMA rate α: Higher α = smoother estimates but slower adaptation; UCB confidence values: Higher values = more exploration but potential noise; Score combination: Multiplicative vs. additive vs. learned combination

- Failure signatures: High variance in uncertainty scores across training steps; Low correlation between uncertainty and actual sample difficulty; Data-inconsistency scores not affecting training performance; Acquisition time becoming prohibitive with large unlabeled pools

- First 3 experiments: 1. Implement EMA-only version (remove UCB) to verify stability improvement 2. Test data-inconsistency only version to measure standalone impact 3. Compare multiplicative vs. additive score combination on CIFAR-10 with small labeled set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal balance between labeling cost and training cost in ASSL across different tasks and datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "Trade-off of labeling cost and training cost" as a discussion point, noting that adjusting the balance between these costs according to each task would make the proposed method more efficient.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide specific guidance or experimental results on how to optimize this trade-off for different scenarios. It only suggests that such optimization is possible and would improve efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different ratios of labeled to unlabeled data and training steps across multiple tasks, showing the optimal configuration for each scenario.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can data-inconsistency samples be better utilized or handled in ASSL and SSL?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses that samples with high data-inconsistency are "not trainable" and create "noisy supervisory signals," but notes that further research is needed on whether to ignore, remove, or find alternative uses for these samples.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but does not explore potential solutions or their effectiveness. It leaves open questions about whether ignoring, removing, or transforming these samples would improve performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing different strategies for handling high data-inconsistency samples (ignoring, removing, transforming, or using weighted loss functions) across multiple datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the proposed ASSL method be adapted and optimized for tasks beyond image classification, such as semantic segmentation or human pose estimation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "semi-supervised learning methods based on consistency regularization has been studied continuously for many computer vision tasks, such as segmentation and human pose estimation" and suggests that applying ASSL to these methods could be beneficial.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates results on image classification tasks and does not provide evidence or methodology for adapting the approach to other computer vision tasks with different data structures and labeling costs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementation and evaluation of the ASSL framework on semantic segmentation and human pose estimation tasks, showing improved efficiency compared to existing methods.

## Limitations
- The core mechanism claims about temporal instability and data-inconsistency improving SSL performance lack direct empirical validation
- Limited evidence showing that EMA/UCB actually improves uncertainty estimates compared to simpler temporal averaging methods
- Data-inconsistency metric relies on KL divergence between augmentations without ablation studies demonstrating its effectiveness

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The computational efficiency improvement (5.3× speedup) is well-supported by the experimental setup comparing training steps
- **Medium confidence**: The 8.11% improvement over state-of-the-art AL methods is based on benchmark datasets but may not generalize to more complex domains
- **Low confidence**: The core mechanism claims about temporal instability and data-inconsistency improving SSL performance lack direct experimental validation

## Next Checks

1. **Temporal stability validation**: Run experiments comparing EMA/UCB uncertainty scoring against simple temporal averaging to quantify the actual benefit from the proposed method
2. **Data-inconsistency ablation**: Test whether high data-inconsistency samples consistently correspond to samples that hurt consistency regularization, or if the metric captures different phenomena
3. **Generalization testing**: Evaluate ASSL performance on domain-shifted datasets or with different SSL frameworks (beyond FixMatch) to assess robustness of the acquisition strategy
