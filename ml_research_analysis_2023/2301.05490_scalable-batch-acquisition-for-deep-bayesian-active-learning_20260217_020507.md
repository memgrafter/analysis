# Scalable Batch Acquisition for Deep Bayesian Active Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05490
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05490
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces Large BatchBALD, an approximation of the BatchBALD algorithm for deep Bayesian active learning that reduces computational complexity by using pairwise mutual information instead of joint entropy.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Large BatchBALD, an efficient approximation of the BatchBALD algorithm for deep Bayesian active learning. By replacing the computationally expensive joint entropy calculation with pairwise mutual information, Large BatchBALD enables scalable batch selection on large datasets. The method is evaluated across image datasets (MNIST, CIFAR, SVHN) and text datasets (AG News), demonstrating that it outperforms BALD and matches BatchBALD in accuracy while being significantly faster, especially for large batch sizes. The paper also introduces randomized extensions (PowerLBB and PowerBALD) that add diversity to the selection process.

## Method Summary
Large BatchBALD approximates the BatchBALD criterion by replacing the computationally expensive joint entropy calculation with pairwise mutual information terms. The total correlation between outputs in a batch is approximated by summing pairwise mutual information terms, which avoids the exponential computation of joint entropy. The method uses deep ensembles or MC-dropout to provide uncertainty estimates, then computes acquisition scores based on the difference between mutual information with parameters and pairwise mutual information between predictions. Randomized extensions (PowerLBB and PowerBALD) add stochasticity by raising scores to a power α, normalizing into a distribution, and sampling from it to improve batch diversity.

## Key Results
- Large BatchBALD reduces computational complexity from O(|Dpool|²·k·c) to a pairwise approximation while maintaining comparable accuracy
- PowerLBB and PowerBALD extensions improve batch diversity by adding stochastic sampling to the acquisition process
- Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR, SVHN, and AG News show Large BatchBALD outperforms BALD and matches BatchBALD in accuracy while being significantly faster
- Dolan-More curves across multiple datasets demonstrate Large BatchBALD's effectiveness compared to state-of-the-art baselines

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Large BatchBALD reduces computational complexity by approximating the BatchBALD criterion using pairwise mutual information instead of full joint entropy.
- Mechanism: The total correlation between outputs in a batch is approximated by summing pairwise mutual information terms, which avoids the exponential computation of joint entropy.
- Core assumption: Pairwise mutual information terms are sufficient to capture most of the dependence structure in the batch.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Large BatchBALD reduces computational complexity by using pairwise mutual information instead of joint entropy"
  - [section 2.3] "Using the approximation of total correlation, we obtain IBB(y1:b;θ)≈∑ᵢI(yi,θ)−∑ᵢ∑ⱼ≠ᵢI(yi;yj)"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on batch acquisition and approximations, but none explicitly mention pairwise MI approximation.
- Break condition: If higher-order dependencies (triplets or more) are critical for batch diversity, the approximation may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Power distributions (PowerLBB, PowerBALD) improve batch diversity by adding stochasticity to the acquisition function.
- Mechanism: Scores are raised to a power α > 0, normalized into a distribution, and samples are drawn from it. Lower α increases randomness, reducing redundancy.
- Core assumption: Greedy selection is suboptimal for diversity; stochastic sampling better captures diverse examples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Randomized extensions (PowerLBB and PowerBALD) further improve performance by adding diversity"
  - [section 2.5] "The magnitude of the power α... determines how much of a stochastic effect is present"
  - [corpus] Weak - stochastic sampling in active learning is mentioned, but not in the context of power distributions.
- Break condition: If α is too low, important high-scoring samples may be missed; if too high, diversity gain diminishes.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Deep ensembles and MC-dropout provide reliable uncertainty estimates that enable effective active learning.
- Mechanism: Multiple forward passes (MC-dropout) or multiple trained models (ensembles) approximate the posterior distribution over model parameters.
- Core assumption: The ensemble or MC-dropout samples accurately represent the posterior, enabling meaningful mutual information calculations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.1] "We use both MC-dropout and deep ensembles approaches... Both can be seen as instances of the general Bayesian formulation"
  - [section 3] "We compared the performance... for both ensembles and MC-dropout uncertainty estimates"
  - [corpus] Weak - uncertainty estimation is discussed, but not in the context of active learning specifically.
- Break condition: If the posterior is multimodal or highly complex, simple ensembles or MC-dropout may poorly approximate it.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data pipeline -> Model (ResNet-18/DistilBERT) -> Uncertainty estimation (MC-dropout/ensemble) -> Acquisition (BALD/LBB/Large BatchBALD/Power variants) -> Training loop

- Critical path:
  1. Forward pass to get predictions and uncertainty estimates
  2. Compute acquisition scores (BALD/LBB)
  3. Apply power distribution (if using PLBB/PBALD)
  4. Select top-scoring samples for labeling
  5. Retrain model on augmented labeled set

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pairwise MI vs joint entropy: Speed vs accuracy in diversity
  - Power α value: More randomness vs exploitation
  - MC-dropout vs ensembles: Speed vs quality of uncertainty estimates
  - Batch size: Faster learning vs higher per-batch computation

- Failure signatures:
  - Accuracy plateaus early: Possible redundancy in batches, check pairwise MI values
  - Runtime explodes with batch size: Likely computing exact joint entropy instead of approximation
  - Model uncertainty remains high: Possible poor uncertainty estimation (check dropout rate or ensemble size)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run Large BatchBALD vs BALD on MNIST with batch size 10; compare accuracy and runtime.
  2. Test PowerLBB with different α values on CIFAR-10; observe trade-off between diversity and informativeness.
  3. Compare MC-dropout vs ensemble uncertainty estimates on SVHN; check calibration and active learning performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the Large BatchBALD algorithm's performance scale with different neural network architectures beyond ResNet-18 and DistilBERT?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only tested Large BatchBALD with ResNet-18 for image datasets and DistilBERT for text data, suggesting potential architecture-specific effects.
- Why unresolved: The paper focused on specific architectures without exploring how performance might vary with deeper networks, different CNN architectures, or other transformer-based models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments comparing Large BatchBALD across multiple architectures (e.g., ResNet variants, Vision Transformers, BERT variants) on the same datasets would reveal architecture-specific scaling properties.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal power α parameter for the randomized extensions (PLBB and PBALD) across different datasets and batch sizes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the magnitude of power α determines the amount of stochasticity but does not provide systematic analysis of optimal values.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a general approach for power α without investigating how this hyperparameter affects performance across different data domains and batch sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive ablation study varying α across datasets, batch sizes, and data modalities would identify patterns for optimal parameter selection.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does Large BatchBALD perform on datasets with class imbalance compared to other acquisition functions?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses balanced initial training sets but does not explore how the algorithm behaves when class distributions are skewed.
- Why unresolved: Real-world datasets often exhibit class imbalance, and the paper's experiments don't address whether Large BatchBALD maintains its advantages in such scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments on intentionally imbalanced datasets (varying imbalance ratios) comparing Large BatchBALD with other methods would reveal its robustness to class distribution variations.

## Limitations
- The pairwise approximation may miss higher-order dependencies critical for optimal batch diversity, particularly in complex datasets
- The paper focuses primarily on classification accuracy without extensive diversity metrics or calibration analysis
- The analysis is limited to specific architectures (ResNet-18 for images, DistilBERT for text) without exploring broader architectural effects

## Confidence
- High confidence in the computational efficiency gains and runtime improvements
- Medium confidence in the approximation quality (performance matches BatchBALD in most cases but doesn't always exceed it)
- Medium confidence in the diversity improvements from Power distributions
