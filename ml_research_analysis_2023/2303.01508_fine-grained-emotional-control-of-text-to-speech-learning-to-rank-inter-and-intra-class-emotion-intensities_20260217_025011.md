# Fine-grained Emotional Control of Text-To-Speech: Learning To Rank Inter- And Intra-Class Emotion Intensities

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01508
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01508
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Proposed Rank model achieves higher emotion intensity recognition accuracy (up to 0.77) and better naturalness (MOS 3.76 ± 0.03) compared to FEC and RFTacotron baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of fine-grained emotional control in Text-To-Speech (TTS) systems by proposing a novel Rank model that learns both inter- and intra-class emotion intensity distances. The approach uses Mixup augmentation to create synthetic speech samples with controlled intensity variations, enabling the model to distinguish not only emotion types but also their intensities. The proposed method is evaluated on the EmoV-DB dataset and demonstrates superior performance in emotion intensity controllability, expressiveness, and naturalness compared to two state-of-the-art baselines.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of a Rank model that captures emotion intensity information through Mixup augmentation and a FastSpeech2 backbone enhanced with frozen intensity representations. The Rank model uses a dual-loss approach combining Mixup loss for emotion classification and rank loss for intensity ordering. During inference, manual intensity labels are mapped to pre-computed intensity representations via binning, enabling fine-grained phoneme-level control. The model is trained on EmoV-DB using 20k iterations for the Rank model and 250k iterations for FastSpeech2, achieving superior performance in emotion intensity controllability and naturalness.

## Key Results
- Emotion intensity recognition accuracy reaches up to 0.77, outperforming baseline models
- Mean Opinion Score for naturalness achieves 3.76 ± 0.03, significantly better than baselines
- Mel-Cepstral Distortion (MCD) of 4.66 dB demonstrates high audio quality
- The model shows superior performance in both objective metrics and subjective evaluations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The Mixup augmentation enables the Rank model to capture intra-class emotion intensity differences by comparing two speech samples with varying amounts of non-neutral emotion. Mixup combines neutral and non-neutral speech with different mixing ratios (λi, λj), creating augmented samples where one has stronger non-neutral emotion than the other. The Rank model learns to distinguish not just emotion class but also the intensity difference within the same class. If the mixing ratios λi and λj are too similar, the augmented samples may not provide enough intensity difference for the model to learn meaningful distinctions.

### Mechanism 2
The Rank model captures both inter-class (emotion type) and intra-class (intensity within emotion) distances through a dual-loss approach combining Mixup loss and rank loss. The model uses Lmixup to learn emotion classification (inter-class) and Lrank to learn intensity ranking (intra-class). This dual objective forces the model to distinguish both what emotion is present and how intense it is. If the intensity representations learned by the Projector cannot effectively capture the nuanced intensity differences, the rank loss may not improve performance.

### Mechanism 3
Freezing the trained Intensity Extractor and using it to generate intensity representations for FastSpeech2 during inference enables fine-grained emotion intensity control through manual intensity labels. During inference, manual intensity labels are mapped to pre-computed intensity representations by averaging representations within intensity bins (Min, Median, Max). This allows phoneme-level intensity control. If the intensity bins do not capture the continuous nature of emotion intensity well, the manual control may feel coarse or unnatural.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Mel-spectrogram → Intensity Extractor → Intensity representations → FastSpeech2 → Mel-spectrogram → Waveform synthesis
- Critical path: Speech → Intensity Extractor → Intensity representations → FastSpeech2 → Mel-spectrogram → Waveform synthesis
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Mixup vs. other augmentation: Mixup provides controlled intensity variation but may create unnatural speech samples
  - Scalar intensity vs. vector: Using scalar scores simplifies ranking but may lose nuanced intensity information
  - Frozen vs. trainable intensity extractor: Freezing ensures stable intensity representations but prevents adaptation during TTS training
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor intensity controllability: Rank model fails to distinguish intensity levels (check rank loss training)
  - Quality degradation: Intensity representations disrupt FastSpeech2 synthesis (check MCD scores)
  - Unstable training: Rank loss causes instability (check loss balance α and β)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train Rank model alone with synthetic intensity-labeled data to verify Mixup-based intensity learning works
  2. Validate that frozen Intensity Extractor produces consistent intensity representations across speakers and emotions
  3. Test phoneme-level intensity control by synthesizing speech with varying intensity labels on specific words

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well does the proposed Rank model generalize to emotions beyond the five used in the study (Amused, Angry, Disgusted, Neutral, and Sleepy)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses the EmoV-DB dataset containing four speakers and five emotions, but does not explore the model's performance on additional emotions.
- Why unresolved: The evaluation is limited to a specific dataset and set of emotions, leaving the model's performance on other emotions untested.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the model on additional emotion datasets with diverse emotions and speakers would demonstrate its generalization capabilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different weight configurations in the total loss function (Ltotal = αLmixup + βLrank) on the model's performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that α and β are set to 0.1 and 1.0 respectively, but does not explore how different configurations affect the results.
- Why unresolved: The study does not experiment with varying α and β values, leaving the optimal configuration unexplored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with different α and β values and comparing the resulting performance metrics would identify the optimal configuration.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed model perform in real-time applications compared to the baseline models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates the model's performance in terms of emotion intensity controllability, expressiveness, and naturalness, but does not assess its real-time capabilities.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on objective and subjective evaluations without considering computational efficiency or latency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Measuring the inference time and computational resources required by the model and comparing them with the baseline models would provide insights into its real-time applicability.

## Limitations
- Limited comparative analysis: Only two baseline models (FEC and RFTacotron) are compared, without exploring other relevant controllable TTS approaches
- Single dataset dependency: All experiments conducted on EmoV-DB (7 hours of speech), limiting generalizability to other datasets or languages
- Simplified intensity representation: Uses scalar intensity scores averaged within bins, which may oversimplify the continuous nature of emotion intensity

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core technical contribution: The Mixup-based Rank model architecture is clearly described and the training procedure is well-specified
- Medium confidence in the empirical claims: Results show improvements but rely heavily on subjective MOS scores with relatively small sample sizes
- Low confidence in the generalizability claims: Broad claims made without systematic ablation studies on key components
