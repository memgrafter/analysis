# Made of Steel? Learning Plausible Materials for Components in the Vehicle Repair Domain

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14745
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14745
- Reference count: 27
- One-line primary result: Novel approach to learn plausible materials for vehicle components using PLMs in a cloze task setting outperforms classic pattern-based algorithms.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel approach to learn plausible materials for vehicle components by probing pretrained language models (PLMs) in a cloze task style setting. The approach aggregates predictions from multiple cloze query templates and uses domain adaptation to boost performance. The paper finds that a distilled PLM outperforms a classic pattern-based algorithm, and that the compositionality assumption can address data sparsity for multiword expressions. The approach is evaluated on a dataset of vehicle components and materials, achieving high accuracy on both component and material levels.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a method for learning plausible materials for vehicle components using pretrained language models (PLMs) in a cloze task style setting. The approach involves constructing 504 semi-automatically generated cloze queries per component, probing PLMs with these queries, and aggregating predictions using the prevalence method. Domain adaptation is employed by fine-tuning the PLMs on small, high-quality corpora specific to the vehicle repair domain. The compositionality assumption is used to address data sparsity for multiword expressions. The approach is evaluated using expert annotation on a dataset of 7,069 vehicle components.

## Key Results
- Cloze query templates exploit semantic predictability in PLMs to retrieve plausible materials for vehicle components.
- Domain adaptation using small, high-quality corpora boosts performance over vanilla PLMs.
- The compositionality assumption allows handling data sparsity for multiword expressions in the domain-specific component dataset.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Cloze query templates exploit semantic predictability in PLMs to retrieve plausible materials for vehicle components.
- Mechanism: By constructing 504 semi-automatically generated cloze queries per component, the PLM's knowledge of distributional semantics is probed. The templates use domain-specific verb relations, noun quantifiers, and limiting contexts to guide the model toward plausible materials.
- Core assumption: PLMs encode plausible materials for components as part of their distributional semantics, and cloze queries can effectively retrieve them.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "probe Pretrained Language Models (PLMs) in a cloze task style setting"
  - [section]: "Our approach can also be applied to similarly specific domains, e.g., to learn which materials or substances are plausibly used in products in the health domain, textile industry, etc."
- Break condition: If the PLM does not encode plausible materials for the target domain, or if the cloze queries do not effectively probe the model's knowledge.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Domain adaptation using small, high-quality corpora boosts performance over vanilla PLMs.
- Mechanism: The RoBERTa and DistilRoBERTa models are fine-tuned on domain-specific corpora (DOMAIN and WIKI) to adapt their knowledge to the vehicle repair domain. This adaptation improves the models' ability to predict plausible materials for vehicle components.
- Core assumption: Fine-tuning on domain-specific corpora can effectively adapt PLMs to the target domain and improve their performance on domain-specific tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "domain-adaptation using either a small, high-quality or a customized Wikipedia corpus boosts performance"
  - [section]: "Domain adaptation of a given model results in a clear increase of plausible materials for items within a given domain"
- Break condition: If the domain-specific corpora are not representative of the target domain or if the fine-tuning process does not effectively adapt the PLMs.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The compositionality assumption allows handling data sparsity for multiword expressions (MWEs) in the domain-specific component dataset.
- Mechanism: Given that 98% of the domain-specific components are MWEs, the approach assumes that many domain-specific compounds are compositional. This allows the model to fall back to information regarding the compound's head (the right-most simplex noun) to improve predictions in the face of data sparsity.
- Core assumption: Many domain-specific compounds are compositional, and their meanings can be predicted based on the meanings of their constituents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "given that 98% of our domain-specific components are multiword expressions, we successfully exploit the compositionality assumption as a way to address data sparsity"
  - [section]: "We tackle the challenge of handling a dataset with a vast majority of multiword expressions... We address the corresponding severe data sparsity by assuming that many domain-specific compounds are compositional"
- Break condition: If the domain-specific compounds are not compositional or if the compositionality assumption does not hold for a significant portion of the dataset.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Cloze query template construction -> PLM probing and prediction aggregation -> Domain adaptation (fine-tuning) -> Evaluation with expert annotators

- Critical path:
  1. Construct cloze query templates for each vehicle component.
  2. Probe the PLM with the templates and aggregate predictions.
  3. Fine-tune the PLM on domain-specific corpora (optional).
  4. Evaluate the predictions with expert annotators.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a larger pool of predictions for aggregation vs. a smaller pool (tradeoff between computational cost and prediction quality).
  - Fine-tuning the PLM on domain-specific corpora vs. using the vanilla PLM (tradeoff between performance and resource requirements).

- Failure signatures:
  - Low accuracy on component or material level.
  - Predictions that are not plausible materials or are out-of-domain.
  - High disagreement among expert annotators.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Probe the vanilla PLM with the constructed cloze query templates and aggregate predictions using the prevalence method.
  2. Fine-tune the PLM on the DOMAIN corpus and compare performance with the vanilla PLM.
  3. Fine-tune the PLM on the WIKI corpus and compare performance with the vanilla and DOMAIN-adapted PLMs.

## Limitations
- The approach relies on the quality and representativeness of the pretraining corpus and the domain-specific corpora used for fine-tuning.
- The compositionality assumption may not hold for all domain-specific compounds, potentially limiting the approach's applicability in certain domains.
- The generalizability of the approach to other domains is uncertain, as the study focuses specifically on the vehicle repair domain.

## Confidence
- High: The effectiveness of the cloze task approach and the benefits of domain adaptation are well-supported by the results.
- Medium: The generalizability of the approach to other domains is uncertain, as the study focuses specifically on the vehicle repair domain.
