# Quantifying lottery tickets under label noise: accuracy, calibration, and complexity

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12190
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12190
- Reference count: 40
- The paper investigates lottery tickets under label noise, focusing on accuracy, calibration, and complexity.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores how iterative magnitude pruning (IMP) behaves under label noise conditions, examining the resulting networks' accuracy, calibration, and complexity. The authors demonstrate that IMP consistently converges to networks of comparable sizes regardless of starting model scale, and these pruned networks show better uncertainty calibration than their full counterparts. The study introduces the concept of effective number of parameters (N_eff) as a measure of task difficulty and shows that pruned models provide more reliable probability estimates for label distributions.

## Method Summary
The authors conduct experiments using iterative magnitude pruning on various neural network architectures across synthetic and real datasets with controlled label noise. They measure network performance using standard accuracy metrics while also evaluating calibration through expected calibration error and other reliability measures. The effective number of parameters is calculated for pruned networks to quantify their complexity relative to the original full models. The study compares pruned models' ability to capture true conditional probability distributions against full models using both synthetic data with known ground truth and real-world datasets.

## Key Results
- IMP converges to networks of comparable sizes across different initial model scales for a given task
- Effective number of parameters (N_eff) in pruned models correlates with task difficulty
- Pruned models better capture true conditional probability distributions and show improved calibration compared to full models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's findings suggest that pruning removes redundant parameters while preserving the essential structure needed for the task, leading to networks that are both more efficient and better calibrated. The convergence to similar network sizes across different initial scales indicates an intrinsic property of the classification task that determines the minimum complexity needed. Improved calibration likely results from the reduced model capacity preventing overconfident predictions on noisy labels.

## Limitations
- The correlation between N_eff and task difficulty is primarily established through synthetic experiments and may not generalize to all real-world scenarios
- Calibration metrics used may not fully capture uncertainty quality, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios
- The findings may be specific to iterative magnitude pruning and not generalize to other pruning methods

## Confidence

High confidence: IMP consistently converges to similar network sizes regardless of initial model scale, as demonstrated across multiple architectures and datasets.

Medium confidence: The correlation between effective number of parameters and task difficulty is supported but may not hold for all real-world datasets with complex feature interactions.

Low confidence: Claims about pruned models providing superior uncertainty representation are based on calibration metrics that may not fully capture all aspects of uncertainty quality.
