# Preserving Pre-trained Features Helps Calibrate Fine-tuned Language Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.19249
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19249
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Joint learning with MLM and classification objectives preserves pre-trained features and achieves the lowest expected calibration error across three NLU tasks, outperforming existing calibration methods.

## Executive Summary
Large pre-trained language models (PLMs) exhibit good calibration on masked language modeling tasks but lose this property after standard fine-tuning due to catastrophic forgetting. This study demonstrates that preserving pre-trained features during fine-tuning significantly improves model calibration, particularly under out-of-domain settings. The proposed joint learning approach combines MLM and classification objectives with knowledge distillation from the pre-trained model, achieving both competitive accuracy and superior calibration compared to existing methods. Experimental results across three natural language understanding tasks show consistent improvements in expected calibration error while maintaining task performance.

## Method Summary
The method employs joint learning that combines masked language modeling (MLM) objectives with downstream classification tasks during fine-tuning. Two variants are proposed: JL-D uses the downstream corpus for MLM, while JL-P uses the original pre-training corpus with knowledge distillation from the pre-trained model. The approach includes regularization techniques like pre-trained weight decay and Mixout to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Knowledge distillation transfers uncertainty information from the pre-trained model through KL-divergence between predictive distributions. The framework is evaluated across three NLU tasks with both in-domain and out-of-domain test sets.

## Key Results
- Joint learning methods achieve the lowest expected calibration error (ECE) across all three NLU tasks
- JL-P variant (with pre-training corpus + knowledge distillation) shows the best balance of calibration and accuracy
- Parameter-efficient tuning methods (Adapter, LoRA, Prefix Tuning) preserve pre-trained features and improve calibration over full fine-tuning
- Significant calibration improvements are observed particularly under out-of-domain settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Preserving pre-trained features through joint MLM and classification objectives improves calibration by maintaining uncertainty modeling capabilities learned during pre-training. The MLM task inherently requires reasoning about uncertainty (choosing from vocabulary distribution), and this capability transfers to classification tasks when preserved through joint training. The pre-trained model's robust uncertainty estimation on diverse corpora gets maintained rather than overwritten during fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2
Regularization techniques that constrain parameters toward pre-trained values help preserve uncertainty modeling by preventing catastrophic forgetting. Methods like Mixout and pre-trained weight decay explicitly penalize deviation from pre-trained parameters, maintaining the feature representations and uncertainty estimation capabilities that get lost in standard fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 3
Knowledge distillation from the pre-trained model during joint learning provides a smoother regularization signal than parameter constraints alone. Instead of just constraining parameters, distillation transfers the pre-trained model's predictive distribution, providing richer uncertainty information that leads to better-calibrated confidence estimates.

  - Why needed here: The primary metric used to evaluate calibration performance across experiments.

  - Why needed here: The pre-training task that the paper argues produces well-calibrated models, and the task used in joint learning approaches.
  - Quick check question: How does the MLM task structure (predicting masked tokens from vocabulary distribution) inherently require uncertainty modeling?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Pre-trained RoBERTaBASE (encoder fϕ + MLM head gθ) -> Fine-tuned model (encoder fϕ + task head hφ) -> Joint learning framework with MLM loss, classification loss, regularization terms, and knowledge distillation module

- **Critical path:**
  1. Load pre-trained RoBERTaBASE
  2. Prepare downstream task data and pre-training corpus (for JL-P)
  3. Configure joint learning with MLM objective and knowledge distillation
  4. Fine-tune with hyperparameter search for αmlm, pmask, βL2, etc.
  5. Evaluate accuracy and ECE on ID/OD test sets

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - MLM corpus choice: JL-D (downstream data) vs JL-P (pre-training corpus) - affects calibration and computational cost
  - Knowledge distillation: adds pre-trained model inference cost but improves calibration
  - Regularization strength: balancing between preserving pre-trained features and adapting to new task

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Overconfidence on OD samples: indicates insufficient preservation of pre-trained uncertainty modeling
  - Significant accuracy drop: indicates over-regularization preventing task adaptation
  - High variance across runs: suggests instability in joint learning optimization

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Implement and validate JL-D (joint learning with downstream corpus, no distillation) on SNLI/MNLI - verify lower ECE than full fine-tuning
  2. Implement JL-P (joint learning with pre-training corpus + knowledge distillation) on QQP/TwitterPPDB - verify best calibration and competitive accuracy
  3. Implement parameter-efficient tuning baselines (Adapter, LoRA, Prefix Tuning) on SWAG/HellaSWAG - verify they preserve pre-trained features and improve calibration over full fine-tuning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do the pre-trained features of language models contribute to better uncertainty estimation in out-of-domain settings, and what specific aspects of these features are most critical for calibration? The paper demonstrates that pre-trained features are beneficial but doesn't explore which components are most effective.

### Open Question 2
What are the limitations of joint learning methods that combine masked language modeling and classification objectives, and how can these limitations be addressed to further enhance calibration performance? While joint learning shows promise, the paper acknowledges room for improvement in balancing feature preservation with task learning.

### Open Question 3
How does the use of knowledge distillation from pre-trained models in joint learning methods affect the trade-off between maintaining pre-trained features and achieving high accuracy in downstream tasks? The paper discusses benefits but doesn't fully explore the impact when pre-training corpora are unavailable.

## Limitations

- The specific causal mechanisms for how MLM preservation transfers to classification calibration remain incompletely characterized
- Evaluation domain shifts may not capture the complexity of real-world domain changes
- The paper doesn't investigate whether corpus domain alignment affects calibration benefits

## Confidence

- **Primary claim (preserving pre-trained features improves calibration)**: High confidence - well-supported by comprehensive experiments across multiple tasks and settings
- **Joint learning mechanism explanation**: Medium confidence - empirical results demonstrate effectiveness but causal mechanisms lack direct validation
- **Out-of-domain generalization claims**: Medium confidence - results show improvement but evaluation domain shifts may not capture real-world complexity

## Next Checks

1. **Mechanism isolation experiment**: Implement MLM-only fine-tuning (no classification head) and evaluate its calibration to determine whether MLM preservation alone is sufficient for calibration improvement.

2. **Extreme domain shift test**: Design an experiment with severe, multi-step domain shifts to test the robustness limits of joint learning calibration improvements.

3. **Pre-trained model calibration dependency**: Fine-tune a poorly-calibrated pre-trained model and test whether joint learning still improves calibration, determining if benefits depend on the pre-trained model being well-calibrated initially.
