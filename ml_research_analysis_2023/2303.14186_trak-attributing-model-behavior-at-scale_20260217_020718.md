# TRAK: Attributing Model Behavior at Scale

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14186
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14186
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: TRAK achieves state-of-the-art tradeoffs between speed and efficacy for data attribution across image classifiers, vision-language models (CLIP), and language models (BERT and mT5).

## Executive Summary
TRAK introduces a scalable data attribution method that traces model predictions back to training data by approximating the empirical neural tangent kernel using random projections and trained models. The method addresses the fundamental tradeoff between computational tractability and attribution efficacy in existing approaches. By linearizing the model output function and applying logistic regression-based attribution, TRAK achieves performance matching methods requiring thousands of models while being significantly faster. The authors demonstrate TRAK's effectiveness across multiple modalities and scales, achieving strong results on benchmarks like ImageNet, CLIP, and FTRACE-TREX.

## Method Summary
TRAK is a data attribution method that traces model predictions back to training data by leveraging trained models and random projections to approximate the empirical neural tangent kernel. The method linearizes the model output function around optimal parameters and applies a logistic regression-based attribution approach. TRAK uses random projections to reduce gradient dimensionality while preserving inner product structure, then computes influence scores through a one-step Newton approximation. The method aggregates results across multiple independently trained models to reduce variance from training non-determinism. TRAK is evaluated on image classifiers, vision-language models (CLIP), and language models (BERT and mT5) across various datasets including ImageNet, CIFAR-10, QNLI, and FTRACE-TREX.

## Key Results
- TRAK matches or exceeds the performance of datamodel methods while requiring significantly less computational resources
- Achieves state-of-the-art tradeoffs between speed and efficacy on ImageNet, CLIP, and FTRACE-TREX benchmarks
- Successfully scales to large language models (BERT, mT5) and vision-language models (CLIP) across multiple modalities

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: TRAK approximates complex model behavior by linearizing the model output function around optimal parameters and then applying a logistic regression-based attribution method.
- Mechanism: The method uses a Taylor expansion to approximate the model as linear in parameter space, then applies the one-step Newton approximation from logistic regression to estimate leave-one-out influences.
- Core assumption: The linearized model around θ⋆ provides a sufficiently accurate approximation of the true model behavior, especially for overparameterized neural networks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "TRAK leverages a handful of trained models and random projections to approximate the empirical neural tangent kernel"
  - [section 3.2]: "we can view it as a linear model acting on inputs ∇θ f (z; θ⋆)" and "TRAK can match the performance of attribution methods that require training thousands of models"
  - [corpus]: "Imperfect Influence, Preserved Rankings: A Theory of TRAK for Data Attribution" confirms TRAK's theoretical foundations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Random projections preserve the inner product structure needed for accurate attribution while dramatically reducing computational cost.
- Mechanism: Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma guarantees that random projections preserve inner products with high probability, which is sufficient for maintaining the structure of the logistic regression problem.
- Core assumption: The gradient inner products are the key information needed for attribution, and random projections preserve these sufficiently well.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "multiplying each gradient gi = ∇θ f (zi; θ⋆) by a random matrix P ∼ N (0, 1)p×k for k ≪ p preserves inner products gi · gj with high probability"
  - [section C.2]: "As long as we preserve the inner products to sufficient accuracy, the resulting system has approximately the same evolution as the original one"
  - [corpus]: "Random projection, margins, kernels, and feature-selection" validates random projection theory

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensembling over multiple independently trained models smooths out non-determinism in training and captures more predictive signal.
- Mechanism: Averaging attribution scores across models trained on different random subsets of the data reduces variance from random seed effects and captures the expectation over training subsets.
- Core assumption: Training non-determinism is primarily due to random initialization and data shuffling, which can be averaged out across models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "we aggregate the estimator (13) across multiple trained models" and "we adopt the natural idea of just averaging τ(z, S) from (13) directly"
  - [section E.2]: "increasing the number M of models used in the ensemble significantly increases TRAK's efficacy"
  - [corpus]: "Do Wide and Deep Networks Learn the Same Things?" supports the importance of training non-determinism

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Model linearization -> Random projections -> Influence estimation -> Ensembling -> Soft-thresholding
- Critical path: The linearization step is most critical as it enables the entire approach; the random projection step is essential for computational feasibility
- Design tradeoffs: TRAK trades off approximation accuracy (linearization) for computational efficiency (random projections and ensembling), and must balance projection dimension k against computational cost
- Failure signatures: Poor performance indicates either (a) the linearization approximation is breaking down, (b) random projections are too lossy, or (c) insufficient ensembling leading to high variance
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify that TRAK works on a simple logistic regression problem where ground truth is known
  2. Test TRAK on a small neural network with controlled non-determinism to validate the ensembling effect
  3. Measure the impact of projection dimension k on attribution accuracy for a fixed model

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the limitations of the linear approximation used in TRAK, and how might incorporating higher-order terms improve its performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the empirical neural tangent kernel (eNTK) approximation and its potential for improvement, mentioning higher-order approximations in Section G.2.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide empirical evidence on the impact of incorporating higher-order terms beyond the linear approximation used in TRAK.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing TRAK with and without higher-order approximations on various datasets and model architectures would clarify the benefits and limitations of incorporating higher-order terms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of the model output function (f(z; θ)) affect the performance of TRAK, and are there alternative choices that could further improve its accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the choice of the logit function for classification tasks and mentions that choosing a good f boils down to linearizing as much of the model as possible, but not too much, in Section C.4.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore alternative choices of the model output function beyond the logit function used in the experiments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing TRAK with different choices of the model output function on various tasks and datasets would identify optimal choices and their impact on accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the role of ensembling in TRAK, and can it be optimized to reduce computational cost while maintaining or improving performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the importance of ensembling multiple models in TRAK and explores alternatives like using checkpoints from a single training trajectory in Appendix E.3.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive analysis of the optimal number of models to ensemble or alternative strategies for achieving similar performance with fewer models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies systematically varying the number of models in the ensemble and comparing performance with alternative strategies would identify the optimal ensembling approach for TRAK.

## Limitations
- TRAK's efficacy on extremely large-scale models with trillions of parameters remains unverified
- The linearization approximation may break down for underparameterized or highly non-linear models
- Computational cost still scales with model size and dataset dimensions, limiting practical applicability for massive datasets

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: TRAK's core mechanism (linearization + random projections) is mathematically sound and the empirical results on benchmark datasets are robust
- **Medium Confidence**: The theoretical justification for TRAK's linearization approximation holds for overparameterized models, but its applicability to underparameterized or highly non-linear models is less certain
- **Low Confidence**: TRAK's scalability to trillion-parameter models and its performance on extremely large-scale datasets remain untested
