# Learning Rewards to Optimize Global Performance Metrics in Deep Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.09027
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09027
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: LR4GPM achieves competitive or superior performance compared to standard RL methods while learning from global performance metrics directly, notably outperforming the winner of a recent autonomous driving competition.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the problem of optimizing global performance metrics in deep reinforcement learning without requiring reward engineering. The proposed method, LR4GPM, alternates between learning a reward function that fits the performance metric and training a policy to optimize an approximation of this metric based on the learned rewards. The approach addresses the non-stationarity of both reward and policy training through several techniques including target reward networks, adaptive learning rates, optimistic policy updates, and reward diversity. Experiments across multiple domains including MuJoCo control tasks, network control (Iroko), and autonomous driving (SMARTS) demonstrate LR4GPM's effectiveness.

## Method Summary
LR4GPM alternates between two phases: (1) learning a reward function that fits the performance metric using a target reward network, and (2) training a policy to optimize an approximation of this metric based on the learned rewards. This alternation allows the reward function to be adapted to the current policy's behavior while the policy learns to maximize the learned reward. The algorithm uses target reward networks and adaptive learning rates to stabilize training in the non-stationary environment created by dynamically updating rewards. Optimistic policy updates and optimistic sampling bias the learning process towards better trajectories, preventing early convergence to suboptimal policies.

## Key Results
- LR4GPM outperforms the winner of a recent autonomous driving competition on the SMARTS benchmark
- The algorithm achieves competitive or superior performance compared to standard RL methods while learning from global performance metrics directly
- Results demonstrate effectiveness across multiple domains including MuJoCo control tasks, network control (Iroko), and autonomous driving (SMARTS)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Alternating between reward learning and policy optimization enables convergence to a policy that optimizes the global performance metric without requiring reward engineering.
- **Mechanism**: The algorithm alternates between two phases: (1) learning a reward function that fits the performance metric using a target reward network, and (2) training a policy to optimize an approximation of this metric based on the learned rewards.
- **Core assumption**: The performance metric can be approximated by a learned reward function, and the policy can be optimized using this learned reward without access to the true reward function.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "LR4GPM alternates between two phases: (1) learning a (possibly vector) reward function used to fit the performance metric, and (2) training a policy to optimize an approximation of this performance metric based on the learned rewards."
  - [section] "Assuming that π is a fixed policy, phase (i) corresponds to this first problem: min ξ,ψ (ρ(Eπ,P [𝝂 (η)]) − ρ̂ξ (Eπ,P [𝒓ψ (η)]))²"
- **Break condition**: If the learned reward function cannot adequately approximate the performance metric, or if the policy optimization fails to converge due to non-stationarity, the algorithm will not find a good policy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Using target reward networks and adaptive learning rates stabilizes training in the non-stationary environment created by dynamically updating rewards.
- **Mechanism**: A target reward network provides stable reward signals during policy training, preventing large reward modifications that could destabilize learning. Adaptive learning rates ensure that the reward learning rate ≥ critic learning rate ≥ actor learning rate, following theoretical analysis of actor-critic algorithms.
- **Core assumption**: The non-stationarity introduced by dynamically updating rewards can be mitigated through stabilization techniques.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "To overcome this issue, we propose several training tricks. We demonstrate the efficiency of LR4GPM on several domains."
  - [section] "Target reward network To avoid the issues of sudden and large reward modifications in the policy learning, we keep a target reward network like in DQN [28]."
- **Break condition**: If the stabilization techniques are insufficient to handle the non-stationarity, or if the adaptive learning rate constraints are not properly enforced, training may become unstable or diverge.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Optimistic policy updates and optimistic sampling bias the learning process towards better trajectories, preventing early convergence to suboptimal policies.
- **Mechanism**: Optimistic policy updates sample multiple sets of trajectories and keep only the best-performing policy based on the performance metric. Optimistic sampling evaluates more trajectory sets than needed and keeps only the best ones for reward learning, biasing the data generation towards better trajectories.
- **Core assumption**: Early convergence to suboptimal policies can be prevented by actively selecting better trajectories during both policy and reward learning phases.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "To alleviate this issue, we propose several training tricks."
  - [section] "Optimistic policy update For the last policy update in one inner loop iteration, we sample m sets of trajectories E1, ..., Em and update the current policy with each set to obtain m new policies."
- **Break condition**: If the optimistic selection becomes too greedy, it may miss exploring potentially better policies, leading to premature convergence to a local optimum.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: State -> Policy Network -> Action -> Environment -> Trajectory -> Reward Network -> Vector Rewards -> Performance Metric Approximator -> Scalar Performance Score -> Critic Network -> Value Function -> Policy Update
- **Critical path**: The outer loop alternates between reward learning (using sampled trajectories and performance metric evaluations) and policy optimization (using the learned reward function). The inner loop performs multiple policy updates per reward update.
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Using vector rewards vs. scalar rewards: Vector rewards can capture multi-dimensional performance aspects but increase complexity
  - Target network update frequency: More frequent updates provide more accurate rewards but may destabilize training
  - Optimistic sampling level (K parameter): Higher values bias toward better trajectories but may reduce exploration
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Reward learning fails to converge: The L2 loss between the performance metric and the approximated metric remains high
  - Policy training instability: Large variance in policy performance across training runs
  - Early convergence to poor policies: Performance plateaus at suboptimal levels without improvement
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Hopper environment with identity performance metric**: Test the basic algorithm on a simple control task where the performance metric is just the sum of rewards, comparing against standard PPO.
  2. **Iroko networking task with throughput-delay tradeoff**: Validate the algorithm on a real-world domain with a non-linear performance metric that combines multiple objectives.
  3. **SMARTS autonomous driving with safety-efficiency tradeoff**: Test the algorithm on a complex domain with multiple performance dimensions and safety constraints.

## Limitations

- Neural network architectures are not fully specified across different domains, making exact reproduction difficult
- The adaptive learning rate constraints are theoretically motivated but lack empirical validation of their necessity
- Performance improvements on autonomous driving tasks are based on comparisons with a single competition winner rather than comprehensive benchmarks

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core algorithmic framework of alternating between reward learning and policy optimization is well-defined and theoretically grounded. The convergence proof for the non-convex setting provides strong theoretical support for the approach.

**Medium Confidence**: The experimental results show consistent improvements across multiple domains, but the lack of hyperparameter sensitivity analysis and ablation studies on individual training tricks limits confidence in which components are most critical for success.

**Low Confidence**: The scalability claims to complex real-world domains are based on limited evaluations, and the algorithm's performance on tasks with high-dimensional state spaces or long time horizons remains untested.
