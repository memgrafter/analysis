# Look, Listen, and Attack: Backdoor Attacks Against Video Action Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00986
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00986
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Video backdoor attacks achieve high attack success rates while maintaining clean data accuracy across multiple datasets and architectures, including I3D, TSM, and SlowFast

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the gap in understanding backdoor attacks against video action recognition models. The authors extend traditional backdoor threat models to incorporate video-specific aspects like temporal dimension and spatial cropping. They propose methods to extend image backdoor attacks to videos through static (same attack across all frames) and dynamic (different attack parameters per frame) approaches. Additionally, they introduce novel natural video backdoor attacks using artifacts like frame lag, motion blur, and video compression glitches. For the first time, they study multi-modal audiovisual backdoor attacks, demonstrating that attacking a single modality is sufficient to achieve high attack success rates. Experiments show these attacks are effective across multiple datasets and architectures while maintaining clean data accuracy.

## Method Summary
The paper extends poisoned-label image backdoor attacks to the video domain through two temporal approaches: static (applying the same trigger across all frames) and dynamic (using different trigger parameters for each frame). They implement five image backdoor attacks (BadNet, Blend, SIG, WaNet, FTrojan) in video settings and propose three novel natural video backdoor attacks using frame lag, motion blur, and video compression artifacts. For multi-modal audiovisual attacks, they demonstrate that attacking a single modality (video or audio) is sufficient for high attack success rates in early fusion setups. The evaluation uses 32-frame clips sampled from videos, with Log-Mel spectrograms (80 Mel bands, 256 temporal length) for audio. Models are fine-tuned on poisoned datasets with 10% poisoning rate, and effectiveness is measured through Clean Data Accuracy (CDA) and Attack Success Rate (ASR).

## Key Results
- Video backdoor attacks achieve high attack success rates while maintaining clean data accuracy across I3D, TSM, and SlowFast architectures
- Natural video backdoor attacks using frame lag, motion blur, and compression glitches are effective and resistant to existing image backdoor defenses
- Attacking a single modality in early fusion audiovisual setups achieves high attack success rates, while late fusion provides some protection
- Proposed attacks are resistant to standard defenses like activation clustering, STRIP, and pruning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Extending 2D backdoor attacks to video domain by applying the same trigger across all frames (static) or different triggers per frame (dynamic) maintains high clean data accuracy while achieving high attack success rates.
- Mechanism: Video recognition models typically sample and crop during evaluation, but by controlling the attack parameters during training, the backdoor trigger remains effective even after subsampling and cropping operations.
- Core assumption: The attacker has control over the training process and knows the evaluation pipeline parameters (subsampling rate, crop size).
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that poisoned-label image backdoor attacks could be extended temporally in two ways, statically and dynamically, leading to highly effective attacks in the video domain."
  - [section] "We also extend five image backdoor attacks into the video domain and propose three novel natural video backdoor attacks."
- Break condition: If the evaluation pipeline uses random sampling that excludes poisoned frames, or if the cropping operation removes the trigger entirely.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Natural video artifacts (frame lag, motion blur, video compression glitches) can serve as backdoor triggers while maintaining high imperceptibility and effectiveness.
- Mechanism: These artifacts occur naturally in videos and don't stand out as artificial modifications, making them harder to detect through human inspection while still creating the trigger-target association during training.
- Core assumption: The chosen natural artifacts are common enough in real-world videos to avoid suspicion but distinctive enough to create reliable trigger-target associations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In addition, we explore natural video backdoors to highlight the seriousness of this vulnerability in the video domain."
  - [section] "We then propose three novel natural video backdoor attacks... We show that poisoned-label image backdoor attacks could be extended temporally in two ways, statically and dynamically, leading to highly effective attacks in the video domain."
- Break condition: If the natural artifacts are too rare or inconsistent across video frames to reliably trigger the target class.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Attacking a single modality (video or audio) is sufficient for achieving high attack success rates in multimodal audiovisual action recognition models.
- Mechanism: The fusion mechanism in multimodal networks allows information from the attacked modality to influence the final prediction, even when other modalities remain clean.
- Core assumption: The fusion mechanism (early or late) effectively propagates the poisoned information from the attacked modality to the final classification decision.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "And, for the first time, we study multi-modal (audiovisual) backdoor attacks against video action recognition models, where we show that attacking a single modality is enough for achieving a high attack success rate."
  - [section] "We show that attacking a single modality is enough to achieve a high attack success rate."
- Break condition: If the fusion mechanism gives overwhelming weight to the clean modality, diluting the effect of the attacked modality.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Video backbone (I3D/TSM/SlowFast) -> Audio backbone (ResNet-18 on Mel-spectrograms) -> Fusion module -> Backdoor trigger generator

- Critical path:
  1. Poison training data with backdoor triggers
  2. Train model on poisoned dataset
  3. Evaluate clean data accuracy on validation set
  4. Test attack success rate with backdoor triggers

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Static vs dynamic attack extension: Static is simpler but may be more detectable; dynamic offers better imperceptibility but requires more sophisticated trigger generation
  - Single modality vs multi-modality attack: Single modality is stealthier but may have lower ASR; multi-modality offers higher ASR but is more detectable
  - Natural vs artificial triggers: Natural triggers are harder to detect but may be less reliable; artificial triggers are more controllable but more detectable

- Failure signatures:
  - Clean data accuracy drops significantly after poisoning
  - Attack success rate is low even with obvious triggers
  - Defenses like activation clustering successfully detect poisoned samples
  - Random sampling during evaluation frequently excludes poisoned frames

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement BadNet attack statically on I3D model using UCF-101 dataset
  2. Extend the same attack dynamically and compare CDA/ASR
  3. Test the attacked model against activation clustering defense

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of backdoor attacks vary across different video action recognition architectures (e.g., 2D CNNs vs. 3D CNNs vs. transformers) and what architectural features make some models more vulnerable than others?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests backdoor attacks against I3D, TSM, and SlowFast architectures and observes varying effectiveness, with even 2D models like TSM being vulnerable to natural video backdoor attacks.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that different architectures exhibit different levels of vulnerability, it does not provide a detailed analysis of why certain architectural features make models more susceptible to backdoor attacks, nor does it compare a broader range of architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing backdoor attacks across a wider variety of video recognition architectures (including transformers and different CNN variants), combined with architectural analysis to identify vulnerability factors.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the long-term implications of backdoor attacks on video action recognition models in real-world deployment scenarios, particularly in terms of detectability over time and potential for adaptive attacks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates that backdoor attacks can achieve high attack success rates while maintaining clean data accuracy, and that some attacks are resistant to existing defenses, suggesting potential challenges for long-term deployment.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on immediate attack effectiveness but does not address how backdoor attacks might evolve over time, whether detection methods might improve, or how attackers might adapt their strategies in response to new defenses.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking the effectiveness of backdoor attacks over extended periods, along with analyses of how both attackers and defenders might adapt their techniques over time.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can multimodal backdoor defenses be developed to effectively protect against audiovisual backdoor attacks, particularly when different modalities have varying levels of vulnerability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that attacking a single modality in early fusion setups can achieve high attack success rates, while late fusion provides some protection, highlighting the need for effective multimodal defenses.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies the vulnerability of multimodal systems to single-modality attacks, it does not propose or evaluate comprehensive defense strategies that could protect against such attacks in both early and late fusion scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of defense mechanisms specifically designed for multimodal systems, including techniques that can detect and mitigate attacks even when only one modality is compromised.

## Limitations
- Claims are based on controlled experimental conditions that may not reflect real-world deployment scenarios
- Attacker's knowledge assumptions (knowing evaluation pipeline parameters, having control over training data) represent a strong threat model
- Evaluation focuses primarily on attack success rate and clean data accuracy, with limited analysis of transferability to different architectures

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Claims about extending image backdoor attacks to video domain (static/dynamic approaches) are well-supported by experimental results across multiple architectures and datasets
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about natural video backdoor attacks show effectiveness but rely on subjective assessments of "naturalness" and may have dataset-specific effectiveness
- **Medium confidence**: Claims about single-modality sufficiency in audiovisual attacks are supported but the fusion mechanism details are somewhat limited

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate the proposed attacks against adaptive defenses that specifically target video backdoor patterns, such as temporal consistency checks or frame-wise anomaly detection
