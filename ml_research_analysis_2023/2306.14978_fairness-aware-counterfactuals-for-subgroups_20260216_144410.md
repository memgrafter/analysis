# Fairness Aware Counterfactuals for Subgroups

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14978
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14978
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces FACTS framework for auditing subgroup fairness through counterfactual explanations, distinguishing micro and macro viewpoints with novel fairness definitions

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces FACTS, a framework for auditing subgroup fairness through counterfactual explanations. The framework distinguishes between micro and macro viewpoints: the micro viewpoint considers individuals independently choosing actions for recourse, while the macro viewpoint treats groups as wholes where actions affect all members collectively. FACTS introduces novel fairness notions such as Equal Effectiveness, Equal Choice for Recourse, and Fair Effectiveness-Cost Trade-Off that are robust to action costs. The framework efficiently computes effectiveness-cost distributions for subgroups, enabling detection and ranking of unfair subgroups through interpretable Comparative Subgroup Counterfactuals (CSCs).

## Method Summary
FACTS is a three-step framework for auditing subgroup fairness. First, it generates subgroups and actions using fp-growth on affected and unaffected populations with a specified support threshold. Second, it computes counterfactual summaries by measuring effectiveness of each action for each subgroup. Third, it constructs CSCs and ranks subgroups based on various fairness definitions including Equal Effectiveness, Equal Choice for Recourse, and Fair Effectiveness-Cost Trade-Off. The framework uses effectiveness-cost distributions (ecd) to capture trade-offs between cost and recourse across subgroups, enabling efficient comparison of fairness across protected subgroups.

## Key Results
- FACTS successfully identifies subgroups with bias against different protected groups across multiple datasets
- Different fairness definitions capture distinct aspects of recourse unfairness
- The framework efficiently computes effectiveness-cost distributions for subgroup comparison
- Fixed action costs across subgroups provide a fairer basis for comparison than individual-level costs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FACTS identifies unfairness by computing effectiveness-cost distributions (ecd) that capture trade-offs between cost and recourse across subgroups.
- Mechanism: Instead of evaluating individual counterfactuals, FACTS enumerates actions and measures how many individuals achieve recourse under each action, then compares distributions between protected subgroups.
- Core assumption: Actions with fixed costs across subgroups provide a fairer basis for comparison than individual-level costs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "FACTS efficiently computes effectiveness-cost distributions for subgroups, enabling detection and ranking of unfair subgroups"
  - [section 2.2] "The function ecd(c; A, G) is the proportion of individuals in G that can achieve recourse through actions A with cost at most c"
- Break condition: If action costs vary significantly within subgroups, the fixed-cost assumption breaks down and may mask individual-level unfairness.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The micro and macro viewpoints capture distinct aspects of recourse fairness.
- Mechanism: Micro viewpoint treats individuals independently choosing minimum-cost actions, while macro viewpoint considers collective action effectiveness on groups.
- Core assumption: Group-level interventions (macro) and individual-level choices (micro) reveal different fairness dimensions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "distinguishes between micro and macro viewpoints: the micro viewpoint considers individuals independently choosing actions for recourse, while the macro viewpoint treats groups as wholes"
  - [section 2.2] "In the micro viewpoint...each individual chooses the action that benefits itself the most...In the macro viewpoint...an action is applied collectively to all individuals in the group"
- Break condition: When subgroup composition is homogeneous or when individual and group behaviors align perfectly.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Cost-oblivious fairness definitions capture fairness without requiring accurate cost functions.
- Mechanism: Definitions like Equal Choice for Recourse compare the number of effective actions available to subgroups rather than their costs.
- Core assumption: The availability of effective actions is a meaningful proxy for fairness when cost functions are unreliable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "introduce notions of subgroup fairness that are robust, if not totally oblivious, to the cost of achieving recourse"
  - [section 2.3] "Equal Choice for Recourse...claims that the classifier is fair if the protected subgroups can choose among the same number of sufficiently effective actions"
- Break condition: When action availability is determined by factors unrelated to fairness, such as feature constraints or model architecture.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Counterfactual explanations
  - Why needed here: FACTS builds on counterfactual reasoning to identify actionable changes for individuals to achieve desired outcomes
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between a factual instance and a counterfactual instance in the context of algorithmic recourse?

- Concept: Group fairness vs individual fairness
  - Why needed here: The paper distinguishes between fairness at individual level (micro) and group level (macro), which is central to its framework
  - Quick check question: How does the micro viewpoint differ from the macro viewpoint in evaluating fairness?

- Concept: Frequent itemset mining (fp-growth)
  - Why needed here: Used to generate subgroups and actions from datasets efficiently
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between support threshold and the number of subgroups generated?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data preprocessing -> Subgroup/action generation (fp-growth) -> Counterfactual summary generation -> Fairness evaluation -> CSC construction
  Each fairness definition acts as a separate scoring module
  Cost functions are pluggable components

- Critical path:
  1. Load dataset and train model
  2. Generate subgroups using fp-growth on affected populations
  3. Generate actions using fp-growth on unaffected population
  4. For each subgroup, compute effectiveness for each action
  5. Evaluate all fairness definitions
  6. Generate CSCs and rank subgroups

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed action costs vs individual costs: Simplifies computation but may mask individual variation
  - Micro vs macro viewpoints: Captures different fairness aspects but requires understanding both
  - Support threshold: Balances computational efficiency with subgroup granularity

- Failure signatures:
  - No subgroups generated: Support threshold too high
  - All subgroups fair: Model may be already fair or definitions not sensitive enough
  - Computational timeout: Large action space or low support threshold

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run FACTS on Adult dataset with gender as protected attribute, verify subgroup generation
  2. Test different support thresholds (0.5%, 1%, 2%) and observe subgroup count changes
  3. Compare micro vs macro viewpoint results on the same subgroup to understand differences

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions but leaves several areas unexplored, particularly around the robustness of fairness metrics to cost function variations, handling multiple protected attributes, and computational complexity for large-scale datasets.

## Limitations
- Framework assumes fixed action costs across subgroups, which may mask individual-level variations in recourse difficulty
- Effectiveness of subgroup detection depends heavily on support threshold parameter with no principled selection method
- Cost functions for different feature types are not fully specified, potentially affecting reproducibility
- Focuses on post-hoc auditing rather than proposing interventions to address identified unfairness

## Confidence
**High confidence** in the core mechanism of using effectiveness-cost distributions for subgroup comparison, as this is well-supported by theoretical foundations in counterfactual explanation literature. **Medium confidence** in the practical utility of the framework, given the experimental results show varying effectiveness across different fairness definitions. **Low confidence** in the generalizability across domains, as the evaluation is limited to four specific datasets with binary classification tasks.

## Next Checks
1. **Cost sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary cost functions across feature types and measure impact on fairness rankings to assess robustness to cost specification.
2. **Support threshold optimization**: Develop a principled method for selecting support thresholds that balances computational efficiency with detection power, rather than relying on arbitrary values.
3. **Cross-domain evaluation**: Apply FACTS to additional domains (e.g., healthcare, finance) with different protected attributes and classification tasks to assess generalizability beyond the current experimental setup.
