# Stratified Adversarial Robustness with Rejection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.01139
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01139
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: CPR method reduces total robust loss by at least 7.3% under both seen and unseen attacks on CIFAR-10

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the limitation in existing adversarial robustness methods where rejection of perturbed inputs incurs zero cost, leading to excessive rejection of slightly perturbed inputs that could be correctly classified. The authors propose a stratified rejection setting where rejection loss functions are monotonically non-increasing in perturbation magnitude. They introduce a novel defense method called CPR (Adversarial Training with Consistent Prediction-based Rejection) that constructs a selective classifier which rejects inputs near decision boundaries while accepting slightly perturbed inputs far from boundaries. Experiments on image datasets demonstrate significant improvements over baseline methods under strong adaptive attacks.

## Method Summary
CPR is a defense method that builds a selective classifier fδ around a base classifier f* trained using AT or TRADES. The method uses PGD to find worst-case perturbations within a consistency radius, rejecting inputs where the base classifier's predictions differ within this neighborhood. The consistency radius is set based on the desired rejection budget α, allowing for controlled rejection of larger perturbations while maintaining correct classification of smaller ones. CPR is evaluated against adaptive attacks including low-confidence inner attacks, prediction-disagreement inner attacks, and consistent high-confidence misclassification outer attacks.

## Key Results
- CPR reduces total robust loss by at least 7.3% under both seen and unseen attacks on CIFAR-10
- CPR outperforms existing methods by 5.1-8.6% on robustness with rejection metric
- CPR demonstrates superior performance across different rejection loss functions including step and ramp losses

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Base classifier (f*) -> CPR selective classifier (g_ϵ̃) -> PGD method for finding worst-case perturbations -> Adaptive attack ensemble for evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Train base classifier using standard adversarial training method
  2. Set consistency radius ϵ̃ and PGD parameters
  3. For each test input:
     a. Use PGD to find worst-case perturbation within ϵ̃
     b. If base classifier predictions differ within this neighborhood, reject input
     c. Otherwise, accept and output base classifier's prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Larger consistency radius improves rejection of near-boundary inputs but may increase false rejection rate
  - More PGD steps improve robustness but increase computational cost
  - Ensemble of attacks provides stronger evaluation but increases computational cost

- Failure signatures:
  - High rejection rate on clean, correctly classified inputs
  - Low robustness with rejection for small α values
  - Computational bottleneck due to excessive PGD steps

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare CPR's robustness with rejection on CIFAR-10 against AT+CR baseline
  2. Evaluate effect of varying consistency radius on rejection rate and robustness
  3. Test CPR's performance against unseen attacks with different rejection loss functions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the proposed CPR method be extended to more general rejection loss functions beyond monotonically non-increasing ones?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on rejection losses that are monotonically non-increasing in perturbation magnitude. However, it mentions that there could be other types of rejection losses, e.g., ℓrej(r, x) that also depend on the input x
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any theoretical analysis or experimental results for rejection loss functions beyond monotonically non-increasing ones. It is unclear whether the theoretical guarantees and practical performance of CPR would hold for more general rejection loss functions
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis showing the conditions under which CPR can achieve good performance for general rejection loss functions, and experimental results comparing CPR to other methods for various types of rejection loss functions

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the computational cost of CPR at prediction time be reduced while maintaining its robustness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that CPR has an increased computational cost at prediction time since it requires m PGD steps to find T(x), which requires m forward and backward passes in addition to the two forward passes required to get the predictions of x and T(x)
- Why unresolved: The paper does not propose any techniques to reduce the computational cost of CPR while maintaining its robustness. It is unclear whether there are more efficient ways to implement CPR or alternative defense methods that can achieve similar robustness with lower computational cost
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis or experimental results comparing the robustness and computational cost of CPR with and without various optimizations or alternative implementations

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of CPR compare to other robust selective classification methods under different attack scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates CPR against several baseline methods under strong adaptive attacks. However, it does not compare CPR to other robust selective classification methods such as those based on conformal prediction or other uncertainty quantification techniques
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on comparing CPR to a specific set of baseline methods, but it does not provide a comprehensive comparison with other robust selective classification approaches. It is unclear how CPR would perform compared to these alternative methods under different attack scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing CPR to other robust selective classification methods under various attack scenarios, including both seen and unseen attacks, and for different rejection loss functions

## Limitations
- Results are primarily demonstrated on image datasets, limiting generalizability to other domains
- The comparison with ATRR baseline is limited by incomplete specification of the auxiliary function A(x;φ)
- The paper does not extensively explore the sensitivity of CPR to the choice of consistency radius and PGD parameters

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The theoretical framework for stratified adversarial robustness with rejection and the formal definition of total robust loss
- **Medium Confidence**: The empirical performance improvements of CPR over baselines under adaptive attacks, given the strong evaluation methodology
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of CPR to non-image datasets and the sensitivity of results to hyper-parameter choices
