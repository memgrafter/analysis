# Extracting Low-/High- Frequency Knowledge from Graph Neural Networks and Injecting it into MLPs: An Effective GNN-to-MLP Distillation Framework

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.10758
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10758
- **Reference count**: 23
- **Key outcome**: FF-G2M improves vanilla MLPs by 12.6% and outperforms teacher GNNs by 2.6% on average across six graph datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the information loss problem in GNN-to-MLP distillation by factorizing GNN knowledge into low- and high-frequency components. The authors propose a novel Full-Frequency GNN-to-MLP (FF-G2M) distillation framework that extracts both low- and high-frequency knowledge from teacher GNNs and injects it into student MLPs. FF-G2M improves over vanilla MLPs by 12.6% and outperforms its corresponding teacher GNNs by 2.6% on average across six graph datasets and three common GNN architectures.

## Method Summary
The FF-G2M framework pre-trains teacher GNNs (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT) on graph data, then trains student MLPs using two complementary distillation objectives. Low-Frequency Distillation (LFD) diffuses teacher GNN knowledge to student MLP neighborhoods using KL-divergence between softmax outputs. High-Frequency Distillation (HFD) preserves pairwise distance differences between nodes using element-wise absolute differences and KL-divergence. The total loss combines classification loss with both distillation losses, weighted by temperature parameters and a balance coefficient.

## Key Results
- FF-G2M improves vanilla MLPs by 12.6% on average across six graph datasets
- FF-G2M outperforms corresponding teacher GNNs by 2.6% on average
- Low- and high-frequency knowledge components are complementary, with combined framework performing better than either method alone

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Low-frequency distillation (LFD) preserves neighborhood smoothing by diffusing teacher GNN knowledge back into student MLP neighborhoods
- Mechanism: LFD uses KL-divergence between teacher GNN softmax outputs and student MLP outputs at neighboring nodes, encouraging the MLP to learn aggregated neighborhood patterns
- Core assumption: The teacher GNN's representations already capture the essential low-frequency graph structure through message passing
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Low-Frequency Distillation (LFD) and Hign-Frequency Distillation (HFD) to fully capture the low-frequency and high-frequency knowledge learned by GNNs"
  - [section]: "Unlike aggregating features from neighborhoods as in GNNs, we directly distill (diffuse) knowledge from teacher GNNs into the neighborhoods of student MLPs"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus papers focus on edge-free approaches or teacher-free frameworks, not neighborhood diffusion

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: High-frequency distillation (HFD) captures pairwise distance differences that are lost in standard node-to-node distillation
- Mechanism: HFD computes element-wise absolute differences between node pairs in teacher and student representations, then applies KL-divergence to preserve relative positioning
- Core assumption: The high-frequency information in GNNs manifests as differences between neighboring node representations that need explicit preservation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the high-frequency knowledge of the pre-trained GNNs may be overwhelmed by the low-frequency knowledge during distillation"
  - [section]: "we propose High-Frequency Distillation (HFD), a GNN knowledge objective that trains student MLPs to preserve the neighborhood pairwise differences"
  - [corpus]: Missing - corpus doesn't discuss pairwise distance preservation specifically

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Combining LFD and HFD captures full frequency spectrum, improving over either method alone
- Mechanism: The total loss function weights classification loss and both distillation losses, allowing complementary capture of smoothing and relative positioning information
- Core assumption: Low-frequency and high-frequency knowledge are complementary and can be learned simultaneously without interference
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Extensive experiments show that high-frequency and low-frequency knowledge are complementary to each other"
  - [section]: "The proposed FF-G2M combines both the two distillation and is better at capturing both low- and high-frequency knowledge than GLNN"

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Teacher GNN -> LFD module -> Combined loss -> Student MLP
  Teacher GNN -> HFD module -> Combined loss -> Student MLP

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-train teacher GNN on labeled data
  2. Freeze teacher GNN and compute representations
  3. Initialize student MLP with same architecture
  4. Forward pass through both models
  5. Compute LFD and HFD losses
  6. Backpropagate combined loss to update student MLP

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Temperature parameters (τ1, τ2) vs. distillation sharpness
  - Loss weight λ vs. balance between classification and knowledge transfer
  - Single vs. multiple teacher GNN architectures
  - Node-to-node vs. neighborhood diffusion approaches

- Failure signatures:
  - Student MLP performs worse than vanilla MLP: Likely issue with teacher GNN quality or distillation hyperparameters
  - Student MLP only improves on some datasets: Dataset-specific graph structure may not align with frequency assumptions
  - Training instability: Temperature or weight parameters may need adjustment

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare vanilla MLP vs. MLP with only LFD on a small dataset
  2. Compare MLP with only HFD vs. MLP with both LFD and HFD
  3. Test different temperature settings (τ1, τ2) to find optimal distillation sharpness

## Limitations

- The framework's effectiveness depends on teacher GNNs adequately capturing both low- and high-frequency graph information, which is not extensively validated
- The element-wise absolute difference metric for high-frequency preservation may not fully capture complex high-frequency patterns in certain graph structures
- The paper does not explore the sensitivity of the method to different low-pass and high-pass filter designs in the spectral domain

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The complementary nature of low- and high-frequency knowledge capture, supported by experimental results showing improved performance over individual distillation methods
- **Medium Confidence**: The specific mechanisms of LFD and HFD, as the theoretical justification relies on spectral graph theory concepts that are not fully elaborated in the paper
- **Medium Confidence**: The generalizability across different GNN architectures (GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT), as the paper demonstrates effectiveness but doesn't explore edge cases or failure modes
