# Comparison of Pre-trained Language Models for Turkish Address Parsing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13947
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13947
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Turkish-specific BERT models with MLP fine-tuning slightly outperform multilingual models on Turkish address parsing, with best token-wise accuracy of 0.792

## Executive Summary
This paper compares multilingual and Turkish-specific pre-trained language models for Turkish address parsing. The authors evaluate BERT, DistilBERT, ELECTRA, and RoBERTa variants using both single-layer and two-layer MLP fine-tuning approaches. Turkish-specific models with MLP fine-tuning show slight performance improvements over multilingual models. The akdeniz27/xlm-roberta-base-turkish-ner model achieved the best results with token-wise accuracy of 0.792 and macro F1 of 0.497. The study also proposes using hyperparameter optimization via Optuna to improve model robustness.

## Method Summary
The authors fine-tune various pre-trained BERT variants on a mid-sized Turkish address parsing corpus (1248 samples) using token classification with 25 labels in IOB format. They compare single-layer linear classifiers versus two-layer MLP heads with dropout (0.4) and batch normalization. Hyperparameter optimization is performed using Optuna with random search across 40 trials, optimizing learning rate, batch size, and optimizer selection. Models are trained for up to 10 epochs with early stopping based on validation loss.

## Key Results
- Turkish-specific models with MLP fine-tuning slightly outperform multilingual fine-tuned models
- akdeniz27/xlm-roberta-base-turkish-ner achieved best results: token-wise accuracy 0.792, macro precision 0.500, macro recall 0.505, macro F1 0.497
- Double-layer MLP performs better than single-layer classifier, particularly for smaller models like ELECTRA and DistilBERT
- Visualization of address token representations indicates effectiveness of BERT variants for classification

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Turkish address query -> Tokenization (lowercase + Turkish 'i' normalization) -> Pre-trained BERT variant -> 2-layer MLP with dropout (0.4) and batch norm -> Classification layer -> Label probabilities (25 classes)

- Critical path:
  1. Data preprocessing → tokenization (lowercase + Turkish 'i' normalization)
  2. Forward pass through BERT → hidden states
  3. MLP (if used) → classification layer
  4. Loss computation (cross-entropy per token)
  5. Backpropagation with chosen optimizer (AdamW/SGD)
  6. Evaluation on validation set for early stopping

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single linear layer: Fewer parameters, faster training, may underfit complex label patterns
  - Two-layer MLP: More capacity, possible overfitting on small datasets, slower convergence
  - Multilingual BERT: Broader coverage, diluted Turkish-specific features
  - Turkish-specific BERT: Richer linguistic priors, smaller pretraining corpus

- Failure signatures:
  - Low per-token accuracy but high per-sample accuracy → model memorizes frequent label sequences
  - High precision, low recall on rare labels → class imbalance or insufficient examples
  - No improvement with MLP → model capacity already sufficient or data too small

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train multilingual BERT with linear classifier; record per-token accuracy, macro F1
  2. Train Turkish-specific BERT (akdeniz27/xlm-roberta-base-turkish-ner) with MLP; compare metrics
  3. Perform ablation: remove MLP from best-performing model to confirm contribution

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the relative performances of Turkish-specific vs. multilingual BERT models change when applied to other Turkish NLP tasks (e.g., sentiment analysis, NER on general text)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that Turkish-specific models outperform multilingual models on the address parsing task and suggests future work on other NLP tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates address parsing; performance on other Turkish NLP tasks is not tested.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the observed advantage of a two-layer MLP over a single-layer classifier persist across different dataset sizes and model architectures (e.g., smaller or larger BERT variants)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors observe that the double-layer MLP outperforms the single-layer in smaller models like ELECTRA and DistilBERT, and suggest further investigation.
- Why unresolved: Only one dataset size and a limited set of model sizes were tested; results may not generalize.

### Open Question 3
- Question: To what extent does pre-training on NER data transfer to improved performance on address parsing for languages other than Turkish?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that models pre-trained on NER tasks perform best on Turkish address parsing and propose investigating this transfer for other languages.
- Why unresolved: Only Turkish data was used; cross-linguistic transfer effects are unstudied.

## Limitations

- The dataset size (1,248 samples) is relatively small for fine-tuning large pre-trained models, potentially limiting generalization
- No ablation study confirms whether MLP layer is the primary driver of improved performance
- The specific Petal Maps dataset used is not publicly available, making exact replication difficult
- Evaluation relies solely on macro-averaged metrics, which may not fully capture practical performance in imbalanced label scenarios

## Confidence

- High confidence: The general trend that Turkish-specific BERT models outperform multilingual ones in Turkish NLP tasks is supported by prior literature and the reported experimental results
- Medium confidence: The specific performance gains attributed to MLP fine-tuning are plausible but not conclusively proven due to lack of ablation studies and statistical significance testing
- Low confidence: The absolute values of token-wise accuracy (0.792) and macro F1 (0.497) are difficult to contextualize without comparisons to baseline or alternative architectures, and without access to the original dataset
