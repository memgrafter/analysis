# Process Knowledge-infused Learning for Clinician-friendly Explanations

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.09824
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09824
- **Reference count**: 6
- **Primary result**: PK-iL improves mental health assessment from social media by integrating clinical process knowledge with language models, achieving 70% agreement with users on explanations and up to 14-23% accuracy gains over baseline models.

## Executive Summary
Process Knowledge-infused Learning (PK-iL) addresses the challenge of explaining mental health assessments from social media by integrating clinical diagnostic processes with language models. The method combines fine-tuned language model embeddings with clinician-defined process knowledge structures like CSSRS and PHQ-9 to generate both accurate predictions and interpretable explanations. PK-iL demonstrates significant improvements over baseline models, achieving 70% agreement with users on explanations compared to 47% for traditional XAI methods, while also improving prediction accuracy by 14-23%.

## Method Summary
PK-iL is a framework that layers clinical process knowledge structures onto language model outputs to provide clinician-friendly explanations for mental health assessments. The method uses fine-tuned embeddings from language models (BERT, RoBERTa, SBERT) and evaluates them against condition embeddings derived from clinical guidelines using similarity functions. PK-iL optimizes a small number of threshold parameters that determine when conditions are satisfied, mapping these to final diagnostic labels. The approach generates explanations by listing the satisfied clinical conditions, making predictions interpretable within familiar diagnostic frameworks.

## Key Results
- PK-iL achieves 70% agreement with users on explanations, significantly outperforming baseline XAI methods at 47%
- Inter-rater agreement among annotators was 0.72
- PK-iL improves prediction accuracy by up to 14-23% over baseline models
- Explanations generated by PK-iL are grounded in clinical diagnostic criteria rather than opaque feature attributions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PK-iL improves model explainability by grounding predictions in clinician-defined diagnostic criteria.
- Mechanism: PK-iL layers clinical process knowledge structures (like CSSRS and PHQ-9) onto language model outputs, enabling predictions to be explained using familiar clinical concepts rather than opaque feature attributions.
- Core assumption: Clinicians can better interpret model decisions when explanations map to established diagnostic processes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] PK-iL provides clinician-friendly explanations for model predictions, addressing the limitations of existing approaches that do not leverage clinical diagnostic processes and lack interpretable outputs.
  - [section] PKiL incorporates process knowledge structures to provide explanations for model predictions using concepts that are familiar to a clinician.
- Break condition: If the process knowledge structures are incomplete or not aligned with actual clinical practice, the explanations may mislead rather than inform clinicians.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PK-iL improves performance by constraining the model to satisfy diagnostic process knowledge during training.
- Mechanism: By optimizing parameters so that language model embeddings satisfy condition thresholds derived from clinical guidelines, PK-iL ensures predictions align with clinically meaningful criteria, improving both accuracy and interpretability.
- Core assumption: Clinical process knowledge captures essential patterns for accurate mental health assessment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] PK-iL also improves performance, achieving up to 14-23% accuracy gains over baseline models.
  - [section] We want to solve for the unknown parameters θCj that yields minimum error between the parameterized approximation L(x, {θCj }) and the ground truth L(x).
- Break condition: If the process knowledge is overly rigid or mismatched to the data, the performance gains may not materialize, or the model may fail to generalize.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PK-iL bridges the gap between black-box language models and clinician trust by providing transparent, process-based explanations.
- Mechanism: Instead of relying on surrogate XAI models like LIME or SHAP, PK-iL directly uses clinical process knowledge to generate explanations, which clinicians find more trustworthy and actionable.
- Core assumption: Clinicians trust explanations that align with their own diagnostic reasoning processes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] PK-iL achieves 70% agreement with users on explanations, significantly outperforming other XAI methods at 47% (inter-rater agreement 0.72).
  - [section] PKiL explanations scored 70% vs 47% for the baseline models. We recorded an inter-annotator agreement of 0.72.
- Break condition: If clinicians do not recognize or accept the process knowledge as valid, the explanations will not improve trust or adoption.

  - Why needed here: These provide the clinical framework that PK-iL uses to generate interpretable explanations and constrain model predictions.
  - Quick check question: Can you name the six conditions in the CSSRS scale used for suicidality assessment?

- Concept: Embedding similarity and thresholding
  - Why needed here: PK-iL uses cosine similarity or Gaussian kernels to evaluate whether input text satisfies clinical conditions based on language model embeddings.
  - Quick check question: How does PK-iL determine if a condition like "wish to be dead" is satisfied in a given post?

- Concept: Optimization with few parameters
  - Why needed here: PK-iL only optimizes a small number of threshold parameters (one per condition), making training efficient while leveraging large language models.
  - Quick check question: Why does PK-iL only need to train a few additional parameters compared to fine-tuning the entire language model?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input: Social media text posts -> Language Model: Fine-tuned embeddings (BERT, RoBERTa, etc.) -> Process Knowledge: Clinical guidelines (CSSRS, PHQ-9) -> PK-iL Layer: Similarity evaluation and thresholding -> Output: Mental health assessment label + clinician-friendly explanations

- Critical path:
  1. Embed input text using fine-tuned language model.
  2. Compute similarity between text embeddings and condition embeddings.
  3. Apply learned thresholds to determine which conditions are satisfied.
  4. Map satisfied conditions to final diagnostic label.
  5. Generate explanation by listing satisfied conditions.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. interpretability: PK-iL prioritizes explanations aligned with clinical guidelines, which may limit flexibility compared to pure language models.
  - Data dependency: Requires process knowledge-augmented datasets; performance drops if such data is scarce or noisy.
  - Model complexity: Adds a lightweight inference layer but requires careful threshold tuning.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low inter-rater agreement on explanations: Indicates explanations are not clinically meaningful.
  - Performance worse than baseline: Suggests process knowledge is misaligned with data patterns.
  - Thresholds converge to extreme values: May indicate poor embedding alignment or incompatible process knowledge.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Reproduce baseline accuracy on CSSRS 2.0 without PK-iL to establish performance floor.
  2. Train PK-iL with cosine similarity and grid search thresholds; compare accuracy and explanation agreement.
  3. Swap in a different language model (e.g., SBERT) and repeat to test robustness across models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does PK-iL perform on datasets from other domains beyond mental health (e.g., physical health, education, or finance)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that PK-iL is a general framework that can leverage explicit representations of publicly available knowledge of processes and guidelines from various domains.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates PK-iL on mental health datasets (CSSRS 2.0 and PRIMATE), so its effectiveness in other domains is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating PK-iL on benchmark datasets from other domains (e.g., medical diagnosis guidelines, educational assessment rubrics, financial risk assessment processes) and comparing its performance to baseline methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does PK-iL handle dynamic or evolving process knowledge, such as updates to clinical guidelines or new symptoms/diagnostic criteria?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that PK-iL incorporates process knowledge structures, but do not discuss how it handles changes to these structures over time.
- Why unresolved: Clinical guidelines and diagnostic criteria can change, but the paper does not address how PK-iL adapts to such changes or whether it requires retraining from scratch.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing PK-iL's performance when process knowledge is incrementally updated or when the model is fine-tuned on new data while retaining previous knowledge.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the computational overhead of PK-iL compared to baseline language models, and how does it scale with larger datasets or more complex process knowledge?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that PK-iL requires training of additional parameters equal to the number of process knowledge conditions, but do not provide detailed computational complexity analysis.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows performance improvements, it does not discuss the computational cost or scalability of PK-iL compared to standard fine-tuning approaches.
- What evidence would resolve it: Runtime comparisons between PK-iL and baseline models on datasets of varying sizes, and analysis of how training/inference time scales with the number of process knowledge conditions or dataset size.

## Limitations

- The clinical interpretability claims are based on relatively small sample sizes (27 annotators) and may not generalize to broader clinical populations.
- Exact process knowledge condition embeddings and hyperparameter settings are not fully specified, making faithful reproduction challenging.
- The study relies on specific datasets (CSSRS 2.0, PRIMATE) that may not be publicly available, creating barriers to external validation.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core PK-iL architecture and its integration of clinical process knowledge with language models is well-specified and technically sound. The reported performance improvements (14-23% accuracy gains) and explanation agreement rates (70% vs 47% for baselines) are internally consistent with the described methodology.
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism by which PK-iL improves both accuracy and interpretability is plausible and supported by evidence, but the exact contribution of each component (embedding model choice, threshold optimization method, process knowledge quality) to the final performance is not fully isolated.
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the clinical interpretability results to diverse clinical settings and populations, and the robustness of PK-iL to variations in process knowledge quality or completeness.

## Next Checks

1. **Embedding Sensitivity Analysis**: Reproduce the main results using different embedding models (e.g., SBERT vs. RoBERTa) to assess how sensitive PK-iL performance and explanation quality are to the choice of language model.
2. **Process Knowledge Completeness Test**: Systematically remove or modify individual conditions from the CSSRS and PHQ-9 process knowledge and measure the impact on both accuracy and explanation agreement to identify which conditions are most critical.
3. **Clinical Expert Validation**: Conduct a follow-up study with a larger, more diverse group of clinical experts to validate the usability and trustworthiness of PK-iL explanations in real-world diagnostic scenarios, including assessment of potential biases or misinterpretations.
