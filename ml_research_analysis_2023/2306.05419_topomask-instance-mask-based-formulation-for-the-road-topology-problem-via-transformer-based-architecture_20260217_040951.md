# TopoMask: Instance-Mask-Based Formulation for the Road Topology Problem via Transformer-Based Architecture

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05419
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05419
- Reference count: 28
- Primary result: Achieved 4th place in OpenLane-V2 Score (OLS) and 2nd in centerline F1-score in OpenLane Topology Challenge 2023

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TopoMask, a novel instance-mask-based method for centerline detection in road topology that employs a transformer-based architecture. Unlike existing approaches that rely on keypoints or parametric methods, TopoMask directly predicts centerline instances as masks using Mask2Former in bird's eye view (BEV) space. The method incorporates a direction label representation to enrich mask instances with flow information, enabling ordered centerline point extraction. TopoMask demonstrates competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art methods like TopoNet, achieving high scores in both Frechet-based and Chamfer-based centerline detection metrics without using scene graph neural networks.

## Method Summary
TopoMask reformulates centerline detection as an instance segmentation problem using Mask2Former with a transformer-based architecture. The method processes multi-camera images through a BEV feature extractor (LSS) to create high-resolution bird's eye view maps, then applies Mask2Former to predict centerline instances as masks. A novel direction label representation assigns flow information based on point set monotonicity, allowing ordered centerline extraction during post-processing. The model is trained using AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0003 for 24 epochs on the OpenLane-V2 dataset, which contains 22,480 training samples from 6 cities.

## Key Results
- Ranked 4th in OpenLane-V2 Score (OLS) and 2nd in centerline F1-score in OpenLane Topology Challenge 2023
- Achieved competitive performance on Frechet-based centerline detection metrics
- Outperformed TopoNet on Chamfer-based centerline detection without using scene graph neural networks
- Demonstrated instance-mask formulation effectiveness without complex post-processing requirements

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Instance-mask-based formulation directly predicts centerline instances as masks, eliminating the need for complex post-processing like instance embedding or direction head fusion.
- Mechanism: Mask2Former treats BEV features as 2D image features and predicts each centerline as an instance mask. Each mask corresponds to a unique centerline instance.
- Core assumption: The BEV representation preserves the spatial topology needed to distinguish centerline instances as separate masks.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "TopoMask utilizes an instance-mask based formulation with a transformer-based architecture"; [section] "In this approach, each centerline instance is represented as a mask instance."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Direction label representation enriches mask instances with flow information, enabling ordered centerline point extraction without relying on parametric models.
- Mechanism: Direction labels (up, down, left, right) are assigned based on dominant monotonicity of the ground truth point set. During post-processing, points are ordered along the dominant axis.
- Core assumption: Most real centerlines are monotonic along one axis, so a 4-way direction label is sufficient to capture flow direction.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "A similar segmentation-based approach is employed in HDMapNet [7]. However, HDMapNet relies on semantic segmentation and requires additional instance embeddings and direction outputs... In contrast, our proposed formulation solely relies on the instance mask output with a novel direction label representation"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Transformer-based object detector (DAB-DETR) and BEV feature extractor (LSS) provide strong backbone for instance segmentation of centerlines in BEV space.
- Mechanism: DAB-DETR generates object queries that attend to BEV features; LSS creates high-resolution BEV map from multi-camera images.
- Core assumption: BEV features capture sufficient geometric and contextual information for accurate centerline instance segmentation.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "The study utilizes DAB-DETR [12] for the 2D object detection and LSS [17] for the BEV feature map creation with 200 × 104 BEV resolution."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Camera -> BEV Feature Extractor (LSS) -> Mask2Former -> Direction Prediction -> Post-processing -> Centerline output
- Critical path: Camera → BEV → Mask2Former → Direction Labels → Post-processing → Centerline output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using instance masks simplifies the pipeline but requires high BEV resolution
  - Direction labels add minimal overhead but assume monotonic centerlines
  - No scene graph neural network reduces complexity but may limit topological reasoning
- Failure signatures:
  - Low mAP in Chamfer/Frechet metrics → BEV resolution or mask quality issue
  - Incorrect centerline ordering → Direction label assignment error
  - Missing centerlines → Instance mask merging or false negatives
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate mask prediction mAP with ground truth direction labels to isolate mask quality
  2. Vary BEV resolution and measure impact on centerline detection performance
  3. Compare 4-way vs 8-way direction labeling on validation set with complex centerline geometries

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the inclusion of z-coordinate information impact TopoMask's centerline detection performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that TopoMask cannot predict z information and that assigning zero values to ground truth z leads to a Frechet-based detection score of approximately 0.92, indicating potential for improvement.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore methods to incorporate z-coordinate information into the model or evaluate its impact on performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing TopoMask's performance with and without z-coordinate information, or with different methods of incorporating z-coordinates.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of the direction label assignment strategy on the accuracy of centerline detection, especially for cases where point sets are not monotonic?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that some point sets of centerlines are not monotonic, but direction labels are assigned based on dominant monotonicity of each axis. It suggests that these cases should be addressed differently in future research.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how the direction label assignment strategy affects the accuracy of centerline detection, particularly for non-monotonic cases.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed analysis of the impact of direction label assignment on detection accuracy, including experiments with alternative assignment strategies for non-monotonic cases.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does TopoMask's performance compare to other state-of-the-art methods when evaluated on datasets other than OpenLane-V2?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates TopoMask's performance on the OpenLane-V2 dataset and compares it to TopoNet, but does not explore its performance on other datasets or in comparison to other methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison of TopoMask's performance across different datasets or against a wider range of state-of-the-art methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating TopoMask on multiple datasets and comparing its performance to other state-of-the-art methods on each dataset.

## Limitations

- The direction label representation assumes monotonic centerline geometry, which may not generalize to complex urban scenarios with frequent loops and sharp turns
- Performance gains are demonstrated only on the OpenLane-V2 dataset, limiting claims about broader applicability
- Computational overhead of transformer-based instance segmentation at high BEV resolution is not fully characterized

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The comparative performance claims against TopoNet on OpenLane-V2 benchmarks, as these are directly measured and reported
- Medium Confidence: The claim that instance masks eliminate the need for complex post-processing, as this is demonstrated but the break conditions for non-monotonic centerlines are not extensively validated
- Low Confidence: The generalization of the 4-way direction label representation to diverse real-world scenarios, as this novel contribution lacks extensive ablation studies across different geographic regions
