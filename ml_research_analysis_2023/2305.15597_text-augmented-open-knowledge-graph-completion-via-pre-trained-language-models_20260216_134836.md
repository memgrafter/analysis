# Text-Augmented Open Knowledge Graph Completion via Pre-Trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15597
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15597
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: TAGREAL achieves state-of-the-art performance on FB60K-NYT10 and UMLS-PubMed datasets, especially with limited training data.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces TAGREAL, an end-to-end framework for knowledge graph completion that leverages pre-trained language models (PLMs) and textual information from large corpora. The method automatically generates quality query prompts through pattern mining and retrieves support information to enhance PLM knowledge probing for KG completion. TAGREAL demonstrates superior performance compared to existing embedding-based, graph-based, and PLM-based methods, particularly in low-resource settings.

## Method Summary
TAGREAL consists of two main components: prompt generation and support information retrieval. The framework uses textual pattern mining methods (MetaPAD and TruePIE) to automatically discover reliable prompts from large text corpora, then retrieves relevant support texts using BM25. These prompts and support information are combined to probe knowledge from PLMs, which are then fine-tuned on labeled triples for KG completion tasks. The method employs an optimized prompt ensemble approach that weights prompts based on their individual performance on the PLM.

## Key Results
- TAGREAL achieves state-of-the-art performance on FB60K-NYT10 and UMLS-PubMed datasets
- The method outperforms existing embedding-based, graph-based, and PLM-based methods
- TAGREAL shows significant improvements particularly when training data is limited
- Optimized prompt ensemble outperforms manual prompts and equally weighted ensembles

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Prompt mining generates higher-quality prompts than manual prompt design
- Mechanism: Pattern mining methods (MetaPAD and TruePIE) discover reliable prompts from large text corpora using frequency, informativeness, completeness, and coverage as quality criteria
- Core assumption: Textual pattern mining methods are applicable to LM prompt mining because they share similar objectives and performance criteria
- Evidence anchors: Automatic generation of quality query prompts from large text corpora using pattern mining methods
- Break condition: If mined prompts fail to capture semantic relationships needed for KG completion

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Support information retrieval enhances PLM knowledge probing by providing contextual information
- Mechanism: BM25 retrieves relevant support texts that are concatenated with prompts to help PLMs understand the knowledge being probed
- Core assumption: Retrieved support information is relevant and helpful for PLM understanding
- Evidence anchors: Retrieval of support information from large text corpora to enhance PLM knowledge probing
- Break condition: If retrieved support information is not relevant or helpful

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Optimized prompt ensemble outperforms manual prompts and equally weighted ensembles
- Mechanism: Prompts are weighted based on their individual performance on the PLM to reduce negative effects of poor-quality prompts
- Core assumption: Weighting prompts based on PLM performance improves overall ensemble quality
- Evidence anchors: Optimized prompt weighting approach for ensemble creation
- Break condition: If weighting prompts doesn't improve ensemble quality

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pattern mining (MetaPAD, TruePIE) -> Prompt optimization -> Support information retrieval (BM25) -> PLM fine-tuning -> KG completion
- Critical path: Generate quality prompts → Retrieve support information → Create PLM input → Fine-tune PLM on labeled triples → Perform inference for link prediction
- Design tradeoffs: Automatic prompt generation trades manual effort for potential quality improvements but introduces pipeline complexity
- Failure signatures: Poor prompt quality, irrelevant support information retrieval, or ineffective PLM fine-tuning leads to poor KG completion performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the quality of automatically generated prompts be further improved beyond MetaPAD and TruePIE methods?
- Basis in paper: The paper acknowledges that some manually created prompts may outperform automatically generated ones due to higher quality
- Why unresolved: Current methods might still include lower-quality prompts in the ensemble
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing TAGREAL with alternative prompt generation methods (e.g., using large language models or incorporating semantic similarity metrics)

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does TAGREAL performance vary with different sizes of entity set used for re-ranking (hyperparameter X)?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions a trade-off between efficiency and accuracy when using re-ranking framework with different X values
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't explore the impact of varying X within each training size
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing different X values for each training data size

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does TAGREAL perform on datasets with different levels of noise in the knowledge graph?
- Basis in paper: The paper acknowledges that KG completion model performance degrades with noisy data
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't evaluate TAGREAL's performance on datasets with varying noise levels
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing TAGREAL on datasets with different noise levels

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of pattern mining methods for prompt generation relies on assumptions about their applicability to LM prompt mining without direct quality evaluation
- Lack of ablation studies isolating the contribution of support information retrieval versus prompt quality to overall performance
- The choice of X entities for re-ranking is mentioned but not thoroughly analyzed for efficiency versus accuracy trade-offs

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Core empirical results showing TAGREAL outperforming baseline methods on both datasets, particularly under low-resource settings
- **Medium Confidence**: Effectiveness of prompt optimization framework and weighting scheme, as improvement is demonstrated but optimal weighting methods aren't established
- **Medium Confidence**: Claim that support information retrieval meaningfully enhances PLM knowledge probing, as inclusion improves results but specific contribution isn't quantified

## Next Checks

1. **Prompt Quality Evaluation**: Conduct human evaluation comparing automatically mined prompts against manually designed prompts, assessing relevance, informativeness, and ability to capture semantic relationships

2. **Support Information Ablation**: Perform controlled experiments varying quantity and relevance of support information (top-k retrieval, random retrieval, no retrieval) to quantify marginal contribution to performance

3. **Cross-Dataset Prompt Transferability**: Test whether prompts optimized for one dataset can be effectively transferred to similar KGs with different corpora, evaluating generalizability across knowledge domains
