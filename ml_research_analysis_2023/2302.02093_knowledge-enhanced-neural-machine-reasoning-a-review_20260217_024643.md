# Knowledge-enhanced Neural Machine Reasoning: A Review

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.02093
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02093
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First comprehensive survey of knowledge-enhanced neural machine reasoning techniques published after 2020

## Executive Summary
This survey provides the first comprehensive overview of knowledge-enhanced neural machine reasoning techniques published after 2020. The authors propose a novel taxonomy categorizing existing methods into explicit knowledge (knowledge graphs, conceptual/encyclopedic knowledge, and rules) and implicit knowledge (pre-trained models and memory-augmented networks). The survey covers major application domains including question answering, knowledge completion, explainable recommendation, and visual reasoning, while identifying key challenges and future research directions.

## Method Summary
The authors systematically reviewed and categorized knowledge-enhanced neural machine reasoning techniques published after 2020. They developed a novel taxonomy that distinguishes between explicit knowledge sources (knowledge graphs, conceptual knowledge, rules) and implicit knowledge sources (pre-trained models, memory networks). The survey methodology involved identifying major application domains, analyzing technical approaches, and synthesizing challenges and future directions from the literature.

## Key Results
- Proposed novel taxonomy categorizing knowledge-enhanced neural machine reasoning into explicit and implicit knowledge approaches
- Identified rule-induced representations and rule-guided inference as key techniques for explicit knowledge integration
- Highlighted discriminative answer prediction and conditional knowledge generation from pre-trained models for implicit knowledge integration
- Covered major application domains including question answering, knowledge completion, explainable recommendation, and visual reasoning
- Identified key challenges including effective knowledge integration, bridging external knowledge with deep models, and tractability/optimization issues

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The survey works by providing a systematic framework for understanding how external knowledge can be integrated with neural networks for reasoning tasks. The taxonomy separates knowledge sources into explicit (structured knowledge like KGs and rules) and implicit (pattern-based learning from pre-trained models), which helps clarify the different approaches to knowledge integration. This separation allows researchers to understand when and how to apply different techniques based on the nature of the knowledge and the reasoning task.

## Foundational Learning
- Knowledge Graph Integration: Understanding how to represent and integrate structured knowledge from knowledge graphs into neural architectures
  - Why needed: Provides structured, interpretable knowledge for reasoning systems
  - Quick check: Can the system retrieve and utilize relevant facts from a KG for a given query

- Rule-based Reasoning: Ability to incorporate logical rules and constraints into neural reasoning processes
  - Why needed: Enables explainable and interpretable reasoning with clear decision paths
  - Quick check: Can the system follow logical inference chains and justify its conclusions

- Pre-trained Language Models: Understanding how to leverage large language models for implicit knowledge integration
  - Why needed: Provides access to vast amounts of world knowledge learned during pre-training
  - Quick check: Can the system generate relevant knowledge or answers conditioned on input queries

- Memory Networks: Understanding attention-based memory architectures for storing and retrieving knowledge
  - Why needed: Enables dynamic storage and retrieval of knowledge during reasoning
  - Quick check: Can the system effectively store and retrieve relevant memories for reasoning tasks

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Knowledge Source -> Integration Module -> Reasoning Engine -> Output Layer

Critical Path:
1. Knowledge Retrieval from external sources
2. Knowledge Representation and Encoding
3. Integration with neural reasoning components
4. Reasoning and Inference
5. Answer Generation or Prediction

Design Tradeoffs:
- Explicit vs Implicit Knowledge: Explicit knowledge provides interpretability but requires structured data, while implicit knowledge is more flexible but less explainable
- Retrieval-based vs Embedding-based Integration: Retrieval provides exact knowledge but is computationally expensive, while embeddings are efficient but may lose information
- Rule-based vs Pattern-based Reasoning: Rules provide clear logic but are rigid, patterns are flexible but may lack justification

Failure Signatures:
- Poor performance when external knowledge is missing or incorrect
- Computational inefficiency when retrieving large amounts of knowledge
- Inability to handle novel situations not covered by available knowledge
- Difficulty balancing between explicit rules and implicit patterns

First Experiments:
1. Simple QA task with knowledge graph integration to test basic knowledge retrieval and utilization
2. Rule-based reasoning task to verify logical inference capabilities
3. Pre-trained model fine-tuning on a specific domain to test implicit knowledge integration

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can knowledge-enhanced neural machine reasoning systems effectively balance between explicit rule-based reasoning and implicit pattern-based learning from large language models?
- Basis in paper: "it is an open research problem to develop a 'work in tandem' technique for both methodologies" mentioned in the challenges section
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a key challenge but does not provide specific solutions or frameworks for achieving this balance, which requires understanding when to rely on explicit knowledge versus implicit knowledge in different reasoning scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and empirical evaluation of hybrid systems that can dynamically switch between rule-based and pattern-based reasoning, with clear criteria for when each approach should be applied

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the optimal methods for integrating external knowledge into deep learning models while minimizing computational overhead and maintaining model efficiency?
- Basis in paper: "Issues in Bridging the External Knowledge and Deep Models" section discusses challenges with retrieval, memory reuse, and computational power requirements
- Why unresolved: The paper outlines multiple approaches (retrieval-based vs embedding-based) but does not provide conclusive evidence about which methods offer the best trade-off between performance and efficiency across different application domains
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies across multiple domains measuring both reasoning performance and computational efficiency for different knowledge integration methods

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can knowledge-enhanced reasoning systems advance from association-based reasoning to genuine causal reasoning as described in Pearl's ladder of causation?
- Basis in paper: "The Ladder of Causation" section mentions that current approaches are "still stuck at the beginning level" of the causation ladder
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies this as a future direction, it does not propose specific architectures or methodologies for moving from correlation-based to intervention and counterfactual-based reasoning in knowledge-enhanced systems
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstration of knowledge-enhanced systems successfully performing intervention and counterfactual reasoning tasks with measurable improvements over association-only approaches

## Limitations

- Taxonomy may oversimplify complex relationships between explicit and implicit knowledge integration methods
- Emphasis on future directions remains speculative without concrete implementation examples
