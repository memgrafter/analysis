# Once Detected, Never Lost: Surpassing Human Performance in Offline LiDAR based 3D Object Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12315
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12315
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposes track-centric learning approach that achieves 0.48% missed objects rate, surpassing human annotation performance on Waymo Open Dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel offline LiDAR-based 3D object detection system that achieves human-level annotation accuracy by leveraging track-centric learning inspired by human annotation behavior. The key innovation is a two-stage approach where objects are first detected and then their complete tracks are refined using bidirectional tracking and temporal coherence. The method significantly outperforms previous state-of-the-art approaches on the Waymo Open Dataset, with only 0.48% of objects completely missed.

The approach consists of three main components: a base detector that generates initial proposals, a bidirectional tracking module that propagates information forward and backward through time, and a track-centric learning module that refines all proposals in a track simultaneously. By focusing on track-level refinement rather than frame-by-frame detection, the system mimics how human annotators label objects consistently across multiple frames.

## Method Summary
The proposed method takes a track-centric approach to offline 3D object detection by first detecting objects frame-by-frame using a base detector, then refining detections through bidirectional temporal propagation. The system samples 9 frames of LiDAR data using a frame-skipping strategy and processes them through a two-stage pipeline. In the first stage, a base detector generates initial proposals for each frame. In the second stage, a bidirectional tracking module propagates detections forward and backward in time to fill in missing detections and extend tracks. Finally, a track-centric learning module refines all proposals within each track simultaneously, leveraging temporal coherence to improve detection accuracy. This approach is inspired by human annotation behavior where annotators label objects consistently across multiple frames rather than treating each frame independently.

## Key Results
- Achieves 0.48% missed objects rate on Waymo Open Dataset, surpassing human annotation performance
- Significant improvement over state-of-the-art methods in terms of detection recall and precision
- Track-centric learning effectively leverages temporal coherence to refine object detections across multiple frames

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by mimicking human annotation behavior, where objects are first detected and then their tracks are refined consistently across multiple frames. By using bidirectional tracking, the system can propagate information both forward and backward in time, filling in missing detections and extending tracks. The track-centric learning module then refines all proposals in a track simultaneously, leveraging temporal coherence to improve detection accuracy. This approach addresses the limitations of frame-by-frame detection methods, which can miss objects due to occlusions or sensor noise in individual frames.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Base Detector -> Bidirectional Tracking -> Track-centric Learning Module

**Critical Path**: The critical path for inference involves processing 9 sampled frames through the base detector to generate initial proposals, then propagating these proposals through the bidirectional tracking module, and finally refining the proposals using the track-centric learning module.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades increased computational complexity (due to processing multiple frames and refining entire tracks) for improved detection accuracy and robustness to occlusions. The frame-skipping strategy balances temporal resolution with computational efficiency.

**Failure Signatures**: The system may struggle with objects that have non-linear motion patterns, as the bidirectional tracking assumes temporal coherence. Objects that are occluded for extended periods may have incomplete tracks, leading to less accurate refinements.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of the proposed method scale with the number of input frames used for detection?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that they sample 9 frames of data and use a frame-skipping strategy, but does not explore the effect of varying the number of input frames.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed track-centric approach, but does not conduct an ablation study on the number of input frames.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying numbers of input frames (e.g., 3, 6, 12) and comparing the performance would provide insights into the optimal number of frames for the proposed method.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the proposed method handle occlusions and object interactions in complex scenes?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the bidirectional tracking module helps to fill in missing frames and extend tracks, but does not provide a detailed analysis of how the method handles occlusions and object interactions.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the overall performance of the proposed method, but does not provide a comprehensive analysis of its behavior in challenging scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with datasets that contain a high number of occlusions and object interactions, and analyzing the performance of the proposed method in these scenarios, would provide insights into its robustness and limitations.

- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the potential benefits of using different sensor modalities or the impact of sensor fusion on the performance of the proposed method.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that compare the proposed method to state-of-the-art methods that use different sensor modalities, and analyzing the performance of sensor fusion approaches, would provide insights into the advantages and limitations of the proposed method in different sensor setups.

## Limitations

- The evaluation primarily focuses on detection recall and may not fully capture other important factors like localization precision, classification accuracy, or false positive rates.
- The method's reliance on temporal coherence may limit its effectiveness in scenarios with abrupt object movements, occlusions, or sensor failures.
- The reported 0.48% missed objects rate is impressive but should be interpreted cautiously without detailed breakdown across object classes, occlusion levels, and distance ranges.

## Confidence

- Performance claims on Waymo Open Dataset: High confidence in relative performance improvements over stated baselines, but Medium confidence in absolute human performance comparison due to limited transparency in human annotation evaluation methodology.
- Track-centric learning effectiveness: High confidence in the architectural innovation and its theoretical advantages, but Medium confidence in generalization to diverse real-world scenarios not represented in the evaluation dataset.
- Missing objects rate: High confidence in the reported statistic, but Low confidence in its generalizability without more detailed error analysis across different conditions.
