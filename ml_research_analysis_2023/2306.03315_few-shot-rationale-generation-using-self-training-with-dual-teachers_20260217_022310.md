# Few Shot Rationale Generation using Self-Training with Dual Teachers

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.03315
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03315
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Primary result:** Achieves up to 5% higher accuracy and 5 BLEU points better explanation quality than few-shot baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a dual-teacher self-training framework for generating free-text explanations (rationales) in low-data settings. The approach decomposes the joint label-rationale prediction task into separate conditional models—a predictor for task labels and a rationalizer for generating explanations conditioned on both input and predicted labels. The rationalizer is trained with a Masked Label Regularization (MLR) loss that encourages faithfulness by forcing maximal uncertainty when label tokens are masked. Pseudo-labels from both teachers are used to train a joint student model via knowledge distillation. Evaluated on three benchmark datasets, the method achieves significant improvements over few-shot baselines while narrowing the gap to fully supervised performance.

## Method Summary
The proposed dual-teacher self-training framework separates the joint label-rationale prediction task into two components: a predictor that outputs task labels and a rationalizer that generates explanations conditioned on both input and predicted labels. The rationalizer uses Masked Label Regularization (MLR) loss to enforce faithfulness by penalizing confident predictions when label tokens are masked. Both teachers generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled data, which are then used to train a joint student model through knowledge distillation. This decomposition allows each component to specialize in its respective task while maintaining coherence between labels and rationales.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 5% higher accuracy compared to few-shot baselines
- Improves explanation quality by up to 5 BLEU points
- Increases faithfulness scores (simulatability) by over 10%
- Significantly narrows the performance gap to fully supervised methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by decomposing the complex joint prediction task into specialized components that can be trained more effectively in low-data regimes. The predictor focuses solely on accurate label prediction without the distraction of generating explanations, while the rationalizer specializes in producing faithful explanations conditioned on both input and predicted labels. The MLR loss ensures the rationalizer remains faithful to the predicted labels by forcing uncertainty when label information is unavailable. Self-training with dual teachers allows the model to leverage unlabeled data effectively, while knowledge distillation transfers the specialized capabilities of both teachers to a unified student model.

## Foundational Learning
- **Dual-teacher self-training:** Using two specialized models to generate pseudo-labels for training a student model; needed to handle the complexity of joint prediction tasks in low-data settings
- **Masked Label Regularization (MLR):** A loss function that encourages faithfulness by penalizing confident predictions when label tokens are masked; needed to ensure rationales align with predicted labels
- **Knowledge distillation:** Transferring knowledge from teacher models to a student model; needed to combine the strengths of specialized components into a unified model
- **Conditional generation:** Generating outputs conditioned on both input and predicted labels; needed to produce coherent rationales that explain specific predictions
- **Faithfulness evaluation:** Measuring whether generated rationales accurately reflect the reasoning behind predictions; needed to assess the quality of explanations beyond standard metrics

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Predictor -> Pseudo-labels -> Student (joint model) & Rationalizer -> Pseudo-rationales -> Student (joint model)

**Critical path:** Input text → Predictor → Predicted label → Rationalizer → Generated rationale → Student training (via knowledge distillation)

**Design tradeoffs:** Specialization vs. integration (separate teachers for better performance vs. increased complexity), faithfulness vs. fluency (MLR loss may reduce natural language quality), computational cost vs. performance (dual teachers require more resources but achieve better results)

**Failure signatures:** Inconsistent labels and rationales (teachers disagree), poor-quality pseudo-labels propagating through self-training, MLR loss causing overly cautious or unnatural rationale generation, knowledge distillation failing to merge teacher capabilities effectively

**3 first experiments:** 1) Test predictor accuracy in isolation to verify label prediction capability, 2) Evaluate rationalizer faithfulness with varying MLR loss weights, 3) Compare student model performance with different teacher pseudo-label combinations

## Limitations
- Reliance on synthetic pseudo-labels may propagate errors, especially in extremely low-data regimes
- MLR loss assumes uniform label token distribution when masked, which may not hold across all datasets
- Evaluation metrics like simulatability and BLEU may not fully capture free-text rationale quality and faithfulness
- Method effectiveness may depend on having sufficiently capable teacher models for generating reasonable pseudo-labels

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in overall framework design and reported improvements over baselines
- **Medium confidence** in generalization of results across different few-shot scenarios and datasets
- **Low confidence** in absolute quality of generated rationales beyond specific evaluation metrics used
