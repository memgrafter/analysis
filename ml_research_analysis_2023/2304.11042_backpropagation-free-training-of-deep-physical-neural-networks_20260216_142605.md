# Backpropagation-free Training of Deep Physical Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11042
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11042
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 0
- Physical neural networks trained without backpropagation using model-free forward-forward algorithm

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a model-free forward-forward (MF-FF) training algorithm for physical neural networks (PNNs) that eliminates the need for backpropagation through digital models. The approach uses two physical forward passes—one with correct labels and one with incorrect labels—to create a local training signal for each layer without requiring detailed knowledge of the nonlinear physical layers' properties. The method was experimentally validated on three different wave-based physical systems (acoustic, microwave, and optical) for vowel and image classification tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to in-silico training and physics-aware backpropagation, particularly in maintaining accuracy when physical systems undergo unpredictable external perturbations.

## Method Summary
The MF-FF algorithm trains physical neural networks by using two physical forward passes through the system—a positive pass with correct labels and a negative pass with incorrect labels. Each layer's trainable matrix multiplication is updated by comparing the "goodness" (sum of squared activities) between these two passes, creating a local training signal without gradients. This eliminates the need for backpropagation through digital models while maintaining training accuracy even when physical systems experience unpredictable perturbations. The approach was experimentally implemented using acoustic chaotic cavities with nonlinear scatterers, microwave cavities with programmable metasurfaces, and optical multimode fibers with readout nonlinearity.

## Key Results
- Acoustic-PNN achieved 97.62% test accuracy for vowel classification
- Microwave-PNN reached 95.76% accuracy for vowel classification
- Optical-PNN obtained 96.73% and 97.21% accuracy for vowel and MNIST datasets respectively
- MF-FF maintained accuracy during unpredictable perturbations while PA-BP failed

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The model-free forward-forward algorithm trains physical neural networks without requiring a differentiable model of the nonlinear physical layers by using two physical forward passes—one with correct labels (positive) and one with incorrect labels (negative). The trainable weights are updated by comparing the "goodness" of these two passes locally in each layer, enabling contrastive learning directly in the physical system.

### Mechanism 2
The MF-FF approach maintains training accuracy during unpredictable external perturbations because it trains each layer locally using only physical forward passes, avoiding gradient accumulation errors through backpropagation. When perturbations occur, the physical forward passes automatically reflect the new system state, allowing the network to adapt without requiring a perfect digital model.

### Mechanism 3
MF-FF reduces digital computation and power consumption during training compared to backpropagation-based methods by eliminating the backward pass through a digital model. This removes the need for gradient computation, backpropagation implementation, and storage of intermediate values for gradient calculation, with training happening entirely through physical forward passes.

  - Why needed here: The entire approach relies on using physical systems (acoustic, microwave, optical) as nonlinear computational elements
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes a physical neural network from a traditional digital neural network?

  - Why needed here: The architecture combines fixed physical nonlinear transformations with trainable linear multiplications, similar to but different from traditional reservoir computing
  - Quick check question: How does training only the linear layers while keeping physical transformations fixed affect network expressivity?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data encoding -> Physical nonlinear transformer -> Trainable linear multiplication -> Measurement readout -> Goodness calculation -> Weight update

- Critical path: 1. Encode input data onto physical input medium 2. Pass through physical nonlinear transformer 3. Apply trainable linear multiplication 4. Measure output 5. Calculate goodness for positive and negative passes 6. Update trainable weights based on goodness difference

- Design tradeoffs: Fixed vs trainable physical nonlinearities, number of physical forward passes per training iteration (two: positive and negative), digital vs physical implementation of trainable multiplications, readout mechanism for accessing intermediate layer outputs

- Failure signatures: Inconsistent measurements between positive and negative passes indicate physical system instability, poor convergence suggests inadequate nonlinearity or insufficient mixing in physical system, high energy consumption per forward pass reduces efficiency gains

Implement two-layer network with skip connections and test on multi-class classification (full vowel dataset)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the MF-FF training algorithm perform when scaling to larger, deeper networks with billions of parameters, as required for state-of-the-art deep learning models?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the potential of PNNs to improve energy efficiency and speed in deep learning, but notes that scaling to billions of parameters remains a challenge
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on experimental validation of MF-FF with relatively small networks (2-6 layers) for classification tasks
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing successful training of MF-FF on PNNs with billions of parameters, demonstrating comparable performance to digital training methods while maintaining energy efficiency and speed benefits

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical limit of perturbation intensity that MF-FF can tolerate before accuracy degradation becomes irreversible?
- Basis in paper: The paper demonstrates MF-FF's robustness to perturbations by adding Gaussian noise to the transmission matrix of an optical setup
- Why unresolved: The paper tests a range of perturbation intensities but does not establish the theoretical limit of perturbation tolerance
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying perturbation intensity across multiple orders of magnitude, establishing a clear threshold where MF-FF fails to recover accuracy

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the energy consumption of MF-FF training compare to digital backpropagation when accounting for the complete hardware setup including wave-to-digital readout mechanisms?
- Basis in paper: The paper claims that MF-FF reduces power consumption in physical systems, particularly optics, and decreases digital computations
- Why unresolved: While the paper discusses theoretical advantages in energy efficiency, it lacks quantitative energy consumption data for the entire training process
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive energy consumption measurements for both MF-FF and digital backpropagation across multiple training epochs, including all hardware components

## Limitations

- Physical implementation details for the "goodness" function measurement are underspecified across different systems
- Energy consumption comparisons between MF-FF and digital backpropagation lack complete accounting for hardware overhead
- Scalability to billion-parameter networks has not been demonstrated experimentally

## Confidence

- Algorithm correctness: High - mathematical framework is clearly specified and theoretically sound
- Experimental reproducibility: Medium - physical system descriptions lack complete technical specifications
- Claims about perturbation robustness: Medium - results are promising but need independent validation across different physical systems
