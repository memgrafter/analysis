# Vocabulary-informed Zero-shot and Open-set Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2301.00998
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00998
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Achieves 90.79% accuracy on AwA and 9.26% on ImageNet zero-shot tasks with vocabularies up to 310K classes

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes vocabulary-informed learning, a unified framework for supervised, zero-shot, generalized zero-shot, and open set recognition that leverages a large open vocabulary of semantic entities. The core method is a weighted maximum margin embedding that jointly learns visual-semantic mappings by enforcing distance constraints between training samples and their correct class prototypes, while also maintaining separation from both supervised and unsupervised vocabulary atoms. Class-specific weighting derived from margin distributions further improves the embedding. Experiments on Animals with Attributes and ImageNet show significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods in all recognition settings, achieving up to 90.79% accuracy on AwA and 9.26% on ImageNet zero-shot tasks, even with only a few training samples per class and vocabularies of up to 310K labels.

## Method Summary
The method learns a linear projection from image features to semantic space such that labeled training samples are closer to their correct class prototypes than to any other prototype (both from supervised and unsupervised vocabularies). This enforces large margin constraints across the entire open vocabulary. Class-specific weighting derived from margin distributions addresses hubness and class density imbalances. The framework jointly optimizes data terms (projecting samples to correct prototypes) and triplet terms (ensuring separation from incorrect prototypes), using class weights estimated from extreme value theory on margin distributions.

## Key Results
- Achieves 90.79% accuracy on Animals with Attributes (AwA) zero-shot learning
- Achieves 9.26% accuracy on ImageNet zero-shot learning with 310K vocabulary
- Demonstrates consistent improvements across supervised, zero-shot, G-ZSL, and open-set settings
- Performs well with only a few training samples per class

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Joint visual-semantic embedding with vocabulary-informed constraints improves generalization across supervised, zero-shot, and open-set settings. The model learns a linear projection from image features to semantic space such that labeled training samples are closer to their correct class prototypes than to any other prototype (both from supervised and unsupervised vocabularies). This enforces large margin constraints across the entire open vocabulary.

### Mechanism 2
Class-specific weighting derived from margin distributions improves embedding quality by addressing hubness and class density imbalances. The model estimates the density/coverage of each known class in semantic space using extreme value theory on margin distributions. This weighting is then used to adjust the importance of each class during embedding learning.

### Mechanism 3
Vocabulary-informed learning transfers discriminative information from open vocabulary to improve few-shot learning performance. By incorporating constraints from 310K vocabulary atoms, the model learns to distinguish between classes even with limited training data per class. This leverages the semantic relationships encoded in word vectors.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Visual feature extractor (ResNet101/VGG-19) → Linear projection W → Semantic space → Margin constraints → Class weights → Nearest neighbor classification
- **Critical path:** Visual features → Linear embedding W → Semantic distance computation → Nearest neighbor classification
  - W is learned to satisfy margin constraints across vocabulary
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Linear vs. deep embedding: Linear is faster and interpretable but may lack capacity for complex mappings
  - Vocabulary size: Larger vocabularies provide more constraints but increase computational cost
  - Margin constants (C): Balance between fitting training data and enforcing discrimination
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor zero-shot performance: Likely semantic embeddings don't capture class relationships well
  - Degradation on supervised tasks: Vocabulary constraints may be too restrictive for source classes
  - Slow convergence: Too many margin constraints or inappropriate learning rate
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Verify embedding W maps training samples closer to correct prototypes than random ones in semantic space
  2. Test margin distribution estimation by comparing estimated Weibull parameters to empirical distributions
  3. Evaluate class weighting by training with and without weights on few-shot source classes

## Limitations
- Semantic word vectors may not adequately capture class relationships for fine-grained distinctions or culturally specific concepts
- Linear projection may limit representational capacity compared to deeper architectures
- Weibull distribution assumption for margin density estimation could fail with limited samples

## Confidence

- **High**: Core experimental results showing performance improvements over baselines (9.26% ImageNet zero-shot accuracy, 90.79% AwA accuracy)
- **Medium**: Theoretical claims about EVT-based weighting effectiveness and generalization across all recognition settings
- **Low**: Claims about the framework's applicability to truly open-world scenarios with continuously evolving vocabularies
