# DiffSketching: Sketch Control Image Synthesis with Diffusion Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18812
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18812
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: First diffusion model approach for cross-domain sketch-to-image synthesis, achieving FID 6.46 and IS 89.91 on ImageNet/Sketchy

## Executive Summary
DiffSketching introduces the first diffusion model method for cross-domain sketch-to-image synthesis, enabling high-quality image generation directly from user sketches without requiring paired sketch-image datasets. By integrating cross-domain constraints—perceptual sketch loss and image identity loss—with classifier guidance, the model steers the denoising process toward images that match both the sketch structure and target categories. It outperforms prior GAN-based approaches (USPS, MUNIT, Sketch-YOG) on standard benchmarks and supports editing and interpolation tasks conditioned on sketches.

## Method Summary
DiffSketching leverages a pretrained diffusion model and modifies its denoising process by adding cross-domain constraints to preserve sketch fidelity and image identity. It uses perceptual sketch loss to maintain structural alignment with the input sketch, image identity loss to retain source image features when applicable, and classifier guidance to improve category alignment. The approach works with unpaired data, avoiding the need for large paired sketch-image datasets. During training, these losses are combined with the diffusion model's standard loss, enabling end-to-end optimization while conditioning on sketches.

## Key Results
- Achieves FID of 6.46 and IS of 89.91 on ImageNet and Sketchy benchmarks
- Outperforms baselines (USPS, MUNIT, Sketch-YOG) in both quantitative metrics and human evaluation
- Supports image editing and interpolation tasks conditioned on sketches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
DiffSketching succeeds by integrating sketch-specific guidance into the diffusion denoising process, allowing the model to preserve sketch structure while generating realistic images. The cross-domain losses (perceptual sketch and identity) ensure structural and feature consistency, while classifier guidance enforces category alignment without requiring paired data. This design overcomes limitations of GAN-based methods that struggle with unpaired data and domain gaps.

## Foundational Learning
- **Cross-domain constraints**: Needed to bridge the gap between sketches and images when paired data is unavailable; quick check: verify loss terms preserve sketch structure and image features.
- **Classifier guidance**: Required to steer generation toward target categories without paired supervision; quick check: ensure classifier outputs align with desired categories.
- **Diffusion model conditioning**: Essential for incorporating sketch information during denoising; quick check: confirm conditioning layers correctly modulate the generation process.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Pretrained diffusion model -> Cross-domain loss integration -> Classifier guidance -> Output image
- **Critical path**: Input sketch -> Conditioning injection -> Denoising steps with losses -> Final image
- **Design tradeoffs**: Avoids paired dataset requirement (flexibility) but depends on external pretrained models (dependency risk)
- **Failure signatures**: Poor sketch-structure preservation (loss terms ineffective), category misalignment (classifier guidance weak), or unrealistic outputs (diffusion model limitations)
- **First experiments**: 1) Test perceptual sketch loss alone on simple sketches; 2) Evaluate classifier guidance impact on category alignment; 3) Assess identity loss contribution when source images are available

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on curated benchmarks that may not reflect real-world sketch variability
- Dependency on pretrained diffusion models and classifiers limits reproducibility and control
- Lack of rigorous ablation studies to isolate the impact of individual loss terms
- Assumes reliable pretrained classifiers exist for target domains, which may not hold universally

## Confidence
- High confidence in the core innovation of integrating cross-domain constraints and classifier guidance into diffusion models for sketch control
- Medium confidence in superiority over GAN-based baselines, as comparisons are limited to specific models and datasets
- Low confidence in generalization to highly abstract or out-of-distribution sketches, as this is not extensively tested

## Next Checks
1) Conduct ablation studies to isolate the impact of perceptual sketch loss, identity loss, and classifier guidance on final outputs
2) Test the model on user-generated sketches from diverse domains to evaluate robustness beyond curated benchmarks
3) Evaluate performance degradation or stability when pretrained diffusion models or classifiers are replaced with alternatives from different training regimes
