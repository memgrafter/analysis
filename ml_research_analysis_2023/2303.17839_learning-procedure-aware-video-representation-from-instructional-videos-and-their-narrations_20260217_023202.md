# Learning Procedure-aware Video Representation from Instructional Videos and Their Narrations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17839
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17839
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Joint learning of video encoder and diffusion model improves step classification (+2.8%/+3.3%) and forecasting (+7.4%) on procedural video datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach for learning procedure-aware video representations from instructional videos and their narrations without requiring human annotations. The method jointly trains a video encoder to capture individual step concepts and a diffusion-based probabilistic model to model temporal dependencies among steps. By leveraging CLIP embeddings as pseudo labels, the model learns from large-scale instructional video datasets like HowTo100M without manual annotations. Experiments demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods on both step classification and forecasting tasks across COIN and EPIC-Kitchens-100 datasets, even in zero-shot settings.

## Method Summary
The proposed method learns procedure-aware video representations by jointly optimizing a video encoder (TimeSformer) and a diffusion-based denoising model. The approach uses narrations from automatic speech recognition (ASR) to create pseudo labels through CLIP embeddings, avoiding the need for manual annotations. The diffusion model captures the distribution of video representations for missing steps in procedural sequences, allowing the model to handle temporal dependencies and variations in step ordering. During training, the model randomly masks one video clip and uses the diffusion process to predict its representation based on neighboring steps, then matches the prediction to text embeddings from CLIP.

## Key Results
- Step classification accuracy improves by +2.8% on COIN and +3.3% on EPIC-Kitchens-100 compared to state-of-the-art methods
- Step forecasting accuracy improves by +7.4% on COIN dataset
- Zero-shot inference capabilities demonstrated without requiring fine-tuning on target datasets
- The model can generate diverse and plausible predictions for incomplete procedures by sampling from the learned probabilistic model

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The diffusion model captures temporal dependencies and variations in step ordering more effectively than masked prediction methods.
- Mechanism: By modeling the conditional probability of a missing step's video representation given its neighbors through a diffusion process, the model learns to characterize the distribution of plausible steps rather than just the most likely one.
- Core assumption: The temporal ordering of steps in procedural activities can be effectively captured by a diffusion process that gradually adds and removes noise from video representations.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Video encoder (TimeSformer) -> Diffusion model (Transformer) -> Classification head
  - Text encoder (CLIP) -> Pseudo label generator -> Video-text alignment loss

- Critical path:
  1. Input video clips are encoded by the video encoder
  2. One clip is randomly masked, and its representation is predicted by the diffusion model
  3. The predicted representation is matched to text embeddings from CLIP
  4. Loss is computed based on the alignment between predicted and true representations

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a diffusion model allows for multiple plausible predictions but increases computational complexity
  - Relying on CLIP for pseudo labels reduces noise but may introduce biases from CLIP's pre-training
  - Joint learning of representation and temporal model provides better understanding but requires careful balancing of objectives

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor step classification performance: Video encoder not capturing step concepts effectively
  - Inaccurate step forecasting: Diffusion model not learning temporal dependencies properly
  - Mode collapse in predictions: Diffusion model not capturing the full distribution of plausible steps

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate step classification performance with and without the diffusion model to confirm its contribution to representation learning
  2. Test zero-shot step forecasting capability to verify the model's ability to predict future steps without fine-tuning
  3. Analyze the diversity of sampled predictions from the diffusion model to ensure it's capturing multiple plausible step orderings

## Limitations

- The model's performance on highly specialized or domain-specific procedures outside common household tasks remains untested
- The computational complexity of the diffusion model may limit its applicability to real-time or resource-constrained scenarios
- The reliance on CLIP for pseudo labels introduces potential biases that may not transfer well to procedural video understanding

## Confidence
