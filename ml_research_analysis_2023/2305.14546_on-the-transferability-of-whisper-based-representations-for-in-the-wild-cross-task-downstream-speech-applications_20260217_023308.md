# On the Transferability of Whisper-based Representations for "In-the-Wild" Cross-Task Downstream Speech Applications

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14546
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14546

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the transferability of Whisper, a transformer-based model trained on weakly supervised data for automatic speech recognition (ASR), to four other speech tasks: speaker identification, keyword spotting, intent classification, and emotion recognition. The authors evaluate the model's robustness under varying noise and reverberation conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that Whisper achieves promising results across tasks and environmental conditions, outperforming state-of-the-art models on three out of four tasks and showing robustness to noise and reverberation. The findings suggest that weakly supervised training with large-scale datasets can lead to generalized, reusable, and robust representations.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates Whisper's transferability by applying its pre-trained representations to four downstream speech tasks. The authors assess performance under varying noise and reverberation conditions to test robustness. Comparative analysis is conducted against state-of-the-art models to establish Whisper's effectiveness. The evaluation focuses on the model's ability to generalize across tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Key Results
- Whisper outperforms state-of-the-art models on three of four downstream tasks.
- Demonstrated robustness to noise and reverberation under tested conditions.
- Weakly supervised training with large-scale datasets leads to generalized, reusable, and robust representations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Whisper model's generalizability across diverse speech tasks is supported by experimental results, but several limitations and uncertainties must be acknowledged. The evaluation primarily focuses on four downstream tasks (speaker identification, keyword spotting, intent classification, and emotion recognition), which may not represent the full spectrum of potential speech applications. The "in-the-wild" conditions tested include only noise and reverberation variations, leaving other environmental factors unexplored. Additionally, the comparisons with state-of-the-art models are limited to specific benchmarks, and the extent to which these results generalize to other competitive approaches remains uncertain. The weakly supervised training data's composition and potential biases could also influence the model's robustness, though this aspect is not explicitly analyzed.
