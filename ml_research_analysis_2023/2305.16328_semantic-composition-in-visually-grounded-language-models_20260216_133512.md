# Semantic Composition in Visually Grounded Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16328
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16328
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: New state-of-the-art Winoground performance with CACR (39.25 text, 17.75 image scores)

## Executive Summary
This thesis addresses the challenge of semantic composition in vision-language models, which struggle to represent compositional structure despite their ability to handle complex language. The work introduces five approaches: WinogroundVQA benchmark for testing generative models, Syntactic Neural Module Distillation for measuring compositionality, causal tracing for locating compositional circuits, Cross-modal Attention Congruence Regularization (CACR) for relation alignment, and syntactic meanpool for compositional inductive bias. The research demonstrates that syntax-guided composition is largely linear and that soft cross-modal equivalence outperforms hard equivalence in relation alignment.

## Method Summary
The core method involves introducing CACR as a self-supervised objective that aligns relations across vision and language modalities by measuring similarity between projected attention matrices. The approach converts Winoground captions to yes/no questions for testing generative models, uses neural module distillation to measure compositional ability through syntax trees, and applies causal tracing to locate where composition occurs in image captioning models. The syntactic mean pooling approach injects compositional bias by hierarchically averaging token embeddings according to syntax parse trees rather than standard mean pooling.

## Key Results
- CACR achieves new state-of-the-art Winoground performance (39.25 text, 17.75 image scores)
- Syntax-guided composition is largely linear, as shown through distillation experiments
- Soft cross-modal equivalence (CACR) outperforms hard cross-modal equivalence (IAIS) for relation alignment
- Syntactic mean pooling improves compositional geometry by increasing Euclidean distance between distinct caption pairs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Cross-modal Attention Congruence Regularization
Cross-modal attention matrices encode transformations between compositional structures of different modalities. By projecting visual attention into the language basis and comparing it to actual language attention using symmetric KL divergence, CACR encourages models to align relations across modalities. This works because cross-modal attention captures meaningful relational transformations that can be used to measure and encourage compositional alignment.

### Mechanism 2: Syntactic Neural Module Distillation
Syntax trees provide a strong causal model for how sentence meanings should be composed. A neural module network constructed from a sentence's syntax parse tree, where each module corresponds to a syntactic rule, is trained to approximate a sentence embedding model's output. The approximation error reveals how much compositional structure is captured by syntax versus other factors, with lower error indicating better compositional ability.

### Mechanism 3: WinogroundVQA Reformulation
Converting Winoground captions to yes/no questions enables testing generative vision-language models' compositional abilities. The reformulation solves issues with models that don't produce match scores and provides a more cognitively plausible reasoning framework. Correctly answering compositional questions requires understanding the compositional structure of both visual scenes and linguistic queries.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Vision encoder → Text encoder → Multimodal transformer → Cross-modal attention matrices → Projection and regularization modules → WinogroundVQA conversion module

- **Critical path**: 1) Encode image and text to get token embeddings 2) Pass through multimodal transformer 3) Extract intra-modal and cross-modal attention matrices 4) Compute CACR loss by projecting visual attention into language basis 5) Backpropagate CACR loss 6) For WinogroundVQA, convert captions to questions and query generative model

- **Design tradeoffs**: CACR uses soft cross-modal equivalence (weighted sum) vs IAIS's hard equivalence (argmax), trading computational efficiency for potentially better alignment. Linear vs non-linear SynNaMoN modules test if syntax alone is sufficient versus capturing complex relationships. Question formulation in WinogroundVQA balances naturalness versus preserving compositional distinctions.

- **Failure signatures**: CACR training failure when cross-modal attention matrices become flat or uniform. SynNaMoN training failure with high MSE between output and target embeddings. WinogroundVQA failure when models answer correctly but fail to capture compositional distinctions internally.

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Implement CACR loss calculation and verify it reduces Winoground score compared to baseline without CACR 2) Train SynNaMoN with different architectures on BERT embeddings and compare distillation performance 3) Convert Winoground captions to questions using GPT-3.5 and test a few generative models on the resulting dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the compositional geometry of syntax-guided operations in sentence embedding models generalize across different parts of speech? The study only examined one specific syntactic rule and limited parts of speech, leaving open questions about generalization across the full range of syntactic operations.

### Open Question 2
Does soft cross-modal equivalence (CACR) consistently outperform hard cross-modal equivalence (IAIS) across different vision-language models and datasets? The comparison was limited to a single baseline model and benchmark without testing across multiple models and datasets.

### Open Question 3
Can syntactic mean pooling be successfully integrated into vision-language models during pre-training to improve their compositional abilities? The approach was only tested on pre-trained models, without exploring its effectiveness when used as a compositional inductive bias during training.

## Limitations

- Cross-modal attention matrices may not encode meaningful compositional relationships as assumed, potentially exploiting spurious correlations rather than learning genuine compositionality
- WinogroundVQA reformulation may not fully preserve the compositional challenges of the original task, potentially introducing new biases
- Syntax trees may not provide a good causal model for compositional structure due to non-compositional phrases and idiomatic expressions in natural language

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Empirical results showing CACR improves Winoground scores and that syntactic mean pooling outperforms standard mean pooling are well-supported with clearly specified methodology.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that syntax-guided composition is "largely linear" is plausible but requires additional validation beyond the suggestive evidence from comparing different SynNaMoN architectures.

**Low confidence**: Broader claims about understanding "how" vision-language models compose semantics remain more suggestive than conclusive, as the field lacks comprehensive understanding of compositional mechanisms.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-modal attention interpretability**: Apply causal tracing or attention visualization to examine whether cross-modal attention matrices actually encode compositional relationships and whether projected attention matrices capture expected relational alignments.

2. **WinogroundVQA compositional fidelity**: Conduct ablation studies on question formulation to determine which aspects of compositional structure are preserved or lost, testing whether models can distinguish subtle compositional variations maintained versus eliminated.

3. **SynNaMoN generalization beyond syntax**: Test Syntactic Neural Module Distillation on sentence pairs containing known non-compositional phrases or idiomatic expressions to determine whether the approach can distinguish between compositional and non-compositional meaning construction.
