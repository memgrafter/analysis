# Cluster-based Deep Ensemble Learning for Emotion Classification in Internet Memes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.08343
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08343

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes CDEL, a hybrid model for emotion classification in memes that combines deep learning with clustering algorithms to exploit facial features. The method involves selecting optimal clustering and deep learning models, concatenating their outputs, and using them for classification. Evaluated on the Memotion dataset, CDEL achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing methods with a MacroF1 score of 0.3669. Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of its components, particularly the integration of clustering and deep learning.

## Method Summary
CDEL integrates clustering algorithms with deep learning models to enhance emotion classification in memes by leveraging facial features. The approach selects optimal clustering and deep learning models, concatenates their outputs, and uses this combined representation for classification. The method is evaluated on the Memotion dataset, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance. The architecture exploits facial features through clustering while deep learning models capture semantic and visual content, with their outputs merged for final classification.

## Key Results
- CDEL achieves state-of-the-art performance on Memotion dataset with MacroF1 score of 0.3669
- The model excels in classifying positive memes but struggles with negative ones due to dataset imbalance
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of both clustering and deep learning components

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component map**: Preprocessing -> Clustering module -> Deep learning module -> Feature concatenation -> Classification layer

**Critical path**: Raw meme image → Face detection and feature extraction → Clustering (k-means/another algorithm) → Deep learning backbone (CNN/RNN) → Concatenate clustered features with DL features → Classification

**Design tradeoffs**: The integration of clustering with deep learning creates computational overhead but potentially improves emotion classification accuracy. The clustering approach may struggle with diverse meme styles where faces are absent or stylized, while the deep learning component requires substantial training data.

**Failure signatures**: Poor performance on negative emotion classification suggests dataset imbalance issues; inconsistent results across different meme styles may indicate clustering limitations; high computational cost during inference due to dual-model architecture.
