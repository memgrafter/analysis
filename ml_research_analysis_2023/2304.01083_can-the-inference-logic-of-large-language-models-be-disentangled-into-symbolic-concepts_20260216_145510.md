# Can the Inference Logic of Large Language Models be Disentangled into Symbolic Concepts?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01083
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01083
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: LLMs encode sparse symbolic concepts that can estimate inference scores and explain prediction errors

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) can encode sparse symbolic concepts similar to traditional deep neural networks. The authors propose disentangling LLM inference scores for dialogue tasks into a small number of symbolic concepts extracted through game-theoretic interaction metrics. They demonstrate that a few sparse concepts can accurately estimate all inference scores on arbitrarily masked input states, exhibit high transferability across similar sentences, and explain prediction errors.

## Method Summary
The authors use game-theoretic interaction metrics (specifically Harsanyi interaction) to quantify the effects of input variable combinations on LLM output. They extract top-ranked interaction concepts and verify their faithfulness by comparing real versus approximated outputs on all 2^n masked sentence variants. The method involves computing interaction effects for all concept sets, ranking them by magnitude, and testing approximation accuracy. They evaluate concept transferability through Jaccard similarity between concept distributions and analyze error attribution by identifying concepts with positive effects on incorrect predictions.

## Key Results
- Sparse concepts (50-200 interactions) can accurately estimate all inference scores on masked sentences
- Extracted concepts show high transferability across semantically similar input sentences
- Symbolic concepts can explain exact reasons for LLM prediction errors
- Concept discrimination power correlates with semantic similarity between sentences

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLM inference scores can be disentangled into sparse symbolic concepts
- Mechanism: Game-theoretic interaction metrics quantify effects of input variable combinations on output
- Core assumption: Only a few interactions have salient effects despite large parameter count
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] sparse concepts well estimate inference scores on all masked states; [section] few salient interactions estimate DNN outputs on 2^n masked samples
- Break condition: If interaction sparsity disappears (many comparable magnitudes), disentanglement fails

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Extracted concepts transfer across similar input sentences
- Mechanism: Similar contexts activate similar interaction patterns
- Core assumption: Semantic similarity implies conceptual similarity in LLM representations
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] symbolic concepts exhibit high transferability; [section] salient interactions found across samples in same category
- Break condition: If concept distributions diverge significantly between semantically similar sentences

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Concepts explain LLM prediction errors
- Mechanism: Error analysis identifies concepts with positive effects on incorrect predictions
- Core assumption: Concept effects directly contribute to generation decisions
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] concepts explain exact reasons for prediction errors; [section] extracted exact reasons accountable for errors
- Break condition: If concept effects don't correlate with prediction correctness, error explanation fails

  - Quick check question: Can you compute the Harsanyi interaction for a 3-word concept using the masking formula?

- Concept: Concept transferability
  - Why needed here: Enables cross-sample concept analysis and generalization of findings
  - Quick check question: How would you measure similarity between concept distributions across two sentences?

- Concept: Error attribution methodology
  - Why needed here: Links concept analysis to practical debugging and LLM improvement
  - Quick check question: What defines a "culprit concept" for a prediction error?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: OPT-1.3b → Interaction calculator → Concept extractor → Error analyzer
- Critical path: Input sentence → Compute interactions → Rank concepts → Validate on masked samples → Analyze errors
- Design tradeoffs: Concept granularity vs. interpretability; computational cost of interaction computation; handling of padding tokens
- Failure signatures: Poor concept ranking (many similar-magnitude interactions); low transferability between sentences; weak correlation between concept effects and errors
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify concept sparsity on diverse sentence types
  2. Test transferability between semantically related sentences
  3. Apply error attribution to known LLM failure cases

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the sparsity of symbolic concepts in LLMs be proven mathematically rather than just empirically observed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Findings obtained based on empirical observations without solid proof
- Why unresolved: Paper only provides empirical verification, not theoretical proof
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical proof showing exact decomposition into small number of interaction concepts

### Open Question 2
- Question: Do LLMs encode more complex concepts than traditional DNNs due to larger parameter size, or sparser concepts because of more sophisticated training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Two conflicting understandings about concept complexity vs. sparsity
- Why unresolved: Paper empirically verifies sparse concepts but doesn't determine root cause
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing sparsity/complexity in LLMs vs. traditional DNNs with varying parameters/training

### Open Question 3
- Question: How transferable are symbolic concepts across different LLMs with varying architectures and training data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] Paper examines transferability within single LLM, not across different LLMs
- Why unresolved: Experiments focus on single LLM concept transferability
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing concepts extracted from multiple LLMs with different architectures/training data

## Limitations

- Computational complexity grows exponentially with input length (2^n masked combinations), limiting scalability to longer texts
- Method relies on game-theoretic interaction metrics that may miss non-linear or higher-order interactions
- Results based on specific OPT-1.3b model and generated dialogue sentences, limiting generalizability

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The existence of sparse interaction effects in LLMs - well-supported by mathematical framework and approximation results
- **Medium confidence**: Concept transferability across similar sentences - supported by Jaccard similarity but needs clearer semantic similarity operationalization
- **Medium confidence**: Error explanation capability - demonstrated through case studies but requires more rigorous causal validation

## Next Checks

1. Apply concept extraction method to different LLM architectures (GPT-2, LLaMA) and verify sparse concept encoding persists across varying sizes and training objectives

2. Systematically vary semantic similarity between input pairs and measure how concept transferability degrades, establishing quantitative thresholds

3. Design controlled experiments where specific concepts are artificially amplified/suppressed, then measure corresponding changes in prediction accuracy to establish causal relationships between concepts and errors
