# Adversarial alignment: Breaking the trade-off between the strength of an attack and its relevance to human perception

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03229
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03229
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Adversarial attacks remain a significant vulnerability in deep neural networks, where imperceptible perturbations can mislead models. This study evaluates how adversarial robustness has evolved as models have improved on ImageNet. We measure robustness via perturbation tolerance (average ℓ2 distance between clean and attacked images) and adversarial alignment (correlation between attacks and human diagnostic features). Surprisingly, larger models exhibit greater perturbation tolerance but less adversarial alignment, indicating a trade-off. Vision transformers show high tolerance but poor alignment, while convolutional networks show the reverse. The neural harmonizer training method partially resolves this trade-off by improving both perturbation tolerance and adversarial alignment, suggesting that aligning models with human perceptual features is key to developing robust AI systems.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the evolution of adversarial robustness as deep neural networks (DNNs) have improved on ImageNet. By evaluating 283 DNNs, the research reveals a surprising trade-off: larger, more accurate models require larger perturbations to be fooled (higher perturbation tolerance) but are less aligned with human perception of what makes objects recognizable (lower adversarial alignment). Vision transformers show particularly high perturbation tolerance but poor alignment, while convolutional networks show the opposite pattern. The neural harmonizer training method addresses this trade-off by aligning DNN representations with human feature importance maps, achieving both high perturbation tolerance and adversarial alignment.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 283 DNNs (127 CNNs, 123 ViTs, 15 hybrids) from TIMM using ℓ2 PGD attacks to find minimum perturbations that change model outputs. Perturbation tolerance is measured as the average ℓ2 distance between clean and attacked images, while adversarial alignment is quantified as the Spearman correlation between attacks and ClickMe human feature importance maps. The neural harmonizer training method is applied to 14 DNNs, using a loss function that encourages alignment between model gradients and human feature importance while maintaining classification accuracy. Robust ResNetv2-50s trained for ℓ∞-bounded attacks serve as a comparison baseline.

## Key Results
- DNNs have become significantly more tolerant to adversarial attacks as they improved on ImageNet, with perturbation tolerance increasing by 300% over recent years.
- Despite increased perturbation tolerance, adversarial attacks on high-accuracy DNNs have become less aligned with human diagnostic features for recognition.
- Vision transformers exhibit the highest perturbation tolerance but lowest adversarial alignment among model types.
- The neural harmonizer training method improves both perturbation tolerance and adversarial alignment, achieving the best of both worlds.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does adversarial robustness scale with model size beyond the ImageNet-trained models studied here?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Large-scale vision transformers are as robust to non-adversarial image perturbations as humans and it is possible that this means larger adversarial perturbations are needed to attack these models [16, 17]"
- Why unresolved: The paper only examined models up to the largest available ImageNet models. It's unclear if the trends of increasing perturbation tolerance and decreasing adversarial alignment continue as models scale further.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the same metrics on models trained on much larger datasets (e.g., JFT-300M, LAION-5B) or models with significantly more parameters than those studied here.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can adversarial alignment be improved without requiring explicit human feature importance data during training?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The neural harmonizer requires ClickMe human feature importance maps, but the authors suggest "it is possible that advances could also be made without ClickMe feature importance maps by inducing more human-like developmental principles onto models"
- Why unresolved: The neural harmonizer approach is resource-intensive and limited by the availability of human feature data. Alternative methods that achieve similar alignment through architectural or training innovations would be more scalable.
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstrating improved adversarial alignment in models trained with alternative methods such as self-supervised learning with human-like pretext tasks, biologically-inspired architectures, or training curricula that mimic human visual development.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the trade-off between perturbation tolerance and adversarial alignment hold across different types of adversarial attacks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] "Despite the power of the Carlini-Wagner (C&W) attack, it is known for being extremely slow to compute" but the authors found similar patterns with CW attacks in a small-scale experiment
- Why unresolved: The paper primarily focused on ℓ2 PGD attacks. While initial results suggest the trade-off holds for other attacks, comprehensive testing across diverse attack types (gradient-based, score-based, decision-based) is needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematically measuring perturbation tolerance and adversarial alignment across multiple attack types (e.g., C&W, DeepFool, decision-based attacks) on the same model zoo to determine if the trade-off is universal.

## Limitations

- The neural harmonizer's effectiveness across diverse architectures beyond the 14 tested models remains uncertain.
- The relationship between ImageNet accuracy and robustness metrics shows strong correlations but causation is not definitively established.
- The ClickMe dataset represents human perception in only one specific experimental paradigm which may not generalize to all visual recognition tasks.
- The evaluation focuses on ℓ2-bounded attacks, potentially missing robustness insights for other perturbation norms.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: Perturbation tolerance increases with model scale and ImageNet accuracy (supported by correlation r=0.80 with extensive model zoo evaluation).

**Medium confidence**: Adversarial alignment decreases as models become more accurate (supported by correlation r=-0.56, but causal mechanism not fully established).

**Medium confidence**: Neural harmonizer improves both perturbation tolerance and adversarial alignment (supported by direct comparison to unharmonized models, but sample size limited to 14 models).
