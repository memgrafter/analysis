# Label-noise-tolerant medical image classification via self-attention and self-supervised learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09718
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09718
- Reference count: 29
- One-line primary result: Achieves up to 27% accuracy improvement under high label noise in medical image classification

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of label noise in medical image classification by proposing a noise-robust training framework that combines contrastive learning with intra-group attention mixup. The method uses self-supervised learning to enhance visual representations and dynamically downweights noisy samples within mini-groups to generate cleaner feature representations. Experiments demonstrate superior classification accuracy compared to state-of-the-art baselines across synthetic and real-world medical datasets, with improvements of up to 27% in accuracy under high noise levels.

## Method Summary
The proposed framework integrates contrastive learning for feature extraction and intra-group attention mixup for noise suppression. It employs a two-stage training procedure: first using only contrastive loss to learn robust features, then combining weighted decision loss with contrastive loss for fine-tuning. The method groups samples by label and assigns learnable attention weights to suppress noisy samples during mixup. ResNet-18 serves as the backbone, with projection and mixup heads for attention weights.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 27% accuracy improvement over default training methods under high noise levels
- Demonstrates superior performance across different noise types (symmetric, asymmetric, instance-dependent)
- Shows effectiveness on both synthetic and real-world medical datasets including Retina OCT, Blood Cell, and Colon Pathology

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Contrastive learning enhances feature representation by pulling together positive pairs and pushing apart negative pairs.
- Mechanism: The model generates two augmented views of each sample, processes them through a shared encoder, and minimizes the contrastive loss between their projected embeddings.
- Core assumption: Views of the same image share the same label and should be grouped closer in feature space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The contrastive learning for feature extractor helps to enhance visual representation of DNNs."
  - [section] Equation (2) defines the contrastive loss D(Ui2, Ui3) measuring similarity between views.
  - [corpus] "How does self-supervised pretraining improve robustness against noisy labels across various medical image classification datasets?" supports contrastive pretraining's robustness benefits.
- Break condition: If the temperature τ is set too high or too low, the contrastive loss may collapse or fail to separate classes effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Intra-group attention mixup generates cleaner feature representations by dynamically downweighting noisy samples.
- Mechanism: Samples are grouped by label; each sample gets a learnable attention weight, and a weighted average of features is used for training.
- Core assumption: Within a group of same-labeled samples, clean samples will have higher confidence scores and dominate the weighted average.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The intra-group attention mixup module constructs groups and assigns self-attention weights for group-wise samples."
  - [section] Equation (5) defines the weighted mixup Vmix = ∑wiVi / ∑wi.
  - [corpus] "GeMix: Conditional GAN-Based Mixup for Improved Medical Image Augmentation" shows mixup's effectiveness in medical imaging.
- Break condition: If group size is too small, noise cannot be effectively suppressed; if too large, unique sample characteristics are lost.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Two-stage training first learns robust features via contrastive loss, then fine-tunes with mixup and supervised loss.
- Mechanism: Stage 1 uses only contrastive loss to build stable embeddings; Stage 2 adds mixup loss and supervised loss with weighted combination.
- Core assumption: Contrastive pretraining stabilizes feature space before fine-tuning on noisy labels.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In the first stage... the contrastive learning loss function is solely exerted to guide the updating procedure of the feature extractor."
  - [section] "In the second stage... the weighted decision loss function plays a leading role, and the Lc can be viewed as a regularization."
  - [corpus] "Learning Robust Self-attention Features for Speech Emotion Recognition with Label-adaptive Mixup" demonstrates staged training benefits.
- Break condition: If Stage 1 is too short, features may not stabilize; if too long, adaptation to task-specific labels may be insufficient.

## Foundational Learning

  - Why needed here: To learn invariant visual features without relying on noisy labels, improving robustness.
  - Quick check question: What is the role of the temperature parameter τ in contrastive loss?

  - Why needed here: To estimate sample reliability within a group and suppress noisy samples dynamically.
  - Quick check question: How does the attention weight wi influence the contribution of each sample in the mixup?

- Concept: Mixup augmentation
  - Why needed here: To generate virtual samples that reduce overfitting to noisy labels and smooth decision boundaries.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between intra-class and inter-class mixup in terms of label consistency?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input image → weak augmentation → feature extraction → supervised loss; Input image → strong augmentation → feature extraction → projection head → contrastive loss; Group samples by label → compute attention weights → mixup features → supervised loss

- Critical path: 1) Input image → weak augmentation → feature extraction → supervised loss; 2) Input image → strong augmentation → feature extraction → projection head → contrastive loss; 3) Group samples by label → compute attention weights → mixup features → supervised loss

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Group size vs. noise suppression: larger groups suppress noise better but risk losing sample uniqueness.
  - Number of FC layers in projection head: deeper heads capture more complex patterns but increase computation.
  - Temperature τ in contrastive loss: balances attraction/repulsion of samples.

- Failure signatures:
  - If accuracy plateaus early: contrastive loss may be too weak or augmentation insufficient.
  - If test accuracy lags behind training: overfitting to noise, check mixup weight balance.
  - If model diverges: learning rates too high or regularization missing.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run with only contrastive loss (Stage 1) to verify feature stability.
  2. Add mixup head with fixed weights to test intra-group attention impact.
  3. Combine all losses with λ=0.1 and monitor accuracy vs. noise ratio.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal architecture and dimensionality for the noisy weight module in the proposed framework?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper conducts experiments with different architectures (FC(1), FC(2), FC(3)) and dimensions for the noisy weight module, but does not conclude which is universally optimal across all datasets.
- Why unresolved: The optimal architecture may vary depending on the specific dataset and noise characteristics, and the paper does not provide a general recommendation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of classification performance using different noisy weight architectures and dimensions across multiple datasets with varying noise levels and types.

### Open Question 3

- Why unresolved: The performance on real-world noisy medical data may differ from synthetic noise scenarios, and the paper does not explore this extensively.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extensive testing of the method on multiple real-world noisy medical datasets, comparing performance against state-of-the-art methods and analyzing robustness to different noise types and ratios.

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the integration of contrastive learning and attention mixup strategies impact the feature representation learning in noisy medical image classification?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes combining contrastive learning and attention mixup to enhance visual representations and suppress noise, but does not provide a detailed analysis of their individual contributions.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents ablation studies but does not fully explore how these strategies interact and contribute to the overall performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed ablation studies isolating the effects of contrastive learning and attention mixup, including visualizations of feature representations and quantitative analysis of their impact on classification accuracy.

## Limitations
- The exact architecture of the projection head and detailed implementation of attention weight calculation are not fully specified.
- Evaluation is limited to specific medical datasets, and generalizability to other domains is not thoroughly explored.
- The effectiveness of the intra-group attention mixup strategy relies on the assumption that clean samples have higher confidence scores, which may not hold for all noise distributions.

## Confidence

- **High**: The core mechanism of combining contrastive learning with intra-group attention mixup is well-supported by theoretical reasoning and empirical results.
- **Medium**: The effectiveness of the two-stage training procedure is supported by the ablation study, but the optimal hyperparameters for each stage are not thoroughly explored.
- **Low**: The claim of superior performance compared to state-of-the-art baselines may be influenced by the specific choice of datasets and noise injection methods used in the experiments.
