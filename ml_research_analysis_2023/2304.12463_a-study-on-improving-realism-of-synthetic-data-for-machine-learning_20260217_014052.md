# A Study on Improving Realism of Synthetic Data for Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12463
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12463
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: Proposed GAN-based synthetic-to-real model improves realism of synthetic images, reducing FID score from 60.82 to 55.71 and improving semantic segmentation mIoU on real images

## Executive Summary
This study proposes a GAN-based synthetic-to-real generative model to improve the realism of synthetic data for machine learning. The model refines synthetic images to make them look more realistic while preserving annotation information through a combination of adversarial and reconstruction losses. Evaluation on the GTA-V to Cityscapes dataset demonstrates that the refined images have better quality metrics (lower FID, higher SSIM) and lead to improved performance in downstream semantic segmentation tasks when tested on real images.

## Method Summary
The method employs a GAN architecture with a refiner network and discriminator network. The refiner transforms synthetic images into more realistic versions while preserving annotation information through perceptual and reconstruction losses. The discriminator is trained to classify images as real or refined. The system is pre-trained with perceptual loss and then fine-tuned using adversarial training between the refiner and discriminator. The approach is evaluated on the GTA-V to Cityscapes dataset, with quantitative metrics (FID, SSIM) and downstream semantic segmentation tasks used to assess performance.

## Key Results
- Refined images achieve lower FID score (55.71) compared to synthetic images (60.82)
- Refined images show higher SSIM score (0.0026) compared to synthetic images (0.0023)
- Models trained on refined images achieve better accuracy and mIoU on real images compared to models trained on synthetic or SimGAN-refined images

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The refiner network learns to transform synthetic images into more realistic ones by minimizing both adversarial and reconstruction losses.
- Mechanism: The refiner network is trained to fool the discriminator by generating images that are indistinguishable from real images, while the reconstruction loss preserves annotation information by keeping the refined images similar to the original synthetic images.
- Core assumption: The synthetic-to-real translation problem can be effectively addressed using GANs with both adversarial and reconstruction objectives.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our approach builds on previous work in this area but focuses on evaluating and comparing different adversarial training setups on general-purpose datasets."
  - [section 3.2] "LAdv(θ; xi; Y ) is the adversarial loss that adds realism to the synthetic image xi. It is defined by Equation (3), where we ask the discriminator to classify refined images and request it to fail to classify refined images as synthetic."
- Break condition: The mechanism fails if the adversarial loss dominates and destroys the annotation information, or if the reconstruction loss prevents the refiner from generating realistic images.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The perceptual loss during pre-training helps the refiner learn an identity network that preserves high-level semantic features.
- Mechanism: By minimizing the perceptual loss, which measures the similarity of refined images to synthetic images at a high-level semantic level, the refiner learns to preserve object shapes and textures while making the images more realistic.
- Core assumption: The perceptual loss effectively captures high-level semantic similarities between synthetic and refined images.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "Perceptual loss is defined by Equation (5), where ψ is a pre-trained inception network... By minimizing the perceptual loss, we are able to make refined images perceptually similar to synthetic images in terms of high-level features such as object shapes and textures."
- Break condition: The mechanism fails if the pre-trained inception network does not capture the relevant high-level features for the specific domain.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The discriminator's ability to distinguish real from refined images drives the refiner to generate more realistic synthetic data.
- Mechanism: The discriminator is trained to classify images as real or refined, and the refiner is trained to fool the discriminator. This adversarial training process gradually improves the realism of the refined images.
- Core assumption: The adversarial training process can effectively improve the realism of synthetic data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "GANs consist of two neural networks, a generator and a discriminator, which are trained to compete against each other."
  - [section 3.1] "The discriminator, Dφ, is trained to classify images as real vs refined, where φ are the parameters of the discriminator network."
- Break condition: The mechanism fails if the discriminator becomes too strong or too weak, leading to mode collapse or failure to improve the refiner.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Synthetic images -> Refiner network -> Refined images -> Discriminator network -> Real/refined classification

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-train the refiner using perceptual loss with synthetic images.
  2. Pre-train the discriminator using synthetic and real images.
  3. Alternately update the refiner and discriminator parameters using adversarial and reconstruction losses.
  4. Evaluate the refined images using qualitative and quantitative metrics and downstream semantic segmentation tasks.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Perceptual loss vs. reconstruction loss: Perceptual loss preserves high-level semantic features, while reconstruction loss preserves pixel-level information.
  - Discriminator strength: A strong discriminator can effectively drive the refiner to generate more realistic images, but may also lead to mode collapse.
  - Image size: Smaller image sizes reduce computational requirements but may lose important details.

- Failure signatures:
  - Mode collapse: The refiner generates a limited variety of images, failing to capture the full diversity of the real domain.
  - Discriminator overfitting: The discriminator becomes too strong and fails to provide useful gradients for the refiner.
  - Loss of annotation information: The refined images become too realistic and lose the original annotation information.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the refiner and discriminator on a small subset of the GTA-V to Cityscapes dataset and evaluate the refined images using FID and SSIM scores.
  2. Train the refiner and discriminator on the full dataset and evaluate the refined images using a downstream semantic segmentation task with a simple model like U-NET.
  3. Compare the performance of the refined images with the original synthetic images and SimGAN-refined images using the same semantic segmentation task and metrics.
