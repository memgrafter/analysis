# Identification of Systematic Errors of Image Classifiers on Rare Subgroups

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05072
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05072
- Reference count: 40
- This work presents PromptAttack, a method for identifying systematic errors of image classifiers on rare subgroups using text-to-image models and combinatorial testing.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces PromptAttack, a novel method for identifying systematic errors in image classifiers on rare subgroups. The approach leverages text-to-image models to synthesize images representing specific subgroup characteristics and evaluates classifier performance on these synthetic samples. By using combinatorial testing to manage the exponential growth of subgroup combinations, PromptAttack efficiently identifies targeted misclassifications that may be missed in standard evaluation. The method successfully identifies concrete examples of systematic errors, such as misclassifying minivans as snowplows under snowy conditions and demographic-based misclassifications.

## Method Summary
PromptAttack uses text-to-image diffusion models to generate synthetic images representing rare subgroups by encoding subgroup characteristics in text prompts. The method ranks these subgroups based on the target classifier's performance on the generated images. To handle the exponential growth of possible subgroup combinations, combinatorial testing is employed to select a representative subset of subgroups for evaluation. The approach identifies systematic errors by finding subgroups where the classifier consistently performs poorly, revealing biases or limitations in the model's ability to handle rare or specific conditions.

## Key Results
- Successfully identifies targeted misclassifications on rare subgroups using text-to-image synthesis
- Demonstrates specific examples including misclassifying minivans as snowplows and pickups under snowy conditions
- Identifies demographic-based systematic errors, such as misclassifying persons as apes for certain subgroups
- Shows effectiveness on both synthetic benchmarks and real ImageNet classifiers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
PromptAttack works by leveraging the generative capabilities of text-to-image models to create synthetic examples of rare subgroups that are difficult to collect in real-world datasets. By systematically varying attributes in text prompts and evaluating classifier performance on the generated images, the method can efficiently explore the space of possible subgroup variations. The combinatorial testing approach allows for managing the exponential complexity of subgroup combinations while still capturing meaningful interactions between attributes.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Text prompts → Text-to-image model → Synthetic images → Classifier evaluation → Performance ranking

**Critical Path:** Prompt generation → Image synthesis → Classification → Performance measurement → Subgroup ranking

**Design Tradeoffs:** The method balances comprehensive subgroup coverage against computational efficiency through combinatorial testing, sacrificing some potential subgroup combinations for practical evaluation feasibility.

**Failure Signatures:** False positive systematic errors may occur when text-to-image models generate out-of-class samples, and biases in generative models may propagate to the systematic error identification process.

**First Experiments:**
1. Validate prompt effectiveness by comparing classifier performance on real vs. synthetic images for known subgroups
2. Test combinatorial testing coverage by comparing results with exhaustive subgroup evaluation on a small subset
3. Evaluate different text-to-image models to assess impact of generative quality on systematic error identification

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we improve the reliability of PromptAttack in identifying systematic errors when the text-to-image model generates out-of-class (OOC) samples?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses OOC samples as a limitation of PromptAttack, where generated samples do not belong to the source class, potentially leading to false positive systematic errors.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests prompt engineering and robust estimation as mitigation strategies, but these may not completely eliminate OOC samples, especially for complex subgroups.
- What evidence would resolve it: A rigorous evaluation comparing the performance of PromptAttack with and without OOC samples, using a benchmark with ground truth systematic errors, would provide insights into the impact of OOC samples and the effectiveness of mitigation strategies.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can we extend PromptAttack to identify systematic errors on subgroups that are difficult to describe in natural language, such as those with specific geometric layouts?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions that certain coherent subsets of data, like those with geometric layouts, may be challenging to describe in text prompts, limiting the applicability of PromptAttack.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not propose a solution for this limitation, suggesting that future work on using alternative conditioning information, such as scene layouts, could be explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating a variant of PromptAttack that uses scene layouts or other non-textual conditioning information to identify systematic errors on subgroups with specific geometric properties would demonstrate the feasibility of this approach.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we mitigate the propagation of biases from text-to-image models to the systematic error identification process in PromptAttack?
- Basis in paper: The paper acknowledges that biases in text-to-image models may propagate to biases in PromptAttack, as the method relies on these models for generating samples.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not propose a solution to this issue, highlighting the need to further reduce bias in text-to-image models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating the impact of different text-to-image models with varying levels of bias on the systematic error identification results of PromptAttack would provide insights into the extent of bias propagation and potential mitigation strategies.

## Limitations
- Dependence on text-to-image models introduces potential biases that may affect systematic error identification
- Combinatorial testing approach may miss important interactions between subgroup attributes not included in test design
- Evaluation primarily focuses on ImageNet classifiers, limiting generalizability to other domains or tasks
