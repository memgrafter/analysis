# Sample-Efficient Multi-Objective Learning via Generalized Policy Improvement Prioritization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07784
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07784
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces a novel algorithm that improves sample efficiency in MORL through principled prioritization of weight vectors and experiences based on GPI improvements

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the sample efficiency challenge in multi-objective reinforcement learning (MORL) by introducing two key innovations: GPI-LS, a weight vector prioritization method that selects preferences based on formally guaranteed performance improvements via Generalized Policy Improvement (GPI), and GPI-PD, a model-based Dyna-style approach that prioritizes relevant experiences for learning policies. The algorithm is theoretically guaranteed to converge to optimal or ε-optimal solutions while maintaining anytime learning properties. Empirical results demonstrate superior performance compared to state-of-the-art MORL algorithms across challenging tasks with both discrete and continuous state and action spaces.

## Method Summary
The method combines GPI-LS for weight vector selection with GPI-PD for experience prioritization in a model-based MORL framework. GPI-LS computes corner weights of the current value set and selects weight vectors that maximize the guaranteed improvement gap achievable via GPI. GPI-PD learns a model of the environment and generates prioritized simulated experiences based on the GPI gap, using both real and model experiences to update a neural network Q-function conditioned on state, action, and weight vector. The approach maintains theoretical convergence guarantees even with sub-optimal policies and demonstrates significant sample efficiency improvements through active learning of both which preferences to train on and which experiences to prioritize.

## Key Results
- Outperforms state-of-the-art MORL algorithms in terms of expected utility and maximum utility loss
- Achieves anytime learning with monotonically improving solution quality
- Guaranteed convergence to optimal or ε-optimal solutions in finite steps
- Demonstrates effectiveness across discrete and continuous state and action spaces

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - GPI-LS -> Weight vector selection module that computes corner weights and selects the most promising preferences
  - GPI-PD -> Experience prioritization and Dyna planning module that learns the model and performs prioritized updates
  - Model 𝑝𝜑 -> Ensemble of probabilistic neural networks predicting next states and multi-objective rewards
  - Q𝜃 network -> Neural network conditioned on state and weight vector approximating multi-objective action-values
  - Buffers B and Bmodel -> Storage for real experiences and model-generated experiences respectively

- Critical path:
  1. GPI-LS selects weight vector w based on corner weights and guaranteed improvement
  2. GPI-PD learns model 𝑝𝜑 from experiences in buffer B
  3. GPI-PD generates prioritized model experiences and stores in Bmodel
  4. Both real and model experiences are used to update Q𝜃
  5. GPI policy is computed from Q𝜃 to select actions in the environment

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model-based vs model-free: Model-based provides sample efficiency but requires accurate model learning
  - Experience prioritization vs uniform sampling: Prioritization focuses on important experiences but may miss rare but valuable ones
  - Single network vs multiple networks: Single conditioned network is more parameter-efficient but may be harder to train

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor convergence: May indicate inaccurate model predictions or suboptimal weight vector selection
  - High variance in performance: Could suggest instability in the GPI policy computation or model learning
  - Slow improvement: Might indicate that prioritized sampling is not focusing on the right experiences

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement GPI-LS on Deep Sea Treasure with tabular Q-learning to verify weight vector prioritization works
  2. Add model learning to GPI-LS and test on Minecart to verify Dyna planning with prioritized sampling
  3. Scale to continuous actions with MOTD3 extension and test on MO-Hopper to verify the full pipeline

## Limitations
- Theoretical convergence guarantees assume optimal or ε-optimal policies, but algorithm handles sub-optimal policies
- Computational complexity grows exponentially with number of objectives, limiting scalability
- Performance depends heavily on accuracy of learned dynamics model in continuous spaces

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The fundamental GPI theorem and its application to MORL is well-established in the literature. The weight vector prioritization mechanism (GPI-LS) is theoretically sound and directly follows from GPI guarantees.
- **Medium Confidence**: The integration of Dyna planning with GPI-based experience prioritization (GPI-PD) is novel and the theoretical framework appears sound, but the empirical validation is limited to specific benchmark tasks.
- **Medium Confidence**: The anytime learning property and monotonic improvement claims are supported by theory, but the practical performance depends heavily on the quality of the underlying RL algorithm and model learning.
