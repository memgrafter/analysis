# NTK-SAP: Improving neural network pruning by aligning training dynamics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.02840
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02840
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes NTK-SAP pruning method that leverages NTK spectrum analysis to identify high-performing sub-networks, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines across multiple datasets and architectures

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of identifying high-performing sub-networks through pruning before training by proposing Neural Tangent Kernel Spectrum-Aware Pruning (NTK-SAP). The method focuses on maintaining the training dynamics of the original dense network by preserving its NTK spectrum. NTK-SAP employs a multi-sampling formulation and a "new-input-new-weight" trick to efficiently estimate the NTK spectrum in a data-agnostic manner. The approach consistently outperforms state-of-the-art foresight pruning methods like SNIP, GraSP, and Synflow across various datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet) and architectures (ResNet-20, ResNet-18, VGG-16, ResNet-50), particularly at extreme sparsity ratios.

## Method Summary
NTK-SAP introduces a novel foresight pruning method that leverages the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) spectrum as a metric to guide pruning decisions. The key insight is that preserving the NTK spectrum aligns the training dynamics of the pruned network to its dense counterpart. The method employs a multi-sampling formulation to estimate the NTK spectrum efficiently, making it data-agnostic through a "new-input-new-weight" trick. This allows the method to identify connections that have the least influence on the NTK spectrum and prune them, thus finding trainable sub-networks that maintain performance close to the dense network. The approach is evaluated against several state-of-the-art foresight pruning methods across various datasets and architectures, demonstrating superior performance especially at extreme sparsity levels.

## Key Results
- NTK-SAP consistently outperforms state-of-the-art foresight pruning methods (SNIP, GraSP, Synflow) across multiple datasets
- The method shows particular effectiveness at extreme sparsity ratios (>95%), where it maintains significantly better performance than baselines
- NTK-SAP achieves data-agnostic pruning with performance close to the dense network while reducing computational overhead

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
NTK-SAP works by leveraging the Neural Tangent Kernel spectrum as a proxy for training dynamics. The NTK spectrum captures how different input features influence the network's learning trajectory during training. By preserving this spectrum during pruning, the method ensures that the pruned network maintains similar learning dynamics to the original dense network. The multi-sampling formulation provides robust estimation of the NTK spectrum while the "new-input-new-weight" trick enables data-agnostic pruning decisions. This approach effectively identifies redundant connections that minimally impact the network's ability to learn, allowing for aggressive pruning without sacrificing performance.

## Foundational Learning
**Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK)**: A kernel that describes the training dynamics of infinitely wide neural networks in the lazy training regime. Why needed: Provides theoretical framework for understanding how neural networks learn. Quick check: Verify NTK approximation holds for finite-width networks used in experiments.

**Training Dynamics Alignment**: The concept that preserving certain spectral properties ensures similar learning trajectories between pruned and dense networks. Why needed: Forms the theoretical basis for why NTK spectrum preservation matters. Quick check: Compare training loss curves between pruned and dense networks.

**Foresight Pruning**: Pruning decisions made before any training occurs based on initial network properties. Why needed: Enables finding sparse architectures without expensive retraining cycles. Quick check: Measure time saved compared to iterative pruning approaches.

**Multi-sampling Estimation**: Using multiple input samples to robustly estimate the NTK spectrum. Why needed: Reduces variance in spectrum estimation for reliable pruning decisions. Quick check: Analyze sensitivity of results to number of samples used.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input data -> NTK Spectrum Estimator -> Influence Score Calculator -> Pruning Decision Module -> Sparse Network

**Critical Path**: The NTK spectrum estimation and influence scoring represent the computational bottleneck, as these determine which connections to prune. The pruning decision module applies the scores to remove weights, while the sparse network inherits the learned structure.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades computational overhead during the pruning phase for improved final performance and reduced training time. Using a data-agnostic approach sacrifices some precision compared to data-specific methods but gains significant flexibility and efficiency.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor performance may indicate: (1) NTK spectrum estimation failure due to insufficient samples, (2) Pruning decisions that remove critical connections for specific tasks, or (3) Mismatch between theoretical assumptions (e.g., lazy training regime) and practical network behavior.

## Limitations
- Theoretical connection between NTK spectrum preservation and generalization performance lacks rigorous proof
- Computational efficiency gains from the "new-input-new-weight" trick are not quantified relative to standard NTK computation methods
- Performance at extreme sparsity levels (>99%) requires additional verification across more diverse architectures
