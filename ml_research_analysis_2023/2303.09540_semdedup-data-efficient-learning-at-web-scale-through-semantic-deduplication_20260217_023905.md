# SemDeDup: Data-efficient learning at web-scale through semantic deduplication

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.09540
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09540
- Reference count: 40
- Removes 50% of LAION-440M data with minimal performance loss and 2x faster training

## Executive Summary
SemDeDup addresses data redundancy in large web-scale datasets by using pre-trained foundation models to embed data, clustering embeddings, and removing semantic duplicates within each cluster. Applied to LAION-440M, it removes 50% of the data while preserving performance and achieving 2x faster training. The approach also improves out-of-distribution performance and achieves 15% efficiency gains on C4 compared to prior methods.

## Method Summary
SemDeDup embeds web-scale data using pre-trained foundation models (CLIP for images, OPT for text), clusters embeddings via k-means, and removes semantic duplicates within clusters based on cosine similarity thresholds. The method is applied to LAION-440M and C4, training CLIP and T5 models on the deduplicated subsets and evaluating zero-shot accuracy, perplexity, and efficiency gains.

## Key Results
- Removes 50% of LAION-440M data with minimal performance loss
- Achieves 2x faster training on deduplicated data
- Improves out-of-distribution performance on 6 datasets
- Achieves 15% efficiency gains on C4 over prior approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Removing semantic duplicates speeds up learning by reducing redundant gradient updates.
- **Mechanism:** Duplicate data points produce highly similar embeddings and gradients; training on them multiple times wastes compute without new information gain.
- **Core assumption:** The pre-trained foundation model embeddings capture semantic similarity such that duplicates are nearby in embedding space and thus have similar gradients.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Removing semantic duplicates preserves performance and speeds up learning." [section] "We posit that successive learning iterations involving semantic duplicates yield redundant information, thereby wasting valuable computation on data points that are highly similar to those the model has already seen."
- **Break condition:** If embeddings are poor or domain-mismatched, semantic duplicates may not cluster, leading to missed redundancy.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Clustering embeddings enables tractable deduplication by reducing pairwise comparisons from O(n²) to O(n²/k).
- **Mechanism:** K-means partitions the embedding space into k clusters; duplicates are searched only within each cluster, dramatically reducing computation.
- **Core assumption:** Duplicates of any given data point are likely to fall within the same cluster.
- **Evidence anchors:** [section] "The k-means clustering step in SemDeDup reduces this complexity substantially from O(n²) to O(n²/k) assuming approximately uniform cluster size."
- **Break condition:** If k is too small relative to dataset size, cluster size N/k becomes too large and duplicates span clusters.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Training on deduplicated data can improve out-of-distribution performance.
- **Mechanism:** Removing duplicates reduces overfitting to frequent patterns, improving generalization to unseen data.
- **Core assumption:** Redundant data overemphasizes specific patterns, hurting OOD generalization.
- **Evidence anchors:** [abstract] "Moreover, performance increases out of distribution." [section] "We also evaluated out-of-distribution robustness on 6 datasets... SemDeDup increased average performance over baseline."
- **Break condition:** If redundancy is beneficial for certain OOD patterns, deduplication could hurt.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Semantic similarity in embedding space
  - **Why needed here:** SemDeDup relies on pre-trained embeddings to detect duplicates; understanding how embeddings capture semantics is key to why it works.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might two perceptually different images have similar CLIP embeddings?

- **Concept:** K-means clustering and its computational scaling
  - **Why needed here:** Clustering is the bottleneck step; understanding its complexity helps reason about tractability.
  - **Quick check question:** How does increasing k affect both cluster size and the probability of capturing all duplicates?

- **Concept:** Cosine similarity and nearest-neighbor search
  - **Why needed here:** Duplicates are identified by cosine similarity thresholds; understanding this metric is essential for tuning ϵ.
  - **Quick check question:** What happens to the number of detected duplicates as ϵ increases?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data loader -> Foundation model embedding extractor -> K-means clusterer -> Pairwise cosine similarity filter -> Data subset writer
- **Critical path:** Embedding extraction -> Clustering -> Within-cluster deduplication
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Larger k -> smaller clusters -> more likely to capture duplicates but higher clustering cost
  - Smaller ϵ -> stricter deduplication -> fewer duplicates kept but risk of removing useful variation
- **Failure signatures:**
  - High cluster size variance -> duplicates missed across clusters
  - ϵ too low -> no data removed, no speed gain
  - ϵ too high -> too much data removed, performance drop
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run SemDeDup on a small subset (10% of clusters) with k=10K, 25K, 50K; measure intersection ratio to assess k sensitivity.
  2. Train CLIP on deduplicated LAION-440M at ϵ=0.03 vs random pruning; compare ImageNet top-1 accuracy.
  3. Measure training time reduction for 50% data kept vs baseline; confirm 2x speedup claim.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** What is the optimal data pruning policy for finding the smallest possible data subset under computational tractability and performance constraints?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "the optimal data pruning policy for finding the smallest possible data subset under computational tractability and performance constraints remains, as ever, an extremely difficult open question."
- **Why unresolved:** This is a broad, fundamental question in machine learning that involves balancing multiple competing factors including data quality, computational efficiency, and model performance. The paper acknowledges this is an open problem without providing a definitive answer.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A comprehensive theoretical framework and empirical validation showing how to optimally balance these factors across different domains and model architectures.

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How would SemDeDup's performance change when applied to foundation models of different scales compared to the small models used in this study?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "It is possible that results may change with scale, though following [13], it is likely increasing scale would further improve the benefits of data curation."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only tested on relatively small models (125M and 1.3B parameters) compared to the best available models. The scalability of the approach to larger models remains untested.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Systematic experiments applying SemDeDup to increasingly large foundation models (e.g., 10B, 100B parameters) while measuring efficiency gains and performance changes.

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** What other aspects of semantic redundancy exist beyond those captured by SemDeDup that could further reduce dataset size without sacrificing performance?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] "While SemDeDup does an effective job of removing semantic duplicates and some semantically redundant data points, it is only one way to remove uninformative data points. In particular, this work does not capture many aspects of semantic redundancy..."
- **Why unresolved:** The paper acknowledges limitations in its approach and suggests there are additional forms of redundancy not captured by current methods.
- **What evidence would resolve it:** Identification and quantification of additional types of semantic redundancy, along with methods to detect and remove them while maintaining model performance.

## Limitations
- The mechanism's generality across domains remains untested despite experimental evidence
- The relationship between dataset deduplication and OOD generalization is not well-established
- Empirical evidence linking duplicate embeddings to similar gradients is indirect

## Confidence
- **High confidence:** The basic premise that large web-scale datasets contain significant redundancy, and that removing some redundancy can speed up training.
- **Medium confidence:** The mechanism by which semantic deduplication works (reducing redundant gradient updates).
- **Low confidence:** The claim about OOD performance improvement.

## Next Checks
1. **Cluster quality validation:** Measure the variance in cluster sizes across different k values (10K, 25K, 50K) on a 10% sample of LAION-440M to empirically validate whether duplicates are likely to be captured within clusters.
2. **Embedding quality assessment:** Evaluate whether CLIP embeddings truly capture semantic similarity by measuring the correlation between embedding cosine similarity and human-judged semantic similarity on a held-out subset.
3. **Gradient analysis experiment:** For a small subset of duplicate pairs identified by SemDeDup, measure the actual gradient similarity during training to directly test the hypothesis that semantic duplicates produce redundant gradient updates.
