# You Don't Have to Be Perfect to Be Amazing: Unveil the Utility of Synthetic Images

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.18337
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18337
- **Reference count:** 14
- **Key outcome:** Novel framework evaluates synthetic medical images across fidelity, variety, privacy, and utility, revealing task-dependent trade-offs.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a comprehensive framework for evaluating synthetic medical images across four dimensions: fidelity, variety, privacy, and utility. The authors propose novel disentangled metrics, particularly addressing privacy by measuring membership inference risks and adjusting fidelity evaluation to avoid entanglement. Through extensive experiments on over 100k chest X-ray images, they demonstrate that fidelity, variety, and privacy are in trade-off, while utility depends on the downstream task. For intra-task classification, high-fidelity (even mode-collapsed) images improve accuracy, whereas cross-task tasks benefit from high variety. Notably, the study shows that synthetic images can achieve both high utility and privacy in transfer learning, providing strong rationale for using generative models in privacy-preserving medical applications. The work offers practical guidance for selecting synthetic models based on task-specific requirements and advances the understanding of utility-aware deep generative models in medical image synthesis.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a comprehensive evaluation framework for synthetic medical images that disentangles four key aspects: fidelity (image quality), variety (diversity), privacy (protection against membership inference), and utility (performance in downstream tasks). They introduce novel metrics to measure each dimension independently, addressing the challenge of metric entanglement. The framework is validated through extensive experiments using StyleGAN2 trained on over 100k chest X-ray images from the CheXpert dataset. The utility evaluation is performed across multiple downstream tasks including disease classification, segmentation, and transfer learning scenarios. The study systematically analyzes how these four dimensions trade off against each other and identifies task-specific requirements for optimal synthetic image generation.

## Key Results
- High-fidelity synthetic images improve intra-task classification accuracy even when mode-collapsed, while cross-task applications require high variety
- Fidelity, variety, and privacy exist in a three-way trade-off relationship, with no single model excelling at all three simultaneously
- Synthetic images can achieve both high utility and strong privacy protection in transfer learning scenarios, validating their use in privacy-preserving medical applications

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by systematically disentangling the evaluation of synthetic medical images across four distinct dimensions that are typically conflated in existing approaches. By measuring fidelity (image quality), variety (diversity), privacy (membership inference risk), and utility (downstream task performance) separately, the authors reveal that these dimensions are inherently in trade-off relationships. This disentanglement allows for task-specific optimization: high-fidelity models benefit classification tasks by providing clean, consistent examples, while high-variety models support cross-task generalization by covering the full data distribution. The privacy dimension is particularly important as it quantifies the risk of exposing patient information through synthetic samples. The framework's effectiveness stems from recognizing that different downstream applications have fundamentally different requirements for synthetic data quality.

## Foundational Learning

- **Membership inference risk**: Why needed - Quantifies privacy leakage from synthetic samples; Quick check - Test if attackers can distinguish real patient data from synthetic samples
- **Utility-aware generation**: Why needed - Different tasks require different synthetic data properties; Quick check - Validate utility improvements across multiple downstream tasks
- **Trade-off analysis**: Why needed - Reveals fundamental limitations in synthetic data generation; Quick check - Confirm no single model excels at fidelity, variety, and privacy simultaneously
- **Transfer learning validation**: Why needed - Demonstrates practical utility of synthetic data; Quick check - Measure performance improvements when fine-tuning on synthetic data

## Limitations
- Generalizability to other medical imaging modalities (CT, MRI) remains uncertain and requires validation
- Privacy analysis is limited to membership inference attacks and may not capture all potential privacy risks
- Findings are based on chest X-ray images, which may limit applicability to other anatomical regions or disease types

## Confidence
- Trade-off relationships (fidelity, variety, privacy): High
- Utility dependence on downstream tasks: High
- Privacy protection through synthetic images: Medium
- Generalization across medical imaging modalities: Low
