# Meta-learning approaches for few-shot learning: A survey of recent advances

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.07502
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07502
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Comprehensive survey categorizing recent meta-learning methods for few-shot learning into metric-based, memory-based, and learning-based approaches, with performance analysis on Omniglot, miniImageNet, and CUB-200-2011

## Executive Summary
This survey provides a comprehensive overview of recent advances in meta-learning for few-shot learning, organizing methods into three main categories: metric-based, memory-based, and learning-based approaches. The paper examines how these methods address the challenge of learning from limited data by leveraging knowledge from previous tasks. Through systematic analysis of state-of-the-art algorithms and their performance on benchmark datasets, the survey identifies key challenges and future research directions in the field.

## Method Summary
The survey systematically categorizes meta-learning approaches for few-shot learning, examining metric-based methods that learn similarity measures (Siamese networks, prototypical networks, relation networks), memory-based methods that leverage external or internal memory components, and learning-based methods that learn initialization, parameters, or optimizers (including MAML). The paper reviews performance across standard benchmark datasets like Omniglot, miniImageNet, and CUB-200-2011, discussing various algorithms within each category and their relative strengths and limitations.

## Key Results
- Metric-based methods achieve strong performance on simple datasets like Omniglot but struggle with complex datasets like miniImageNet
- MAML and its variants represent the most prominent learning-based approaches, learning initialization parameters for rapid adaptation
- Memory-based methods show promise but face challenges in effectively managing and retrieving relevant information
- Current methods face limitations in uncertainty estimation, catastrophic forgetting, and stability across domain shifts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Meta-learning enables rapid adaptation to new tasks with few samples by leveraging knowledge from previous tasks
- Mechanism: Meta-learning algorithms learn a shared initialization or parameterization that allows quick adaptation to new tasks with minimal fine-tuning
- Core assumption: Tasks encountered during meta-training are related enough that knowledge transfer is possible and beneficial
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Meta-learning is a promising approach that addresses these issues by adapting to new tasks with few-shot datasets."
  - [section 2.1] "Meta-learning refers to a variety of techniques that aim to improve adaptation through transferring generic and accumulated knowledge (meta-data) from prior experiences with few data points to adapt to new tasks quickly without requiring training from scratch."
  - [corpus] "Found 25 related papers (using 8). Average neighbor FMR=0.606, average citations=0.0."
- Break Condition: If tasks are too dissimilar, the meta-learned knowledge may not transfer effectively, leading to poor performance on new tasks

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Metric-based meta-learning methods learn similarity measures to compare query and support samples for few-shot classification
- Mechanism: These methods learn an embedding function that maps samples into a lower-dimensional space where similar samples are close together. Classification is then performed by comparing the distance between query samples and support samples in this embedding space
- Core assumption: The learned embedding space preserves semantic similarity between samples
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Metric-based methods learn similarity measures to compare query and support samples, including Siamese networks, prototypical networks, and relation networks."
  - [section 3.1] "The key concept in 'learning the metric space' methods is comparing the query set samples to support set samples. Then, the labels of query samples are predicted based on proximity to the support set."
  - [corpus] "Top related titles: A Complete Survey on Contemporary Methods, Emerging Paradigms and Hybrid Approaches for Few-Shot Learning"
- Break Condition: If the embedding function fails to capture the relevant features for similarity, the distance comparisons will be ineffective

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Memory-based meta-learning methods use external or internal memory to retrieve knowledge for few-shot tasks
- Mechanism: These methods employ memory components to store information from previous tasks or samples, allowing for quick retrieval and adaptation to new tasks
- Core assumption: The memory can effectively store and retrieve relevant information for new tasks
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Memory-based methods leverage external or internal memory for knowledge retrieval."
  - [section 3.2] "Memory-based meta-learning techniques have a more dynamic mechanism since they are adaptive to the presented tasks. Principally, memory-based meta-learners own the memory component (internally or externally) that enables them to retrieve information from previous inputs."
  - [corpus] "Found 25 related papers (using 8). Average neighbor FMR=0.606, average citations=0.0."
- Break Condition: If the memory component is not properly managed or the retrieved information is not relevant, the adaptation to new tasks will be hindered

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a meta-learning approach that is confident in its decisions when classifying novel classes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "However, during episodic training, unrelated tasks or even less related tasks may result in divergent optimization directions as well as disparate performance, which eventually may lead to uncertainty in unseen class prediction."
- Why unresolved: Most current meta-learning methods do not explicitly model or quantify uncertainty in their predictions, leading to potential unreliability when dealing with novel classes
- What evidence would resolve it: A meta-learning approach that demonstrates significantly improved accuracy and reduced uncertainty (e.g., lower entropy in predicted probabilities) on benchmark datasets like miniImageNet when classifying novel classes

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we improve the stability of metric-based meta-learning methods across different datasets, particularly more complex ones like miniImageNet?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes "Although GCR, a metric-based meta-learner, achieved the best performance on the Omniglot dataset, metric-based models cannot compete with other non-metric-based in miniImageNet, a colored dataset that is more complex than Omniglot."
- Why unresolved: Current metric-based methods seem to struggle with datasets that have more complex features or larger domain shifts, limiting their applicability
- What evidence would resolve it: A metric-based meta-learning method that achieves comparable or better performance to state-of-the-art methods on both simple datasets (e.g., Omniglot) and complex datasets (e.g., miniImageNet)

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we effectively develop meta-learning approaches for multi-domain, multi-modal, and cross-domain scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the challenges of "Training multi-domain, multi-modal and cross-domain meta-learning" and notes that "As the difference between DBase and DNovel increases, most meta-learning performance will decline."
- Why unresolved: Current meta-learning methods are primarily designed and evaluated on single-domain datasets, limiting their real-world applicability where data often comes from diverse sources
- What evidence would resolve it: A meta-learning approach that demonstrates robust performance across multiple domains (e.g., combining images from different sources like remote sensing and agricultural pests) or modalities (e.g., images and text) without significant performance degradation

## Limitations
- The survey primarily synthesizes results from multiple papers rather than presenting unified experimental results
- Several challenges mentioned (like catastrophic forgetting and stability across datasets) lack quantitative evidence in the survey itself
- The discussion of future research directions appears comprehensive but is largely speculative without concrete validation
- Confidence in reported performance metrics depends heavily on specific implementations and hyperparameters from cited works
