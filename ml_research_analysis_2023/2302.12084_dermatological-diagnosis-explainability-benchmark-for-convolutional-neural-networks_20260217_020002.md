# Dermatological Diagnosis Explainability Benchmark for Convolutional Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12084
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12084
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Xception achieves highest explainability (F1=0.46) among 11 ConvNets, but no architecture reaches expert-level performance

## Executive Summary
This paper benchmarks 11 common convolutional neural network architectures for dermatological diagnosis explainability using Grad-CAM. The study evaluates architectures including DenseNet121, EfficientNet-B0, InceptionV3, InceptionResNetV2, MobileNet, MobileNetV2, NASNetMobile, ResNet50, ResNet50V2, VGG16, and Xception. After pre-training on a clinical dataset and fine-tuning on DermXDB, the validation results show explainability F1 scores ranging from 0.35-0.46. NASNetMobile achieves the highest characteristic-level explainability sensitivity despite mediocre diagnosis performance, highlighting the divergence between explainability and classification accuracy.

## Method Summary
The method involves pre-training 11 ConvNet architectures on a proprietary clinical dataset, then fine-tuning on a DermXDB subset containing 524 images across six skin conditions. Grad-CAM is used to generate explainability maps, which are compared against dermatologist annotations. Performance is evaluated using F1 score, sensitivity, and specificity at both image-level and characteristic-level granularity. The study identifies architectural differences in explainability performance and examines how different ConvNets focus on diagnostic regions.

## Key Results
- Xception achieves highest overall explainability F1 score of 0.46
- NASNetMobile reports highest characteristic-level explainability sensitivity despite mediocre diagnosis performance
- InceptionResNetV2, ResNet50, and VGG16 show lowest explainability performance
- No ConvNet reaches expert-level overall performance, though several match expert-level for specific diseases (actinic keratosis and seborrheic dermatitis)
- Some architectures (EfficientNet-B0, MobileNet, MobileNetV2, VGG16) overfit to watermarks rather than diagnostic features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ConvNet explainability via Grad-CAM correlates with diagnostic performance only when architectures have sufficient feature extraction depth.
- Mechanism: Deeper networks like Xception process more hierarchical features, allowing Grad-CAM to localize meaningful diagnostic regions; shallower architectures focus on coarse patterns that may not map well to expert annotations.
- Core assumption: The number and quality of learned features directly impact the quality of Grad-CAM explanations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "validation results show explainability F1 scores between 0.35-0.46, with Xception achieving the highest (0.46) and InceptionResNetV2, ResNet50, and VGG16 the lowest"
  - [section]: "older ConvNet architectures... tend to focus on small areas that contain characteristics relevant for the diagnosis, e.g. focusing on a single plaque in the psoriasis diagnosis example, while more modern ConvNets pay attention to the entire area covered by diagnosis-relevant lesions"
  - [corpus]: "weak—no direct Grad-CAM vs. depth analysis found in neighbors"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: NASNetMobile's high characteristic-level sensitivity despite low diagnosis performance indicates that explainability can diverge from classification accuracy.
- Mechanism: NASNetMobile learns discriminative features for fine-grained lesion characteristics (e.g., comedones, pustules) that are not necessarily tied to disease-level decision boundaries, allowing better localization even if disease classification fails.
- Core assumption: Feature representations for sub-lesion characteristics can be learned independently of disease class boundaries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "NASNetMobile reports the highest characteristic-level explainability sensitivity, despite its mediocre diagnosis performance"
  - [section]: "most ConvNets report high sensitivity on characteristics often associated with acne and viral warts... while reporting a lower performance on characteristics associated with actinic keratosis and seborrheic dermatitis"
  - [corpus]: "weak—no corpus paper directly addresses explainability vs. accuracy divergence"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Overfitting to training set artifacts (e.g., watermarks) degrades explainability and leads to misleading Grad-CAM focus.
- Mechanism: When a ConvNet overfits to non-diagnostic cues, Grad-CAM will highlight those spurious features rather than clinically relevant regions, producing high internal confidence but poor explanation quality.
- Core assumption: The presence of non-diagnostic artifacts in training data can dominate learned representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "several ConvNets, namely EfficientNet-B0, MobileNet, MobileNetV2, and VGG16 seem to have overfit on the training set, focusing on the watermark rather than the image itself when diagnosing the vitiligo case"
  - [corpus]: "weak—corpus neighbors do not mention watermark overfitting"

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Grad-CAM gradient flow from prediction back to convolutional feature maps
  - Why needed here: Understanding how gradients identify relevant spatial regions is key to interpreting why different architectures yield different explanation quality.
  - Quick check question: In Grad-CAM, which layer's activations are used to generate the localization map, and why is it chosen?

- Concept: F1 score, sensitivity, and specificity for multi-class classification
  - Why needed here: Diagnostic and explainability performance are reported using these metrics; misinterpreting them could lead to wrong architectural choices.
  - Quick check question: If a model has high sensitivity but low specificity for a disease, what does that imply about its predictions?

- Concept: Characteristic-level vs. image-level explanation maps
  - Why needed here: The benchmark distinguishes between localizing entire lesions vs. specific sub-lesion features; conflating them would obscure performance differences.
  - Quick check question: Why might a ConvNet excel at characteristic-level sensitivity but fail at image-level F1 score?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Pre-training pipeline -> Fine-tuning pipeline -> Grad-CAM generation -> Explanation map comparison -> Metric aggregation
- Critical path: Fine-tuning -> Grad-CAM generation -> explanation map comparison -> metric aggregation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Smaller architectures (e.g., NASNetMobile) train faster but may underfit diagnostic complexity
  - Larger architectures (e.g., Xception) yield better explanations but risk overfitting on small datasets
  - Choice of last fixed layer during fine-tuning impacts feature adaptation vs. stability
- Failure signatures:
  - Grad-CAM focuses on watermarks or irrelevant regions -> overfitting to artifacts
  - All ConvNets show uniformly low sensitivity on certain diseases -> dataset bias or annotation inconsistency
  - High variance across runs -> insufficient training data or unstable fine-tuning
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run baseline fine-tuning on all 11 architectures, record per-disease F1 and explainability metrics.
  2. Generate and visualize Grad-CAMs for correctly classified images, compare focus patterns between architectures.
  3. Evaluate explainability at both image and characteristic levels, identify which architectures diverge and why.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- How do visual transformers compare to convolutional neural networks in terms of explainability performance for dermatological diagnosis?
- Which explainability method (Grad-CAM, saliency maps, LIME) provides the most reliable and human-aligned explanations for dermatological diagnosis across different ConvNet architectures?
- How do bias factors such as gender, age, and ethnicity affect the explainability performance of ConvNet architectures in dermatological diagnosis?

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on a proprietary clinical dataset for pre-training, which may introduce undisclosed domain-specific biases
- The DermXDB dataset size (524 images) is relatively small, raising concerns about statistical significance and potential overfitting
- The Grad-CAM method itself has known limitations in capturing fine-grained localization

## Confidence
- High confidence in the architectural performance rankings (Xception > others) due to consistent metric patterns across multiple evaluation dimensions
- Medium confidence in the mechanism linking architectural depth to explainability quality, as direct empirical validation is limited
- Low confidence in the generalizability of findings beyond the specific dataset composition and preprocessing pipeline

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the experiment with a larger, publicly available dermatological dataset to test robustness of architectural rankings
2. Compare Grad-CAM explainability with alternative methods (e.g., SHAP, LIME) to validate that Xception's superior performance isn't method-specific
3. Conduct ablation studies removing watermark artifacts from training data to confirm overfitting hypothesis for MobileNet variants and VGG16
