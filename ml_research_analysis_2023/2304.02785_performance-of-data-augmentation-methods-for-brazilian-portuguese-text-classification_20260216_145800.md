# Performance of Data Augmentation Methods for Brazilian Portuguese Text Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.02785
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02785
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: Data augmentation methods provide minimal performance gains for Brazilian Portuguese text classification

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates data augmentation techniques for Brazilian Portuguese text classification, comparing Easy Data Augmentation (EDA), synonym replacement (Syn), and back translation (BT) across three datasets. The research addresses the challenge of limited Portuguese corpora by testing whether English-developed augmentation methods transfer effectively to this language. While slight F1-score improvements were observed, particularly for smaller datasets using EDA and Syn, statistical significance was minimal. The Syn approach achieved the best overall performance, though the study concludes that language-specific tuning and larger Portuguese corpora are needed for meaningful gains.

## Method Summary
The study trained 2,700 models across three datasets (Tweets, B2W product reviews, and Mercado Libre purchase histories) using SVM classifiers with FastText embeddings. For each dataset, subsets of varying sizes (500-3,000 instances) were created, and three augmentation methods were applied at different percentages (0-100%). The augmentation techniques included Easy Data Augmentation (synonym replacement, random insertion/deletion/swap), synonym replacement using semantic similarity, and back translation through machine translation. Models were evaluated using F1-score, and results were compared against baseline models without augmentation.

## Key Results
- Slight F1-score improvements observed across augmentation methods, but minimal statistical significance
- Synonym replacement (Syn) achieved the best overall performance among augmentation techniques
- EDA and Syn methods showed particular promise for smaller datasets, suggesting context-dependency
- Back translation (BT) performed worse than EDA and Syn in most configurations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Data augmentation techniques work by creating synthetic training examples that expose models to linguistic variations while preserving semantic meaning. These methods expand the effective training data size without requiring additional human-labeled examples, potentially helping models generalize better to unseen variations in language use. For low-resource languages like Brazilian Portuguese, augmentation can partially compensate for limited training data by artificially increasing dataset diversity.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Subset → Augmentation Method (EDA/Syn/BT) → SVM Classifier → F1 Score Evaluation

**Critical Path**: Clean original data → Create random subsets → Apply augmentation at varying percentages → Train SVM with FastText → Evaluate F1 score → Compare against baseline

**Design Tradeoffs**: SVM classifiers offer simplicity and interpretability but may miss complex semantic relationships that transformer models could capture. FastText embeddings provide efficient subword modeling but lack contextual awareness compared to contextual embeddings like BERT.

**Failure Signatures**: Non-significant F1-score improvements suggest either augmentation methods don't generalize well to Portuguese or the baseline models already perform near optimal given the data constraints. Poor back translation performance indicates machine translation quality may degrade semantic preservation.

## Limitations
- Exclusive use of SVM classifiers with FastText embeddings may not capture semantic nuances as effectively as transformer-based models
- Relatively small sample sizes (500-3,000 instances) limit generalization of findings
- Focus on only three augmentation techniques without exploring more sophisticated approaches like contextual synonym replacement

## Confidence
**Low confidence**: Minimal performance gains from augmentation methods - Limited by statistical significance testing, exclusive use of SVM/FastText, and small sample sizes
**Medium confidence**: Synonym replacement performs best overall - Observed across multiple datasets but requires further validation with significance testing

## Next Checks
1. Replicate experiments using transformer-based classifiers (BERT, XLMR) to assess whether richer contextual embeddings yield stronger augmentation effects
2. Conduct significance testing on F1-score differences to determine whether observed improvements are statistically meaningful
3. Test augmentation on genuinely small datasets (<500 instances) to validate whether EDA and Syn methods' effectiveness scales down as suggested
