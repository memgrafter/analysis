# A Comprehensive Survey of AI-Generated Content (AIGC): A History of Generative AI from GAN to ChatGPT

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04226
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04226
- Reference count: 40
- This survey provides the first comprehensive review of AI-generated content (AIGC), tracing the evolution from early generative models like GANs to modern large-scale systems like ChatGPT and DALL-E-2.

## Executive Summary
This survey presents the first comprehensive examination of AI-generated content (AIGC), systematically tracing the evolution from early generative adversarial networks (GANs) through modern transformer-based architectures to large-scale systems like ChatGPT and DALL-E-2. The authors analyze the foundational techniques enabling AIGC, including transformer architectures, reinforcement learning from human feedback, and distributed training infrastructure. The survey covers both unimodal (text and image) and multimodal generation, addresses efficiency challenges through prompt learning and model compression, and discusses critical concerns around factuality, toxicity, and privacy. By synthesizing the complete landscape of AIGC technologies and their applications, the work identifies key open problems and future research directions for this rapidly advancing field.

## Method Summary
The survey employs a systematic literature review approach, examining foundational papers and recent advances in AIGC across multiple domains. The authors analyze technical architectures, training methodologies, and application areas through comprehensive examination of published research. The methodology includes categorization of techniques by generation modality, identification of efficiency approaches, and assessment of ethical concerns. The survey synthesizes findings from over 40 references to construct a cohesive narrative of AIGC evolution and current state.

## Key Results
- Comprehensive historical tracing of AIGC evolution from GANs through transformers to modern large-scale systems
- Systematic analysis of technical foundations including transformer architectures, reinforcement learning from human feedback, and distributed training
- Coverage of both unimodal (text and image) and multimodal generation systems
- Identification of efficiency challenges and solutions through prompt learning and model compression
- Discussion of critical concerns around factuality, toxicity, and privacy in AIGC systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The survey succeeds by providing a structured framework for understanding the rapidly evolving field of AI-generated content. By tracing the historical progression from early GANs through transformer architectures to modern large-scale systems, the authors establish clear developmental pathways that help readers contextualize current capabilities. The systematic categorization of techniques by modality and application area enables comprehensive coverage while maintaining analytical coherence. The inclusion of efficiency techniques and risk assessments addresses both practical deployment considerations and ethical implications, making the survey relevant for both technical practitioners and policy stakeholders.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Input Processing -> Foundation Model (GPT/CLIP/Diffusion) -> Output Generation -> Post-processing

**Critical Path**
Data Input -> Model Processing -> Generation Sampling -> Output Filtering/Refinement

**Design Tradeoffs**
Quality vs. Efficiency: Larger models provide better quality but require more resources
Speed vs. Accuracy: Faster sampling methods may sacrifice generation quality
Safety vs. Creativity: Content filtering may restrict creative outputs

**Failure Signatures**
- Hallucination: Generation of factually incorrect information
- Repetition: Model gets stuck in loops producing similar outputs
- Mode Collapse: Limited diversity in generated outputs
- Toxicity: Generation of harmful or inappropriate content

**Three First Experiments**
1. Test basic text generation capabilities with simple prompts across different models
2. Evaluate image generation quality using standard prompts for different architectures
3. Measure inference latency and memory usage for compressed vs. full models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The survey identifies several open research questions in the AIGC field, including: how to improve factuality and reliability of generated content, methods for reducing computational requirements while maintaining quality, techniques for better multimodal integration, approaches to detect and prevent harmful content generation, and strategies for enabling more controllable and customizable generation while preserving safety.

## Limitations

- Rapidly evolving nature of AIGC technologies may render some technical details obsolete within months
- Survey relies heavily on publicly available research, potentially missing proprietary methods from industrial labs
- Coverage completeness across all AIGC subfields remains uncertain given the breadth of the topic
