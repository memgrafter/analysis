# Unsupervised Domain Transfer with Conditional Invertible Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.10191
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10191
- **Reference count**: 34
- **Primary result**: cINN-based domain transfer achieves up to 37% relative improvement in balanced accuracy for PAT and 21% for HSI compared to simulations.

## Executive Summary
This work introduces a novel approach for unsupervised domain transfer of spectral images using conditional invertible neural networks (cINNs). The method addresses the domain gap between simulated and real spectral data, a critical challenge in synthetic medical image generation for training neural networks. By leveraging the invertible architecture of cINNs, the approach inherently guarantees cycle consistency while enabling efficient maximum likelihood training. The method is applied to hyperspectral imaging (pixel-level) and photoacoustic tomography (image-level) modalities, demonstrating significant improvements in synthetic data realism and downstream classification performance compared to state-of-the-art methods.

## Method Summary
The approach uses a conditional invertible neural network (cINN) architecture to perform domain transfer between simulated and real spectral data. The cINN employs affine coupling blocks conditioned on both domain labels (simulated vs. real) and tissue/organ labels (for simulated data). The network learns a shared latent space through maximum likelihood estimation, with two multiscale discriminators ensuring high visual quality via adversarial training. For hyperspectral imaging, the method operates at the pixel level, while for photoacoustic tomography, it works at the image level. The training combines maximum likelihood loss for statistical fidelity with adversarial loss for visual realism, evaluated on downstream classification tasks including vein/artery and organ classification.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 37% relative improvement in balanced accuracy for PAT and 21% for HSI compared to using simulated data directly
- Outperforms UNIT approach on downstream classification tasks across both spectral imaging modalities
- Demonstrates successful unsupervised domain transfer with inherent cycle consistency through cINN architecture

- Claim: cINN architecture inherently guarantees cycle consistency.
- Mechanism: The invertible nature of cINNs means that encoding and decoding are exact mathematical inverses, ensuring that the output can be perfectly reconstructed from the input.
- Core assumption: The invertibility of the neural network architecture is preserved during training and inference.

- Core assumption: The tissue labels contain sufficient information to guide the spectral transfer process.

- Break condition: If the tissue labels do not accurately represent the spectral characteristics of the data, the transfer may introduce spectral artifacts or inconsistencies.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Maximum likelihood training combined with adversarial training ensures both statistical fidelity and high visual quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input Data -> Conditional Invertible Neural Network (cINN) -> Shared Latent Space -> Domain-Specific Outputs (Simulated/Real) -> Discriminators

- **Critical path**:
  1. Data is encoded into a shared latent space using the cINN.
  2. The latent representation is conditioned on domain and tissue labels.
  3. The conditioned latent representation is decoded to either the simulated or real domain.
  4. The discriminators evaluate the realism of the generated data.
  5. The model is trained using a combination of maximum likelihood and adversarial losses.

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - The high dimensionality of the parameter space of the cINN implies that the method is not suitable for arbitrarily high dimensions.
  - The need for tissue labels for simulated data adds an additional requirement for data preparation.
  - The combination of maximum likelihood and adversarial training may require careful tuning of the loss weights.

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Spectral inconsistencies in the generated data.
  - Mode collapse or hallucinations in the generated data.
  - Poor performance on downstream tasks despite high visual quality.

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train the cINN on a small subset of the data to verify that cycle consistency is maintained.
  2. Evaluate the spectral consistency of the generated data using PCA or other statistical measures.
  3. Test the performance of the cINN on a downstream classification task to ensure that the generated data is useful for the intended application.

## Limitations
- The high dimensionality of the cINN parameter space restricts the method to moderately sized datasets and prevents application to arbitrarily high-dimensional data.
- The requirement for tissue labels on simulated data introduces an additional data preparation burden that may not always be available.
- The method's performance relies heavily on the quality and representativeness of the simulated data, as tissue labels are only available for simulated samples.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The fundamental mechanism of cycle consistency through invertibility is mathematically guaranteed by the cINN architecture, assuming proper implementation and training. The experimental results showing improved downstream classification performance are directly measured and verifiable.

**Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the conditional conditioning approach for maintaining spectral consistency is supported by the experiments but relies on the assumption that tissue labels contain sufficient information for spectral guidance. The combination of maximum likelihood and adversarial training for achieving both statistical fidelity and visual quality is theoretically sound but requires careful implementation.

**Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the approach to other domain transfer scenarios and imaging modalities has not been established. The long-term stability of the method when applied to different data distributions or when the domain gap changes significantly is unknown.
