# Learning Spatio-Temporal Aggregations for Large-Scale Capacity Expansion Problems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08996
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08996
- Reference count: 17
- Key outcome: Reduces network from 88 to 6 nodes and selects representative days from one year, providing 33% lower upper bounds than standard spatial aggregation and 10% lower than standard temporal aggregation

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the computational challenges of solving large-scale Capacity Expansion Problems (CEPs) by developing a graph convolutional autoencoder approach for spatio-temporal aggregation. The method learns to identify similar nodes and operational periods through graph pooling and dimensionality reduction, enabling significant network reduction while maintaining solution quality. Applied to a coupled power and natural gas system in New England, the approach demonstrates substantial improvements over traditional aggregation methods by simultaneously optimizing spatial and temporal reductions.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses a graph convolutional autoencoder with MinCutPool operations to learn spatio-temporal aggregations. The encoder identifies node groups with similar characteristics through graph pooling, while the decoder reconstructs original features from aggregated representations. A multi-objective loss function balances reconstruction accuracy, pooling quality, and entropy regularization. The approach processes block-diagonal feature matrices containing node attributes, compresses them into latent space, and applies clustering to identify representative operational periods.

## Key Results
- Reduces network size from 88 to 6 nodes while maintaining solution quality
- Selects representative operational days from a full year of data
- Provides upper bounds on the original problem that are 33% lower than standard spatial aggregation and 10% lower than standard temporal aggregation approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Graph pooling identifies groups of nodes with similar spatio-temporal patterns, enabling spatial aggregation while preserving critical demand variations.
- Mechanism: MinCutPool loss minimizes cut loss between strongly connected nodes and maximizes orthogonality of node assignments, ensuring that nodes with similar input patterns are grouped together.
- Core assumption: Nodes with similar demand patterns exhibit similar expansion needs and operational characteristics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our autoencoder architecture leverages graph pooling to identify nodes with similar characteristics and minimizes a multi-objective loss function."
  - [section]: "By incorporating graph pooling, we constrain the autoencoder to identify groups of nodes that demonstrate similar patterns in their corresponding parameters."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains no direct discussion of graph pooling for spatio-temporal aggregation in capacity expansion problems.
- Break condition: If node similarities are not driven by demand/production patterns but by other factors (e.g., geographical constraints), the pooling may group irrelevant nodes together.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Low-dimensional latent embeddings from the autoencoder preserve distances between operational periods while reducing noise, enabling better temporal clustering than raw high-dimensional features.
- Mechanism: The encoder compresses high-dimensional spatio-temporal data into low-dimensional latent space, where distances between operational periods better reflect true similarity than in high-dimensional space where noise dominates.
- Core assumption: The bottleneck dimension d' is sufficiently small to filter noise but large enough to preserve meaningful temporal variations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Importantly, these models learn to map high-dimensional inputs into lower-dimensional latent spaces, and they are frequently used in practice to learn latent distances among high-dimensional data points while limiting the confounding effects of random noise."
  - [section]: "These feature matrices can then be vectorized into (d'|N'|)-dimensional vectors, and a generic clustering method, e.g. k-Medoids clustering, can be applied to identify a set of representative operational periods and their corresponding weights."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains no direct discussion of autoencoder-based temporal aggregation for capacity expansion problems.
- Break condition: If the bottleneck dimension is too small, temporal patterns may be lost; if too large, noise dominates and clustering performance degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The multi-objective loss function balances reconstruction accuracy, pooling quality, and entropy regularization to find optimal trade-offs between tractability and solution quality.
- Mechanism: Hyperparameters {αR, αP, αH} weight reconstruction loss, pooling loss, and negative entropy loss respectively. Tuning these balances the fidelity of aggregated model to original versus computational tractability.
- Core assumption: There exists a hyperparameter setting that simultaneously preserves solution quality and reduces problem complexity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "This loss function is specifically tailored to induce desirable spatial and temporal aggregations in terms of tractability and optimality of CEPHN."
  - [section]: "The hyperparameters {αR, αP, αH} weight the reconstruction, pooling, and entropy losses respectively. Altogether, we must specify up to 3 + |S| hyperparameters to weight the various objectives considered in this loss function."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains no direct discussion of multi-objective loss functions for spatio-temporal aggregation in capacity expansion problems.
- Break condition: If optimal hyperparameter settings are highly problem-specific and cannot be determined efficiently, the approach may not generalize well.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) for learning node representations
  - Why needed here: CEPHN problems have underlying network structure where nodes interact based on geographical proximity and physical interdependencies (e.g., gas flow to power plants). GCNs can capture these interactions better than standard ML methods.
  - Quick check question: How does a graph convolution operation aggregate information from neighboring nodes?

- Concept: Dimensionality reduction and the "curse of dimensionality"
  - Why needed here: The spatio-temporal dataset for CEPHN problems is extremely high-dimensional (88 nodes × 24 hours × 365 days × multiple features). Standard clustering algorithms fail in such high dimensions due to noise dominating distance calculations.
  - Quick check question: Why do distance metrics become less meaningful as dimensionality increases?

- Concept: Multi-objective optimization and hyperparameter tuning
  - Why needed here: The aggregation must balance multiple competing objectives - reconstruction accuracy, computational tractability, and solution quality. Finding the right trade-off requires understanding how to tune multiple loss components.
  - Quick check question: What happens to solution quality if you prioritize reconstruction loss over pooling loss in the multi-objective function?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input matrix → GCN encoder → MinCutPool → Feature aggregation → Decoder → Reconstruction → Loss calculation → Backpropagation
- Critical path: Input → GCN encoder → MinCutPool → Feature aggregation → Decoder → Reconstruction → Loss calculation → Backpropagation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pooling vs. Reconstruction: Higher pooling loss yields fewer nodes but may lose important details
  - Encoder depth: Shallow networks (like this one) work better with limited data but may miss complex patterns
  - Temporal vs. Spatial aggregation: Can be learned jointly or separately depending on problem structure
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor reconstruction loss but good pooling loss → Aggregation captures spatial structure but loses temporal patterns
  - High entropy loss → Pooling produces uneven node group sizes or assigns nodes to multiple groups
  - Convergence to uniform pooling → All nodes assigned to all groups (degenerate solution)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with only pooling loss (αR=0, αP=1, αH=0) and visualize node groupings to verify spatial aggregation works
  2. Train with only reconstruction loss (αR=1, αP=0, αH=0) and cluster the latent space to verify temporal aggregation works
  3. Vary the bottleneck dimension d' and measure reconstruction error vs. clustering performance to find optimal compression level

## Limitations
- The method requires careful hyperparameter tuning (up to 3+|S| parameters) which may limit scalability to different problem instances
- Performance depends heavily on the quality of node feature representations and their ability to capture true similarities
- Computational overhead of training the autoencoder is not discussed
- Assumes that similar demand patterns lead to similar expansion needs, which may not hold for all infrastructure systems

## Confidence

### Major Uncertainties and Limitations

- Mechanism 2 (Latent embeddings for temporal clustering): Medium confidence - logical mechanism but no ablation studies showing impact of bottleneck dimension
- Mechanism 3 (Multi-objective loss function): Low confidence - theoretical formulation present but limited discussion of hyperparameter sensitivity and optimization challenges
