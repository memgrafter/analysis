# Computational Language Acquisition with Theory of Mind

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.01502
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01502
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: Theory of Mind components improve speaker performance and lead to more complex languages when trained on harder distractors

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a computational framework for modeling language acquisition in image referential games, incorporating Theory of Mind (ToM) into speaker models. The approach adds pragmatic reranking of candidate utterances using a learned internal listener, enabling more sophisticated language development. The study also investigates how increasing distractor difficulty affects language complexity and fluency, finding that more challenging training conditions produce richer linguistic outputs.

## Method Summary
The framework models language acquisition through an image referential game where speakers must describe target images while considering distractors. The core innovation is adding a Theory of Mind component that allows speakers to rerank candidate utterances based on predictions from a learned internal listener model. The training process involves progressively increasing distractor difficulty by upweighting semantically and visually similar images, creating a curriculum that challenges the speaker to develop more nuanced and precise language.

## Key Results
- ToM-enhanced speaker models show measurable performance improvements over baseline approaches
- Training on harder distractors leads to the development of more complex and fluent languages
- The combination of ToM reasoning and difficult distractors produces the most sophisticated linguistic outputs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by integrating pragmatic reasoning into the language acquisition process. The Theory of Mind component enables speakers to consider how listeners will interpret their utterances, allowing them to select more effective descriptions. By progressively increasing distractor difficulty, the system is forced to develop finer-grained distinctions in its language, moving beyond simple categorical descriptions to more nuanced characterizations.

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map:** Speaker Model -> Theory of Mind Module -> Internal Listener -> Utterance Reranker

**Critical Path:** Target image → Speaker generates candidates → ToM module evaluates each → Internal listener predicts listener interpretation → Best utterance selected

**Design Tradeoffs:** The ToM approach trades computational efficiency for improved pragmatic reasoning. The internal listener adds complexity but enables more sophisticated language. The distractor difficulty manipulation requires careful curriculum design but produces better linguistic outcomes.

**Failure Signatures:** If the internal listener is poorly trained, ToM reasoning will be ineffective. If distractor difficulty increases too quickly, learning may fail entirely. If the speaker model is too simple, it cannot benefit from ToM enhancements.

**First Experiments:** 1) Test speaker performance with and without ToM on simple referential tasks 2) Vary distractor difficulty while keeping ToM constant 3) Compare different internal listener architectures for ToM component

## Limitations
- Limited to specific image referential game setting, may not generalize to broader language acquisition
- Computational complexity of ToM component may limit scalability to larger domains
- Distractor difficulty manipulation relies on specific metrics that may not capture all pragmatic aspects

## Confidence
High confidence in the core finding that ToM improves speaker performance. Medium confidence in the generalizability of results to other domains. Low confidence in the scalability claims without additional testing.

## Next Checks
