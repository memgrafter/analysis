# Inference Time Evidences of Adversarial Attacks for Forensic on Transformers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13356
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13356
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: Frequency ratios and posterior entropy show promise for detecting adversarial attacks on ViTs, with model-specific attention patterns offering additional insights

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates inference-time signatures for detecting adversarial attacks on Vision Transformers. The authors propose four quantification methods - frequency ratio, posterior entropy, attention profile, and CKA similarity - and evaluate them across multiple ViT and T2T-ViT models under various attack types (FGSM, PGD, C&W). Results show that frequency-based and entropy-based quantifications are most effective for strong attacks, while attention and CKA-based methods reveal model-specific vulnerabilities. The study demonstrates that adversarial perturbations create measurable signatures in both input and latent representations, though effectiveness varies significantly with attack strength.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates four inference-time quantifications on ViT and T2T-ViT models using ImageNet data. For each sample, the authors compute: (1) frequency ratio from DCT coefficients, (2) posterior entropy from softmax outputs, (3) attention distances across all heads, and (4) CKA similarity between layer representations. These quantifications are compared between clean and adversarially attacked samples (FGSM, PGD, C&W) using Bhattacharyya coefficient to measure separability. The analysis spans multiple model architectures and attack configurations with varying perturbation strengths.

## Key Results
- Frequency ratio and posterior entropy show the strongest discrimination between clean and attacked samples, especially for strong attacks (ϵ≥0.03)
- Attention-based and CKA-based quantifications reveal model-specific patterns but require cherry-picking heads for practical detection
- ViTs maintain high-confidence predictions (entropy near 0) even under attacks, unlike T2T-ViTs which show more varied distributions
- Adversarial perturbations affect latent vectors after projection layers and MLPs of certain blocks, while attention heads remain relatively stable

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Low-frequency components in adversarial images carry more energy than clean images, making frequency ratio a discriminative signature
- Mechanism: Adversarial perturbations constrained in spatial domain concentrate energy in lower frequencies to maintain perceptual similarity while affecting decision boundaries
- Core assumption: Energy spectrum of adversarial noise differs systematically from natural image noise
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Low-frequency components in adversarialy perturbed images carry more energy"
  - [section] "This conﬁrms that ViTs are also vulnerable to low frequency when given enough perturbation budget"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus focuses on forensic detection of synthetic media, not frequency-based attack signatures
- Break condition: If attacks use frequency-aware optimization or perturbation budget is too small

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adversarial attacks increase entropy of posterior probability distributions across classes
- Mechanism: Well-crafted adversarial examples force model uncertainty about correct class without strongly committing to any alternative class
- Core assumption: Attacks reduce confidence in true class without necessarily maximizing confidence in specific wrong class
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the entropy of posterior probability over all classes (for classiﬁcation tasks) increases"
  - [section] "The output f(x) (after softmax) of any classiﬁer is usually interpreted as the posterior probabilities for respective classes"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains forensic detection work but not specifically on posterior entropy analysis
- Break condition: If attacks are optimized to minimize entropy or softmax temperatures are manipulated

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Certain attention heads and latent layers show systematic deviations in attention profiles when processing adversarial inputs
- Mechanism: Adversarial perturbations interact differently with learned representations at various layers, with some heads more sensitive to perturbations
- Core assumption: Model's learned attention mechanisms create specific "sweet spots" where perturbations have maximum impact
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Attention-based and CKA-based quantifications offer less discriminative power but reveal model-specific patterns"
  - [section] "perturbations either diversify or shrink ADs on several heads, mostly in the ﬁrst blocks"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains work on transformer robustness but not specifically on attention profile analysis
- Break condition: If attacks are designed to preserve attention profile characteristics or model architecture changes significantly

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) for frequency domain analysis
  - Why needed here: Frequency ratio quantification requires computing energy distribution across frequency components
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between DCT and DFT, and why is DCT preferred for image analysis?

- Concept: Self-attention mechanism and attention distance computation
  - Why needed here: Understanding attention distance computation is crucial for interpreting attention profile analysis
  - Quick check question: How does attention distance metric capture locality of dependency in vision transformers?

- Concept: Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) for representation similarity
  - Why needed here: CKA is used to analyze how adversarial perturbations affect latent representation structure
  - Quick check question: What does a high CKA value between two layers indicate about their learned representations?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image → Patch embedding → Transformer blocks → Classification head → Analysis of intermediate representations
- Critical path: Image → Patch embedding → Transformer layers → Classification → Analysis of intermediate representations (attention profiles, CKA matrices)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Frequency analysis provides good discrimination for strong attacks but fails for subtle perturbations
  - Attention profile analysis offers model-specific insights but requires cherry-picking heads
  - CKA similarity captures representation-level changes but needs batch processing and shows weak discrimination
- Failure signatures:
  - High BC values (>0.95) indicate no meaningful separation between clean and adversarial samples
  - Frequency ratio distributions overlapping completely between conditions
  - Attention distances showing minimal variation across samples
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement frequency ratio computation and visualize distributions for clean vs. FGSM-attacked samples
  2. Compute attention distances for all heads and identify which show largest variance under attacks
  3. Calculate CKA similarity matrices for clean and attacked batches, focusing on layer pairs showing most variation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why do vision transformers exhibit different entropy behaviors in posterior probabilities compared to CNNs, and what underlying architectural differences account for this?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] ViTs consistently output very high confidence predictions (entropy near 0) even under adversarial attacks, while T2T-ViTs show more varied distributions with higher entropy
- Why unresolved: Paper identifies phenomenon but doesn't provide mechanistic explanation for why ViTs learn to produce confident predictions differently than T2T-ViTs
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of how attention mechanisms and patch embeddings affect decision boundaries and confidence calibration

### Open Question 2
- Question: What specific architectural layer positions in vision transformers are most vulnerable to adversarial perturbations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] CKA analysis reveals adversarial perturbations primarily affect latent vectors after projection layers and MLPs of certain blocks
- Why unresolved: Paper observes these patterns but doesn't explain why perturbations localize to specific layers or how this relates to attack success rate
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of gradient flow, attention patterns, and feature representations across all layers during successful versus unsuccessful attacks

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can observed divergence in attention distances for specific heads serve as reliable indicators for adversarial attack detection across different model architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Certain attention heads show significant divergence in attention distances under attacks with BC values improving from 0.97 to 0.85-0.98
- Why unresolved: Paper demonstrates some heads are more volatile but doesn't establish consistency across different models, datasets, or attack types
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic testing of attention distance divergence across diverse model architectures, datasets, and attack families

## Limitations
- Quantifications show strong discrimination only for stronger adversarial perturbations (ϵ≥0.03), failing for subtle attacks
- Attention profile and CKA-based methods require cherry-picking specific heads or layers, making them impractical for general deployment
- Model-specific patterns may not generalize across different architectures

## Confidence
- High confidence: Frequency ratio and posterior entropy quantifications for strong attacks (ϵ≥0.03)
- Medium confidence: Model-specific attention head patterns for attack detection
- Low confidence: Generalizability of CKA-based detection across different ViT architectures
