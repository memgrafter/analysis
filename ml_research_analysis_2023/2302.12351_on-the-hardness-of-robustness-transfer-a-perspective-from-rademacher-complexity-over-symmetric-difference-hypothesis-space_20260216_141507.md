# On the Hardness of Robustness Transfer: A Perspective from Rademacher Complexity over Symmetric Difference Hypothesis Space

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12351
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12351
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: This paper investigates the fundamental hardness of transferring adversarial robustness across domains. The authors propose analyzing the adversarial Rademacher complexity over the symmetric difference hypothesis space, showing it is always greater than the non-adversarial counterpart for linear models. This reveals that robust domain adaptation is intrinsically harder than standard domain adaptation. The authors establish upper bounds on this complexity for linear models and extend results to ReLU neural networks. Although robust risk transfer is provably hard, they discover that adversarial training can benefit standard domain adaptation: small source robust risk implies small target standard risk with a variance controlled by the adversarial budget. Experiments on Digits datasets support these findings, showing adversarial robustness transfer is harder than standard accuracy transfer, and that adversarial training improves standard accuracy on target domains. The paper provides the first theoretical analysis of adversarial robust domain adaptation and offers insights for distributed robust learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the fundamental hardness of transferring adversarial robustness across domains by analyzing the adversarial Rademacher complexity over the symmetric difference hypothesis space. The authors prove that this complexity is always greater than its non-adversarial counterpart for linear models, revealing that robust domain adaptation is intrinsically harder than standard domain adaptation. They establish upper bounds on this complexity for linear models and extend results to ReLU neural networks. Surprisingly, the authors find that while robust risk transfer is provably hard, adversarial training on the source domain can benefit standard accuracy on the target domain, with the residual error controlled by the adversarial budget. Experiments on Digits datasets validate these theoretical findings.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes domain adaptation under adversarial robustness by defining adversarial Rademacher complexity over the symmetric difference hypothesis space (H∆H). For linear models, the authors prove that this complexity exceeds the non-adversarial version and establish upper bounds that depend on the adversarial budget ϵ and regularization norm. They extend these results to ReLU neural networks using Lipschitz continuity arguments. The connection between source robust risk and target standard risk is established through a combinatorial measure V* related to the Subset Sum Problem with Structural Objective. Experiments use Digits datasets (MNIST, MNIST-M, SVHN, SynthDigits, USPS) with convolutional networks trained using k-step PGD attacks (k=7, ϵ=8/255) and ℓ1 regularization for linear models.

## Key Results
- Adversarial Rademacher complexity over H∆H is always greater than non-adversarial complexity for linear models, proving robust domain adaptation is intrinsically harder
- Small source adversarially robust risk implies small target standard risk with residual error controlled by adversarial budget
- ℓ1 regularization helps adversarial transfer by suppressing Rademacher complexity the most among all norms
- Adversarial training improves standard accuracy on target domains despite robust risk transfer being hard

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial Rademacher complexity over symmetric difference hypothesis space is always greater than its non-adversarial counterpart
- Mechanism: The adversarial version considers worst-case perturbations within an ℓ∞ ball, which increases the function class complexity by forcing the model to account for potential adversarial examples
- Core assumption: The adversarial risk function properly extends the standard risk by incorporating the maximum loss over perturbations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For linear models, we show that adversarial version of this complexity is always greater than the non-adversarial one"
  - [section 4.1] Theorem 1 establishes both upper and lower bounds showing the gap between adversarial and non-adversarial complexity
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus papers discuss related concepts but don't directly prove this specific claim
- Break condition: If the loss function doesn't satisfy triangle inequality or isn't properly defined over the perturbed space

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Small source adversarially robust risk implies small target standard risk with adversarial budget-dependent variance
- Mechanism: Adversarial training improves feature robustness, which transfers better to target domains even when evaluating standard accuracy
- Core assumption: The labeling function is consistent across domains and the adversary budget ϵ controls the approximation error
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "small source adversarially robust risk will almost guarantee small target domain standard risk, with the residual error controlled by the adversarial budget"
  - [section 6] Lemma 5 formalizes this relationship using Subset Sum Problem with Structural Objective
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers mention transfer learning benefits but don't establish this specific theoretical connection
- Break condition: When source and target domains have fundamentally different labeling functions or when ϵ is too small to provide meaningful robustness

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ℓ1 regularization helps adversarial transfer by suppressing Rademacher complexity
- Mechanism: Controlling ℓ1-norm of model parameters directly reduces the dimension-dependent terms in complexity bounds
- Core assumption: The Rademacher complexity upper bound has minimal dependence on model dimension when using ℓ1-norm constraints
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] Theorem 1 shows adversarial complexity has smallest dimension dependence when using ℓ1-norm (p=1)
  - [section 7] Experiments demonstrate ℓ1 regularization reduces transfer accuracy drops
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus papers discuss regularization but not specifically for adversarial transfer scenarios
- Break condition: If the model architecture inherently requires larger norms for adequate performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the gap between adversarial and non-adversarial Rademacher complexities over H∆H be quantified for non-linear models beyond ReLU neural networks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper establishes upper bounds for linear models and ReLU neural networks, but the authors acknowledge the need for lower bound analysis for ReLU networks and mention extending the analysis to more complex models as future work.
- Why unresolved: The current theoretical framework relies on specific properties of linear models and ReLU activations, which may not generalize to more complex architectures. The proof techniques for lower bounds may require fundamentally different approaches for non-linear models.
- What evidence would resolve it: A general theoretical framework that characterizes the gap between adversarial and non-adversarial Rademacher complexities for arbitrary hypothesis classes, potentially leveraging concepts from statistical learning theory or functional analysis.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Under what conditions does adversarial training on the source domain guarantee improved standard accuracy on the target domain, and what is the precise relationship between source robust risk, target standard risk, and the adversarial budget?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors establish a connection between source robust risk and target standard risk using a combinatorial measure (V*), showing that larger adversarial budgets lead to smaller residual errors. However, they do not provide precise conditions or quantify the exact relationship.
- Why unresolved: The current analysis relies on a specific hypothesis class (sign linear classifiers) and binary classification setting. Generalizing this result to multi-class classification and more complex models requires further investigation.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical characterization of the conditions under which source robust risk implies target standard risk for various hypothesis classes and loss functions, potentially leveraging concepts from domain adaptation theory and adversarial robustness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of norm (ℓp-norm) for regularizing the model parameters affect the transfer of adversarial robustness across domains, and what is the optimal norm for different scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show that controlling the ℓ1-norm of model parameters suppresses the adversarial Rademacher complexity over H∆H the most. They also provide empirical evidence that ℓ1 regularization reduces the drop in robust accuracy when transferring across domains.
- Why unresolved: The analysis focuses on the impact of ℓ1 regularization on the Rademacher complexity and transfer performance. However, it does not provide a comprehensive comparison of different norms (e.g., ℓ2, ℓ∞) or consider their interaction with other factors such as model architecture and dataset characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing the effectiveness of different norms for regularizing model parameters in terms of transfer performance and robustness, potentially leveraging techniques from regularization theory and empirical risk minimization.

## Limitations
- The theoretical analysis primarily focuses on linear models and ReLU networks, with limited extension to more complex architectures
- Experimental validation is limited to Digits datasets, which may not capture the full complexity of real-world transfer scenarios
- The complexity bounds are asymptotic rather than tight for finite sample sizes, limiting practical applicability

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The claim that adversarial Rademacher complexity over H∆H exceeds non-adversarial complexity for linear models (supported by Theorem 1 with rigorous proofs)
- **Medium Confidence**: The assertion that adversarial training benefits standard domain adaptation (supported by experiments but theoretical bounds have residual terms)
- **Low Confidence**: The extension of results to general neural networks (proof relies on Lipschitz continuity assumptions that may not hold for all architectures)
