# Upper Bound of Real Log Canonical Threshold of Tensor Decomposition and its Application to Bayesian Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05731
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05731
- Reference count: 24
- Key outcome: This paper establishes the upper bound of the real log canonical threshold (RLCT) for tensor decomposition models and derives the Bayesian generalization error.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes the upper bound of the real log canonical threshold (RLCT) for tensor decomposition models and derives the Bayesian generalization error. The RLCT is a birational invariant that characterizes the generalization performance of singular statistical models. For a tensor decomposition model p(X|A,B,C) = 1/(2π)^(IJK/2) exp(-1/2 ||X - A◦B◦C||^2), where A◦B◦C denotes the tensor product, the paper proves that the RLCT λ satisfies λ ≤ H0(I+J+K) - 2/2 + min{m1,m2,m3}, where m1, m2, and m3 are functions of the dimensions and ranks of the tensor components. The proof involves analyzing the singularities of the model using algebraic geometry techniques and resolving them through coordinate transformations. Numerical experiments validate the theoretical bound and show that the RLCT decreases as the model complexity increases.

## Method Summary
The paper uses algebraic geometry techniques, specifically resolution of singularities, to derive an upper bound on the RLCT of a tensor decomposition model. The method involves decomposing the error term into two parts - one related to the deviation from a diagonal core tensor, and another from higher-rank components. Lemma 3.1 (product and sum properties of RLCT) is then applied to compute the bound as the sum of two RLCTs. The bound formula λ ≤ H0(I+J+K) - 2/2 + min{m1,m2,m3} is derived and validated through numerical experiments.

## Key Results
- Proved an upper bound on RLCT for tensor decomposition: λ ≤ H0(I+J+K) - 2/2 + min{m1,m2,m3}
- Derived the bound using algebraic geometry techniques and resolution of singularities
- Numerical experiments validate the bound and show RLCT decreases with model complexity

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The RLCT decreases as model complexity (rank H) increases due to more poles in the zeta function from additional equivalent parameter sets.
- **Mechanism**: When H is large, there are more ways for the tensor decomposition to represent the same distribution (non-identifiability), increasing the number of poles of the zeta function. Since RLCT is the largest pole, more poles lead to a smaller maximum.
- **Core assumption**: The tensor decomposition model is singular (non-identifiable), so multiple parameter sets can yield the same distribution.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] The paper notes that tensor decomposition is "singular" and discusses the RLCT's dependence on model complexity.
  - [section] Section 4.1 explicitly states that as H increases, the number of parameter combinations satisfying A◦B◦C = A0◦B0◦C0 increases, leading to more poles and a smaller RLCT.

- **Break condition**: If the model were regular (identifiable) or if the prior suppressed equivalent parameter sets, the RLCT would not decrease with complexity.

- **Claim**: The upper bound formula λ ≤ H0(I+J+K) - 2/2 + min{m1,m2,m3} captures the trade-off between model rank and tensor dimensions in determining generalization error.

- The bound is shown to be loose for large model ranks (H) in numerical experiments, suggesting it may not be tight in complex models.
- The paper does not provide explicit error bounds for the numerical approximation of RLCT, leaving uncertainty about the precision of the validation results.
