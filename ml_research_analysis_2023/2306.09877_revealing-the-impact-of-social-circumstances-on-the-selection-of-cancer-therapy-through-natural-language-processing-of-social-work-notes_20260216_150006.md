# Revealing the impact of social circumstances on the selection of cancer therapy through natural language processing of social work notes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09877
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09877
- Reference count: 0
- BERT-based model predicts targeted therapy administration from social work notes with AUROC of 0.675

## Executive Summary
This study investigated how social determinants of health influence cancer treatment decisions by analyzing clinical social work notes using natural language processing. Researchers developed UCSF-BERT-MS, a domain-specific BERT model trained on social work documentation to predict targeted therapy administration. The model outperformed publicly available language models, demonstrating that social work notes contain valuable information about factors influencing clinical decision-making. By analyzing 3,323 patients with multiple myeloma at UCSF, the research revealed that social factors like abuse history, risk of death, and social support significantly impact treatment predictions, highlighting the role of social determinants in cancer therapy selection.

## Method Summary
The researchers developed a domain-specific BERT model called UCSF-BERT-MS by fine-tuning a publicly available BERT model on 1.4 million social work notes from UCSF. They created a patient-level representation by averaging the embeddings of each clinical note per patient. The model was trained to predict whether patients received targeted therapy (IMiD) using only social work documentation. Performance was evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation on 3,323 patients, with comparison to general language models including BioBERT and RoBERTa. Feature importance analysis was conducted using SHAP values to identify which social factors most influenced treatment predictions.

## Key Results
- UCSF-BERT-MS achieved AUROC of 0.675 and Macro F1 score of 0.599 in predicting targeted therapy administration
- Model performance improved when incorporating multiple clinical notes per patient
- Social factors including abuse history, risk of death, and social support were identified as significantly influential in treatment predictions
- Domain-specific model outperformed general language models (BioBERT, RoBERTa) on social work note data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The model captures social determinants of health encoded in clinical narratives that influence treatment decisions. By leveraging domain-specific language patterns in social work notes, the fine-tuned BERT model learns representations that reflect how clinicians document and respond to patients' social circumstances.

## Limitations
- Model trained and tested on single institution data, limiting generalizability
- Moderate predictive performance (AUROC 0.675) may restrict clinical utility
- Analysis shows associations but cannot establish causal relationships
- Social work notes represent only a subset of clinical documentation

## Confidence
High confidence in: Development of domain-specific BERT model outperforming general language models on social work note data
Medium confidence in: Identification of specific social factors as influential in treatment predictions
Low confidence in: Generalizability of findings to other institutions and clinical settings
