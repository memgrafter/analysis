# HYperbolic Self-Paced Learning for Self-Supervised Skeleton-based Action Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06242
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06242
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: HYSP outperforms state-of-the-art methods on PKU-MMD I and two out of three downstream tasks on NTU-60 and NTU-120 using only positive pairs

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HYSP, a hyperbolic self-paced learning model for self-supervised skeleton-based action recognition. HYSP leverages hyperbolic uncertainty—derived from the radius of embeddings in the Poincaré ball—to determine the learning pace, prioritizing samples with lower uncertainty during training. The model avoids the computational burden of negative mining by using only positive pairs, combined with a curriculum learning strategy that transitions from Euclidean to hyperbolic optimization to prevent initialization instability.

## Method Summary
HYSP uses an ST-GCN encoder within a BYOL framework, projecting embeddings into hyperbolic space via an exponential map. A curriculum learning schedule (first 50 epochs using only angular/cosine distance, then full hyperbolic Poincaré distance) prevents vanishing gradients at initialization. Hyperbolic uncertainty, defined as 1 - ||h|| where h is the embedding, weights the loss to prioritize less uncertain samples. Training uses RiemannianSGD with weight decay and momentum, transferring to downstream tasks via linear evaluation, semi-supervised (10%), or fine-tuning protocols.

## Key Results
- HYSP achieves state-of-the-art performance on PKU-MMD I dataset
- Outperforms baselines on two of three downstream tasks (linear evaluation and semi-supervised) on NTU-60 and NTU-120
- Only uses positive pairs, avoiding complex negative mining procedures required in contrastive methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hyperbolic uncertainty (1 - ||h||) acts as a self-paced learning signal, weighting training gradients inversely to sample ambiguity.
- Mechanism: During training, the Riemannian gradient of the Poincaré distance depends on the target embedding's radius, so more certain (smaller radius) targets drive learning more strongly. This happens end-to-end without extra supervision.
- Core assumption: Sample uncertainty correlates with learning difficulty and can be reliably estimated by the embedding radius during self-supervised training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "HYSP adopts self-supervision... We propose to use hyperbolic uncertainty to determine the algorithmic learning pace..."
  - [section]: "HYSP stems from the belief that the uncertainty of samples matures during the SSL training and that more certain ones should drive the training more prominently..."
  - [corpus]: Weak—corpus papers focus on contrastive learning and occlusion robustness, not hyperbolic self-pacing.
- Break condition: If uncertainty does not correlate with difficulty, self-pacing fails; if uncertainty collapses to trivial values, gradients vanish.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Positive-only learning avoids ill-posed uncertainty matching between negatives.
- Mechanism: In hyperbolic space, repulsing negatives based on uncertainty is not justifiable because uncertainty is specific to each sample. BYOL's positive-only design sidesteps this issue.
- Core assumption: Uncertainty is intrinsic to a sample and cannot be meaningfully compared across different samples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "HYSP only uses positive pairs and bypasses therefore the complex and computationally-demanding mining procedures required for the negatives in contrastive techniques."
  - [section]: "However, repulsing negatives in terms uncertainty is not really justiﬁable, as the sample uncertainty is detached from the sample identity."
  - [corpus]: Weak—corpus focuses on contrastive and multi-view mining, not hyperbolic geometry.
- Break condition: If negative samples carry different uncertainty semantics, contrastive learning could still be valid; if positives collapse, model fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Curriculum learning prevents vanishing gradients at initialization by starting with Euclidean cosine distance.
- Mechanism: Random initialization places embeddings at the Poincaré ball edge, causing vanishing gradients. Early training uses cosine distance (Euclidean) to stabilize learning, then gradually transitions to full hyperbolic distance.
- Core assumption: Early-stage hyperbolic embeddings are too uncertain, making gradient descent unstable; Euclidean optimization is stable at initialization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "So initially, we only consider angles, which coincides with starting from the conformal Euclidean optimization."
  - [section]: "The risk of stall is avoided by means of curriculum learning... which considers only their angle for the first 50 epochs..."
  - [corpus]: Weak—corpus papers do not discuss initialization or curriculum strategies for hyperbolic spaces.
- Break condition: If embeddings do not start near the boundary or if early cosine optimization misleads later hyperbolic training, performance drops.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Self-paced learning
  - Why needed here: HYSP must decide which samples to prioritize without labels; self-pacing orders training by sample difficulty.
  - Quick check question: How does HYSP determine which samples are "easier" or "harder" during training?

- Concept: Hyperbolic geometry and uncertainty
  - Why needed here: Skeleton action representations can be modeled as points in hyperbolic space where radius encodes uncertainty; this enables uncertainty-driven learning.
  - Quick check question: What is the relationship between embedding radius and sample uncertainty in the Poincaré ball?

- Concept: Contrastive learning and positive pairs
- Why needed here: HYSP needs a self-supervised signal; positive pairs (augmented views) provide this without requiring negative mining.
  - Quick check question: Why does HYSP avoid negatives, and what problem does this solve?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Encoder (ST-GCN) -> Projector (MLP) -> Hyperbolic space (Poincaré ball) -> Predictor (MLP) -> Memory Bank (Queue, positives-only)

- Critical path:
  1. Input augmentation → two views
  2. Encode both views → online & target embeddings
  3. Project to hyperbolic space
  4. Apply curriculum-weighted loss
  5. Backpropagate gradients

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Positives-only vs. negatives: Simpler pipeline but loses discriminative negative force
  - Curriculum vs. direct hyperbolic: More stable but longer training
  - Uncertainty weighting: Adaptive learning but relies on accurate uncertainty estimation

- Failure signatures:
  - Vanishing gradients at start → check curriculum schedule
  - Overfitting late in training → check for collapsing uncertainty
  - Low performance vs. baselines → verify embedding radius correlates with difficulty

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with only cosine loss (no curriculum) → verify instability
  2. Train with negatives included → measure performance drop
  3. Train with fixed uncertainty weighting → confirm importance of dynamic weighting

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the following unresolved issues emerge from the limitations:

1. How does the hyperbolic uncertainty calibration affect the model's performance and interpretability across different datasets and action classes?
2. Can the HYSP model be effectively applied to image classification tasks, and what modifications would be necessary for optimal performance?
3. How does the model's performance and learning dynamics change when using different backbone architectures (e.g., Transformer-based encoders) instead of ST-GCN?
4. What is the optimal curriculum learning schedule (e values) for different datasets and action recognition tasks, and how sensitive is the model to these hyperparameters?
5. How does the hyperbolic self-paced learning approach compare to alternative self-paced learning methods in terms of sample selection strategies and performance?

## Limitations
- The correlation between hyperbolic embedding radius and sample uncertainty remains empirically validated but not theoretically grounded
- Avoiding negative samples removes a powerful discriminative signal present in contrastive methods
- Hyperbolic uncertainty estimation is specific to the Poincaré ball model; generalization to other hyperbolic geometries is unclear

## Confidence

- High confidence: Curriculum learning prevents initialization instability
- Medium confidence: Hyperbolic uncertainty correlates with sample difficulty and drives self-paced learning
- Low confidence: Positive-only learning is inherently superior to contrastive approaches for skeleton-based action recognition

## Next Checks

1. Ablation study: Train HYSP with negative samples included to quantify the contribution of positive-only learning versus other innovations
2. Uncertainty correlation analysis: Systematically measure the relationship between embedding radius and downstream classification accuracy across different difficulty levels
3. Curriculum necessity test: Remove the curriculum (train directly with full hyperbolic distance from initialization) to verify its role in preventing training collapse
