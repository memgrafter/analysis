# Friend-training: Learning from Models of Different but Related Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13683
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13683
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Friend-training is the first cross-task self-training framework that leverages supervision from related tasks for better selection of pseudo-labels. It introduces a translation matcher to map predictions from different tasks into the same space and compute matching scores, and an augmented selector to choose high-quality pseudo-labels based on both model confidence and matching scores. Experiments on conversational semantic role labeling and dialogue rewriting show that friend-training significantly outperforms strong baselines in both domain generalization and few-shot learning scenarios.

## Executive Summary
Friend-training introduces a novel cross-task self-training framework that leverages models trained on related but different tasks to improve pseudo-label selection. Unlike traditional self-training that relies solely on model confidence, friend-training incorporates a translation matcher that maps predictions from different tasks into a common space and computes matching scores. The augmented selector then combines both confidence scores and matching scores to identify high-quality pseudo-labels. This approach addresses the challenge of selecting reliable pseudo-labels in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or domain shift exists between training and test data.

## Method Summary
The friend-training framework operates by training a target task model and a source task model on their respective datasets. During pseudo-label selection, the framework uses a translation matcher to project predictions from both tasks into a shared semantic space, where it computes matching scores between the two sets of predictions. The augmented selector then combines these matching scores with the target model's confidence scores to identify high-quality pseudo-labels. This process enables the framework to leverage the structural relationships between related tasks to improve pseudo-label quality, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data or domain shift.

## Key Results
- Friend-training significantly outperforms strong self-training baselines in both domain generalization and few-shot learning scenarios
- The translation matcher effectively maps predictions from different tasks into comparable spaces, enabling meaningful matching scores
- The augmented selector successfully combines confidence and matching scores to identify high-quality pseudo-labels
- Experiments on conversational semantic role labeling and dialogue rewriting demonstrate the framework's effectiveness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Friend-training works by exploiting the complementary information available from models trained on related tasks. The translation matcher creates a bridge between tasks by mapping their predictions into a shared space, allowing the framework to identify cases where both models agree on important structural elements. This cross-task agreement serves as an additional signal beyond individual model confidence, helping to filter out unreliable pseudo-labels that might be accepted based on confidence alone.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Source Task Model -> Translation Matcher -> Matching Scores
Target Task Model -> Augmented Selector -> High-Quality Pseudo-Labels

Critical Path: Source Task Model → Translation Matcher → Matching Scores → Augmented Selector → Pseudo-Label Selection

Design Tradeoffs:
The framework trades increased computational complexity (computing matching scores between tasks) for improved pseudo-label quality. The translation matcher must balance between capturing meaningful task relationships and avoiding spurious correlations. The augmented selector must weight confidence and matching scores appropriately to avoid being overly conservative or too permissive.

Failure Signatures:
- Poor translation matcher design leading to meaningless matching scores
- Over-reliance on matching scores when source task is not sufficiently related
- Computational bottleneck due to expensive matching score computation
- Suboptimal weight tuning between confidence and matching scores in the selector

## Limitations
- The framework assumes the existence of a related but different task, which may not always be available in practical scenarios
- Evaluation focuses on only two specific NLP tasks, limiting generalizability to other task pairs or domains
- The computational overhead of computing matching scores between tasks is not thoroughly discussed
- The translation matcher's effectiveness depends heavily on the quality of the cross-task mapping
