# Modeling Cross-Cultural Pragmatic Inference with Codenames Duet

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02475
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02475
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces CULTURAL CODES, a dataset for modeling how sociocultural backgrounds influence pragmatic reasoning in collaborative games.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CULTURAL CODES, a dataset for modeling how sociocultural backgrounds influence pragmatic reasoning in collaborative games. It operationalizes pragmatic inference in Codenames Duet, a word reference game, collecting 794 games with 7,703 turns from 153 players. Alongside gameplay, it gathers players' demographics, personalities, and moral values. The paper defines five tasks modeling clue giving and guessing, showing that incorporating sociocultural priors significantly improves model performance across all tasks compared to baselines, demonstrating that cultural background plays a vital role in gameplay decisions.

## Method Summary
The study operationalizes pragmatic inference in Codenames Duet by collecting gameplay data from 153 players across 794 games. The dataset includes not only the gameplay turns (7,703 total) but also player demographics, personality assessments, and moral value surveys. The authors define five specific tasks for modeling clue giving and guessing behaviors, then evaluate models with and without sociocultural priors to assess their impact on performance.

## Key Results
- CULTURAL CODES dataset contains 794 games with 7,703 turns from 153 players
- Incorporating sociocultural priors significantly improves model performance across all five tasks
- Cultural background plays a vital role in gameplay decisions and pragmatic reasoning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by explicitly modeling the relationship between players' sociocultural backgrounds and their pragmatic reasoning patterns in a controlled game environment. By collecting rich metadata alongside gameplay data, the model can learn to associate specific cultural features with different inference strategies. The game's structured nature (limited vocabulary, clear win conditions) provides a controlled environment for observing pragmatic choices while the sociocultural priors help the model capture systematic variations in reasoning across different cultural groups.

## Limitations
- The dataset size (794 games, 7,703 turns, 153 players) may be insufficient to capture full complexity of cultural variation in pragmatic reasoning
- Self-reported demographics, personality traits, and moral values may not fully capture nuanced cultural influences on pragmatic inference
- The operationalization in Codenames Duet may not generalize to broader pragmatic inference scenarios

## Confidence
High: The dataset collection methodology and task definitions are clearly described and reproducible
Medium: The claim that cultural background significantly influences gameplay decisions is supported by data but may overstate the practical impact
Low: The generalizability of findings to broader pragmatic inference scenarios beyond the Codenames Duet context

## Next Checks
1. Conduct a power analysis to determine whether the current sample size (153 players) is sufficient to detect meaningful cultural differences in pragmatic reasoning
2. Test model performance on out-of-distribution cultural groups not represented in the training data
3. Compare model predictions against human judgments in a controlled study where players must explain their reasoning behind clue-giving decisions
