# Measuring Gender Bias in West Slavic Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05783
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05783
- Reference count: 18
- This paper presents the first systematic study of gender bias in West Slavic language models (Czert, SlovakBERT, and PolBERT)

## Executive Summary
This study introduces the first systematic investigation of gender bias in West Slavic language models, focusing on Czert, SlovakBERT, and PolBERT. The authors developed a novel dataset of 923 sentence templates in Czech, Slovak, and Polish, covering male, female, neutral, and non-binary gender categories. Using automatic completion generation and analysis of valence, arousal, and dominance scores, the research reveals distinct patterns of gender bias across the three language models, with Czert and SlovakBERT showing higher rates of hurtful completions for male subjects while PolBERT exhibits the opposite pattern for female subjects.

## Method Summary
The study employs a template-based approach to measure gender bias, using 923 sentence templates across Czech, Slovak, and Polish that systematically vary gender categories. The methodology involves generating completions for each template using the three language models, then analyzing the outputs based on whether they are hurtful and their emotional scores (valence, arousal, and dominance). The templates are designed to capture gender bias across multiple dimensions, allowing for comparison between male, female, neutral, and non-binary subjects within each language model.

## Key Results
- Czert and SlovakBERT models produce more hurtful completions with men as subjects, often related to violence, death, or sickness
- PolBERT model generates more hurtful completions for female subjects compared to male subjects
- The study provides the first quantitative evidence of gender bias patterns in West Slavic language models, highlighting the need for bias mitigation in these systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The template-based methodology allows for systematic and controlled generation of text across different gender categories, enabling direct comparison of bias patterns between models. By using consistent sentence structures while varying only the gender of subjects, the approach isolates gender-related effects from other linguistic or contextual factors. The combination of automatic completion generation with psychological scoring metrics (valence, arousal, dominance) provides a quantitative framework for measuring and comparing bias across different language models and gender categories.

## Foundational Learning
- Sentence templates: Structured sentence patterns used to generate controlled text variations; needed to systematically test bias across consistent contexts; quick check: verify templates cover diverse scenarios and gender categories
- Valence/arousal/dominance scores: Psychological metrics for measuring emotional content in text; needed to quantify the emotional impact of generated completions; quick check: ensure scoring aligns with established psychological frameworks
- Hurtful content detection: Automated or manual classification of potentially harmful text; needed to identify bias manifestations; quick check: validate classification criteria across multiple annotators
- Cross-linguistic analysis: Comparing bias patterns across different languages; needed to identify language-specific versus universal bias patterns; quick check: verify linguistic equivalence of templates across languages
- Gender category representation: Inclusion of male, female, neutral, and non-binary subjects; needed for comprehensive bias assessment; quick check: ensure equal representation and treatment of all categories

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Sentence templates -> Language models (Czert/SlovakBERT/PolBERT) -> Completion generation -> Emotional scoring (valence/arousal/dominance) -> Hurtful content classification -> Bias analysis
Critical path: Template selection → Model completion generation → Scoring computation → Bias pattern identification
Design tradeoffs: Automated scoring vs. human validation (speed/efficiency vs. accuracy); template-based approach vs. open-ended generation (control vs. ecological validity)
Failure signatures: Inconsistent scoring across similar templates; model-specific artifacts in completions; linguistic bias in template translation
First experiments: 1) Generate completions for a small subset of templates and manually verify scoring accuracy; 2) Compare model outputs for identical templates across languages; 3) Test template variations to identify sensitivity to specific linguistic features

## Limitations
- Relies on automatic completion generation without human validation of what constitutes "hurtful" content
- Dataset of 923 templates may not capture the full complexity of gender bias expressions
- Focuses on valence, arousal, and dominance scores but does not examine occupational stereotypes or social role associations

## Confidence
- Main findings (gender bias patterns in West Slavic models): Medium
- Interpretation of directional bias patterns: Medium
- Generalizability across West Slavic languages: Medium
- Methodological approach: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Conduct human evaluation studies to validate the automatic classification of hurtful completions and refine the scoring criteria
2. Expand the template dataset to include occupational and social role contexts to capture a broader spectrum of gender bias manifestations
3. Perform cross-linguistic analysis to determine whether observed differences reflect genuine linguistic or cultural factors versus differences in model training data or architecture
