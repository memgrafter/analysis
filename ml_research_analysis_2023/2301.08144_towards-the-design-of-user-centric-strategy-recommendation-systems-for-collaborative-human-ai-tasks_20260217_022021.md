# Towards the design of user-centric strategy recommendation systems for collaborative Human-AI tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08144
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08144
- Reference count: 40
- Single-line primary result: Personality traits and perceived alignment significantly influence strategy recommendation system usability and perceived intelligence in Human-AI collaborative tasks

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how user preferences and personality traits influence the perception and usability of strategy recommendation systems for collaborative Human-AI tasks. Participants completed tasks in a Risk-based environment with four different recommendation conditions: single, similar, diverse, or all strategy recommendations. The research validated an automatic method to infer user strategy preferences from gameplay choices and examined the impact of personality traits on user experience metrics.

The findings demonstrate that higher perceived alignment with user preferences leads to increased usability and perceived intelligence of the recommendation system. Personality traits play a significant role, with conscientiousness impacting perceived usability and agreeableness increasing task workload. Age also emerged as a factor, with older participants preferring single-strategy recommendations over similar ones.

## Method Summary
The study employed a between-subjects design with 60 participants randomly assigned to one of four experimental conditions: single strategy recommendation, similar strategy recommendations, diverse strategy recommendations, or all available strategy recommendations. Participants engaged in collaborative Human-AI tasks within a Risk-based environment, completing multiple gameplay scenarios. User strategy preferences were automatically inferred from gameplay choices and validated against ground truth data. Personality traits were measured using standardized assessments, and users provided feedback on perceived usability, workload, and alignment with their preferences. Statistical analyses examined the relationships between personality traits, recommendation conditions, and user experience metrics.

## Key Results
- Conscientiousness significantly impacted perceived usability (p < 0.01)
- Agreeableness increased task workload (p < 0.05)
- Higher perceived alignment with user preferences led to increased usability (p < 0.01) and perceived intelligence (p < 0.01) of the recommendation system
- Older participants preferred single-strategy recommendations
- Users generally favored single over similar strategy recommendations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The study's approach works by aligning strategy recommendations with individual user preferences and personality characteristics. When recommendation systems match a user's inferred strategy preferences, users perceive the system as more intelligent and usable. This alignment creates a sense of understanding between the human and AI, enhancing the collaborative experience. Personality traits influence how users interact with and perceive these recommendations, with conscientiousness users being more sensitive to usability factors and agreeableness users experiencing higher cognitive load.

## Foundational Learning
- Strategy preference inference: Automatically deducing user strategies from behavior is crucial for personalization; quick check: validate against multiple task types
- Personality-driven design: Different personality types interact differently with AI systems; quick check: test across diverse personality profiles
- Perceived alignment: User satisfaction depends heavily on feeling understood by the AI; quick check: measure alignment perception vs actual preference match
- Recommendation diversity: The number and type of recommendations affects user experience; quick check: test optimal recommendation set sizes
- Age-related preferences: Demographic factors influence recommendation preferences; quick check: segment by age groups
- Task environment impact: The collaborative task domain affects recommendation effectiveness; quick check: test across different collaborative scenarios

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: User -> Task Environment -> Strategy Inference Engine -> Recommendation Generator -> User Interface -> User Feedback

Critical path: User behavior collection → Strategy preference inference → Recommendation generation → User interaction → Feedback collection → System adaptation

Design tradeoffs: Single recommendations provide clarity but may limit options; diverse recommendations offer choice but increase cognitive load. The system must balance personalization with usability.

Failure signatures: Misalignment between inferred preferences and actual user strategies leads to decreased usability; personality mismatches result in unexpected workload increases.

First experiments:
1. Test strategy inference accuracy across multiple task types
2. Compare user satisfaction between single vs. diverse recommendation conditions
3. Analyze personality trait correlations with different recommendation strategies

## Limitations
- Sample size of 60 participants may limit generalizability
- Single Risk-based task environment may not represent other collaborative AI applications
- Correlational design prevents establishing causal relationships
- Focus on perceived outcomes rather than actual performance improvements

## Confidence
- Strategy preference inference method: High
- Personality trait effects on usability and workload: Medium
- Demographic preferences (age): Low
- Preference for single-strategy recommendations: Medium

## Next Checks
1. Replication with a larger, more diverse sample to verify demographic effects and personality trait impacts
2. Testing across multiple task domains beyond Risk to assess generalizability
3. Longitudinal study to examine whether alignment-based personalization leads to sustained improvements in Human-AI collaboration outcomes rather than just perceived usability
