# EvalAttAI: A Holistic Approach to Evaluating Attribution Maps in Robust and Non-Robust Models

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.08866
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08866
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: EvalAttAI produces consistent faithfulness rankings across different model types and datasets by avoiding the pixel removal error inherent in Deletion/Insertion.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of evaluating attribution map faithfulness in robust and non-robust deep neural networks, which is critical for explainable AI applications in medical imaging. The authors propose EvalAttAI, a novel evaluation metric that incrementally perturbs pixels based on attribution scores rather than removing them, thereby avoiding artifacts introduced by existing Deletion/Insertion methods. Experiments on CIFAR10, PathMNIST, DermaMNIST, and BloodMNIST datasets demonstrate that EvalAttAI produces consistent results where Deletion/Insertion fail. The findings indicate that Vanilla Gradient and SmoothGrad are the most faithful attribution methods, and Bayesian deep neural networks trained via Variational Density Propagation (VDP) are more explainable than standard and robustly trained models when used with Vanilla Gradient.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes EvalAttAI as a novel evaluation metric for attribution map faithfulness. Instead of removing and replacing pixels (as in Deletion/Insertion methods), EvalAttAI incrementally perturbs all pixels proportionally to their attribution scores using a perturbation scale ε. The method maintains image continuity and prevents distribution shifts that occur with pixel replacement. The evaluation compares six attribution methods (Vanilla Gradient, Grad x Image, Guided Backprop, Integrated Gradients, SmoothGrad, GradCAM) across three model types (standard ResNet18, robustly-trained ResNet18, and Bayesian VDP-CNN) on multiple datasets, measuring faithfulness via area under the curve (AUC) of normalized accuracy curves.

## Key Results
- EvalAttAI produces consistent faithfulness rankings across different model types and datasets, while Deletion/Insertion produce contradictory results
- Vanilla Gradient and SmoothGrad are the most faithful attribution methods across all tested models and datasets
- Bayesian deep neural networks trained using Variational Density Propagation (VDP) are more explainable than standard and robustly trained neural networks when used with Vanilla Gradient

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: EvalAttAI produces consistent faithfulness rankings by avoiding the pixel removal error inherent in Deletion/Insertion.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of removing and replacing pixels (which introduces artifacts the model wasn't trained on), EvalAttAI incrementally perturbs all pixels proportionally to their attribution scores, maintaining image continuity and preventing distribution shifts.
- **Core assumption**: The attribution map accurately ranks pixel importance such that small perturbations following these scores will cause proportional changes in model confidence.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "EvalAttAI evaluates attribution methods by incrementally perturbing pixels based on attribution scores rather than removing them, thus avoiding errors introduced by existing methods."
  - [section] "EvalAttAI avoids this issue by perturbing the pixels rather than removing them and replacing with arbitrary values."
- **Break condition**: If the attribution scores are not reliable rankings of pixel importance, or if the perturbation scale ε is too large/small, the method may not correlate well with true faithfulness.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Bayesian deep neural networks trained via VDP are more explainable than standard and robustly-trained models when used with Vanilla Gradient.
- **Mechanism**: VDP propagates uncertainty through the network by maintaining distributions over weights, which inherently captures model confidence and leads to more stable, interpretable gradients for attribution.
- **Core assumption**: Uncertainty propagation improves the quality of attribution maps by providing smoother, more robust gradients that better reflect true feature importance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "the findings indicate that Bayesian deep neural networks trained using the Variational Density Propagation technique are more explainable than standard and robustly trained neural networks when used with Vanilla Gradient."
  - [section] "Based on these results, we can conclude that Bayesian CNNs (trained using the VDP framework) are more explainable than the standard and robustly trained neural networks when attributions are generated using Vanilla Gradient."
- **Break condition**: If the VDP training does not actually improve gradient quality, or if other attribution methods work better with VDP than Vanilla Gradient, the conclusion may not hold.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Vanilla Gradient and SmoothGrad are the most faithful attribution methods across all tested models and datasets.
- **Mechanism**: These methods compute gradients directly from the trained model without additional modifications, providing a direct measure of how input changes affect the output.
- **Core assumption**: Direct gradient computation from the loss function captures the true importance of input features to the model's decision.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "Using our novel evaluation, we found that Bayesian deep neural networks using the Variational Density Propagation technique were consistently more explainable when used with the best performing attribution method, the Vanilla Gradient."
  - [section] "We also observed that the Vanilla Gradient consistently performed better than all other methods on our evaluation metric. We argue that this is expected since this method works directly on the neural network without any alterations or modifications."
- **Break condition**: If other attribution methods capture different aspects of model behavior not reflected in accuracy drops, or if the evaluation metric itself is biased toward gradient-based methods, these may not be the most faithful overall.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Faithfulness vs. Plausibility in XAI
  - **Why needed here**: The paper distinguishes between visual plausibility (how convincing explanations look to humans) and faithfulness (how accurately explanations reflect the model's internal reasoning), which is central to evaluating attribution methods.
  - **Quick check question**: If an attribution map highlights the object of interest (plausible) but perturbing those pixels doesn't affect model confidence much, is it faithful or just plausible?

- **Concept**: Robustness in deep learning models
  - **Why needed here**: The paper evaluates whether robust models (trained with noise/Bayesian methods) produce more faithful explanations, requiring understanding of what makes a model robust.
  - **Quick check question**: How does training with Gaussian noise differ from Bayesian weight distributions in terms of the type of robustness they provide?

- **Concept**: Attribution map evaluation metrics
  - **Why needed here**: Understanding Deletion, Insertion, and the proposed EvalAttAI method is essential for grasping why the new metric is needed and how it improves upon existing approaches.
  - **Quick check question**: Why might replacing removed pixels with the image mean introduce errors that don't occur with the EvalAttAI perturbation approach?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: 
  Data preprocessing -> Model training -> Attribution method generation -> EvalAttAI perturbation -> Accuracy tracking -> Comparison

- **Critical path**: Train models → Generate attribution maps → Apply EvalAttAI perturbations incrementally → Record accuracy changes → Compare AUC across methods → Analyze consistency

- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  ε scaling balances perturbation magnitude vs. maintaining original image structure
  Choosing 45% pixel modification threshold vs. full image modification
  Using accuracy vs. other metrics (confidence scores, logits) for faithfulness

- **Failure signatures**: 
  Inconsistent rankings across datasets suggest metric issues
  High variance in AUC with error bars indicates instability
  Attribution methods causing minimal accuracy drop may be unreliable

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement EvalAttAI with ε=0.1 on a simple model (e.g., logistic regression) and verify it produces meaningful accuracy drops for known important features
  2. Compare EvalAttAI vs. Deletion/Insertion on CIFAR10 with ResNet18 to reproduce the contradictory results shown in the paper
  3. Test all six attribution methods on the standard ResNet18 using EvalAttAI to identify which produce the steepest accuracy drops

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How does the faithfulness of attribution maps differ between naturally trained and adversarially trained robust models?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper mentions that robust models produce more visually plausible explanations but does not directly compare the faithfulness of attribution maps between naturally trained and adversarially trained robust models.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only compares standard, robustly trained, and Bayesian models, but does not specifically examine the difference between naturally trained and adversarially trained robust models.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct experiments comparing the faithfulness of attribution maps for naturally trained and adversarially trained robust models using the proposed EvalAttAI metric.

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How does the faithfulness of attribution maps change when the perturbation scale (epsilon) in the EvalAttAI method is varied?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper mentions that the EvalAttAI method can be tuned using the epsilon parameter, but does not explore the effect of varying this parameter on the faithfulness of attribution maps.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper uses a fixed epsilon value of 0.1 for all experiments, but the optimal value may depend on the specific model and attribution method being evaluated.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Perform experiments with different epsilon values for the EvalAttAI method and analyze the impact on the faithfulness of attribution maps.

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does the faithfulness of attribution maps vary across different medical imaging tasks and datasets?
- **Basis in paper**: [explicit] The paper evaluates the proposed EvalAttAI metric on three medical imaging datasets (PathMNIST, DermaMNIST, and BloodMNIST) but does not explore the variation in faithfulness across different medical imaging tasks.
- **Why unresolved**: The paper only considers classification tasks for three specific medical imaging datasets, but there may be differences in the faithfulness of attribution maps for other medical imaging tasks (e.g., segmentation, detection) or datasets.
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Conduct experiments evaluating the faithfulness of attribution maps using the EvalAttAI metric for various medical imaging tasks and datasets beyond those considered in the paper.

## Limitations
- The claim that EvalAttAI completely avoids distribution shift artifacts remains unverified - while perturbation is better than replacement, the cumulative effect of scaled perturbations may still introduce novel patterns
- The exclusive focus on accuracy-based faithfulness may miss other aspects of attribution quality (e.g., localization precision, robustness to input transformations)
- The comparison with VDP-trained models relies heavily on Vanilla Gradient performance, but other attribution methods may show different patterns

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: EvalAttAI provides more consistent rankings than Deletion/Insertion across different model types and datasets
- **Medium confidence**: Vanilla Gradient and SmoothGrad are the most faithful attribution methods overall
- **Low confidence**: VDP-trained models are definitively more explainable than robustly-trained models across all attribution methods
