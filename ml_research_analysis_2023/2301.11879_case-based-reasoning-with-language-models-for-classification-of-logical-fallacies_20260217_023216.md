# Case-Based Reasoning with Language Models for Classification of Logical Fallacies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11879
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11879
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: CBR framework with enriched case representations outperforms vanilla LMs on fallacy classification, especially out-of-domain.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a case-based reasoning (CBR) method to classify logical fallacies by retrieving and adapting similar historical cases. It enriches case representations with counterarguments, goals, explanations, and argument structure, using language models for similarity retrieval and adaptation. Experiments show CBR improves accuracy over vanilla LMs, especially on out-of-domain data, with countermeasures yielding the strongest performance. Results are robust across models, benefit from few retrieved cases, and remain stable with varying case database sizes.

## Method Summary
The CBR framework retrieves k similar cases from a database using an LM-based retriever, then adapts them using a multi-headed attention mechanism before classification. Case representations are enriched with counterarguments, goals, explanations, and argument structure using ChatGPT/Codex. The adapter combines the new case and retrieved cases through attention, and a classifier (2-layer fully connected network with gelu activation) predicts the final fallacy class. The method is evaluated on LOGIC and LOGIC Climate datasets with 13 fallacy types, comparing against vanilla LMs (BERT, RoBERTa, ELECTRA) and few-shot Codex.

## Key Results
- CBR consistently outperforms vanilla LMs on in-domain and out-of-domain settings across different model sizes
- Peak performance achieved with only one retrieved case, demonstrating efficiency
- Case representation enrichment improves performance, with countermeasures showing strongest gains
- Framework remains effective across varying case database sizes (0.1x to 1.0x training data)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Reasoning over similar cases helps the model better understand and classify logical fallacies.
- Mechanism: By retrieving and adapting labeled cases similar to the new case, the model can leverage contextual information and reasoning patterns from past cases, which helps it understand the logical structure and context of the new case more effectively.
- Core assumption: Similar cases provide relevant information that enhances the model's understanding of the new case.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Case-Based Reasoning improves the accuracy and generalizability of language models."
  - [section 2] "The integration of similar labeled cases helps the model understand the new fallacious argument better and classify it more accurately."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Enriching case representations with additional information improves the effectiveness of the CBR framework.
- Mechanism: By adding information about counterarguments, goals, explanations, and argument structure, the model can gain a more comprehensive understanding of the cases, which helps it identify and classify logical fallacies more accurately.
- Core assumption: The additional information in the enriched representations provides valuable context and insights that are not available in the plain text of the arguments.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3] "We design four complementary strategies to enrich input representation for our model, based on external information about goals, explanations, counterarguments, and argument structure."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The CBR framework is robust to the size of the case database and the number of retrieved cases.
- Mechanism: The model's performance remains stable across different sizes of the case database and varying numbers of retrieved cases, indicating that it can effectively leverage a small number of relevant cases without being overwhelmed by too much information.
- Core assumption: The model can effectively process and utilize the information from a small number of relevant cases without being negatively impacted by the size of the case database or the number of retrieved cases.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5] "The CBR framework consistently outperforms the vanilla LM baseline on in- and out-of-domain settings. This trend stands regardless of the size of the case database."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Retriever -> Adapter -> Classifier
- Critical path: Retriever -> Adapter -> Classifier
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using LMs for retrieval and adaptation provides strong encoding and similarity computation capabilities but may introduce computational overhead.
  - Enriching case representations with additional information can improve model performance but may also introduce noise or irrelevant information.
  - Limiting the number of retrieved cases can prevent the model from being overwhelmed by too much information but may also miss relevant cases.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor retrieval of relevant cases due to ineffective similarity computation or a small case database.
  - Inability to effectively process and utilize the information from retrieved cases due to the complexity of the adapter or a lack of relevant information.
  - Incorrect classification due to an ineffective classifier or a lack of relevant information in the adapter output.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement and test the retriever component using an LM to compute cosine similarity between the new case and cases in the database.
  2. Implement and test the adapter component using an LM encoder and a multi-headed attention mechanism to process the new case and retrieved cases.
  3. Implement and test the classifier component as a fully connected neural layer with a depth of 2 and a gelu activation function to predict the final fallacy class based on the adapter output.

## Limitations

- The methodology's effectiveness depends on the quality and relevance of retrieved cases, which may vary across domains and fallacy types
- The framework's performance with extremely small or large case databases and retrieval sizes beyond tested ranges remains unexplored
- The specific contribution of each enrichment strategy (counterarguments, goals, explanations, structure) to overall performance is not isolated through ablation studies

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The overall effectiveness of the CBR framework in improving accuracy and generalizability compared to vanilla LMs, supported by experimental results across multiple datasets and model configurations.
- **Medium Confidence**: The robustness of the CBR framework to the size of the case database and the number of retrieved cases, as the paper provides evidence but does not extensively test the model's performance at the extremes of these variables.
- **Low Confidence**: The specific impact of each enrichment strategy (counterarguments, goals, explanations, and argument structure) on the model's performance, as the paper does not conduct ablation studies to isolate the contribution of each strategy.
