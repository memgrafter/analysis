# Trade-Offs Between Fairness and Privacy in Language Modeling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.14936
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14936
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
This study investigates the trade-offs between fairness and privacy in language models by training GPT-2 with debiasing and differential privacy (DP) objectives. Using six experimental setups, the authors evaluate bias via BEC-Pro, SEAT, and StereoSet, privacy via membership inference attacks, and language modeling utility via GLUE and perplexity. Results show that CDA combined with DP reduces privacy leakage more than DP alone and marginally improves bias, while CDA mitigates DP's negative effect on perplexity. Debiasing alone (CDA or dropout) does not consistently reduce bias or privacy leakage. DP has negligible effect on fairness in language modeling, unlike in classification tasks. Overall, CDA+DP yields the best balance between privacy, fairness, and utility.

## Method Summary
The study fine-tunes GPT-2-medium (reduced via LoRA to 0.393M parameters) on BookCorpus with six experimental setups: baseline, CDA, dropout, DP, CDA+DP, and dropout+DP. Training runs for 3 epochs with learning rate 1e-5, batch size 2, and gradient accumulation steps of 8 or 128. Bias is evaluated using BEC-Pro, SEAT (all gender-specific tests), and StereoSet, privacy is measured via white-box membership inference attacks, and utility is assessed through GLUE benchmark and perplexity scores. CDA augments the dataset by duplicating and modifying sentences containing target bias words.

## Key Results
- CDA combined with DP reduces privacy leakage more than DP alone
- DP has negligible effect on fairness in language modeling, unlike in classification tasks
- CDA mitigates DP's negative effect on perplexity
- Debiasing alone (CDA or dropout) does not consistently reduce bias or privacy leakage

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
CDA reduces membership inference attack recall more than DP alone by augmenting the dataset with modified sentences containing target bias words, increasing diversity and reducing model confidence in predicting original training samples. Core assumption: increased data diversity lowers the model's ability to memorize specific training samples. Evidence anchors include abstract findings and section discussions, though corpus evidence is weak. Break condition: if augmentation introduces predictable patterns exploitable by attackers.

### Mechanism 2
DP has negligible effect on bias in language modeling because minority groups are defined by stereotypical associations rather than underrepresentation. DP's noise injection doesn't disproportionately affect these associations since the model already "knows" them from corpus-level patterns. Core assumption: language models learn stereotypes from patterns rather than class imbalances. Evidence anchors include abstract findings and section discussions, though corpus evidence is weak. Break condition: if dataset contains strong class imbalances correlating with bias attributes.

### Mechanism 3
CDA mitigates DP's negative effect on perplexity by increasing effective training data size and diversity, providing robust signals that counteract DP's degradation of predictive accuracy. Core assumption: augmented data offers more training signals to offset DP's noise effects. Evidence anchors include abstract findings and section discussions, though corpus evidence is weak. Break condition: if augmentation introduces ineffective noise or artifacts.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: BookCorpus → subsampling → tokenization → (optional) CDA augmentation → train/dev split → GPT-2-medium (LoRA reduced) → fine-tuning with debiasing/DP objectives → evaluation (BEC-Pro, SEAT, StereoSet, MIA, GLUE, perplexity)
- **Critical path**: 1) Prepare and augment data (if CDA) 2) Initialize reduced GPT-2 with LoRA 3) Fine-tune with chosen objectives 4) Evaluate bias, privacy, and utility
- **Design tradeoffs**: LoRA reduces memory but may limit model capacity; CDA increases training time but may improve both bias and privacy; white-box MIA is more sensitive but may overestimate risks
- **Failure signatures**: High MIA recall with low DP guarantees indicates insufficient privacy protection; increased bias after CDA suggests new stereotypes introduced; perplexity spike after DP indicates utility loss
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Baseline GPT-2 fine-tuning without CDA or DP 2) GPT-2 + CDA only 3) GPT-2 + DP only

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
Does DP in language models have a consistent effect on bias across different demographic groups beyond gender? The paper focused only on gender bias, leaving impact on other groups unexplored. Resolving this would require replicating experiments with benchmarks measuring multiple demographic dimensions while applying DP.

### Open Question 2
What is the precise mechanism by which CDA mitigates DP's negative impact on perplexity? The paper identifies the phenomenon but doesn't explain why counterfactual examples specifically counteract DP's degradation. Resolving this requires comparative experiments isolating different aspects of CDA.

### Open Question 3
How does dropout effectiveness as a debiasing technique vary with different rates or architectural applications? The study used fixed dropout rate of 0.15, but authors acknowledge implementation details significantly affect outcomes. Resolving this requires systematic experiments varying dropout configurations.

### Open Question 4
Does CDA+DP's positive interaction in reducing privacy leakage persist with stronger privacy definitions or sophisticated attacks? The study used MIA as proxy rather than formal DP guarantees. Resolving this requires computing formal (ε,δ) guarantees and comparing with MIA results.

## Limitations

- Weak empirical evidence for key mechanisms, particularly CDA+DP privacy gains and DP's task-specific fairness effects
- Evaluation scope limited to gender bias, potentially missing other bias types
- Resource constraints led to LoRA usage, which may affect comparison validity
- Results based on BookCorpus may not generalize to other domains

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: CDA mitigates DP's negative effect on perplexity has strong experimental support
- **Medium confidence**: CDA+DP reduces privacy leakage more than DP alone is supported by MIA results but lacks detailed methodology
- **Medium confidence**: DP has negligible effect on fairness in language modeling is based on experimental results but lacks direct classification comparison
- **Low confidence**: Debiasing alone doesn't consistently reduce bias lacks comprehensive analysis across all conditions
