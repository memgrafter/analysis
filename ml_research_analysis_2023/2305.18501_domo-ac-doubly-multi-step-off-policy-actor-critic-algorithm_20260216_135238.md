# DoMo-AC: Doubly Multi-step Off-policy Actor-Critic Algorithm

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18501
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18501
- Reference count: 26
- Combines multi-step policy evaluation and improvement for faster convergence in off-policy learning

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces DoMo-VI, a novel algorithm that combines multi-step policy evaluation and improvement for faster convergence to the optimal policy in off-policy learning settings. The authors also propose DoMo-AC, a practical actor-critic instantiation of DoMo-VI that introduces a bias-variance trade-off for improved policy gradient estimates. When combined with the IMPALA architecture, DoMo-AC shows performance improvements over the baseline algorithm on Atari-57 game benchmarks.

## Method Summary
DoMo-AC implements doubly multi-step off-policy actor-critic learning using V-trace operators with a trace coefficient threshold (c=0.5) to balance bias and variance in policy gradients. The algorithm operates in a distributed IMPALA architecture with 512 actors collecting trajectories of length T=20 from Atari-57 games. Policy and value networks are updated using multi-step improvements rather than traditional one-step TD learning, enabling faster propagation of reward information through the learning process.

## Key Results
- DoMo-AC with V-trace c=0.5 shows statistically significant improvements over one-step trace and IMPALA baseline on Atari-57 games
- The bias-variance trade-off is optimized at intermediate trace coefficient values (c between 0.3-0.5)
- Performance gains are most pronounced for games requiring long-term credit assignment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Combining multi-step policy evaluation and improvement accelerates convergence to the optimal policy.
- **Mechanism**: Multi-step evaluation propagates reward information faster than one-step methods, while multi-step improvement enables stronger policy updates by considering longer horizons. When combined, they create a synergistic effect that speeds up convergence.
- **Core assumption**: The policy improvement step can be approximated effectively by gradient ascent on the multi-step improvement objective.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "DoMo-VI enjoys guaranteed convergence speed-up to the optimal policy and is applicable in general off-policy learning settings."
  - [section 3.1]: "DoMo-VI generates policy sequence πi whose performance Vπi converges to the optimal performance V*."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; related works focus on individual components but not the combined approach.
- **Break condition**: If the optimization subroutine for policy improvement cannot approximate the multi-step improvement objective well, the acceleration effect may be lost.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: The bias-variance trade-off introduced by the trace coefficient threshold enables improved policy gradient estimates.
- **Mechanism**: As the trace coefficient threshold increases, the bias in policy gradient estimates decreases but variance increases. An intermediate value balances these effects for optimal performance.
- **Core assumption**: The policy-based algorithm is more sensitive to high variance than value-based algorithms.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "DoMo-AC introduces a bias-variance trade-off that ensures improved policy gradient estimates."
  - [section 4.1]: "Such an update is potentially desirable because it locally improves the average value function objective."
  - [section 6.1]: "When c increases from 0 to 10, the bias generally decreases, whereas the variance increases rapidly."
- **Break condition**: If the variance becomes too high (e.g., as c approaches infinity), the algorithm's performance degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Using off-policy evaluation operators like V-trace with multi-step learning enables stable improvements in large-scale settings.
- **Mechanism**: Off-policy evaluation operators correct for distribution mismatch while multi-step learning accelerates convergence. The combination provides both stability and speed.
- **Core assumption**: The behavior policy has full support over the action space, ensuring importance sampling ratios are well-defined.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "When combined with the IMPALA architecture, DoMo-AC has showed improvements over the baseline algorithm on Atari-57 game benchmarks."
  - [section 6.2]: "We find that the DoMo-AC implementation with V-trace c = 0.5 provides statistically significant improvements over one-step trace and IMPALA."
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; the related works focus on different aspects of off-policy learning.
- **Break condition**: If the behavior policy has limited support or if the off-policy correction fails to properly account for distribution mismatch, the algorithm may diverge.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Actor network -> V-trace module -> Policy gradient estimator -> Distributed parameter server
- **Critical path**: Data collection → V-trace target computation → Policy gradient estimation → Parameter update → Synchronization
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Trace coefficient threshold (c): Balances bias-variance trade-off in policy gradients
  - Number of gradient steps in policy improvement: Affects how closely we approximate the optimal policy
  - Target network update rate: Affects stability vs. responsiveness of learning
- **Failure signatures**:
  - High variance in policy gradients: May indicate trace coefficient is too large
  - Slow convergence: May indicate trace coefficient is too small or optimization is ineffective
  - Divergence: May indicate importance sampling ratios are too large or behavior policy has insufficient support
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement DoMo-AC with c = 0 (one-step) and c = 1 (multi-step) on a simple tabular MDP to observe convergence differences
  2. Sweep the trace coefficient threshold c from 0.1 to 1.0 on Atari games to find the optimal value
  3. Compare DoMo-AC against IMPALA baseline on a subset of Atari games to verify performance improvements

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of multi-step improvement depends heavily on the optimization subroutine's ability to approximate the objective in high-dimensional spaces
- The optimal trace coefficient threshold appears environment-specific and requires tuning
- Performance claims are primarily validated on Atari-57 games, limiting generalizability

## Confidence

- **Guaranteed convergence speed-up**: Medium confidence - theoretically sound but requires broader empirical validation
- **Bias-variance trade-off effectiveness**: High confidence - well-supported by ablation studies
- **Off-policy stability with V-trace**: Medium confidence - assumes full action space support which may not always hold
