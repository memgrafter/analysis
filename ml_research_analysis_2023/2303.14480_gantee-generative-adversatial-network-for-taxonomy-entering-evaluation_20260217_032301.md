# GANTEE: Generative Adversatial Network for Taxonomy Entering Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14480
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14480
- Reference count: 10
- The paper proposes GANTEE, a pluggable framework that improves taxonomy expansion methods by addressing noisy query concepts and limited training data using a generative adversarial network.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses two key limitations in existing taxonomy expansion methods: low efficiency due to noisy query concepts and low effectiveness from limited training data. To tackle these issues, the authors propose GANTEE, a pluggable framework that combines a generative adversarial network with taxonomy entering evaluation. The framework uses a generative model to create high-quality training data and a discriminative model to efficiently filter out noisy query concepts. The experimental results on three large-scale datasets in English and Chinese demonstrate that GANTEE improves the performance of taxonomy expansion methods in both effectiveness and efficiency, achieving state-of-the-art results in less than one-third of the time compared to previous methods.

## Method Summary
GANTEE is a pluggable framework that uses a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) to address noisy query concepts and limited training data in taxonomy expansion. The framework consists of a GPT-2-based generator that produces synthetic query concepts, an LSTM-based rollout discriminator that provides short-term rewards for concept validity during generation, and a BERT-based hyper discriminator that provides long-term rewards for taxonomic relevance after generation. PLMs are used to quickly retrieve concept representations for efficient filtering. The framework is evaluated on three large-scale real-world taxonomies (MAG-CS, MAG-FoS, CN-Probase) in English and Chinese, with 60% of leaf concepts added as noisy query concepts.

## Key Results
- GANTEE improves taxonomy expansion performance in both effectiveness and efficiency
- Achieves state-of-the-art results in less than one-third of the time compared to previous methods
- Demonstrates improved performance on three large-scale datasets in English and Chinese

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
The GAN framework improves both effectiveness and efficiency by generating high-quality training data and filtering noisy query concepts. The generator produces synthetic query concepts that are semantically valid, while the two discriminators (hyper and rollout) provide complementary rewards that guide the generator to produce more realistic data. This synthetic data enriches the training set, allowing better representation learning for unseen concepts. The discriminators also act as a filter to remove noisy concepts before expensive expansion operations. Core assumption: Synthetic concepts generated by the GAN are sufficiently realistic to improve model training, and the discriminator-based filtering reliably removes noisy concepts.

### Mechanism 2
Using PLMs to obtain concept representations enables fast filtering of noisy concepts without retraining models. PLMs (e.g., BERT) are used to quickly retrieve embeddings for both existing and query concepts. These embeddings feed into the rollout discriminator, which evaluates whether a concept is valid. This avoids the computational overhead of training new representations for each query concept. Core assumption: PLM embeddings are sufficiently discriminative for the "isA concept" and "isA query" tasks, even if they lack fine-grained semantics.

### Mechanism 3
The two-stage reward system (short-term from rollout discriminator, long-term from hyper discriminator) guides the generator toward producing concepts that are both valid and taxonomically relevant. During generation, the rollout discriminator provides immediate feedback on whether the partial sequence is a valid concept. After generation, the hyper discriminator evaluates the full concept's hypernymy relation to the anchor. This combination ensures the generator produces concepts that are both semantically coherent and taxonomically appropriate. Core assumption: The rollout discriminator's short-term rewards are sufficiently informative to guide the generator, and the hyper discriminator's long-term rewards align with the ultimate goal of taxonomy expansion.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: GPT-2 Generator -> LSTM Rollout Discriminator -> BERT Hyper Discriminator -> Taxonomy Expansion Model
- Critical path:
  1. Generate synthetic concepts using the generator.
  2. Filter noisy concepts using the rollout discriminator.
  3. Evaluate taxonomic relevance using the hyper discriminator.
  4. Train the taxonomy expansion model on enriched data.
  5. Apply the model to real query concepts.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using PLMs for fast filtering trades off some accuracy for efficiency.
  - The two-stage reward system adds complexity but improves generation quality.
  - Synthetic data generation may introduce biases if not carefully controlled.
- Failure signatures:
  - Generator produces nonsensical or irrelevant concepts.
  - Discriminators fail to distinguish noisy from valid concepts.
  - Taxonomy expansion model overfits to synthetic data.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the generator's ability to produce valid concepts using a held-out validation set.
  2. Evaluate the rollout discriminator's accuracy in filtering noisy concepts.
  3. Measure the impact of synthetic data on the taxonomy expansion model's performance.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the size of the negative sampling pool affect the final performance of GANTEE in both taxonomy expansion and entering evaluation tasks? The paper only provides qualitative observations about the effect of negative sampling size, but does not provide specific quantitative results or an optimal range for the negative sampling size. Systematic experiments varying the negative sampling size and reporting the corresponding performance metrics for both tasks would provide quantitative evidence to determine the optimal negative sampling size.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of the number of generated high-quality samples by GANTEE on the final performance of the taxonomy expansion task? The paper states that generating more high-quality training samples improves performance, but does not provide specific quantitative results on how the number of generated samples affects the final performance. Experiments varying the number of generated high-quality samples and reporting the corresponding performance metrics for the taxonomy expansion task would provide quantitative evidence on the impact of the number of generated samples.

### Open Question 3
How does the performance of GANTEE change with the size of the taxonomy? The paper states that larger taxonomies show more improvement through GANTEE, but does not provide specific quantitative results on how performance changes with taxonomy size. Experiments on taxonomies of different sizes and reporting the corresponding performance metrics would provide quantitative evidence on how the performance of GANTEE changes with the size of the taxonomy.

## Limitations

- The paper's claims about efficiency improvements rely heavily on PLM-based filtering, but lack detailed ablation studies showing the contribution of each component.
- The synthetic data generation process is not fully specified, raising questions about potential biases introduced during training.
- The noisy query concept injection method (60% of leaf concepts) appears somewhat arbitrary and may not reflect real-world data distributions.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The overall framework design and its integration of GAN with taxonomy expansion is well-motivated and technically sound.
- Medium Confidence: The experimental results showing improved effectiveness and efficiency are compelling but lack sufficient detail on hyperparameter tuning and ablation studies.
- Low Confidence: The claim that GANTEE achieves state-of-the-art results in less than one-third of the time compared to previous methods is not fully supported by the provided evidence.

## Next Checks
