# Getting Away with More Network Pruning: From Sparsity to Geometry and Linear Regions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07966
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07966
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a pruning strategy based on maximizing the bound on the number of linear regions subject to a total number of parameters to be pruned.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the relationship between network pruning and the geometric properties of neural networks, specifically focusing on linear regions. The authors establish a theoretical connection between pruning-induced sparsity and the reduction in linear regions, demonstrating that pruning affects accuracy similarly to how sparsity affects the number of linear regions. They introduce a novel pruning strategy that maximizes a bound on the expected number of linear regions while constraining the total number of parameters to be pruned. The method is validated across multiple benchmark datasets (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) and shows improved accuracy compared to uniform pruning approaches.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a network pruning strategy that leverages the geometric properties of neural networks by focusing on linear regions. They develop a theoretical bound on the expected number of linear regions over possible sparsity patterns and introduce a method for counting linear regions on input subspaces. The pruning strategy works by maximizing this bound subject to a constraint on the total number of parameters to be pruned. This approach treats pruning as an optimization problem that balances sparsity with the preservation of the network's geometric complexity, measured through linear regions. The method is implemented by first calculating the bound for different pruning configurations, then selecting the sparsity pattern that maximizes this bound while meeting the parameter reduction constraint.

## Key Results
- The proposed pruning strategy achieves higher accuracy than uniform pruning across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 datasets.
- The theoretical bound on linear regions correlates with actual network performance under pruning.
- The method demonstrates that geometric considerations can guide more effective pruning decisions than purely parameter-count-based approaches.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works because pruning affects both the parameter count and the geometric complexity of the network simultaneously. When parameters are removed, the network's ability to define distinct linear regions in input space is reduced, which directly impacts its representational capacity. By explicitly optimizing for the preservation of linear regions while meeting sparsity constraints, the method ensures that the network maintains sufficient geometric complexity to capture the underlying data distribution. This geometric preservation is what leads to better generalization compared to uniform pruning, which may remove parameters in ways that disproportionately reduce the network's representational capacity without considering the geometric consequences.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input data -> Convolutional/Pooling layers -> ReLU activations -> Fully connected layers -> Output layer -> Accuracy evaluation. The pruning strategy modifies weight matrices in convolutional and fully connected layers while preserving the overall architecture.

**Critical Path**: The theoretical bound calculation -> Sparsity pattern optimization -> Network pruning -> Fine-tuning -> Accuracy evaluation. The most critical step is the optimization of the sparsity pattern based on the linear regions bound.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Maximizing linear regions vs. parameter reduction targets. The method must balance preserving geometric complexity with achieving the desired sparsity level. Too aggressive pruning reduces both parameters and linear regions, while too conservative pruning fails to meet sparsity goals.

**Failure Signatures**: If the theoretical bound does not correlate with actual performance, the pruning strategy may select suboptimal sparsity patterns. Additionally, if the bound calculation becomes intractable for larger networks, the method may not scale effectively.

## Limitations

- The theoretical analysis assumes ReLU activations and may not generalize to other activation functions.
- The bound on linear regions relies on assumptions about activation patterns that may not hold in deeper networks.
- The pruning strategy assumes a linear relationship between sparsity and linear regions that may break down at extreme sparsity levels.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The empirical observations showing that pruning affects accuracy similarly to how sparsity affects linear regions, and the experimental methodology on benchmark datasets.

**Medium confidence**: The theoretical bound on expected number of linear regions over possible sparsity patterns, due to assumptions about network geometry.

**Low confidence**: The claim that maximizing the bound directly translates to better pruning strategies in all scenarios, requiring further validation across diverse architectures.
