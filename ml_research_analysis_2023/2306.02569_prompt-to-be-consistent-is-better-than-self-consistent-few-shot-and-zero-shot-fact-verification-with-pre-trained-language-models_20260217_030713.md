# Prompt to be Consistent is Better than Self-Consistent? Few-Shot and Zero-Shot Fact Verification with Pre-trained Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02569
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02569
- Reference count: 17
- Key outcome: ProToCo achieves up to 30.4% relative improvement in F1 for few-shot fact verification compared to state-of-the-art baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of few-shot and zero-shot fact verification by proposing ProToCo, a method that enhances pre-trained language models' (PLMs) factuality assessment through consistency constraints. The approach generates multiple variants of claims with different relations and uses these to create a consistency mechanism that constrains predictions. By updating PLMs using parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT), ProToCo demonstrates superior performance over existing baselines across three public verification datasets, showing particular strength in few-shot settings.

## Method Summary
ProToCo improves few-shot and zero-shot fact verification by generating multiple variants of a claim with different relations and using these variants to create consistency constraints. The method employs parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) to update pre-trained language models (PLMs), enabling more accurate predictions while requiring fewer parameters to be updated. The consistency mechanism ensures that predictions across the different claim variants remain compatible, leading to improved factuality assessment. The approach is evaluated on three public verification datasets and compared against state-of-the-art few-shot baselines and zero-shot learners.

## Key Results
- ProToCo achieves up to 30.4% relative improvement in F1 for few-shot fact verification over state-of-the-art baselines
- With a small number of unlabeled instances, ProToCo outperforms the strong zero-shot learner T0-3B
- ProToCo outperforms both OPT-30B and Self-Consistency-enabled OPT-6.7B in both few- and zero-shot settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
ProToCo works by leveraging the consistency across multiple claim variants to improve fact verification accuracy. By generating variants with different relations and enforcing consistency constraints on predictions, the model can better identify the true relationship between claims and evidence. The parameter-efficient fine-tuning approach allows for effective adaptation of PLMs without requiring full model retraining, making the method more computationally efficient while maintaining strong performance.

## Foundational Learning
- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT)**: Methods that update only a small subset of model parameters during adaptation, reducing computational costs while maintaining performance. Needed because full fine-tuning of large PLMs is computationally expensive.
- **Consistency Mechanisms in NLP**: Approaches that enforce agreement across multiple model outputs or inputs to improve robustness and accuracy. Needed to leverage the redundancy in claim variants for better verification.
- **Claim Variant Generation**: Creating multiple semantically related versions of claims with different relations to capture different aspects of the fact verification task. Needed to provide the basis for consistency constraints.
- **Fact Verification Pipeline**: The process of assessing whether claims are supported by evidence, typically involving evidence retrieval, claim verification, and justification generation. Needed as the target application domain.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Claim Variants -> Consistency Constraints -> Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning -> Fact Verification Prediction

**Critical Path**: Claim variant generation → Consistency enforcement → PEFT adaptation → Final prediction

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades increased inference complexity (due to multiple variant processing) for improved accuracy through consistency constraints. The use of PEFT reduces computational requirements compared to full fine-tuning but may limit the model's ability to make large adaptations.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Poor performance when generated claim variants are of low quality or fail to capture relevant aspects of the verification task
- Reduced effectiveness when evidence is noisy or incomplete
- Potential overfitting to the specific patterns in the training data when consistency constraints are too strict

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust is ProToCo's performance when using non-oracle (noisy or incomplete) evidence rather than gold evidence?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges this limitation, stating that retrieved evidence in real-world settings can be noisy and incomplete, and that ProToCo's performance on non-oracle evidence requires further study.
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates on gold evidence, which is an idealized scenario not reflective of real-world conditions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluation of ProToCo on fact verification tasks using retrieved evidence from real-world sources, measuring performance degradation compared to gold evidence.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can ProToCo's consistency training be extended to other stages of fact-checking beyond claim verification, such as evidence retrieval and justification generation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose exploring few- and zero-shot solutions for other stages of fact-checking in future work, suggesting this as a direction for extending ProToCo's methodology.
- Why unresolved: The current work focuses solely on the claim verification stage of fact-checking.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of consistency training approaches for evidence retrieval and justification generation tasks, demonstrating improvements over baseline methods.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to three public fact verification datasets, which may not capture full diversity of real-world scenarios
- Performance sensitivity to quality of generated claim variants is not thoroughly analyzed
- Computational efficiency and memory requirements of generating multiple variants and enforcing consistency constraints are not discussed
