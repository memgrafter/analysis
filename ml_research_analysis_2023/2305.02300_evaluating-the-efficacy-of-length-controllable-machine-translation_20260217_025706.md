# Evaluating the Efficacy of Length-Controllable Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02300
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02300
- Reference count: 24
- This work evaluates 18 metrics for length-controllable machine translation and identifies BLEURT and COMET.wmt20-comet-da as the most reliable metrics overall.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of 18 automatic metrics for length-controllable machine translation, where translations are generated at specific length ratios (80% and 50% of reference length). The study compares these metrics against human judgments across two translation directions (English→Chinese and Chinese→English) using 270 sentences per direction. Through systematic correlation analysis at both system and segment levels, the authors identify BLEURT and COMET.wmt20-comet-da as the most reliable metrics for this task, with BLEU variants showing inconsistent performance. The research provides practical guidance for researchers and practitioners working on length-controllable translation systems.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates 18 automatic metrics on length-controllable machine translation outputs generated by two models (Length Embedding and Translate-then-Summarize) at 80% and 50% of reference length. Human evaluation was conducted using the Appraise framework with three professional annotators per translation, measuring translation quality on a continuous scale. The authors computed Pearson and Kendall correlations between automatic metrics and human ratings, along with significance testing using Zou's method and PERM-BOTH hypothesis testing. The evaluation covered two translation directions (En-Zh and Zh-En) using data from newstest2019, with sentences filtered to 128 subwords maximum after BPE preprocessing.

## Key Results
- BLEURT and COMET.wmt20-comet-da achieve the highest correlation with human ratings across both translation directions
- BLEU variants show inconsistent performance, with BLEU4 performing well at segment level but poorly at system level
- ROUGE-based metrics demonstrate poor correlation with human judgments in the length-controllable MT context

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: BLEURT and COMET.wmt20-comet-da outperform other metrics due to training on diverse evaluation datasets
- Mechanism: These metrics are pre-trained on ratings data from WMT Metrics Shared Tasks and synthetic data, allowing better generalization to length-controllable MT scenarios
- Core assumption: Evaluation datasets capture key aspects of translation quality relevant for length-controllable MT
- Evidence anchors:
  - "BLEURT and COMET have the highest correlation with human evaluation and are most suitable as evaluation metrics for length-controllable machine translation"
  - "BLEURT-20 is a pre-trained RemBERT model fine-tuned on ratings from the WMT Metrics Shared Task and synthetic data"
- Break condition: If evaluation datasets don't capture relevant quality aspects for length-controllable MT, performance may degrade

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multilingual training helps BLEURT and COMET.wmt20-comet-da handle cross-lingual length-controllable MT evaluation
- Mechanism: Multilingual training allows better handling of linguistic differences between source and target languages
- Core assumption: Linguistic differences in length-controllable MT are similar to those in multilingual training data
- Evidence anchors:
  - "We use two popular length-controllable translation models to obtain translations with different lengths: 80% and 50% of the reference length"
  - "BLEURT-20 is a pre-trained RemBERT model fine-tuned on ratings from the WMT Metrics Shared Task and synthetic data"
- Break condition: If linguistic differences aren't well-represented in multilingual training data, performance may suffer

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: BLEURT and COMET.wmt20-comet-da are optimized for machine translation characteristics
- Mechanism: Their architecture and training are specifically designed for translation quality aspects like adequacy and fluency
- Core assumption: Translation characteristics relevant to BLEURT/COMET are also applicable to length-controllable MT
- Evidence anchors:
  - "We can use automatic summarization or machine translation evaluation metrics for length-controllable machine translation, but this is not necessarily suitable and accurate"
  - "BLEURT BiLingual Evaluation Understudy with Representations from Transformers (BLEURT) is a metric trained on ratings data"
- Break condition: If translation characteristics aren't relevant to length-controllable MT, performance may match summarization metrics

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do evaluation metrics for length-controllable machine translation perform across languages other than English and Chinese?
- Basis in paper: The authors note experiments are based on English and Chinese, which are distant languages, and cannot guarantee results on other languages far from En and Zh
- Why unresolved: Study only evaluates two translation directions, limiting generalizability
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing same 18 metrics on additional language pairs, particularly those linguistically distant from English and Chinese

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the evaluation framework need to adapt for different domains beyond news?
- Basis in paper: Authors state data are limited to news domain, implying potential limitations for other domains
- Why unresolved: Different domains may have varying text characteristics, lengths, and translation challenges
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating metrics on corpora from multiple domains (legal, medical, conversational)

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of varying length ratios (e.g., 60%, 70%, 90%) on metric performance?
- Basis in paper: Study only tests 80% and 50% length ratios, but practical applications may require different compression levels
- Why unresolved: Paper doesn't explore how metric performance changes with different length constraints
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing metrics across broader range of length ratios to reveal performance patterns

### Open Question 4
- Question: How can manual evaluation methods be improved to achieve higher inter-annotator agreement?
- Basis in paper: Authors note English data shows relatively low annotation consistency, highlighting challenges of evaluating natural language generation tasks
- Why unresolved: Low inter-annotator agreement undermines reliability of human evaluations
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies testing different annotation frameworks, instructions, or training procedures

## Limitations

- Limited language coverage with evaluation only on English-Chinese translation directions
- Restricted domain to news text, which may not generalize to other text types
- Evaluation of only two specific length ratios (80% and 50%), leaving uncertainty about performance at other compression levels
- Potential impact of BPE tokenization on length control and metric evaluation not fully explored

## Confidence

- High: The methodology for correlation analysis and significance testing is correctly implemented
- Medium: The identification of BLEURT and COMET.wmt20-comet-da as top-performing metrics
- Low: The universal applicability of these findings to all length-controllable MT scenarios

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-linguistic validation**: Evaluate the same set of metrics on a different language pair (e.g., English-German) using the same evaluation protocol to verify if BLEURT and COMET.wmt20-comet-da maintain their superior performance across language families.

2. **Domain adaptation test**: Apply the evaluation to a different text domain (e.g., biomedical or legal texts) to assess whether the identified top metrics maintain their performance when the linguistic characteristics and terminology differ significantly from news text.

3. **Extended length settings**: Generate translations at additional length ratios (e.g., 90%, 70%, 60%) beyond the 80% and 50% settings used in the study to determine if metric performance varies across a broader range of length control parameters and identify any breakpoints where metric reliability decreases.
