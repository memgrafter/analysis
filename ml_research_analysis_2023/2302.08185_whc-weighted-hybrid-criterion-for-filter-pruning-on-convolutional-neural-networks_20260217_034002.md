# WHC: Weighted Hybrid Criterion for Filter Pruning on Convolutional Neural Networks
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.08185
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08185
- Reference count: 0
- One-line primary result: WHC outperforms pure norm- or relationship-based pruning methods by combining filter norm and weighted linear dissimilarity, achieving >42% FLOPs reduction on ResNet-50 ImageNet without top-5 accuracy loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces WHC, a weighted hybrid criterion for filter pruning that combines filter norm with linear dissimilarity measured via cosine angle. WHC assigns higher weights to dissimilarity terms when the counterpart filter has a larger norm, addressing limitations of existing pruning methods that rely solely on filter magnitude or pairwise relationships. Evaluated in a one-shot setting across CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, WHC demonstrates state-of-the-art performance, particularly achieving >42% FLOPs reduction on ResNet-50 without any top-5 accuracy loss.

## Method Summary
WHC operates by evaluating each filter using a composite score that integrates both the filter's norm and its linear dissimilarity to other filters, with the dissimilarity term weighted by the norm of the compared filter. This hybrid approach aims to preserve informative filters while removing redundant ones, improving robustness over single-metric methods. The criterion is applied in a one-shot pruning framework, where filters are ranked and pruned in a single step rather than iteratively.

## Key Results
- WHC achieves >42% FLOPs reduction on ResNet-50 ImageNet with no top-5 accuracy loss.
- Outperforms existing pruning criteria that rely solely on filter norm or pairwise relationships.
- Ablation studies confirm the benefit of combining norm and dissimilarity information with weighted terms.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
WHC improves pruning accuracy by capturing both the importance (via filter norm) and redundancy (via weighted linear dissimilarity) of filters. The weighting scheme ensures that dissimilarity comparisons are more influential when comparing against stronger filters, leading to more discriminative pruning decisions. This dual consideration helps preserve critical filters that might be missed by methods focusing on a single metric.

## Limitations
- Unclear if WHC maintains advantage at pruning ratios beyond those reported.
- Not validated on compact architectures like MobileNet or EfficientNet.
- One-shot pruning may miss gains from iterative refinement.
- FLOPs reduction does not account for hardware-specific execution or memory transfer overheads.
- No cross-dataset generalization tests for robustness to domain shift.

## Confidence
- **Core claim (norm + dissimilarity hybrid improves pruning)**: High
- **Top-5 accuracy preservation at 42% FLOPs reduction**: Medium (single network/dataset)
- **Hardware efficiency claims**: Low (no runtime/energy metrics provided)
