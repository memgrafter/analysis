# Complex-valued Adaptive System Identification via Low-Rank Tensor Decomposition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16428
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16428
- Reference count: 32
- Primary result: Complex-valued tensor-LMS architectures outperform trivial extensions with minimal computational overhead

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of extending tensor-based system identification to complex-valued signals. The authors propose three architectures for complex-valued adaptive system identification using low-rank tensor decomposition. The key innovation is developing fully complex-valued tensor operations that significantly outperform trivial extensions (separately processing real and imaginary components) while maintaining modest computational overhead. The work bridges a critical gap in tensor-LMS frameworks, enabling their application to complex-valued signal processing domains such as communications and radar.

## Method Summary
The authors extend the tensor-LMS framework to handle complex-valued signals through three distinct architectural approaches. The first architecture processes real and imaginary parts separately, representing a trivial extension of existing methods. The second architecture introduces partial complex operations while maintaining some real-valued processing components. The third, fully complex-valued architecture implements all operations in the complex domain, including complex-valued tensor decompositions and update rules. The proposed methods maintain the low-rank tensor decomposition structure while incorporating complex arithmetic throughout the learning process, with careful consideration given to the computational implications of complex operations.

## Key Results
- The fully complex-valued architecture significantly outperforms both trivial extensions and partially complex architectures across all tested scenarios
- Performance gains are achieved with only modest computational overhead compared to real-valued processing
- The proposed architectures demonstrate improved convergence rates and lower steady-state error compared to baseline methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The superior performance of the fully complex-valued architecture stems from its ability to leverage the inherent correlations between real and imaginary components of complex signals. By processing the signal as a unified complex entity rather than separate real and imaginary parts, the system can capture phase relationships and amplitude-phase coupling that are lost in trivial extensions. The low-rank tensor decomposition enables efficient representation of the complex-valued system dynamics while maintaining computational tractability through dimensionality reduction.

**Complex-Valued System Identification**: Modeling systems that process complex signals requires preserving phase information; quick check: validate that phase relationships are maintained through all processing stages.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input Signal -> Tensor Decomposition -> Complex LMS Update -> Parameter Estimation -> Output Signal

**Critical Path**: The complex LMS update stage represents the critical computational path, where tensor operations and complex arithmetic must be carefully synchronized to maintain numerical stability.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The fully complex architecture trades increased per-operation complexity for improved signal representation and convergence, while trivial extensions minimize computational complexity at the expense of information loss.

**Failure Signatures**: Performance degradation typically manifests as phase distortion in the output signal or slow convergence rates, indicating improper handling of complex correlations.
