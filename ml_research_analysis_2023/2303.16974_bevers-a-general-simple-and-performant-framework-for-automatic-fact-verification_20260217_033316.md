# BEVERS: A General, Simple, and Performant Framework for Automatic Fact Verification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16974
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16974
- Reference count: 5
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on FEVER and SciFact datasets

## Executive Summary
BEVERS is a fact verification system that achieves state-of-the-art performance on the FEVER and SciFact datasets through careful optimization of a standard pipeline. The system employs a three-stage approach: document retrieval, sentence selection, and claim classification. What sets BEVERS apart is its meticulous hyperparameter tuning and the strategic combination of different methods at each stage, rather than introducing novel architectures. The authors demonstrate that systematic optimization of existing techniques can yield substantial improvements in fact verification performance.

## Method Summary
BEVERS implements a standard fact verification pipeline with careful attention to hyperparameter optimization. For document retrieval, it combines TF-IDF (using both concatenated and separated approaches) with fuzzy string search to maximize recall. Sentence selection employs a point-wise approach using evidence-based re-retrieval, with models like RoBERTa Large and DeBERTa V2 XL MNLI. Claim classification uses a mixed approach of singleton and concatenated evidence with a gradient boosting classifier for aggregation. The system's key innovation lies not in novel architectures but in the systematic optimization and combination of existing techniques, with particular emphasis on maximizing recall at each stage to ensure comprehensive evidence collection.

## Key Results
- Achieves the highest FEVER score and label accuracy among all systems on the FEVER dataset
- Attains the highest label accuracy on the SciFact dataset
- Demonstrates that careful hyperparameter tuning can significantly improve performance without architectural changes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BEVERS succeeds by optimizing each component of the fact verification pipeline through systematic hyperparameter tuning rather than introducing new architectures. The combination of TF-IDF with fuzzy string search for document retrieval ensures high recall by capturing both exact matches and semantically similar documents. The point-wise sentence selection with evidence-based re-retrieval allows the system to iteratively refine its evidence collection, while the gradient boosting classifier for claim classification provides a robust mechanism for aggregating evidence. This approach maximizes the quality and quantity of evidence while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Document Retrieval -> Sentence Selection -> Claim Classification

Critical Path: The critical path involves document retrieval with combined TF-IDF and fuzzy search, followed by point-wise sentence selection with evidence-based re-retrieval, and finally claim classification using gradient boosting aggregation.

Design Tradeoffs: The system prioritizes recall over precision at early stages to ensure comprehensive evidence collection, accepting increased computational cost for improved accuracy. This approach may not scale well to very large document collections but provides superior performance on benchmark datasets.

Failure Signatures: The system may struggle with complex or nuanced claims where semantic similarity is more important than lexical overlap, and may face scalability challenges with larger document collections due to the iterative nature of sentence selection and evidence aggregation.
