# User Retention-oriented Recommendation with Decision Transformer
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06347
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06347
- Reference count: 40
- Proposes DT4Rec, a Decision Transformer-based framework for user retention in sequential recommender systems

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of optimizing user retention in sequential recommender systems using reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the difficulties of offline RL such as stability problems and unreliable performance evaluation. The authors propose DT4Rec, which applies Decision Transformer to translate reinforcement learning into an autoregressive problem, thereby overcoming traditional RL challenges. The framework introduces three key innovations: an auto-discretized reward prompt to model numerical rewards while maintaining partial order relationships, weighted contrastive learning to resolve data discrepancies between training and inference, and robust offline metrics (model-based and similarity-based user retention scores) for reliable performance evaluation. Experimental results on two benchmark datasets demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines in both prediction accuracy and user retention metrics.

## Method Summary
DT4Rec tackles the offline reinforcement learning challenge in sequential recommendation by reformulating it as an autoregressive sequence modeling problem using Decision Transformer. The core innovation lies in three technical components: first, an auto-discretized reward prompt that discretizes continuous reward values while preserving their ordinal relationships; second, weighted contrastive learning that mitigates the distributional shift between training and inference phases; and third, the development of offline user retention metrics (MB-URS and SB-URS) that provide reliable evaluation without requiring online deployment. By transforming the RL problem into a sequence prediction task, DT4Rec avoids the exploration-exploitation dilemma inherent in traditional RL approaches while maintaining the ability to optimize long-term user engagement objectives.

## Key Results
- DT4Rec significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across multiple evaluation metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, NDCG, HR) on IQiYi and ML-1m datasets
- The framework achieves substantial improvements in user retention metrics (MB-URS, SB-URS, IUR, NRC), demonstrating enhanced long-term user engagement
- Experimental results validate the effectiveness of the proposed auto-discretized reward prompt and weighted contrastive learning components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The approach works by reframing reinforcement learning as autoregressive sequence modeling, which inherently addresses the exploration-exploitation dilemma and distribution shift problems common in offline RL. The auto-discretized reward prompt maintains the ordinal relationships of numerical rewards while enabling the model to learn from discrete reward representations, making the learning process more stable. Weighted contrastive learning explicitly addresses the training-inference discrepancy by aligning the representations learned during training with those needed during inference. The model-based and similarity-based user retention scores provide reliable offline evaluation by simulating future user behavior patterns and measuring similarity to historical retention behaviors, respectively, eliminating the need for costly online A/B testing during development.

- **Contrastive Learning**: A self-supervised learning technique that learns representations by contrasting positive and negative samples, needed to bridge training-inference distribution gaps; quick check: confirm that positive pairs are from same user sessions and negatives from different sessions
- **Offline RL Metrics**: Evaluation methods that assess performance without online deployment, needed because online testing is expensive and risky; quick check: validate that MB-URS and SB-URS correlate with actual online retention metrics
- **Autoregressive Modeling**: Predicting future tokens sequentially based on previous context, needed to transform sequential decision-making into a supervised learning problem; quick check: ensure proper causal masking in transformer architecture
- **Ordinal Discretization**: Converting continuous values into discrete categories while preserving order relationships, needed to make reward learning more stable; quick check: verify that discretized rewards maintain monotonic relationships with original values
- **Distribution Alignment**: Techniques to ensure training and inference distributions match, needed to prevent performance degradation from distribution shift; quick check: measure representation similarity between training and inference distributions

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: User Behavior History -> Transformer Encoder -> Reward Prediction Head + Item Recommendation Head -> Contrastive Loss + Standard Loss
**Critical Path**: The sequence flows from user behavior history through the transformer encoder to both the reward prediction and item recommendation heads, with contrastive learning applied to the encoder outputs to align training and inference distributions
**Design Tradeoffs**: The framework trades off the exploration capabilities of traditional RL for the stability and scalability of autoregressive sequence modeling, while accepting the computational overhead of contrastive learning for improved generalization
**Failure Signatures**: Potential failures include reward discretization losing critical ordinal information, contrastive learning misalignment causing distribution shift, and offline metrics failing to capture true online performance due to data distribution differences
**First Experiments**: 1) Ablation study removing contrastive learning to measure its contribution to performance gains, 2) Testing different discretization granularities for reward prompt to find optimal balance between stability and expressiveness, 3) Comparing MB-URS and SB-URS metrics against actual online retention metrics on a small-scale deployment

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions in the provided content, but implicit questions remain regarding the scalability of the approach to larger, more complex datasets and the robustness of the proposed offline metrics under different data distributions.

## Limitations
- Scalability to larger, more complex datasets is not thoroughly analyzed, which is critical for real-world deployment scenarios
- The robustness of proposed offline metrics (MB-URS and SB-URS) lacks thorough validation against potential biases or limitations in training data
- The framework's ability to generalize to unseen user behaviors with the auto-discretized reward prompt is not explicitly explored

## Confidence
- High confidence in the effectiveness of DT4Rec on benchmark datasets due to significant performance improvements across multiple metrics
- Medium confidence in the generalizability of results to real-world scenarios due to lack of scalability analysis
- Medium confidence in the reliability of proposed offline metrics due to insufficient validation of robustness
