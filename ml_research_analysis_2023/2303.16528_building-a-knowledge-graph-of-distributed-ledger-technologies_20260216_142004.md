# Building a Knowledge Graph of Distributed Ledger Technologies

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16528
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16528
- Reference count: 38
- Key outcome: Comprehensive knowledge graph and ontology for distributed ledger technologies covering 115 classes, 15 properties, and 746 triples

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fragmented and ambiguous terminology in the distributed ledger technology (DLT) space by developing a comprehensive knowledge graph and ontology. Moving beyond the common blockchain-cryptocurrency focus, the work systematically surveys technical aspects, use cases, standards, and regulations to create a unified model of the broader DLT ecosystem. The resulting resources include 115 classes and 15 properties organized around core concepts of DLT systems, their components, security considerations, and real-world applications.

The ontology was developed following semantic web best practices using Protégé and RDFS, with evaluation through competency questions and SPARQL queries. The knowledge graph covers three main areas: standards and legal authorities, technical details and vulnerabilities, and use cases across business sectors. This modular approach provides a foundation for better understanding DLT systems, supporting security analysis, and facilitating interoperability and standardization efforts across the industry.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a comprehensive literature survey on distributed ledger technologies, covering technical components, use cases, standards, regulations, and security considerations. From this survey, they derived competency questions to guide ontology development. Using Protégé and following semantic web best practices, they created an RDFS-based ontology with 115 classes and 15 properties. The ontology was then instantiated into a knowledge graph with 746 triples by extracting information from the surveyed literature. Evaluation was performed through SPARQL queries designed to answer the competency questions, ensuring the ontology's coverage and consistency. The resulting resources were made publicly available under an open license with online documentation and a SPARQL query interface.

## Key Results
- Ontology developed with 115 classes and 15 properties following semantic web best practices
- Knowledge graph instantiated with 746 triples covering standards/legal, technical details/vulnerabilities, and use cases/business sectors
- Comprehensive coverage of DLT variants beyond blockchains (DAGs, hashgraphs, holochains)
- SPARQL interface provided for querying the knowledge graph

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The ontology provides a unified terminology for DLTs that reduces ambiguity and misunderstanding in the field.
- **Mechanism:** By systematically surveying existing literature and standards, the ontology captures the full spectrum of DLT technologies (blockchains, DAGs, hashgraphs, holochains) and their applications, moving beyond the blockchain-cryptocurrency conflation.
- **Core assumption:** A comprehensive, standardized vocabulary will improve communication and adoption of DLTs across industries.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "This work therefore establishes a modular ontology, which encompasses and relates the following concepts and areas: Distributed ledger technology with a broader perspective than just a limited focus on blockchains..."
  - [section] "One way to unify the attempts of providing clarity on distributed ledgers is to use a knowledge graph to capture different technical capabilities and the wider ecosystem."
- **Break condition:** If new DLT variants emerge faster than the ontology can be updated, or if industry stakeholders do not adopt the terminology.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The knowledge graph enables better security analysis and risk assessment for DLT systems.
- **Mechanism:** By explicitly modeling vulnerabilities, attacks, and technical components, security analysts can query the graph to identify threats specific to a given DLT system or component.
- **Core assumption:** Security personnel can effectively use SPARQL queries on the knowledge graph to assess risks and plan mitigations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Such a knowledge graph improves the overall understanding of distributed ledgers, reveals their strengths, and supports the work of security personnel, i.e. analysts and system architects."
  - [section] "The model and subsequent knowledge graph created in this work can be used in a multitude of ways: Besides the already mentioned perspective of the entire ecosystem, it also allows for a more distinct observation of the included subjects. For instance, it can be used for threat analysis and risk assessments by identifying attacks and vulnerabilities of a system."
- **Break condition:** If the knowledge graph is not kept up-to-date with emerging threats or if users lack SPARQL expertise.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The ontology supports interoperability and standardization efforts for DLT systems.
- **Mechanism:** By linking DLT systems to relevant standards, regulations, and legal authorities, the ontology helps organizations ensure compliance and facilitates the development of interoperable systems.
- **Core assumption:** Organizations will use the ontology to map their DLT implementations to existing standards and regulations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The ontology was developed following semantic web best practices and includes 115 classes and 15 properties, organized around core concepts of DLT systems, their components, security considerations, and real-world applications."
  - [section] "The knowledge graph consists of three parts: (i) standards and legal authorities [11], (ii) technical details, vulnerabilities and security aspects [14], and (iii) use cases and business sectors [26]."
- **Break condition:** If standards and regulations change faster than the ontology can be updated, or if organizations prefer proprietary models.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  Ontology (115 classes, 15 properties, 571 triples) -> Knowledge Graph (746 triples) -> SPARQL Interface (Comunica SPARQL Widget)

- **Critical path:**
  1. Information collection from literature on DLTs, standards, regulations, and use cases
  2. Definition of competency questions to guide ontology development
  3. Ontology creation using Protégé, following semantic web best practices
  4. Knowledge graph instantiation from literature data
  5. Evaluation via SPARQL queries against competency questions
  6. Publication and documentation of resources

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Breadth vs. depth: The ontology aims for a holistic view but may lack detailed technical implementations
  - Modularity vs. complexity: A modular approach allows flexibility but increases the number of classes and relationships
  - Manual vs. automated data collection: Manual research ensures quality but is time-consuming and may not scale

- **Failure signatures:**
  - SPARQL queries return incomplete or incorrect results
  - Users struggle to map their specific DLT implementations to the ontology
  - The ontology does not capture new DLT variants or emerging standards quickly enough

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Execute the provided SPARQL queries for competency questions T1-T4 to validate the ontology's coverage
  2. Use the SPARQL interface to query for vulnerabilities and attacks specific to a given DLT component (e.g., consensus algorithm)
  3. Attempt to map a real-world DLT use case (e.g., supply chain) to the ontology's business sector and use case classes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the need for a comprehensive and modular approach to model the broader DLT ecosystem, including various technical capabilities, security considerations, and real-world applications.
- Why unresolved: The DLT landscape is constantly evolving with new technologies, standards, and applications emerging regularly. Capturing this dynamic nature in a knowledge graph requires ongoing updates and adaptations to the ontology and knowledge graph structure.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and implementation of automated or semi-automated methods for updating the ontology and knowledge graph to incorporate new DLT systems, standards, and use cases as they emerge. Evaluation of the effectiveness and scalability of these methods in maintaining an up-to-date and comprehensive knowledge graph.

### Open Question 2

- Why unresolved: DLT systems and platforms often use different terminologies, data structures, and formats, making it difficult to seamlessly integrate information from diverse sources into a unified knowledge graph. Standardization efforts are ongoing but not yet fully realized.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of data integration and transformation techniques specifically designed for the DLT domain, enabling the seamless incorporation of heterogeneous data sources into a unified knowledge graph. Demonstration of the effectiveness of these techniques in handling the diversity and complexity of DLT-related data.

## Limitations
- The ontology may not capture emerging DLT variants or rapidly evolving standards and regulations in real-time
- Manual literature survey approach ensures quality but introduces potential temporal gaps between new developments and ontology updates
- SPARQL-based evaluation may not fully capture practical usability challenges that users might face when applying the ontology to real-world scenarios

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The ontology's foundational structure and core concepts are well-supported by the literature survey and follow established semantic web best practices. The separation of DLT variants beyond blockchains is clearly justified.
- **Medium Confidence**: The practical utility of the knowledge graph for security analysis and risk assessment depends on how well practitioners can translate their specific use cases into SPARQL queries, which may require domain expertise.
- **Medium Confidence**: The evaluation through competency questions provides good coverage but may not fully represent all possible user queries or edge cases in the DLT domain.
