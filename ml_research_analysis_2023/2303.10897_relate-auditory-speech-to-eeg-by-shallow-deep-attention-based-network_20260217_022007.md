# Relate auditory speech to EEG by shallow-deep attention-based network

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10897
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10897
- Reference count: 0
- This paper proposes a novel Shallow-Deep Attention-based Network (SDANet) to classify the correct auditory stimulus evoking the EEG signal, achieving 80.2% accuracy on the test dataset and 78.94% on the final blind testset.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SDANet, a novel architecture for classifying auditory stimuli from EEG signals using an Attention-based Correlation Module (ACM) and a Shallow-Deep Similarity Classification Module (SDSCM). The model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on the match-mismatch track of the Auditory EEG challenge, achieving 80.2% accuracy on the test dataset and 78.94% on the blind testset. The approach leverages attention mechanisms to discover global correlations between speech and EEG, while using multi-scale embeddings from shallow and deep layers for robust classification.

## Method Summary
The SDANet architecture combines convolutional layers with attention-based modules to learn relationships between auditory speech and EEG signals. The backbone uses convolutional layers with kernel size 3 and powers of 2 dilation rates, followed by BatchNorm and ReLU. The Attention-based Correlation Module (ACM) employs residual attention layers to capture global correlations, while the Shallow-Deep Similarity Classification Module (SDSCM) concatenates embeddings from shallow and deep layers for final classification. The model is trained using Adam optimizer with learning rate 3e-4, weight decay 0.0001, and dropout 0.2 over 100 epochs with SpecAug data augmentation.

## Key Results
- Achieves 80.2% accuracy on the test dataset for the match-mismatch track
- Achieves 78.94% accuracy on the final blind testset
- Outperforms baseline models significantly on the Auditory EEG challenge

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The success of SDANet stems from its ability to capture both global and local relationships between speech and EEG signals. The attention-based correlation module discovers long-range dependencies and temporal patterns in the data, while the shallow-deep similarity classification leverages multi-scale features that capture both fine-grained and abstract representations of the auditory-EEG relationship.

## Limitations
- The model was tested on a relatively simple dataset with envelope-based features, limiting generalizability to more complex speech stimuli
- The specific implementation details of the residual attention layer in ACM are not fully specified
- The approach requires 64-channel EEG data, which may not be practical in all real-world applications
