# A Unified Model and Dimension for Interactive Estimation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06184
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06184
- Reference count: 40
- Introduces interactive estimation framework with new dissimilarity dimension measure

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a unified interactive learning framework called interactive estimation, where a learner queries alternatives and observes similarity-based rewards to estimate an unknown target. The key innovation is introducing the dissimilarity dimension as a new combinatorial complexity measure that characterizes learnability in this model. The authors provide a simple algorithm with both regret and PAC generalization bounds polynomial in this new dimension, while demonstrating how their framework unifies two classic models: statistical-query learning and structured bandits.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes Algorithm 1 which iteratively queries alternatives by optimizing over past observations to estimate an unknown target. The algorithm operates in an interactive setting where the learner receives similarity-based rewards when querying alternatives. The method is analyzed using a novel complexity measure called the dissimilarity dimension, which captures the combinatorial structure of the hypothesis class. Theoretical guarantees are provided in terms of both regret and PAC learning bounds, with polynomial dependence on the dissimilarity dimension. The framework is shown to generalize both statistical-query learning and structured bandit settings through appropriate mappings of their respective complexity measures.

## Key Results
- Introduces dissimilarity dimension as a unifying complexity measure for interactive learning
- Algorithm achieves regret and PAC bounds polynomial in dissimilarity dimension
- Shows polynomial relationships between dissimilarity dimension and strong SQ dimension/eluder dimension
- Provides explicit bounds on dissimilarity dimension for linear and GLM bandit classes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by leveraging similarity-based feedback to progressively refine estimates of the unknown target. Each query provides information about the relative dissimilarity between alternatives and the target, allowing the learner to eliminate large portions of the hypothesis space. The dissimilarity dimension captures the intrinsic complexity of this elimination process, measuring how many adaptively chosen queries are needed to identify the target with high confidence. By connecting to existing complexity measures like the eluder dimension and strong SQ dimension, the framework inherits strong theoretical guarantees while providing a more unified perspective on interactive learning.
