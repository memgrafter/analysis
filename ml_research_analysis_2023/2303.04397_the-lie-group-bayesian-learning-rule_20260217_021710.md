# The Lie-Group Bayesian Learning Rule

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04397
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04397
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: A Lie-group extension of Bayesian Learning Rule that addresses parameterization, gradient computation, and manifold constraints while achieving competitive accuracy on image classification tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses three fundamental difficulties in Bayesian Learning Rule (BLR): requiring specific exponential family parameterizations, complex gradient computation, and updates that may leave the manifold. The proposed solution extends BLR to Lie groups, where posteriors are parametrized through transformations of arbitrary base distributions using the group's exponential map. This approach enables flexible parameterizations, simplified gradient computation through reparameterization, and updates that automatically stay on the manifold. Experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet demonstrate competitive accuracy to state-of-the-art variational inference methods while offering more flexibility in distribution choice.

## Method Summary
The Lie-group BLR extends the standard Bayesian Learning Rule by parametrizing posterior distributions through transformations of an arbitrary base distribution using Lie group actions. Instead of directly parameterizing the distribution, the method uses a Lie group G to transform a base distribution q0, yielding qg = π(g)q0 where π is the group action. Updates are performed via the group's exponential map, which ensures they remain on the manifold. The gradient computation is simplified through a reparameterization trick that pushes derivatives to the loss function, allowing estimation via automatic differentiation. The framework is demonstrated with three specific Lie groups: additive (Euclidean), multiplicative (positive orthant), and affine groups.

## Key Results
- Achieves competitive accuracy to state-of-the-art VI methods on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet
- Multiplicative variant learns sparse, localized, and compositional features demonstrating explainability benefits
- Offers more flexibility in distribution choice compared to standard BLR which requires exponential families
- Updates automatically stay on the manifold without additional constraints or modifications

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Base distribution q0 -> Lie group G -> Group element g -> Loss function ℓ(θ) -> Exponential map exp -> Fisher metric

- Critical path:
  1. Choose base distribution q0 and Lie group G
  2. Initialize group element g
  3. Compute MC samples from qg
  4. Calculate gradient direction using reparameterization trick
  5. Update g using exponential map
  6. Repeat until convergence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Choice of base distribution q0: Affects the flexibility and tractability of the method
  - Choice of Lie group G: Determines the type of transformations available and the computational complexity
  - Number of MC samples: Balances estimation accuracy with computational cost
  - Step size α: Affects convergence speed and stability

- Failure signatures:
  - Numerical instability in exponential map computations
  - Divergence or slow convergence due to poor choice of base distribution or Lie group
  - High variance in gradient estimates due to insufficient MC samples
  - Difficulty in computing Fisher metric or its inverse

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the additive group update for a simple MLP on MNIST, comparing to standard SGD
  2. Try the multiplicative group update for a network with fixed sign weights, observing sparsity patterns
  3. Apply the affine group update to a CNN on CIFAR-10, comparing to VOGN and iVON methods

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out specific open questions, but several areas for future research are implied by the discussion of limitations and potential extensions.

## Limitations
- The framework only explores three specific Lie groups (additive, multiplicative, affine), leaving uncertainty about performance for other Lie groups like special orthogonal or unitary groups
- Computational complexity of the exponential map for different groups is not thoroughly analyzed, potentially limiting scalability to larger models
- The paper does not address how to automatically select between additive and multiplicative variants for a given problem

## Confidence
- High confidence in the mathematical framework: The connection between Lie groups, exponential maps, and distribution parameterization is rigorously established
- Medium confidence in practical implementation: Limited empirical evidence across diverse Lie groups and architectures
- Medium confidence in the claimed advantages: Theoretical justification is strong but comparative analysis focuses primarily on accuracy metrics
