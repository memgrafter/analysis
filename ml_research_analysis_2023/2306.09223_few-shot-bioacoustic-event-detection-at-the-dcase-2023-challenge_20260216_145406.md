# Few-shot bioacoustic event detection at the DCASE 2023 challenge

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09223
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09223
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Six teams participated with the best system achieving an F-score of 63%, up from 60% in 2022

## Executive Summary
This paper describes the 2023 edition of the DCASE few-shot bioacoustic event detection task, where systems must detect sound events using only 5 example events at inference time. The task featured an expanded evaluation set including new species (cows and frogs) and banned ensemble models to encourage truly general systems. Six teams participated with the best system achieving an F-score of 63%, demonstrating steady improvement in few-shot bioacoustic detection capabilities. The results highlight both the progress in this challenging domain and the remaining difficulties across diverse taxonomic groups.

## Method Summary
The task required detecting bioacoustic events with access to only 5 labeled examples per class at inference time. Participants used various approaches including prototypical networks, contrastive learning, and template matching. Systems operated on frame-level embeddings and employed different strategies for few-shot learning, with some incorporating multi-task learning (including voice activity detection) and negative-based losses. The evaluation used event-based F-score with intersection over union and bipartite graph matching.

## Key Results
- Best system achieved 63% F-score, improving from 60% in 2022
- Systems struggled with dolphin sounds (QU dataset) across both years
- Contrastive learning and prototypical networks with squeeze/excitation blocks showed promising results
- Multi-task learning with voice activity detection contributed to the winning submission

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Contrastive learning improves few-shot bioacoustic event detection by learning discriminative embeddings that separate positive and negative event samples.
- Mechanism: The system learns an embedding space where positive events are pushed close together while being pushed far away from negative events. During evaluation, the encoder is fine-tuned on the 5 provided examples and selected negative examples, enabling better discrimination.
- Core assumption: The embedding space learned through contrastive learning can generalize across diverse bioacoustic domains with limited examples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Moummad IMT implemented a system based on Contrastive Learning, a method to learn an embedding space that maximises the distinction between positive events and negative events."
  - [section 4] "Moummad IMT implemented a system based on Contrastive Learning, a method to learn an embedding space that maximises the distinction between positive events and negative events. During the evaluation stage, the encoder is further fine-tuned on the 5 POS examples provided and on selected NEG examples for each audiofile."
- Break condition: If the embedding space learned does not generalize well across different taxonomic groups or if the negative examples selected do not represent the true negative space.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prototypical networks with squeeze/excitation (SE) blocks improve few-shot bioacoustic detection by adaptively weighting feature map channels.
- Mechanism: The SE blocks predict the importance of different channels in the feature map, allowing the network to focus on more discriminative features for each class. This is combined with prototypical networks that use distance metrics in the embedding space.
- Core assumption: Different channels in the feature map have varying importance for different bioacoustic events, and SE blocks can effectively learn these importance weights.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "XuQianHu NUDT largely improved upon the Prototypical Network baseline by including squeeze/excitation (SE) blocks into the encoder part of the network."
  - [section 4] "XuQianHu NUDT largely improved upon the Prototypical Network baseline by including squeeze/excitation (SE) blocks into the encoder part of the network. The idea behind this is to create an adaptive mechanism that assigns different weights to different channels of the feature map, by predicting their importance."
- Break condition: If the SE blocks overfit to the training data or if the channel importance does not transfer well to new taxonomic groups.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multi-task learning with frame-level embeddings and target speaker voice activity detection (VAD) improves few-shot bioacoustic detection by providing complementary supervision signals.
- Mechanism: The system includes a VAD branch alongside the event detection branch, allowing the model to learn better representations of the audio signal. The frame-level embeddings capture fine-grained temporal information.
- Core assumption: VAD and event detection are complementary tasks that share useful representations, and frame-level embeddings provide valuable temporal context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Du NERCSLIP 2 63.78 75.6 Prototypical network with frame level embeddings; multitask learning; Voice activity detection"
  - [section 4] "The winning submission is by the same team that won the previous edition of this task, namely,Du NERCSLIP. The system improves on the last year's submission, [11] by including their frame-level embedding system into a multi-task learning architecture. The new system now includes Target Speaker V oice Activity Detection as one of the branches."
- Break condition: If the VAD task does not provide useful signals for event detection or if the frame-level embeddings do not capture relevant temporal patterns.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Audio files with 5 labeled examples per class -> CNN-based encoder (possibly with SE blocks) -> Embedding space -> Prototypical network head -> Event detection predictions

- Critical path:
  1. Audio preprocessing (feature extraction)
  2. Encoder forward pass
  3. Embedding space computation
  4. Prototype calculation
  5. Distance-based classification
  6. Event detection post-processing

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Prototypical networks vs. metric learning approaches
  - Single-task vs. multi-task learning
  - Template matching vs. learned embeddings
  - Fixed vs. adaptive threshold for event detection

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on certain taxonomic groups (e.g., dolphins)
  - High false positive rate on background sounds
  - Inability to generalize to new species
  - Sensitivity to the quality of the 5 provided examples

  1. Baseline prototypical network with MFCC features
  2. Prototypical network with SE blocks and contrastive pre-training
  3. Multi-task prototypical network with VAD auxiliary task

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do specific dataset characteristics impact the performance of different few-shot learning systems for bioacoustic event detection?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "it is evident that each dataset presents unique challenges for various algorithms" and "we would be interested in analysing how exactly the characteristics of the different datasets impact each system."
- Why unresolved: While the paper observes that different datasets pose varying challenges, it does not provide a detailed analysis of which specific characteristics (e.g., event duration, background noise, acoustic complexity) are responsible for these performance differences.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing system performance across datasets with controlled variations in key characteristics, or a detailed correlation analysis between dataset features and model performance metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can a single general few-shot model effectively detect sound events across multiple taxonomic groups (mammals, birds, amphibians) with equal performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "we would be interested in analysing... if a single general model is indeed capable of predicting many different classes based on such few examples."
- Why unresolved: The paper shows improved results but doesn't conclusively demonstrate that one model can handle diverse taxonomic groups equally well. The QU dataset (dolphin quacks) remains consistently difficult across years.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of multi-domain versus specialized single-domain models across the full range of taxonomic groups, with performance metrics showing no significant degradation when generalizing across groups.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between positive and negative samples in contrastive learning approaches for few-shot bioacoustic event detection?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that "the high imbalance between positive samples and Negative samples creates... problems" and discusses negative-based loss functions as a solution.
- Why unresolved: While the paper introduces contrastive learning approaches and discusses the negative sample problem, it doesn't determine the optimal ratio or sampling strategy for positive to negative examples in the embedding space.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the positive-to-negative sample ratio and measuring impact on F-score across multiple datasets, identifying the optimal balance point for bioacoustic applications.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks detailed ablation studies to isolate the contributions of individual components (contrastive learning vs. SE blocks vs. multi-task learning).
- Limited analysis of failure modes and error patterns across different taxonomic groups and acoustic conditions.
- The evaluation set may not fully represent the diversity of real-world bioacoustic scenarios despite being expanded.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: The overall task design and evaluation methodology are well-established, with clear baselines and metrics. The comparative results between teams and the prohibition of ensemble models provide reliable indicators of genuine progress.
- Medium Confidence: The mechanisms behind individual approaches (contrastive learning, SE blocks, multi-task learning) are theoretically sound, but the specific implementations and their relative contributions to performance improvements are not fully detailed in the paper.
- Low Confidence: The generalizability of the learned representations across diverse taxonomic groups remains uncertain, particularly given the relatively small dataset sizes and the significant domain differences between birds, mammals, insects, and amphibians.
