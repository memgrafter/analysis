# Ensemble-based Blackbox Attacks on Dense Prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14304
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14304
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Ensemble-based blackbox attack method significantly outperforms existing approaches for dense prediction models, achieving state-of-the-art success rates with few queries and the ability to fool multiple models simultaneously.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an ensemble-based blackbox attack framework for dense prediction models like object detectors and semantic segmentation networks. The key innovation is using an ensemble of surrogate models with properly normalized weights to generate adversarial examples that transfer more effectively to unseen blackbox victim models. The method employs weight balancing to normalize contributions from individual models based on their loss magnitudes, and weight optimization to further improve attack success by querying the victim model and adjusting weights accordingly. Extensive experiments demonstrate significant improvements over existing blackbox attack approaches for both targeted and untargeted attacks.

## Method Summary
The method generates adversarial examples for dense prediction models by optimizing an ensemble loss across multiple surrogate models. Weight balancing normalizes the contribution of each surrogate model by adjusting weights based on individual model loss magnitudes, preventing models with larger losses from dominating the optimization. Weight optimization further improves attack performance by iteratively adjusting ensemble weights based on limited queries to the victim model. The approach uses projected gradient descent (PGD) optimization with ℓ∞ norm constraints and demonstrates the ability to generate single perturbations that simultaneously fool multiple blackbox models across different dense prediction tasks.

## Key Results
- The ensemble-based attack significantly outperforms existing blackbox attack methods on COCO, VOC, and Cityscapes datasets for both object detection and semantic segmentation.
- Weight balancing and weight optimization together achieve state-of-the-art attack success rates with only 5-20 queries to the victim model.
- The method can generate single adversarial perturbations that simultaneously fool multiple blackbox models across different dense prediction tasks.
- The approach demonstrates robust transferability across diverse model architectures and output spaces.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Weight Balancing
- Claim: Weight balancing normalizes contributions of different surrogate models by adjusting weights based on individual model loss magnitudes.
- Mechanism: Different dense prediction models have widely varying loss ranges (e.g., YOLOv3 loss ~60x larger than Faster R-CNN). Without normalization, models with larger losses dominate the ensemble optimization, leading to imbalanced attack success rates and reduced transferability to blackbox victims.
- Core assumption: Models with larger loss values contribute disproportionately to the ensemble loss if weights are uniform, and normalizing by loss magnitude will balance contributions effectively.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "normalization of the weights for individual models plays a critical role in the success of the attacks" and [section] "Models with large loss terms heavily influence the optimization procedure, reducing the attack success rate for models with small losses."

### Mechanism 2: Weight Optimization
- Claim: Weight optimization improves attack performance by iteratively adjusting ensemble weights based on victim model feedback through limited queries.
- Mechanism: After initial attack generation with balanced weights, the method queries the victim model and adjusts individual surrogate weights to minimize victim model loss, creating attacks tailored to the specific victim architecture and input/output characteristics.
- Core assumption: Victim model feedback can guide weight adjustments to create more transferable attacks, and small numbers of queries (5-20) are sufficient to optimize weights effectively.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "by adjusting the weights of the ensemble according to the victim model can further improve the performance of the attacks" and [section] "We further observe that such transfer-based attacks can be further improved by optimizing the weights of the ensemble according to the victim model."

### Mechanism 3: Multi-task Transferability
- Claim: Ensemble attacks can generate single perturbations that simultaneously fool multiple blackbox models across different dense prediction tasks.
- Mechanism: By including diverse surrogate models (object detectors + semantic segmentation) in the ensemble and applying weight balancing/optimization, a single adversarial perturbation can transfer to multiple victim models with different architectures and output spaces.
- Core assumption: Different dense prediction tasks share some underlying vulnerability patterns that can be exploited by a single perturbation, and the ensemble approach can capture these shared vulnerabilities.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "our proposed method can also generate a single perturbation that can fool multiple blackbox detection and segmentation models simultaneously" and [section] "We demonstrate that our approach can create adversarial examples to fool multiple blackbox models and tasks jointly."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Clean image -> Surrogate ensemble (multiple pre-trained models) -> Weight balancing module -> PGD optimization -> Initial adversarial example -> Query victim model -> Weight optimization -> Final adversarial example

- Critical path:
  1. Load clean image and specify target output
  2. Compute individual surrogate model losses for target output
  3. Apply weight balancing to normalize ensemble weights
  4. Generate initial adversarial example using PGD with balanced weights
  5. Query victim model with generated example
  6. If attack fails, optimize weights based on victim feedback
  7. Repeat steps 4-6 until success or query budget exhausted
  8. Return final adversarial example

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory vs. attack success: Larger ensembles provide better attack success but require more memory and computation
  - Query budget vs. optimization quality: More queries allow better weight optimization but increase attack cost
  - Perturbation magnitude vs. imperceptibility: Larger perturbations improve attack success but may be more noticeable
  - Ensemble diversity vs. coherence: More diverse surrogates may capture broader vulnerabilities but may be harder to balance

- Failure signatures:
  - Low attack success rate across all victims: Indicates weight balancing may be incorrect or ensemble is too homogeneous
  - High success on some surrogates but low on others: Suggests weight normalization is not properly balancing contributions
  - Rapid plateau in attack success with additional queries: May indicate victim model is too different from all surrogates
  - High computational cost without proportional gain: Could mean ensemble is too large for the problem complexity

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement single surrogate attack baseline: Verify that attacks generated by individual models have low transferability to other models
  2. Implement ensemble with weight balancing: Confirm that balanced ensemble improves attack success compared to single surrogate
  3. Implement weight optimization with limited queries: Test whether 5-10 queries can improve attack success beyond balanced ensemble alone

## Limitations

- The paper's core claims rely heavily on internal experimental validation without strong external corpus support for the proposed weight balancing and optimization mechanisms.
- The claim about generating single perturbations that fool multiple task types simultaneously requires more rigorous ablation studies to confirm generalization beyond the specific models tested.
- The effectiveness of weight balancing for normalizing contributions across models lacks theoretical grounding about why specific normalization factors work better than alternatives.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The ensemble attack framework is technically sound and the experimental methodology is clearly specified. The improvements over baseline methods are well-documented with quantitative metrics.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of weight balancing for normalizing contributions across models is supported by experimental results but lacks theoretical grounding about why specific normalization factors work better than alternatives.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that a single perturbation can simultaneously fool object detectors and segmentation models across different tasks requires more extensive validation across diverse model architectures and tasks.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-task generalization test**: Evaluate the multi-task perturbation capability on a broader range of dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth estimation, instance segmentation) and model architectures not included in the surrogate ensemble to verify true generalization.

2. **Weight normalization ablation**: Compare the proposed loss-based weight balancing against alternative normalization strategies (e.g., model capacity-based, gradient magnitude-based) to establish whether the specific normalization approach is critical to success.

3. **Query efficiency analysis**: Systematically vary the query budget (Q=1, 5, 10, 20, 50) and measure the marginal benefit of additional queries to determine the optimal query-to-performance tradeoff for practical deployment.
