# On the relationship between multivariate splines and infinitely-wide neural networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03459
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03459
- Reference count: 25
- This paper establishes a connection between multivariate splines and infinitely-wide neural networks with homogeneous activation functions.

## Executive Summary
This paper demonstrates a fundamental connection between multivariate splines and infinitely-wide neural networks with homogeneous activation functions (powers of ReLU). The authors show that these neural networks induce a function space equivalent to a Sobolev space, with explicit norm bounds on derivatives. They derive closed-form formulas for the associated kernels in both one dimension and higher dimensions, and demonstrate that neural network random feature expansions offer better numerical stability than classical Fourier features, particularly in terms of leverage scores.

## Method Summary
The paper establishes theoretical equivalence between multivariate splines with kernels proportional to ||x-y||^(2α+1) and infinitely-wide neural networks with homogeneous activation functions. The method involves deriving closed-form kernel formulas, showing RKHS norm equivalence to Sobolev norms, and comparing random feature expansions through leverage scores. The authors provide both theoretical analysis and empirical validation using synthetic 1D data to demonstrate approximation quality and numerical stability advantages of neural network random features over Fourier features.

## Key Results
- Multivariate splines can be represented as infinitely-wide neural networks with homogeneous activation functions
- The induced function space is a Sobolev space with explicit bounds on derivative norms
- Neural network random features show better leverage score scaling (1/√λ) compared to Fourier features (1/λ)
- Empirical comparisons confirm superior approximation quality of neural network approaches

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Sobolev spaces and their relationship to kernel methods
  - Why needed here: Function space equivalence is central to the theoretical foundation
  - Quick check question: What is the order of the Sobolev space for d dimensions and activation power α?
  - Answer: s = (d+1)/2 + α

- Concept: Reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS) and their norms
  - Why needed here: RKHS norm equivalence to Sobolev norms is essential for understanding function space properties
  - Quick check question: How is the RKHS norm for k^(α)_1(f) expressed?
  - Answer: Ω^(α)_1(f)^2 = 2R · (α!)^2 ∫_(-R)^R f^(α+1)(x)^2 dx + Θ^(α)[f^(0)(-R), f^(0)(R), ..., f^(α)(-R), f^(α)(R)]

- Concept: Random feature expansions and their approximation properties
  - Why needed here: Comparison between neural network and Fourier features requires understanding random feature approximation
  - Quick check question: What quantity assesses random feature expansion capacity?
  - Answer: The leverage score v ↦ ⟨ϕ(·,v), (Σ + λI)^(-1)ϕ(·,v)⟩_L^2(Bd(R))

- Comparison focuses on specific regularization regime that may not generalize to all practical applications
