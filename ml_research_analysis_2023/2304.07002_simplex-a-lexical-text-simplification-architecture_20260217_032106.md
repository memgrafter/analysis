# SimpLex: a lexical text simplification architecture

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.07002
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07002
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Transformer-based models outperformed word embedding-based models in SARI score (up to 0.350) while word embedding-based models achieved the largest perplexity decrease (up to 9.8%)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SimpLex, a modular lexical text simplification architecture that identifies complex words and replaces them with simpler alternatives. The system uses either word embeddings (Word2Vec) with perplexity metrics or transformer models (BERT, RoBERTa, GPT2) with cosine similarity to generate simplified English sentences. Evaluated on the WikiNet dataset using SARI and Perplexity Decrease metrics, transformer-based approaches showed better performance in preserving meaning and context during simplification.

## Method Summary
SimpLex employs a modular approach to text simplification, consisting of three main components: complex word identification using a neural network classifier, synonym generation through embeddings or transformer models, and sentence ranking using similarity metrics. The system can operate with either word embedding-based models (Word2Vec) combined with perplexity measures, or transformer-based models (BERT, RoBERTa, GPT2) using cosine similarity. For complex word identification, the system uses a neural network classifier that analyzes features like word length, frequency, and syllable count. Synonym candidates are generated and ranked based on their semantic similarity to the original words, with the final output being the sentence that maximizes simplification while preserving meaning.

## Key Results
- Transformer-based models achieved SARI scores up to 0.350, outperforming word embedding-based models
- Word embedding-based models achieved the largest perplexity decrease of up to 9.8%
- Transformer models showed better performance in preserving meaning and context during simplification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The modular architecture allows for flexible component selection and optimization, enabling the system to leverage the strengths of different approaches for different aspects of simplification. Word embeddings provide efficient semantic similarity calculations for perplexity-based ranking, while transformer models capture richer contextual information for cosine similarity-based selection. The neural network classifier for complex word identification provides a data-driven approach to identifying simplification candidates, and the two-stage ranking process (synonym selection then sentence ranking) ensures that the final output balances simplicity with meaning preservation.

## Foundational Learning

**Text Simplification**: Process of transforming complex text into simpler, more readable versions while preserving meaning
*Why needed*: Core objective of the system
*Quick check*: Can identify when text has been successfully simplified

**Word Embeddings**: Vector representations of words that capture semantic relationships
*Why needed*: Enables semantic similarity calculations for synonym selection
*Quick check*: Can measure cosine similarity between word vectors

**Transformer Models**: Deep learning architectures using attention mechanisms for contextual understanding
*Why needed*: Captures contextual relationships beyond individual word meanings
*Quick check*: Can process sequences with bidirectional context

**SARI Metric**: Automated evaluation metric measuring n-gram overlap with references
*Why needed*: Provides standardized quantitative assessment of simplification quality
*Quick check*: Higher scores indicate better preservation of meaning and appropriate simplification

**Perplexity**: Measure of how well a probability model predicts a sample
*Why needed*: Quantifies the reduction in linguistic complexity
*Quick check*: Lower perplexity indicates simpler, more predictable text

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Complex Word Identification -> Synonym Generation -> Sentence Ranking -> Final Output

**Critical Path**: The core simplification pipeline flows through neural network classification, semantic similarity calculation, and ranking optimization. The system must successfully identify complex words, generate appropriate synonyms, and select the optimal replacement combination.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Word embedding approaches offer computational efficiency and large perplexity decreases but may lack contextual awareness. Transformer approaches provide better semantic preservation and context understanding but require more computational resources. The dual evaluation with both SARI and Perplexity metrics captures different aspects of simplification quality but may not fully align.

**Failure Signatures**: Poor complex word identification leads to inappropriate simplification targets. Inadequate synonym generation results in semantic drift or unnatural replacements. Suboptimal ranking produces sentences that either remain too complex or oversimplify to the point of meaning loss.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to WikiNet dataset, constraining generalizability
- Neural network classifier training data and methodology not disclosed
- SARI metric limitations acknowledged but not fully addressed through complementary evaluation methods

## Confidence
**High confidence**: The system architecture description and modular design are clearly presented and technically sound
**Medium confidence**: The relative performance rankings between transformer and word embedding models are reliable within the tested conditions
**Low confidence**: Claims about superiority of specific approaches for particular aspects of simplification (meaning preservation vs perplexity reduction) due to uncontrolled confounding variables
