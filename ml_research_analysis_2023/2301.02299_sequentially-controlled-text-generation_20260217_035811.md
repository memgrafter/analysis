# Sequentially Controlled Text Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.02299
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02299
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: More structural awareness (past-aware and full-sequence control) combined with editing achieves near-human performance in controlled text generation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces sequentially controlled text generation, a novel task that imposes structure on long-range text by developing a pipeline that combines generation and editing approaches. The method tests different degrees of structural awareness (local-only, past-aware, full-sequence) and control mechanisms (Hidden-State Control and Direct Probability Control). The key insight is that more structural awareness generally results in higher control accuracy, grammaticality, coherency, and topicality, approaching human-level writing performance.

## Method Summary
The approach uses GPT-2 fine-tuned on a large news corpus, with a discriminator trained to predict discourse tags using different structural awareness variants. Generation occurs through either Hidden-State Control (modifying hidden states) or Direct Probability Control (modifying output probabilities), with optional editing using gradient-based feature importance and T5 in-filling. The pipeline generates sentences conditioned on control codes while maintaining coherence across the document.

## Key Results
- Past-aware structural modeling with editing achieves highest class-label accuracy (~89%)
- Full-sequence structural control without editing achieves best grammar and topicality scores
- More structural awareness consistently improves control accuracy, grammaticality, coherency, and topicality
- Human evaluators rated automatically generated text at 4.2-4.4/5 for grammar, approaching human-level performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Discriminator-based control can impose structural coherence even with weak classifiers. The discriminator estimates p(c|x) for control codes and multiplies with LM likelihood p(x) to bias generation toward desired structure. Core assumption: Even weak classifiers provide relative differences between structurally correct and incorrect generations. Evidence: 61% macro F1 discriminator still enables strong accuracy, and weak correlation between perplexity and human quality judgments suggests structural control compensates for weak discriminators.

### Mechanism 2
Past-aware structural modeling improves local coherence more than full-sequence modeling. Past-aware control incorporates previous discourse tags as context, allowing model to maintain narrative flow. Core assumption: Current sentence generation depends primarily on prior context rather than future expectations. Evidence: Past structural information boosts class accuracy most, and Ovid's Unicorn analysis shows unnatural structure without proper discourse flow.

### Mechanism 3
Editing combined with generation provides better local control than generation alone. Post-generation editing identifies high-impact words and re-infills them to better match local control codes. Core assumption: Local sentence structure can be improved without disrupting overall document coherence. Evidence: Editing improves accuracy, grammar and logical flow across different pipelines, though weak correlation between sentence length and grammar suggests editing can fix structural issues.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: GPT-2 language model → Discriminator (sentence classifier) → Generation module → Editing module → Output
- **Critical path**: Generation module (with DPC/HSC) → Sentence completion → Editing module → Final document
- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - DPC simpler to implement but restricts vocabulary to top-k words
  - HSC more flexible but computationally expensive with backpropagation
  - Editing improves local control but risks coherence
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Early sentence termination (likely tokenization issues)
  - Poor grammar correlation with perplexity
  - Low accuracy despite high structural awareness

  1. Test DPC with local-only control vs naive GPT-2 generation
  2. Compare past-aware vs full-sequence control variants
  3. Evaluate impact of editing on accuracy vs coherence metrics

## Limitations

- Discriminator performance limitations: 61% macro F1 classifier still enables effective control, but minimum performance threshold remains unclear
- Limited generalizability: Only tested on news articles with 9 discourse tags from specific dataset
- Computational costs not fully characterized: Editing module requires gradient computation and potentially expensive T5 in-filling

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Structural awareness improves control accuracy - Strong empirical support across multiple pipeline variants
- **High Confidence**: Past-aware structural modeling improves local coherence - Consistent results showing past-aware variants outperforming local-only
- **Medium Confidence**: Editing improves local control without harming coherence - Results show mixed effects, with editing helping accuracy but potentially harming coherence in some cases
- **Medium Confidence**: Human-level performance is approached - Based on human evaluation but limited sample size and evaluation protocol details
- **Low Confidence**: Weak classifiers can effectively control structure - Interesting finding but limited exploration of performance thresholds
- **Low Confidence**: Full-sequence structural control provides best grammar - Counterintuitive result that contradicts the general trend favoring past-aware modeling

## Next Checks

1. **Classifier Performance Threshold Analysis**: Systematically vary discriminator performance (via training data size, architecture complexity, or data augmentation) to identify the minimum F1 score required for effective structural control. This would validate whether the 61% F1 result is a fluke or represents a general principle about weak classifiers in structural control.

2. **Cross-Domain Generalization Test**: Apply the same pipeline to a different domain (e.g., scientific papers, fiction, or technical documentation) with different structural patterns to assess generalizability. This would reveal whether the approach is specific to news discourse or represents a general method for sequential control.

3. **Ablation Study on Editing Module**: Conduct a detailed ablation study isolating the editing module's effects by comparing: (a) generation-only variants, (b) generation + gradient-based word selection without T5 in-filling, and (c) full editing pipeline. This would clarify whether editing genuinely improves control or simply masks generation deficiencies.
