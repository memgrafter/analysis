# RAMM: Retrieval-augmented Biomedical Visual Question Answering with Multi-modal Pre-training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.00534
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00534
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: State-of-the-art performance on Med-VQA2019, Med-VQA2021, VQARAD, and SLAKE datasets using retrieval-augmented pretraining

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses data limitations in biomedical visual question answering by proposing a retrieval-augmented pretraining paradigm. The authors collect PMCPM, a patient-based image-text dataset from PubMed case reports, and pretrain a multi-modal model with masked language modeling, image-text contrastive learning, and image-text matching tasks. During fine-tuning, they retrieve similar image-text pairs from pretraining datasets and fuse them using a novel retrieval-attention module. Experiments demonstrate significant performance improvements across four biomedical VQA benchmarks, achieving state-of-the-art results.

## Method Summary
The approach combines three key innovations: (1) PMCPM dataset collection from PubMed Central case reports providing diverse patient-based image-text pairs, (2) multi-modal pretraining with masked language modeling, image-text contrastive learning, and image-text matching using combined PMCPM, ROCO, and MIMIC-CXR datasets, and (3) retrieval-augmented fine-tuning where similar image-text pairs are retrieved based on image-text contrastive similarity and fused using a retrieval-attention module. The retrieval-attention applies co-attention style fusion after each self-attention and cross-attention layer, using retrieved samples' [CLS] representations as attention keys/values while preserving original representations.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on four biomedical VQA benchmarks: Med-VQA2019, Med-VQA2021, VQARAD, and SLAKE
- PMCPM dataset provides larger and more diverse training data compared to ROCO and MIMIC-CXR
- Retrieval-attention module effectively incorporates knowledge from similar cases during fine-tuning
- Image-based retrieval outperforms question-based retrieval due to question homogeneity in biomedical domain

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Retrieval-augmented pretraining with image-text contrastive learning enables effective cross-modal alignment for biomedical VQA.
- Mechanism: The model uses image-text contrastive similarity scores to retrieve relevant image-text pairs from large pretraining datasets, and these retrieved pairs are fused into the VQA model using a retrieval-attention module. This simulates how doctors solve complicated cases by searching for similar cases and proposing solutions based on previous experience.
- Core assumption: Images in biomedical VQA are more informative than questions for retrieval purposes, as questions tend to be homogeneous and non-informative across the domain.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We propose to retrieve similar image-text pairs based on ITC from pretraining datasets and introduce a novel retrieval-attention module to fuse the representation of the image and the question with the retrieved images and texts."
  - [section]: "Since questions are homogeneous in biomedical VQA, we propose to use images for retrieving. We pretrain the biomedical multi-modal model with three tasks: masked language modeling, image-text contrastive learning, and image-text matching using the combination of our PMCPM dataset and previous widely-used ROCO and MIMIC-CXR datasets."
- Break condition: If the image-text contrastive similarity fails to retrieve truly relevant pairs, or if the retrieval-attention module introduces too much noise from unrelated pairs.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The PMCPM dataset provides diverse, high-quality patient-based image-text pairs that complement existing biomedical pretraining resources.
- Mechanism: PMCPM is collected from PubMed Central case reports, filtering out non-clinical images and focusing on patient-based clinical conditions. This provides more diverse modalities and detailed text descriptions compared to ROCO (limited quantity) and MIMIC-CXR (chest X-ray only).
- Core assumption: Images from diverse patient conditions provide richer information for multi-modal pretraining than limited radiology images alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "PMC-Patients-Multi-modal ( PMCPM), a patient-based image-text dataset filtered from PubMed Central, which is larger than ROCO and MIMIC-CXR with various modalities and conditions of images."
  - [section]: "We thus collect these figures with corresponding captions to build our PMCPM dataset. Figure 3 shows two examples of our PMCPM... The examples show that our patient-based collecting method can target clinical-related figures with more image modality and detailed text descriptions."
- Break condition: If the filtering process fails to remove noisy non-clinical images, or if the dataset size and diversity claims are overstated.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Retrieval-attention module effectively fuses retrieved knowledge into the VQA model's multi-modal representations.
- Mechanism: The retrieval-attention module applies co-attention style fusion layers that use [CLS] representations of retrieved samples as attention keys/values while preserving the original sample's representation. This allows the model to incorporate additional information without losing its own learned features.
- Core assumption: Co-attention style fusion with retrieval samples can effectively combine knowledge from similar cases without overwhelming the original representation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We propose a retrieval-augmented method to better use the limited data. We propose to retrieve similar image-text pairs based on ITC from pretraining datasets and introduce a novel retrieval-attention module to fuse the representation of the image and the question with the retrieved images and texts."
  - [section]: "Retrieval-attention is applied after the self-attention and cross-attention in each multi-modal fusion layer... We perform retrieval-attention on original representations to fuse additional information from retrieved samples."
- Break condition: If the retrieval-attention module causes gradient instability or if the fusion process doesn't improve VQA performance despite retrieving relevant pairs.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Image-Text Contrastive Learning
  - Why needed here: This pretraining task aligns visual and textual representations in the same embedding space, enabling effective retrieval of similar image-text pairs for augmentation.
  - Quick check question: How does image-text contrastive learning create embeddings that can be used for similarity-based retrieval?

- Concept: Co-attention Mechanisms in Multi-modal Fusion
  - Why needed here: The retrieval-attention module builds on co-attention principles to fuse information from multiple modalities while preserving individual representations.
  - Quick check question: What's the difference between self-attention and cross-attention in multi-modal transformers?

  - Why needed here: Understanding medical terminology, imaging modalities, and clinical contexts is crucial for interpreting the VQA tasks and the specialized datasets used.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text encoder (PubMedBERT) -> Image encoder (Swin-Base) -> Multi-modal encoder (L layers of co-attention fusion) -> Retrieval-attention module -> Classification layer

- Critical path:
  1. Pretraining phase: Process PMCPM + ROCO + MIMIC-CXR through MLM, ITC, and ITM tasks
  2. Fine-tuning phase: For each VQA sample, retrieve r similar pairs using ITC similarity
  3. Fusion phase: Apply retrieval-attention to combine retrieved knowledge with original representations
  4. Classification phase: Use fused representations for answer prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fixed retrieval count (r=4) vs. adaptive retrieval based on similarity scores
  - Freezing uni-modal encoders during retrieval vs. fine-tuning them together
  - Using only image representations for retrieval vs. incorporating question information

- Failure signatures:
  - Retrieval attention module causes training instability or NaN values
  - Performance degrades when retrieving too many (8+) pairs vs. fewer (1-4) pairs
  - Model overfits to retrieved samples and performs poorly on novel questions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify retrieval quality: Check top-5 retrieved pairs for 10 random VQA samples and assess relevance
  2. Ablation study: Compare performance with r=0 (no retrieval), r=1, r=2, r=4, r=8 to find optimal retrieval count
  3. Pretraining analysis: Compare representations from models trained with only ROCO+MIMIC-CXR vs. those with PMCPM added

## Limitations
- Retrieval-attention mechanism effectiveness depends heavily on quality of retrieved samples without detailed precision analysis
- Fixed retrieval count of 4 pairs may not be optimal across different VQA samples or datasets
- PMCPM dataset collection relies on keyword-based filtering that may miss relevant clinical images or include noise

## Confidence
- High confidence in the retrieval-augmented pretraining framework design and implementation
- Medium confidence in the PMCPM dataset's quality and diversity claims (lacks independent verification)
- Medium confidence in the retrieval-attention module's contribution (no ablation study isolating its effect)
- High confidence in overall performance improvements across benchmarks

## Next Checks
1. Conduct qualitative analysis of retrieved pairs: For 20 randomly selected VQA samples, manually evaluate the relevance of top-4 retrieved pairs and categorize the types of knowledge being retrieved (e.g., similar pathologies, imaging modalities, or clinical contexts).

2. Perform controlled ablation studies: Test the model with varying retrieval counts (0, 1, 2, 4, 8) on each benchmark dataset to determine optimal retrieval strategy and isolate the retrieval-attention module's contribution from other factors.

3. Analyze retrieval failure cases: Identify and analyze VQA samples where retrieval doesn't improve performance, examining whether this stems from poor retrieval quality, inappropriate fusion, or cases where the original model already has sufficient knowledge.
