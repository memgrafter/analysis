# Does Continual Learning Equally Forget All Parameters?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04158
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04158
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Proposes FPF and k-FPF methods that significantly improve continual learning by fine-tuning only task-specific parameters

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of catastrophic forgetting in continual learning by investigating which neural network parameters are most responsible for forgetting. The authors introduce "forgetting prioritized finetuning (FPF)" which identifies and fine-tunes only the task-specific parameters that are most sensitive to distribution shifts. Through extensive experiments, they demonstrate that only a small portion of parameters are task-specific, and fine-tuning these using a small buffer of previous task data can significantly improve continual learning performance while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a novel approach to continual learning that focuses on identifying and fine-tuning only the task-specific parameters most sensitive to distribution shifts. They develop metrics to measure parameter changes between consecutive epochs and tasks, revealing that only a few modules (like the last FC layer and BN layers) are highly sensitive to task shifts. Their FPF method fine-tunes these sensitive parameters using a small buffer of previous task data, while k-FPF further improves efficiency by replacing frequent replay with occasional FPF steps. The approach is evaluated on multiple benchmarks for class- and domain-incremental learning tasks.

## Key Results
- Empirical studies show only 1.13% of parameters are task-specific and sensitive to distribution shifts
- FPF significantly improves performance of various existing CL methods (ER, A-GEM, iCaRL, FDR, DER, DER++) on multiple benchmarks
- k-FPF achieves comparable performance to state-of-the-art methods with much less computational overhead by eliminating every-step replay

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Only a small portion of parameters are task-specific and sensitive to distribution shifts
- Mechanism: The authors propose metrics to measure parameter changes between consecutive epochs and tasks. By analyzing the training dynamics, they observe that only a few parameters (like the last FC layer and BN layers) change drastically between tasks, while most parameters remain stable and can be shared across tasks.
- Core assumption: Catastrophic forgetting is primarily caused by changes in a small subset of task-specific parameters rather than all parameters equally.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our proposed metrics show that only a few modules are more task-specific and sensitively alter between tasks, while others can be shared across tasks as common knowledge."
  - [section 4.2] "From the plots of different neural networks, the last FC layer is more sensitive to task shift than other layers. When BN is included in the network, BN layers' mean and variance become the most changed parameters."
- Break condition: If the distribution shift pattern changes such that previously stable parameters become task-specific, the mechanism would break.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Fine-tuning only sensitive parameters on a small buffer of previous task data can significantly improve continual learning performance
- Mechanism: The authors propose "Forgetting Prioritized Fine-tuning (FPF)" which identifies the most sensitive parameters and fine-tunes them using a small buffer of previous task data. Since only a small number of parameters are fine-tuned, this approach is computationally efficient.
- Core assumption: The sensitive parameters identified through training dynamics analysis are indeed the primary source of forgetting and can be effectively corrected through fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We attribute forgetting mainly to the former and find that fine-tuning them only on a small buffer at the end of any CL method can bring non-trivial improvement."
  - [section 5] "Due to the small number of finetuned parameters, such 'Forgetting Prioritized Fine-tuning (FPF)' is efficient in computation."
- Break condition: If the sensitive parameters change their sensitivity patterns over time or if the buffer becomes insufficient to represent previous tasks adequately.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Replacing every-step replay with occasional fine-tuning (k-FPF) can achieve comparable performance with significantly less computational overhead
- Mechanism: The authors extend FPF to "k-FPF" which eliminates the need for every-step replay by periodically applying FPF k times during training. This approach reduces computational cost while maintaining performance.
- Core assumption: The task-specific parameters don't require constant updates and can be effectively corrected through occasional fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We further propose a more efficient and simpler method that entirely removes the every-step replay and replaces them by only k-times of FPF periodically triggered during CL."
  - [section 5] "k-FPF applies FPF only k times during CL."
- Break condition: If the distribution shifts occur more frequently than the k-FPF intervals, leading to forgetting between fine-tuning steps.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Catastrophic forgetting in neural networks
  - Why needed here: This is the core problem that the paper addresses. Understanding why neural networks forget previous tasks when learning new ones is essential to appreciate the significance of the proposed solution.
  - Quick check question: What happens to a neural network's performance on previous tasks when it's trained on new tasks without any countermeasures?

- Concept: Continual learning (CL) settings
  - Why needed here: The paper focuses on two CL settings - class-incremental learning (class-IL) and domain-incremental learning (domain-IL). Understanding these settings is crucial to grasp the problem context and evaluation metrics.
  - Quick check question: How does class-incremental learning differ from task-incremental learning in terms of available information during inference?

- Concept: Training dynamics analysis
  - Why needed here: The authors' key insight comes from analyzing how different parameters change during training. Understanding how to measure and interpret these dynamics is essential to follow their methodology.
  - Quick check question: What metrics can be used to quantify how much a parameter changes between training epochs or tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Neural network (e.g., ResNet-18, VGG-11) -> Buffer for previous task data -> Fine-tuning mechanism targeting specific parameters
- Critical path: 1) Train on current task using standard method, 2) Measure parameter sensitivity using training dynamics metrics, 3) Identify sensitive parameters, 4) Fine-tune sensitive parameters using buffer data, 5) Deploy model
- Design tradeoffs: The main tradeoff is between performance (fine-tuning more parameters) and efficiency (fine-tuning fewer parameters). The authors show that fine-tuning only 1.13% of parameters can achieve significant improvements.
- Failure signatures: If the buffer is too small to represent previous tasks adequately, or if the sensitivity metrics don't accurately identify the parameters causing forgetting, the method may fail to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Reproduce the training dynamics analysis on a simple dataset (e.g., Seq-MNIST) with a basic architecture (e.g., MLP) to verify that only a few parameters are sensitive to task shifts.
  2. Implement FPF on top of a standard CL method (e.g., ER) and compare its performance with and without FPF to confirm the improvement.
  3. Test k-FPF with different values of k to find the optimal balance between computational efficiency and performance on a validation set.

## Limitations

- The approach relies on buffer data, which may not always be available in strict privacy-constrained scenarios
- The sensitivity metrics might not capture all forms of forgetting, particularly those arising from complex feature space distortions
- The generalizability to more complex architectures like Transformers remains uncertain

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The empirical observation that only a small portion of parameters are task-specific and sensitive to distribution shifts (supported by clear visualizations and consistent patterns across multiple datasets and architectures)
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of FPF in improving existing CL methods (strong results but dependent on buffer quality and size)
- **Medium Confidence**: The computational efficiency claims of k-FPF (while FLOPs are reduced, wall-clock time and memory overhead need more thorough evaluation)

## Next Checks

1. **Architecture Generalization Test**: Apply FPF to a Transformer-based architecture (e.g., ViT) and verify if the same sensitivity patterns hold, particularly examining whether self-attention parameters exhibit similar task-specific behavior.

2. **Buffer Size Sensitivity Analysis**: Systematically vary buffer sizes (from 10 to 1000 samples) and measure the impact on both FPF and k-FPF performance to determine the minimum effective buffer size for different dataset complexities.

3. **Long-term Forgetting Assessment**: Evaluate the method over 20+ tasks to test whether the identified sensitive parameters remain stable and whether k-FPF intervals need adjustment as the number of tasks increases.
