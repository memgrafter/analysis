# Quantifying the robustness of deep multispectral segmentation models against natural perturbations and data poisoning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11347
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11347
- Reference count: 40
- All models are vulnerable to data poisoning attacks with ASR > 90% for 10% poisoned data

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the robustness of deep multispectral segmentation models to both adversarial attacks and natural perturbations. The study focuses on models trained on both RGB and near-infrared (NIR) imagery, exploring early and late fusion approaches. For adversarial robustness, the authors evaluate the models against data poisoning attacks, specifically targeted backdoor attacks during training. They implement two types of attacks: a fine-grained attack and a physically realizable texture attack. For natural robustness, they extend the ImageNet-C common corruptions to multispectral imagery, creating physically realistic snow and fog perturbations. The models are evaluated on the Urban Semantic 3D dataset.

## Method Summary
The study uses DeepLabv3 with ResNet50 backbone, implementing both early and late fusion of RGB+NIR inputs. Data poisoning attacks involve poisoning 10% of training data with fine-grained line/square and texture attacks. Natural perturbations are created by modifying ImageNet-C snow and fog/haze for multispectral data. Models are evaluated on the Urban Semantic 3D dataset, which contains 8-band multispectral imagery (RGB + NIR) and segmentation labels for 7 classes. Performance is measured using mIOU-B, mPA-B, mIOU-A, mPA-A, ASR for adversarial robustness, and mIOU/mPA for natural robustness across 5 corruption severities.

## Key Results
- All models are vulnerable to data poisoning attacks, regardless of input or fusion architecture, with ASR > 90% for 10% poisoned data
- RGB+NIR models show the best performance but also the worst adversarial robustness
- Early and late fusion approaches show no significant difference in adversarial robustness
- Physically realistic perturbations improve model performance compared to unrealistic ones
- RGB+NIR models, especially early fusion, show improved robustness to natural perturbations over RGB-only models, with significant improvement for the foliage class

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper demonstrates that multispectral models combining RGB and NIR data are more susceptible to adversarial attacks due to the increased model complexity and feature space. However, these same models show improved robustness to natural perturbations, likely due to the additional spectral information providing better discrimination between classes under challenging environmental conditions. The fusion approach (early vs late) does not significantly impact adversarial robustness, suggesting that the increased vulnerability stems from the combined input rather than the fusion strategy itself.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Raw US3D data -> Preprocessing (8-bit, tiling) -> Train/Val/Test split -> Data poisoning attack (10% poisoned data) -> Model training (DeepLabv3, ResNet50) -> Evaluation (mIOU-B, mPA-B, mIOU-A, mPA-A, ASR) + Natural perturbations (modified ImageNet-C snow/fog) -> Robustness evaluation (mIOU, mPA)

Critical path: Data poisoning attack and natural perturbations are the critical components for evaluating model robustness. The choice of attack type (fine-grained vs texture) and perturbation (snow vs fog) significantly impacts the results.

Design tradeoffs: Using 10% poisoned data for attacks balances attack success rate and computational cost. Modifying ImageNet-C perturbations for multispectral data introduces domain-specific challenges but improves physical realism.

Failure signatures: Models showing high ASR (>90%) but low mIOU-B indicate successful poisoning attacks. Models with low mIOU under natural perturbations suggest insufficient robustness to environmental conditions.

## Limitations
- Results are based on a single dataset (Urban Semantic 3D) and may not generalize to other multispectral datasets or urban environments
- Only one backbone architecture (ResNet50) and segmentation model (DeepLabv3) are evaluated, limiting the scope of conclusions about multispectral segmentation robustness
- The study focuses on two specific perturbation types (snow and fog) and may not capture the full range of natural perturbations that models may encounter in real-world applications

## Confidence
- All models are vulnerable to data poisoning attacks (ASR > 90% for 10% poisoned data): Medium-High
- RGB+NIR models show the worst adversarial robustness: Medium
- Physically realistic perturbations improve model performance: Medium-Low
