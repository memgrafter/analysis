# Enriching Neural Network Training Dataset to Improve Worst-Case Performance Guarantees

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13228
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13228
- Reference count: 32
- One-line primary result: Up to 80% reduction in worst-case violations through dataset enrichment for AC-OPF neural networks

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel approach to improve neural network worst-case performance for AC-Optimal Power Flow problems by enriching the training dataset with critical datapoints. The method identifies worst-case constraint violations using mixed-integer linear programming, then samples additional points from the surrounding region to create a more robust training set. Tested on 39-, 57-, 118-, and 162-bus systems, the approach significantly reduces worst-case violations while maintaining average prediction accuracy, addressing a key challenge in deploying neural networks for safety-critical power system applications.

## Method Summary
The algorithm uses physics-informed neural networks (PFNN) trained with both prediction error and power flow constraint violations in the loss function. During training, mixed-integer linear programming identifies input combinations causing the largest generation constraint violations. The method then samples 1,000 new points from a hypercube region around these critical datapoints, adding them to the training set at regular intervals (every 200 iterations). This dynamic dataset enrichment process allows the neural network to learn from its worst-case failure modes, substantially reducing the maximum violation across the input domain.

## Key Results
- Up to 80% reduction in worst-case violations compared to standard power-flow-informed neural networks
- Maintains similar average prediction accuracy while improving worst-case guarantees
- Reduces the region of potential violations, making predictions safer across the input domain
- Successfully demonstrated on 39-, 57-, 118-, and 162-bus test systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Identifying worst-case constraint violations allows targeting the most critical failure modes of the neural network.
- Mechanism: The algorithm uses mixed-integer linear programming to find input combinations that cause the largest generation constraint violations, then samples new data points around those inputs to enrich the training set.
- Core assumption: The worst-case violation points are representative of a broader region of problematic inputs that can be captured through hypercube sampling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method uses mixed-integer linear programming to find inputs causing largest generation constraint violations, then samples additional points from the surrounding region"
  - [section III.A] "The MILP optimization problem for identifying the specific input combination that could lead to a large generation constraint violation"
- Break condition: If the worst-case violation region is non-convex or highly irregular, hypercube sampling may miss critical areas.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Enriching the training dataset during training improves worst-case performance more effectively than using a fixed dataset.
- Mechanism: By adding critical datapoints that cause violations at regular intervals during training, the neural network learns to handle these difficult cases better, reducing overall worst-case violations.
- Core assumption: The additional datapoints added during training provide meaningful gradient signals that improve generalization to similar inputs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "adapting the NN training dataset during training can improve the NN performance and substantially reduce its worst-case violations"
  - [section III] "identifies and enriches the training dataset with critical datapoints that reduce the worst-case violations"
- Break condition: If the sampling rate is too low or the new points are not sufficiently diverse, the improvement may be minimal.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Power-flow-informed neural networks combined with dataset enrichment provide better worst-case guarantees than standard training approaches.
- Mechanism: The loss function includes both prediction error and power flow constraint violations, and the enriched dataset specifically targets regions where constraints are violated, leading to improved worst-case performance.
- Core assumption: The power-flow constraint violations are well-calibrated and accurately reflect real-world operational constraints.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section II.C] "we can use a power-flow-informed NN (PFNN) similar to the ones proposed in [6], and our previous work [11] to improve the generalization capability of the NN"
  - [section IV.A] "we can see in Fig. 3 that in all cases, the proposed NN dataset enriching algorithms (WC-PFNN) result in a steep reduction in worst-case guarantees"
- Break condition: If the constraint formulation is too loose or too tight, the training may not generalize well to actual operational scenarios.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness depends on the assumption that hypercube sampling adequately captures the distribution of problematic inputs
- Computational overhead of running MILP optimization during training could be prohibitive for very large systems
- The method's performance on non-convex violation spaces may be limited
