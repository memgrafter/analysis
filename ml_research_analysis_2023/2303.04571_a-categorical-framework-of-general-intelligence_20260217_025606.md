# A Categorical Framework of General Intelligence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04571
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04571

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a categorical framework for general intelligence that uses category theory to formalize intelligence as a computational process. The framework consists of four core components: a sensor for multi-modal input, a world category for structured representation, a planner with objectives, and an actor for output generation. Central to the approach is the use of presheaves and limits to represent complex concepts and self-state awareness, with self-state awareness defined as a categorical analogue to self-consciousness. The framework proposes using diagrams for precise communication between agents and emphasizes invariance properties derived from category theory as training signals to ensure consistency and alignment with human values.

## Method Summary
The framework proposes a four-component architecture where a sensor processes multi-modal inputs (text, image, audio, touch) and maps them to a world category using neural networks. The world category employs presheaves and limits to represent objects and concepts, with self-state awareness defined as a categorical analogue to self-consciousness. Two algorithms are introduced for learning and evaluating self-state awareness. The planner generates objectives based on self-state, and the actor produces outputs. The framework uses diagrams (functors from indexing categories to the world category) as a universal communication medium, enabling precise representation of thoughts without the ambiguity of natural language. Invariance properties derived from category theory serve as training signals to maintain consistency and alignment with human values.

## Key Results
- Introduces self-state awareness as a categorical analogue to self-consciousness with associated learning and evaluation algorithms
- Proposes diagrams as a universal communication medium that precisely captures context without natural language ambiguity
- Demonstrates how invariance properties from category theory can serve as training signals for consistency and human alignment
- Provides a mathematically rigorous foundation for understanding general intelligence through category theory

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Sensor: Multi-modal input processor (text, image, audio, touch, etc.)
  - World Category: Functor W: N → Cat representing the dynamic world model
  - Planner: Objective-driven decision maker with access to self-state
  - Actor: Output generator (text, actions, etc.)
  - Key subsystems: Presheaf representation layer, diagram communication module, consistency enforcement system

- Critical path: Sensor → World Category update → Planner with self-state awareness → Actor output, with continuous consistency maintenance

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Expressiveness vs. computational efficiency in categorical representations
  - Precision of diagrams vs. practical usability for human interaction
  - Strong mathematical guarantees vs. flexibility to handle edge cases

- Failure signatures:
  - Inconsistent representations across time steps (violated invariance)
  - Poor self-state awareness scores from the evaluation algorithms
  - Communication failures when agents have different world categories
  - Planner generating objectives that conflict with human values
