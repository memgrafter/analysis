# An Experimental Study of Byzantine-Robust Aggregation Schemes in Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.07173
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07173
- **Reference count**: 40
- **One-line primary result**: Byzantine-robust aggregation schemes show limited robustness, especially with Non-IID data.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an experimental study of Byzantine-robust aggregation schemes in federated learning, evaluating eight different aggregation methods against five representative Byzantine attacks. The authors propose a new ClippedClustering scheme that combines clustering with adaptive clipping to defend against attacks that manipulate update magnitudes. Through experiments on MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets with both IID and Non-IID partitions, the study reveals that existing aggregation schemes struggle with Non-IID data distributions, while the proposed ClippedClustering scheme shows promise in IID settings but still faces challenges with heterogeneous data.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates eight Byzantine-robust aggregation schemes (Mean, Krum, GeoMed, AutoGM, Median, TrimmedMean, CC, Clustering, and ClippedClustering) against five attack types (Noise, ALIE, IPM, SF, LF) using MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets. Experiments are conducted with both IID and Non-IID data partitions across 20 clients with 5 Byzantine attackers. The paper uses FedSGD and FedAvg algorithms with learning rate decay, batch sizes of 128/64, and 50 local SGD steps for FedAvg. Test accuracy is measured across 6000 and 600 communication rounds for MNIST and CIFAR-10 respectively.

## Key Results
- Existing Byzantine-robust aggregation schemes show limited effectiveness against attacks, particularly with Non-IID data distributions
- ClippedClustering successfully defends against most attacks under IID local datasets but struggles with Non-IID data
- All aggregation schemes achieve poor performance when data is Non-IID across clients
- The study highlights the need for new defense strategies specifically designed for Non-IID federated learning scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
ClippedClustering prevents Byzantine clients from exploiting clustering by amplifying updates beyond a threshold. Updates are clipped to a maximum norm (τ) before clustering, limiting malicious clients' ability to manipulate clustering outcomes through magnitude. Core assumption: malicious clients attempt to amplify updates to influence clustering, and benign updates have bounded norms. Break condition: if malicious clients can control the median norm of updates, they can adjust τ to allow amplification.

### Mechanism 2
Clustering-based aggregation groups updates by direction similarity, isolating malicious updates that deviate in direction. Cosine similarity is used to group updates into clusters, with the largest cluster (assumed benign) aggregated using mean. Core assumption: the majority of updates are benign and share similar directions, while malicious updates will be outliers in direction. Break condition: if malicious clients can align their updates with the largest benign cluster, they can evade detection.

### Mechanism 3
Adaptive clipping threshold (τ) based on median update norm provides robustness against varying attack magnitudes. τ is set to the median of historical update norms, making it adaptive to benign clients' behavior and resilient to outlier magnitudes. Core assumption: benign clients' update norms are relatively stable and representative of the majority. Break condition: if malicious clients can control the median norm, they can influence the clipping threshold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Server broadcasts global model to clients -> Clients perform local training and upload updates -> Server applies aggregation scheme to combine updates -> Server updates global model and broadcasts to clients

- **Critical path**: 
  1. Server broadcasts global model to clients
  2. Clients perform local training and upload updates
  3. Server applies aggregation scheme to combine updates
  4. Server updates global model and broadcasts to clients

- **Design tradeoffs**: 
  - Robustness vs. Accuracy: More robust schemes may sacrifice some accuracy in benign scenarios
  - Computational Cost: Some schemes (e.g., AutoGM) require iterative optimization
  - Communication Efficiency: Schemes that require less communication are preferred

- **Failure signatures**:
  - High variance in update directions among benign clients
  - Malicious clients successfully manipulating clustering or aggregation
  - Non-IID data causing poor performance of certain aggregation schemes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the ClippedClustering scheme perform against adaptive attacks that specifically target the clipping mechanism? The authors propose an adaptive attack on ClippedClustering but the scheme shows less robustness with Non-IID data. Further experiments testing against a wider range of adaptive attacks would provide more insight.

### Open Question 2
How does the performance of Byzantine-robust aggregation schemes vary with different neural network architectures and datasets? The paper evaluates schemes on CIFAR-10 and MNIST using specific architectures, but performance may vary with different complexities and characteristics. Evaluating across diverse architectures and datasets would help understand generalizability.

### Open Question 3
How does the batch size affect the robustness of Byzantine-robust aggregation schemes in FedAvg? While the authors mention batch size improves robustness in FedSGD, they don't explore this for FedAvg. Conducting experiments to evaluate batch size impact on FedAvg would provide valuable insights.

## Limitations
- Limited experimental scope with only two datasets (MNIST and CIFAR-10) and fixed architectures
- Adaptive clipping mechanism relies on historical norm statistics that may not generalize to dynamic attack scenarios
- Clustering-based schemes assume benign updates share similar directions, which may not hold in real-world heterogeneous environments

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The experimental framework and methodology are well-established and reproducible
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of ClippedClustering against IID attacks, supported by empirical results
- **Low Confidence**: The scalability of findings to larger, more complex models and real-world federated learning scenarios
