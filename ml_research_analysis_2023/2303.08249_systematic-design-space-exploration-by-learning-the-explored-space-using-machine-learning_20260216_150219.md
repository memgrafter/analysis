# Systematic design space exploration by learning the explored space using Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.08249
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08249
- Reference count: 40
- Proposed a machine learning-based method for systematic design space exploration to avoid redundant sample selection.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a systematic design space exploration method that leverages machine learning to track explored regions and avoid redundant sampling. The approach uses a modified robust random-cut forest to learn the geometry of explored space, iteratively identifying peripheral points and sampling new points from hyper-balls centered at these peripheries. The exploration density is controlled by a hyperparameter ε. Experiments in 2D Euclidean space demonstrate the method's ability to systematically explore the design space without sample collision, with performance remaining consistent over 2000 iterations with 50 samples per iteration.

## Method Summary
The method combines machine learning with heuristic approaches to systematically explore design spaces. It uses a modified robust random-cut forest algorithm to learn the geometry of already explored regions in the design space. The core mechanism involves iteratively identifying peripheral points in the explored space and sampling new points from hyper-balls centered at these peripheries. The radius of these hyper-balls is controlled by a hyperparameter ε, which determines the exploration density. Larger ε values result in sparser exploration. The approach is designed to be generic and extendable to higher dimensions, though current experiments are limited to 2D Euclidean space.

## Key Results
- Demonstrated effective systematic exploration in 2D Euclidean space with 2000 iterations and 50 samples per iteration
- Successfully avoided sample collision through tracking of explored regions
- Showed controllable exploration density through ε hyperparameter, with larger values producing sparser exploration
- Method is generic and extendable to higher dimensions (though not experimentally validated)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by learning the geometry of explored regions using a modified robust random-cut forest, which provides a data structure to efficiently identify peripheral points in the explored space. By sampling new points from hyper-balls centered at these peripheries with radius controlled by ε, the approach ensures systematic coverage of the design space while avoiding redundancy. The random-cut forest modification likely improves robustness to sampling density variations, enabling reliable peripheral point detection even as the explored region grows.

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: Data acquisition -> Modified robust random-cut forest -> Peripheral point detection -> Hyper-ball sampling -> New sample generation
- **Critical path**: The random-cut forest construction and peripheral point detection are the most computationally intensive steps that determine exploration efficiency
- **Design tradeoffs**: Larger ε increases exploration coverage but risks missing fine details; smaller ε provides thorough coverage but increases computational cost and potential redundancy
- **Failure signatures**: Performance degradation in high dimensions due to curse of dimensionality; poor peripheral detection when explored regions become highly irregular
