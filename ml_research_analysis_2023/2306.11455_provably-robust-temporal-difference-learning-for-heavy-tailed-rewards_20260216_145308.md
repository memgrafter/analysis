# Provably Robust Temporal Difference Learning for Heavy-Tailed Rewards

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11455
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11455
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Provably robust TD learning and NAC algorithms for heavy-tailed rewards with finite (1+p)-th moments

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of achieving robust value prediction and policy optimization in reinforcement learning when rewards follow heavy-tailed distributions. The authors propose a robust temporal difference learning algorithm enhanced with dynamic gradient clipping, which effectively mitigates the impact of statistical outliers and ensures convergence even under heavy-tailed rewards with infinite variance. By carefully selecting clipping radii, the method achieves a favorable bias-variance tradeoff, leading to near-optimal solutions. The paper provides theoretical guarantees, including sample complexity bounds of order O(ε^(-1/p)) for TD learning and O(ε^(-4-2/p)) for a robust natural actor-critic variant, both under heavy-tailed rewards with finite moments of order (1+p) for some p∈(0,1].

## Method Summary
The paper introduces robust temporal difference learning with dynamic gradient clipping to handle heavy-tailed rewards in reinforcement learning. The core mechanism involves clipping gradients at each iteration to a radius that grows slowly with time, preventing extreme values from dominating updates. This introduces a controlled bias that vanishes as iterations increase, trading variance for bias. The authors prove convergence using Lyapunov drift analysis with carefully chosen clipping radii and step sizes, achieving sample complexity bounds of O(ε^(-1/p)) for TD learning and O(ε^(-4-2/p)) for robust natural actor-critic variants. The method assumes Markov chains are uniformly ergodic and feature matrices satisfy rank conditions.

## Key Results
- Robust TD learning achieves sample complexity of O(ε^(-1/p)) with full-rank features and O(ε^(-1-1/p)) without, under heavy-tailed rewards with finite (1+p)-th moments
- Robust NAC variant achieves sample complexity of O(ε^(-4-2/p))
- Dynamic gradient clipping effectively eliminates impact of heavy-tailed statistical outliers in stochastic gradients
- Theoretical guarantees validated through numerical experiments on synthetic MRPs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Dynamic gradient clipping can eliminate the impact of heavy-tailed statistical outliers in stochastic gradients, enabling robust convergence even when variance is infinite.
- **Mechanism**: At each iteration, gradients are clipped to a radius b_t that grows slowly with t, preventing extreme values from dominating the update. This introduces a controlled bias that vanishes as t increases, trading off variance for bias.
- **Core assumption**: The clipped gradient norm remains bounded in expectation, specifically E[||g_t(Θ(t))||^(1+p)] ≤ u < ∞ under finite (1+p)-th moments.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "favorable tradeoff between bias and variability of the stochastic gradients can be achieved with this dynamic gradient clipping mechanism"
  - [section]: Lemma 1 establishes the tail bounds on gradient norms under clipping
  - [corpus]: Related work on gradient clipping for heavy-tailed SGD (e.g., "From Gradient Clipping to Normalization for Heavy Tailed SGD") supports this approach
- **Break condition**: If the clipping radius b_t grows too slowly, bias accumulates; if too quickly, variance is not sufficiently controlled.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Lyapunov drift analysis with carefully chosen clipping radii and step sizes ensures that the expected error decays at rates O(T^(-p/(1+p))) or O(T^(-p)).
- **Mechanism**: The Lyapunov function L(Θ) = ||Θ - Θ*||_2^2 is used to bound the expected squared error. The drift is decomposed into terms representing progress toward the optimum, bias from clipping, and variance. By controlling these terms through b_t and η_t, convergence is achieved.
- **Core assumption**: The Markov chain is uniformly ergodic and the feature matrix satisfies the required rank conditions for the specific rate.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "we prove that robust versions of TD learning achieve sample complexities of order O(ε^(-1/p)) and O(ε^(-1-1/p)) with and without the full-rank assumption"
  - [section]: Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 provide the formal bounds
  - [corpus]: Weak (no direct evidence in corpus)
- **Break condition**: If the Markov chain is not uniformly ergodic or the feature matrix is rank-deficient beyond the analysis scope.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: The robust NAC algorithm, built on top of robust TD learning, inherits the robustness to heavy-tailed rewards and achieves a sample complexity of O(ε^(-4-2/p)).
- **Mechanism**: Natural policy gradient updates are computed using the robust TD critic. The Lyapunov analysis extends to the policy optimization setting, with concentrability coefficients controlling the bias from function approximation.
- **Core assumption**: The concentrability coefficient C_conc is finite and the feature vectors are bounded.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We show that a robust variant of NAC based on Robust TD learning achieves O(ε^(-4-2/p)) sample complexity"
  - [section]: Theorem 4 provides the formal bounds for robust NAC
  - [corpus]: Weak (no direct evidence in corpus)
- **Break condition**: If the concentrability coefficient is too large or the feature approximation error is unbounded.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Environment -> Robust TD critic -> Robust NAC policy optimizer
- **Critical path**:
  1. Sample state, action, reward, next state transitions from the environment
  2. Compute TD error and gradient with dynamic clipping
  3. Update value function parameters using clipped gradient
  4. For NAC, compute natural policy gradient using robust TD critic
  5. Update policy parameters

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Clipping radius growth rate vs. bias-variance tradeoff
  - Step size decay vs. convergence speed
  - Projection radius vs. stability
  - IID vs. Markovian sampling assumptions

- **Failure signatures**:
  - If clipping radius is too small: Algorithm converges slowly or to a suboptimal solution
  - If step size is too large: Algorithm becomes unstable or oscillates
  - If concentrability coefficient is too large: Policy optimization performance degrades

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Implement robust TD learning on a simple MRP with heavy-tailed rewards and compare to standard TD learning
  2. Vary the clipping radius growth rate and observe its effect on convergence
  3. Implement robust NAC on a small MDP and compare its performance to standard NAC

## Limitations

- Theoretical analysis assumes Markov chains are uniformly ergodic, which may not hold for all MDPs
- Numerical experiments limited to synthetic MRPs with specific heavy-tailed reward distributions
- Clipping mechanism introduces bias that vanishes asymptotically, but finite-time tradeoff is sensitive to parameters

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The Lyapunov drift analysis framework and its application to prove convergence under heavy-tailed rewards (Mechanisms 1 and 2)
- **Medium confidence**: The extension of robust TD to robust NAC and the associated sample complexity bounds (Mechanism 3)
- **Low confidence**: The practical effectiveness of the proposed algorithms on complex, real-world RL problems with heavy-tailed rewards
