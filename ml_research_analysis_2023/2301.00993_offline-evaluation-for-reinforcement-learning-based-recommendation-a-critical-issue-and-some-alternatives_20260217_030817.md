# Offline Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning-based Recommendation: A Critical Issue and Some Alternatives

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00993
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00993
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: The common next-item prediction (NIP) evaluation protocol is unsuitable for offline evaluation of RL-based recommender systems due to three major issues

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies critical flaws in the next-item prediction (NIP) protocol commonly used to evaluate offline reinforcement learning (RL) based recommender systems. The authors argue that NIP fails to capture the true objectives of RL, which aims to optimize long-term user value through sequential decision-making rather than simply predicting immediate user interactions. They demonstrate how NIP's binary target formulation encourages models to replicate the logging policy rather than discover optimal policies, and how it can mask the optimizer's curse that leads to catastrophic failures when RL agents select out-of-distribution items with overestimated values.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes the NIP evaluation protocol used for offline RL-based recommendation systems. NIP evaluates whether the next item a user interacts with appears in the top-k recommendations. The authors systematically identify three fundamental problems with this approach: (1) it only measures short-term accuracy without accounting for long-term causal effects, (2) it treats the next interacted item as a binary target rather than a scalar reward to be maximized, incentivizing policy replication, and (3) it hides the optimizer's curse where out-of-distribution items are overestimated and catastrophically selected. The paper suggests alternative evaluation approaches including online evaluation, counterfactual off-policy evaluation, simulator-based evaluation, intermediate evaluation, and uncertainty-aware evaluation.

## Key Results
- NIP only measures short-term accuracy, ignoring the causal effects of recommendations on user behavior
- NIP incentivizes models to replicate the logging policy instead of finding optimal policies by treating next-item prediction as a binary target
- NIP can hide critical deficiencies of offline RL agents, particularly the optimizer's curse leading to catastrophic failures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: NIP fails to measure long-term user value because it treats next-item prediction as a static one-shot task.
- Mechanism: By rewarding only the immediate next interaction, NIP ignores the sequential causal effects of recommendations on user state, which is the core problem RL is designed to optimize.
- Core assumption: RL's value function is defined over full trajectories, not individual transitions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "NIP only measures short-term accuracy, ignoring the causal effect of recommendations on the user and missing the potential long-term benefits of reinforcement learning (RL)"
  - [section] "it cannot reflect the potential benefits that reinforcement learning (RL) is expected to bring while it hides critical deficiencies of certain offline RL agents"
  - [corpus] Weak: neighbor papers focus on RL-based recommendation improvements, not evaluation protocol critiques
- Break condition: If evaluation includes full-trajectory reward estimates rather than just next-item ranking.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: NIP encourages replication of the logging policy instead of discovering optimal policies.
- Mechanism: By treating the next interacted item as a binary target rather than a scalar reward, NIP incentivizes models to mimic the implicit logging policy that generated the data, rather than maximize expected return.
- Core assumption: The logging policy introduces selection bias that makes observed interactions suboptimal.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "it treats the next interacted item as a binary target rather than a scalar reward to maximize"
  - [section] "By considering the fact that selecting the interacted item is a binary target, instead of a scalar reward to be maximized, the NIP evaluation incentivizes researchers and practitioners to build policies that are close to the (implicit) logging policy"
  - [corpus] Weak: no direct corpus evidence addressing this specific mechanism
- Break condition: When evaluation metric shifts from binary hit/miss to cumulative reward maximization.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: NIP hides the optimizer's curse by masking overestimation of out-of-distribution items.
- Mechanism: NIP evaluates ranking performance where the true next item is always present in the candidate set, but RL agents in production select only their top item. This masks the risk that out-of-distribution items are drastically overestimated and catastrophically selected.
- Core assumption: The deadly triad conditions (bootstrapping + function approximation + off-policy learning) amplify small Q-value errors into catastrophic policy failures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "it can hide critical deficiencies of offline RL agents, particularly the optimizer's curse, where out-of-distribution items are overestimated and selected, leading to catastrophic failures at deployment"
  - [section] "a particular set of conditions has been identified to cause a catastrophic impact of the optimizer's curse and is often called the deadly triad"
  - [corpus] Weak: corpus neighbors focus on RL-based recommendation improvements, not evaluation protocol failures
- Break condition: When evaluation explicitly tests for out-of-distribution item selection and value overestimation.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Markov Decision Process (MDP)
  - Why needed here: RL-based recommendation models the problem as an MDP where states represent user contexts, actions are recommendations, and rewards capture user engagement over time.
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes an MDP from a bandit problem in the context of recommendation?

- Concept: Off-policy learning and distribution shift
  - Why needed here: Offline RL trains on data from a different policy (logging policy), creating a distribution shift between training and deployment that can cause overestimation and failure.
  - Quick check question: How does the support of the logging policy differ from the support of the target policy in offline RL?

- Concept: Counterfactual evaluation and inverse propensity scoring
  - Why needed here: These techniques attempt to estimate policy performance from logged data by reweighting observed rewards, addressing the selection bias inherent in offline evaluation.
  - Quick check question: What is the fundamental trade-off between bias and variance in inverse propensity scoring?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: User interaction data -> Policy training -> Offline evaluation (NIP) -> Online deployment -> Performance assessment
- Critical path: Logged interaction data -> Policy training -> Offline evaluation -> Risk quantification -> Deployment decision
- Design tradeoffs: NIP offers computational efficiency and reproducibility but sacrifices ecological validity; counterfactual methods reduce bias but increase variance; simulators offer controlled testing but may not reflect reality.
- Failure signatures: High NIP metrics but poor online performance; top recommendations frequently include out-of-distribution items; policy performance degrades when evaluated on in-support vs out-of-support actions.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare NIP metrics with counterfactual OPE estimates on the same trained policy to identify discrepancies
  2. Test the policy's performance when constrained to in-support actions only versus unrestricted action selection
  3. Simulate deployment scenarios where the policy must handle out-of-distribution user states and measure performance degradation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we design an offline evaluation protocol that captures both short-term accuracy and long-term user behavior effects in RL-based recommender systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors argue that NIP only accounts for short-term rewards and ignores the causal effect of recommendations on the user, while also failing to capture long-term outcomes induced by sequential decision-making.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a fundamental limitation of NIP but doesn't provide a complete solution that addresses both aspects simultaneously.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of an evaluation protocol that demonstrates correlation with both short-term accuracy metrics and long-term user behavior changes in offline settings, ideally showing better alignment with online performance than NIP.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective methods to detect and mitigate the optimizer's curse in offline RL-based recommendation systems before deployment?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors highlight that NIP cannot detect the optimizer's curse, where out-of-distribution items are overestimated and selected, leading to catastrophic failures at deployment.
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions uncertainty-aware evaluation as a potential approach, it doesn't provide concrete methods or validation of their effectiveness in the recommendation context.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different uncertainty quantification methods and their ability to predict and prevent catastrophic failures in deployed RL-based recommenders.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we define and measure "in-support" versus "out-of-support" actions in non-tabular settings for RL-based recommendation systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors suggest uncertainty-aware evaluation focusing on in-support actions but note that defining distance measures between the support of logging and target policies is not trivial in non-tabular settings.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as an open question but doesn't provide specific methodologies or validation approaches for the recommendation scenario.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of practical metrics for determining action support boundaries in high-dimensional item spaces, with demonstrated effectiveness in improving offline-to-online performance transfer.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks systematic empirical studies comparing NIP evaluation with alternative protocols across multiple RL algorithms and datasets
- Difficulty in quantifying the severity of identified issues without extensive online testing
- Does not provide concrete guidelines for when NIP evaluation might still be appropriate or how to combine it with other evaluation methods

## Confidence

The core critique of NIP evaluation for offline RL-based recommenders is **highly confident** (High), as it directly addresses the fundamental mismatch between evaluation metrics and the optimization objectives of RL. The mechanism linking NIP's binary target formulation to policy replication of logging behavior is **moderately confident** (Medium), supported by theoretical arguments but lacking direct empirical validation in the corpus. The claims about NIP masking the optimizer's curse and catastrophic failures are **lower confidence** (Low) due to limited empirical evidence and the complexity of isolating these effects from other confounding factors in real-world deployment scenarios.
