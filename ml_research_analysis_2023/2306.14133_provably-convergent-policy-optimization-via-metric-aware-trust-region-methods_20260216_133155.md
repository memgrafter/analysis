# Provably Convergent Policy Optimization via Metric-aware Trust Region Methods

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.14133
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14133
- Reference count: 40

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces two novel trust region policy optimization methods, Wasserstein Policy Optimization (WPO) and Sinkhorn Policy Optimization (SPO), which utilize Wasserstein and Sinkhorn divergences to measure policy proximity, respectively. Unlike traditional methods that restrict policies to parametric distributions, these methods directly optimize the policy distribution and derive closed-form policy updates based on Lagrangian duality. Theoretically, the authors prove that WPO guarantees monotonic performance improvement, and SPO converges to WPO as the entropic regularizer diminishes. Moreover, with a decaying Lagrangian multiplier, both methods converge to global optimality, and with a constant multiplier, they converge linearly up to a neighborhood of the optimal value. Experiments on tabular domains, robotic locomotion, and continuous control tasks demonstrate the performance improvement of both approaches, with WPO being more robust to sample insufficiency and SPO achieving faster convergence compared to state-of-the-art policy gradient methods.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two trust region policy optimization methods: Wasserstein Policy Optimization (WPO) and Sinkhorn Policy Optimization (SPO). Both methods use Wasserstein and Sinkhorn divergences as policy proximity measures instead of the traditional KL divergence. The key innovation is deriving closed-form policy updates by solving the constrained optimization problems using Lagrangian duality. WPO directly optimizes the policy distribution using Wasserstein distance as the trust region constraint, while SPO uses Sinkhorn divergence which approximates Wasserstein distance with entropic regularization. Both methods are implemented as practical on-policy actor-critic algorithms that combine with advantage function estimation.

## Key Results
- WPO guarantees monotonic performance improvement through trust region constraints
- SPO provably converges to WPO as the entropic regularizer diminishes
- With decaying Lagrangian multiplier, both methods converge to global optimality with geometric convergence rate
- Experiments demonstrate WPO's robustness to sample insufficiency and SPO's faster convergence compared to TRPO and PPO

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: WPO guarantees monotonic performance improvement through trust region constraints.
- Mechanism: By formulating the policy update as a constrained optimization over the Wasserstein distance, WPO ensures each policy iteration improves the expected return without violating the trust region bound. The Lagrangian dual formulation explicitly bounds the policy deviation via βδ.
- Core assumption: Advantage function estimates are bounded and the true advantage function is accessible or can be estimated with bounded error.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Theoretically, we show that WPO guarantees a monotonic performance improvement"
  - [section 5] Theorem 4 shows the improvement bound includes a term proportional to βk and a bounded estimation error term -2ϵ/(1-γ)
- Break condition: If the advantage function estimation error exceeds the trust region threshold, the monotonic guarantee no longer holds.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SPO converges to WPO as the entropic regularization parameter λ increases.
- Mechanism: Sinkhorn divergence approximates Wasserstein distance by adding entropy regularization. As λ → ∞, the entropic term vanishes and the optimal policy update converges to the Wasserstein policy update. This is proven through uniform convergence of the dual objectives.
- Core assumption: The cost matrix D has finite entries and the advantage function remains bounded.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SPO provably converges to WPO as the entropic regularizer diminishes"
  - [section 4] Theorem 3 proves uniform convergence of the Sinkhorn dual to the Wasserstein dual
- Break condition: If λ grows too slowly relative to the problem scale, the approximation error may prevent practical convergence.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Both WPO and SPO achieve global convergence with decaying Lagrangian multipliers.
- Mechanism: The trust region constraint is gradually relaxed by decaying βk, allowing exploration of larger policy changes while maintaining convergence guarantees. This creates a homotopy path from the initial policy to the global optimum.
- Core assumption: The MDP has finite state and action spaces, and rewards are non-negative.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "with a decaying Lagrangian multiplier to the trust region constraint, both methods converge to global optimality"
  - [section 5] Theorem 5 proves geometric convergence with rate γ and shows limk→∞J(πk) = J⋆
- Break condition: If βk decays too quickly, the algorithm may get stuck in local optima; if too slowly, convergence may be unnecessarily slow.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the theoretical guarantees for the convergence of WPO and SPO in the function approximation regime?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that extending convergence theory to function approximation is beyond the scope of their current work, but remains an interesting open question.
- Why unresolved: The theoretical analysis provided in the paper focuses on tabular domains, and extending these results to settings with function approximation (e.g., neural networks) requires different techniques and assumptions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis proving convergence rates and optimality gaps for WPO and SPO when using function approximation methods (e.g., neural networks) to represent policies and value functions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do WPO and SPO perform in off-policy settings compared to on-policy settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that extending the methods to off-policy frameworks remains an interesting direction for future work.
- Why unresolved: The proposed algorithms and theoretical analysis are presented in the context of on-policy learning, and the challenges and benefits of applying WPO/SPO in off-policy settings are not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results comparing the sample efficiency, convergence speed, and final performance of WPO and SPO in both on-policy and off-policy settings, along with any necessary algorithmic modifications to adapt the methods to off-policy learning.

## Limitations
- Theoretical guarantees rely on exact advantage function computation or bounded estimation error, which may not hold in practice with finite samples
- The paper assumes finite MDPs for theoretical convergence proofs, limiting direct applicability to continuous state/action spaces
- Performance comparison with state-of-the-art methods is limited to a relatively small set of environments
- Implementation details for complex environments like MuJoCo are not fully specified, potentially affecting reproducibility
