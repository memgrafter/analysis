# Cross-domain recommendation via user interest alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11467
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11467
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: COAST achieves up to 10.2% improvement in Hit Ratio and 6.4% in NDCG over state-of-the-art methods

## Executive Summary
COAST addresses fundamental challenges in cross-domain recommendation systems including data sparsity, cold-start problems, and poor handling of overlapping users. The framework introduces a unified heterogeneous graph that jointly models user-item interactions across domains, then employs graph convolutional networks with improved message passing to capture cross-domain similarities. Through contrastive learning and gradient alignment, COAST enforces consistency of overlapping users' interest distributions across domains. Extensive experiments on Douban and industrial datasets demonstrate consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art methods, with robust performance even at low overlap ratios of 25%.

## Method Summary
COAST introduces a unified heterogeneous graph to model user-item interactions across multiple domains simultaneously, addressing the limitation of separate modeling approaches that cannot capture cross-domain similarities. The framework employs graph convolutional networks with an improved message passing mechanism to propagate and aggregate information across the heterogeneous graph, capturing high-order cross-domain similarities. To align user interests across domains, COAST uses contrastive learning to pull together representations of overlapping users from different domains while pushing apart non-overlapping ones. Additionally, gradient alignment is applied to ensure consistency in the interest distributions of overlapping users across domains. The approach is evaluated on two real-world datasets (Douban and industrial data) and demonstrates significant improvements in recommendation quality metrics compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

## Key Results
- COAST achieves up to 10.2% improvement in Hit Ratio and 6.4% in NDCG over state-of-the-art methods
- Robust performance maintained even at low overlap ratios (25% of users)
- Consistent outperformance across both Douban and industrial datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The unified heterogeneous graph modeling allows COAST to capture interactions that span multiple domains, creating natural bridges for knowledge transfer. The GCN-based message passing mechanism effectively propagates information across these bridges, enabling the model to learn representations that reflect cross-domain patterns. The contrastive learning component explicitly enforces similarity between overlapping users' representations across domains while maintaining distinctiveness for non-overlapping users. The gradient alignment further strengthens this by ensuring that the optimization process maintains consistency in how overlapping users are represented across domains. This multi-faceted approach addresses both the structural and semantic challenges of cross-domain recommendation.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data Preprocessing -> Unified Heterogeneous Graph Construction -> GCN with Message Passing -> User Interest Alignment (Contrastive Learning + Gradient Alignment) -> Recommendation Generation

**Critical Path**: The core workflow begins with constructing a unified heterogeneous graph from user-item interactions across domains, followed by GCN-based message passing to learn node representations, then applying user interest alignment through contrastive learning and gradient alignment to ensure consistency for overlapping users, and finally generating recommendations based on the aligned representations.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The unified graph approach trades increased model complexity and computational cost for the ability to capture cross-domain relationships. While separate domain modeling would be simpler and faster, it would miss the valuable information transfer that COAST enables. The use of both contrastive learning and gradient alignment adds training complexity but provides complementary mechanisms for ensuring interest alignment.

**Failure Signatures**: The framework may struggle when domains are highly dissimilar with little transferable patterns, when overlap ratios are extremely low (below 25%), or when user behavior patterns differ significantly across domains. Computational scalability could become an issue with very large datasets due to the GCN operations and contrastive learning components.

**Three First Experiments**:
1. Test performance degradation as overlap ratio decreases from 50% to 10% to establish practical limits
2. Evaluate ablation of the contrastive learning component to measure its individual contribution
3. Compare performance with and without gradient alignment to verify its necessity

## Limitations
- Framework relies on overlapping users as bridges between domains, though performance remains robust at 25% overlap
- Cross-domain interest alignment assumptions may not hold for highly dissimilar domains
- Computational complexity of GCN-based approach with contrastive learning may limit scalability
