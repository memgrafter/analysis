# Dataset of Natural Language Queries for E-commerce
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06355
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06355
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Novel dataset of 3,540 natural language queries for e-commerce product search with detailed annotations

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel dataset of 3,540 natural language queries for e-commerce product search, focusing on laptops and jackets. Unlike existing datasets, these queries were collected through controlled experiments with native English speakers, ensuring natural and unbiased information needs. The dataset includes detailed annotations of key product facts and vague terms for 1,754 laptop queries and 363 jacket queries, providing valuable resources for natural language processing and information retrieval research. The annotations have an inter-annotator agreement of 0.697 for key facts and 0.653 for vague words, addressing the gap in publicly available, controlled datasets for product search.

## Method Summary
The dataset was collected through controlled experiments where native English speakers formulated natural language queries for laptop and jacket products. Researchers collected 3,540 queries in total, with 1,754 for laptops and 363 for jackets. Each query was annotated for key product facts and vague terms by multiple annotators. The annotation process achieved an inter-annotator agreement of 0.697 for key facts and 0.653 for vague words, indicating moderate consistency across annotators. The controlled experiment methodology ensured that queries represented genuine information needs rather than artificially constructed search terms.

## Key Results
- Dataset contains 3,540 natural language queries for e-commerce product search
- Includes detailed annotations of key product facts and vague terms for laptops and jackets
- Inter-annotator agreement scores of 0.697 for key facts and 0.653 for vague words
- Addresses gap in publicly available, controlled datasets for product search research

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The controlled experiment methodology ensures that queries reflect genuine information needs from native English speakers rather than artificially constructed search terms. By collecting queries in a controlled environment, researchers can capture natural language patterns and information-seeking behaviors that are more representative of actual user intent. The detailed annotation of key product facts and vague terms provides structured data that enables researchers to develop more sophisticated query understanding and product classification models.
