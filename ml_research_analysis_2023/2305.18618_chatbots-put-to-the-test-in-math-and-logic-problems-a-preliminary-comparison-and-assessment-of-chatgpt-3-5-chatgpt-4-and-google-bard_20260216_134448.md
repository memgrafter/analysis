# Chatbots put to the test in math and logic problems: A preliminary comparison and assessment of ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4, and Google Bard

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18618
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18618
- Reference count: 27
- ChatGPT-4 outperformed other chatbots on original problems, while Bard excelled on published problems due to internet access

## Executive Summary
This study compared the performance of three leading chatbots—ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4, and Google Bard—in solving mathematics and logic problems. A total of 30 questions were posed, divided into two sets: 15 original problems and 15 published problems. Each question was asked three times to each chatbot. The results showed that ChatGPT-4 outperformed ChatGPT-3.5 and Bard in the original problems, while Bard performed best in the published problems, likely due to its internet access. Overall, the chatbots demonstrated varying levels of success in understanding and solving the problems, with accuracy ranging from 40% to 80%. The study highlights the progress made in chatbot technology but also emphasizes the need for further improvement in accuracy and handling complex problems.

## Method Summary
The study posed 30 math and logic problems to ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4, and Google Bard, with each question asked three times to assess consistency. Problems were divided into two sets: 15 original and 15 published. Responses were recorded and scored based on correctness, with 1 point for correct answers and 0 for incorrect ones. The chatbots' performance was evaluated by comparing their accuracy and problem-solving abilities across both sets of problems.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT-4 outperformed ChatGPT-3.5 and Bard on original problems
- Bard achieved perfect scores on published problems, likely due to internet access
- Accuracy ranged from 40% to 80% across all chatbots and problem types

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bard's superior performance on "Published" problems stems from its direct internet access, allowing it to retrieve existing solutions.
- Mechanism: When presented with a problem already documented online, Bard can query Google Search, locate the solution, and return it directly. This bypasses the need for internal reasoning or problem-solving, effectively leveraging retrieval rather than generation.
- Core assumption: The problem statements in Set B are unique enough to be found via search, and Bard is permitted to access external sources during evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This is probably because Bard has direct access to the internet, in contrast to ChatGPT chatbots which do not have any communication with the outside world."
  - [section 4.2] "Bard's perfect score, may be again due to the fact that the answer can be easily found online."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No explicit citation in corpus showing Bard uses search for these problems, but FMR scores suggest similarity to other retrieval-oriented works.
- Break condition: If the problem wording is altered slightly or the solution is not indexed online, Bard must rely on internal reasoning, which may fail.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ChatGPT-4's higher accuracy than ChatGPT-3.5 is due to larger model size and improved fine-tuning, enabling better comprehension and multi-step reasoning.
- Mechanism: The increased parameter count and enhanced fine-tuning allow ChatGPT-4 to maintain context across longer problem statements, recognize patterns, and apply more sophisticated algorithms (e.g., rational roots theorem, algebraic modeling).
- Core assumption: The tasks require understanding relationships among variables and multi-step solutions, which benefit from improved context handling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.1] "GPT-4 has way more parameters, although the exact number is not known, leading to improved language understanding and generation capabilities."
  - [section 4.1] "Both versions of ChatGPT manage to give correct answers to this question, in all their three attempts" (referring to questions A10, A07).
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No direct citation linking parameter count to accuracy on these specific problems, but FMR scores suggest overlap with works on model scaling.
- Break condition: If the problem is too simple or the reasoning steps are too short, the difference between versions may be negligible.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Inconsistent performance across repeated attempts is due to stochastic decoding strategies and lack of explicit verification in the generation process.
- Mechanism: The models use sampling-based decoding (e.g., beam search) to generate responses. Small variations in the sampling can lead to different solution paths, some correct and others incorrect. Without a built-in verification step, errors propagate unchecked.
- Core assumption: The same prompt yields different internal probability distributions across attempts due to randomness in sampling.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "Consistency is also an issue, as many times a chatbot will provide conflicting answers when given the same question more than once."
  - [section 2.1] "The model's output is a probability distribution over the vocabulary for the next token in the sequence. The token with the highest probability is chosen..."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No explicit mention of stochastic decoding causing inconsistency in the corpus, but FMR scores align with works on model reliability.
- Break condition: If deterministic decoding is enforced or verification is added, consistency should improve.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Understanding mathematical problem structure (identifying unknowns, constraints, and required operations).
  - Why needed here: Chatbots must parse word problems and map them to mathematical models before solving. Failure to identify variables or constraints leads to incorrect solutions.
  - Quick check question: Given "A boy has 5 more apples than a girl. Together they have 15 apples. How many does the boy have?" Can you write the equations?

- Concept: Application of domain-specific algorithms (e.g., rational roots theorem, system of equations).
  - Why needed here: Different problem types require different solution strategies. Selecting the correct algorithm is critical for accuracy.
  - Quick check question: For x³ - 6x² + 11x - 6 = 0, what algorithm would you use to find integer roots?

- Concept: Verification of solutions (substitution, sanity checks).
  - Why needed here: Chatbots often fail to verify their answers, leading to propagation of errors. A verification step can catch obvious mistakes.
  - Quick check question: If you claim x=3 is a root of x² - 5x + 6 = 0, how do you verify it?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input Parser -> Solver Engine -> Response Generator -> (Optional Bard: Retriever)
- Critical path: Parse problem → Identify problem type → Select algorithm → Execute computation → Generate answer → (Optional) Verify → Output response
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs. Speed: Larger models (ChatGPT-4) are slower but more accurate; smaller models (ChatGPT-3.5) are faster but less reliable.
  - Internal reasoning vs. External retrieval: Models without internet access must rely on internal knowledge, while those with access can shortcut via search.
  - Consistency vs. Creativity: Stochastic decoding can yield varied responses but may sacrifice consistency.
- Failure signatures:
  - Incorrect algorithm selection (e.g., using linear equation solver for a quadratic).
  - Misinterpretation of problem constraints (e.g., missing "at least 18 years old" condition).
  - Arithmetic errors in multi-step calculations.
  - Overconfidence in wrong answers without verification.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Consistency test: Ask the same problem three times and compare outputs for variance.
  2. Algorithm probe: Provide problems solvable by multiple methods and check which algorithm the model selects.
  3. Verification check: After a solution is given, ask the model to verify by substitution or reverse calculation.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific improvements in problem-solving accuracy can be achieved by integrating internet search capabilities into ChatGPT-4, similar to Google Bard's access?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that Bard's superior performance on published problems may be due to its internet access, while ChatGPT models lack this capability.
- Why unresolved: The study did not test ChatGPT-4 with internet access to isolate the effect of this feature.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing ChatGPT-4's performance with and without internet search capabilities on the same set of problems.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the accuracy of chatbots vary across different mathematical domains (e.g., algebra, geometry, calculus) and what are the specific limitations in each domain?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions varying performance across problem types but does not provide detailed analysis of specific mathematical domains.
- Why unresolved: The study grouped problems broadly as "math and logic" without domain-specific categorization.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic evaluation of chatbot performance across clearly defined mathematical domains with increasing complexity levels.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of problem wording complexity on chatbot performance, particularly for problems with similar mathematical structure but different linguistic presentation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that question B15, with complex wording ("and a half" repeated), caused ChatGPT models to fail while Bard succeeded, similar to question A12 with simpler wording where ChatGPT performed better.
- Why unresolved: The study did not systematically vary wording complexity while keeping mathematical structure constant.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment varying linguistic complexity of problems with identical mathematical structure to isolate the effect of wording on chatbot performance.

## Limitations

- The exact wording of the 30 questions used in the assessment is not provided, making it difficult to assess problem representativeness.
- The methodology for scoring and interpreting chatbot responses lacks detail, particularly regarding partial credit for correct reasoning.
- The comparison between ChatGPT models and Bard may be biased due to Bard's internet access, which provides an advantage on published problems.

## Confidence

- Medium Confidence: The relative performance ranking of ChatGPT-4 > ChatGPT-3.5 > Bard on original problems.
- Low Confidence: The attribution of Bard's superior performance on published problems solely to internet access.
- Medium Confidence: The claim that chatbots demonstrate "varying levels of success" with accuracy ranging from 40% to 80%.

## Next Checks

1. Independent Replication with Fixed Prompts: Re-run the exact same 30 questions on all three chatbots using identical temperature settings and maximum tokens, recording all three attempts per question to verify consistency claims.

2. Controlled Internet Access Test: Repeat the published problem set evaluation with Bard under restricted conditions where internet access is disabled, to determine whether the performance advantage persists without external retrieval capabilities.

3. Algorithmic Capability Probe: Design a subset of problems that require specific mathematical algorithms (e.g., rational roots theorem, system of equations) and analyze which algorithms each model selects, to better understand the reasoning mechanisms rather than just final answer accuracy.
