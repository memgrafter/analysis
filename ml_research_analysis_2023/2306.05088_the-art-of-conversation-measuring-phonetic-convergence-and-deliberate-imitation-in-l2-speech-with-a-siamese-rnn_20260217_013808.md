# The ART of Conversation: Measuring Phonetic Convergence and Deliberate Imitation in L2-Speech with a Siamese RNN

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a Siamese recurrent neural network (RNN) architecture to measure phonetic convergence in second-language (L2) speech interactions. The authors extend an existing dataset with 20 native Slovak speakers and use an alternating reading task to capture both automatic and deliberate imitation. The model learns low-dimensional speaker embeddings from MFCC features and computes cosine similarity to quantify convergence. Results demonstrate the model effectively captures convergence dynamics and correlates with imitation ability, offering a scalable approach to studying L2 speech adaptation across different native language backgrounds.

## Method Summary
The method uses a Siamese bi-directional RNN with tied weights to process 39-dimensional MFCC features extracted from L2 English speech. The network generates speaker embeddings that are compared using cosine similarity to measure phonetic convergence between speakers. The model is pre-trained on the VCTK corpus of native English speakers and fine-tuned on solo speech data from native Italian, French, and Slovak speakers. Evaluation includes binary speaker verification tasks across solo, interactive, and imitation conditions, with performance measured using precision, recall, F1-score, and AUC metrics.

## Key Results
- Siamese RNN achieves F1-scores above 0.82 across solo, interactive, and imitation conditions
- Model effectively captures convergence dynamics between L2 speakers from different native language backgrounds
- Correlation found between imitation ability and degree of phonetic convergence in interactive speech

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Siamese RNN learns speaker-specific embeddings that capture holistic spectral features of L2 speech, enabling comparison of convergence across native language backgrounds.
- Mechanism: The model processes paired utterances through tied-weight bi-directional RNNs to produce low-dimensional embeddings, then computes cosine similarity to quantify how much two speakers' acoustic characteristics align.
- Core assumption: Speech embeddings in a shared representation space preserve meaningful acoustic distinctions that correlate with convergence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The model learns low-dimensional speaker embeddings from MFCC features and computes cosine similarity to quantify convergence."
  - [section] "The network maps the input vector of dimension din to a representation space of dimension drep (din = 39 and drep = 50 in our experiments)."
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus contains L1 Italian, French, Slovak speakers but no explicit grounding of embedding quality to acoustic similarity.
- Break condition: If MFCC features are too coarse to capture convergence-relevant fine-grained spectral cues, embeddings will not encode convergence.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pre-training on the VCTK corpus improves model robustness to L1-induced speaker variability.
- Mechanism: Weights initialized from a large native-English speaker dataset provide a better starting point for fine-tuning on diverse L2 accents than random initialization.
- Core assumption: General English acoustic features learned from native speakers transfer to accented L2 speech.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Our best result was achieved by initializing the model with weights pre-trained on the VCTK corpus [37], a large spoken sentence dataset with 109 L1 English speakers."
  - [section] "We observed a performance gap when testing the pre-trained VCTK model on L1 Italian, French, and Slovak data, indicating L1-induced data variability."
  - [corpus] Weak: No quantitative comparison of VCTK-pretrained vs. random init accuracy on L2 data.
- Break condition: If L2 speech patterns differ too strongly from native speech, pre-training may introduce bias rather than benefit.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Convergence correlates positively with imitation ability, suggesting a shared underlying speech adaptation skill.
- Mechanism: Intra-speaker similarity decreases more in imitation than in interactive conditions, and speakers with higher imitation ability show larger similarity changes in interaction.
- Core assumption: Imitation ability is a measurable proxy for convergence potential in natural interaction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results show the model effectively captures convergence dynamics and correlates with imitation ability, with F1-scores above 0.82 across solo, interactive, and imitation conditions."
  - [section] "The imitation ability was defined by the average change of intra-speaker similarity across the solo and imitation conditions. The degree of phonetic convergence was measured as a speaker’s average similarity change across the solo and interactive conditions."
  - [corpus] Weak: Corpus contains imitation and interactive conditions but no explicit baseline for imitation ability.
- Break condition: If imitation is not voluntary or controlled enough, the correlation may reflect task compliance rather than true convergence skill.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: MFCC feature extraction and normalization
  - Why needed here: MFCCs provide a compact, speaker-independent representation of spectral envelope, essential for training the Siamese RNN on acoustic similarity.
  - Quick check question: Why does cepstral mean and variance normalization matter for cross-speaker comparison?

- Concept: Siamese network architecture and tied weights
  - Why needed here: Tied weights enforce that both branches learn the same embedding space, enabling meaningful cosine similarity comparison.
  - Quick check question: What would happen to similarity scores if the two RNN branches had separate weights?

- Concept: Bi-directional RNN processing of variable-length sequences
  - Why needed here: Captures both past and future context in each utterance, important for modeling coarticulation and temporal dependencies in speech.
  - Quick check question: How does masking before the bi-directional RNN help when utterances have different lengths?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Audio input -> MFCC extraction (39-dim) -> Masking layer -> Tied-weight Bi-RNN -> Feed-forward layer -> Sigmoid -> Cosine similarity -> Binary classification
- Critical path: MFCC extraction -> Bi-RNN embedding -> Cosine similarity computation -> Loss calculation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Small RNN size (50 nodes) favors fast training but may underfit complex L2 variability
  - Pre-training on VCTK speeds convergence but may bias embeddings toward native speech
  - Tied weights reduce parameters but constrain representational flexibility
- Failure signatures:
  - Low precision/recall in solo condition suggests embeddings collapse speaker distinctions
  - Large performance gap between interactive and solo conditions may indicate embeddings are not capturing contextual convergence cues
  - If VCTK pre-training hurts L2 performance, embeddings may overfit native speech patterns
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train from scratch vs. VCTK-pretrained: Compare F1 on validation set to assess transfer benefit
  2. Swap RNN for LSTM: Measure accuracy and training time to test if more complex memory helps
  3. Vary MFCC dimensionality: Test 13, 26, 39 coefficients to find optimal spectral resolution

## Limitations

- The correlation between imitation ability and convergence is based on weak operationalization of imitation as a controlled, voluntary task
- Benefits of VCTK pre-training are asserted without quantitative comparison to random initialization on L2 data
- The fundamental assumption that MFCC-based embeddings can capture convergence-relevant spectral differences remains unverified

## Confidence

- **High**: The Siamese RNN architecture correctly implements cosine similarity-based speaker verification using tied-weight bi-directional RNNs
- **Medium**: The model achieves F1-scores above 0.82 across conditions as reported
- **Low**: The correlation between imitation ability and convergence represents a meaningful linguistic phenomenon rather than task artifact
