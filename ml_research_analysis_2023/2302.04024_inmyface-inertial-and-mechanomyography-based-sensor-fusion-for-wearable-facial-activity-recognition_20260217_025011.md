# InMyFace: Inertial and Mechanomyography-Based Sensor Fusion for Wearable Facial Activity Recognition

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.04024
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04024
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Multimodal fusion system achieves 85% F1 score for combined sensing and 79% F1 score for cross-user validation with 13 participants

## Executive Summary
This paper presents InMyFace, a wearable facial activity recognition system that uses a sports cap equipped with inertial, planar pressure, and acoustic sensors to monitor facial muscle motion through mechanomyography. The system employs a modular multimodal fusion approach using sensor-dependent neural networks with late fusion, achieving competitive performance with a low memory footprint suitable for embedded deployment. The method demonstrates robustness across different users while maintaining privacy by processing data locally rather than using cameras.

## Method Summary
The system uses FSR, PEF, IMU, and I2S microphones placed on a sports cap to capture facial muscle activity through mechanomyography. Data preprocessing includes normalization, dynamic resampling to 400 samples, and low-pass filtering. Four sensor-specific neural networks (1D-LeNet5 variants for PMMG/IMU and 2D CNN for AMMG) are trained independently and combined through an ensemble layer using late fusion. The model is evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation with a leave-one-session-out scheme across 13 participants performing 9 facial expressions over 5 sessions each.

## Key Results
- System achieves 85% average F1 score with all sensing modalities combined
- Cross-user validation yields 79% F1 score across thirteen participants
- Hybrid fusion approach achieves 82% average F1 score for six classes across eight users
- IMU and PMMG contribute most significantly to recognition performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multimodal fusion improves recognition performance over single modalities
- Mechanism: Each sensor type captures complementary information about facial muscle movements, and late fusion allows sensor-specific NNs to specialize before combining predictions
- Core assumption: Modalities are conditionally independent given class label with uncorrelated errors
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states combined sensing achieves 85% F1 vs 79% for cross-user; multimodal fusion section describes late fusion approach
- Break condition: If modalities are highly correlated or one dominates, fusion gains diminish

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Modular late fusion with sensor-dependent NNs enables deployment on resource-constrained devices
- Mechanism: Small sensor-specific NNs (≤2MB) can run independently on MCUs, preserving privacy by only transmitting predictions
- Core assumption: Sensor-specific NNs achieve sufficient accuracy independently, ensemble layer is lightweight
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions low memory footprint for embedded deployment; section notes NNs are <2MB vs 162MB for camera solution
- Break condition: If sensor-specific NNs are too inaccurate or ensemble layer exceeds device constraints

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pressure mechanomyography (PMMG) and inertial sensing contribute most significantly to recognition performance
- Mechanism: FSR/PEF capture mechanical muscle vibrations while IMU captures head orientation/acceleration related to facial expressions
- Core assumption: Muscle movements produce detectable mechanical/inertial patterns correlating with facial expressions
- Evidence anchors: Abstract identifies IMU and PMMG as most significant contributors; section describes PMMG as flexible and comfortable solution
- Break condition: If muscle movements don't produce reliable mechanical/inertial signals

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept: Multimodal sensor fusion**
  - Why needed here: Facial expressions involve complex, multi-dimensional movements that single sensors cannot capture completely
  - Quick check question: What are the advantages of late fusion over early fusion in this context?

- **Concept: Mechanomyography (MMG)**
  - Why needed here: MMG measures muscle vibrations without electrical contact, making it suitable for unobtrusive wearables
  - Quick check question: How does pressure mechanomyography differ from acoustic mechanomyography in measuring muscle activity?

- **Concept: Neural network architecture for time-series data**
  - Why needed here: Facial expressions are temporal patterns requiring specialized NN structures for effective recognition
  - Quick check question: Why use 1D convolutions for FSR/PEF/IMU data and 2D convolutions for audio data?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Teensy 4.1 microcontroller -> LoRaESP32 transceiver -> Bluetooth to phone -> Sensor-specific NNs -> Ensemble fusion -> Classification output
- **Critical path**: 1. Sensor data collection → 2. Preprocessing → 3. Sensor-specific NN inference → 4. Ensemble fusion → 5. Classification output
- **Design tradeoffs**: Modularity vs. cross-correlation exploitation; Memory footprint vs. model complexity; Privacy vs. sensor placement options; Real-time capability vs. processing accuracy
- **Failure signatures**: Low F1 scores for specific expressions; Sensor-specific NN underperforms ensemble; High variance between user sessions; Audio modality consistently weakest
- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Test individual sensor NNs on single user to establish baseline; 2. Implement late fusion ensemble and compare to best single sensor; 3. Evaluate cross-user performance to assess generalizability

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several areas remain unexplored based on the analysis of limitations and potential extensions.

## Limitations
- Small participant pool of 13 individuals limits demographic generalizability
- Sensor placement constrained to sports cap may restrict detectable expression range
- Cross-user performance degradation (79% F1) indicates sensitivity to individual facial muscle characteristics

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Multimodal fusion improves performance over single modalities (directly measured with F1 scores)
- **Medium Confidence**: Modular architecture enables practical deployment (memory footprint claims supported but real-world validation pending)
- **Low Confidence**: Specific contributions of PMMG and IMU modalities (stated as most significant but correlation analysis limited to pairwise comparisons)

## Next Checks
1. Test system with broader demographic representation (age, gender, facial structure variations) to validate generalizability claims
2. Implement real-time processing on target embedded hardware to verify the ≤2MB memory footprint constraint under actual deployment conditions
3. Conduct ablation studies removing individual sensors to quantify their specific contributions to ensemble performance beyond pairwise correlation analysis
