# Opening the Black Box: Analyzing Attention Weights and Hidden States in Pre-trained Language Models for Non-language Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12198
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12198
- Reference count: 31
- Pre-trained language models like BERT can solve non-language tasks by processing hierarchical problems in a structured manner similar to human problem-solving.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how pre-trained language models, specifically BERT, handle non-language tasks by analyzing attention weights and hidden states. The study focuses on two tasks: arithmetic problems and a Tic Tac Toe game. Through detailed analysis of attention patterns and hidden state representations, the researchers uncover that these models solve hierarchical problems in a structured manner, similar to human problem-solving approaches. The findings reveal that layer 10, rather than the final layer, is optimal for fine-tuning, and the models demonstrate generalization capabilities to longer sequences in the ListOps dataset.

## Method Summary
The study employs a multi-faceted analytical approach to understand model behavior. Researchers use attention weight analysis to track how the model distributes focus across tokens during processing, and examine hidden state representations to understand how information is encoded at different layers. Entropy analysis is used to measure the uncertainty in attention distributions, while token embeddings similarity metrics assess how representations evolve through the network. The methodology involves systematic fine-tuning experiments across different layers to identify optimal parameters for non-language tasks.

## Key Results
- Layer 10, not the final layer, emerges as the optimal layer to unfreeze for fine-tuning pre-trained language models on non-language tasks
- Attention weight analysis reveals structured hierarchical problem-solving patterns similar to human reasoning
- Models demonstrate strong generalization to longer sequences in the ListOps dataset, confirmed through testing on sequences longer than training data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of pre-trained language models on non-language tasks stems from their ability to leverage hierarchical attention patterns and structured information processing. The models appear to break down complex problems into manageable components, processing them through attention mechanisms that mirror human cognitive approaches to problem-solving. This structured approach allows the models to capture relationships between elements and maintain coherence in their reasoning process.

- Entropy measurement: Why needed - to quantify attention certainty; Quick check - track entropy changes through layers
- Token embeddings similarity: Why needed - to assess representation evolution; Quick check - measure similarity between consecutive layers
- Fine-tuning layer optimization: Why needed - to maximize task performance; Quick check - test different layer unfreezing strategies
- Generalization testing: Why needed - to validate model capabilities; Quick check - evaluate on longer sequences than training

## Architecture Onboarding
The analysis pipeline follows: Data Input -> BERT Encoder -> Attention Analysis -> Hidden State Examination -> Entropy Analysis -> Embedding Similarity Assessment -> Fine-tuning Experiments -> Performance Evaluation. Critical design tradeoffs include balancing layer-wise analysis granularity against computational efficiency, and choosing between task-specific versus general optimization strategies. Failure signatures include attention collapse (uniform distributions) and representation stagnation (minimal change between layers). First experiments should: 1) Compare attention patterns across different layers for the same task, 2) Test fine-tuning effectiveness at various depths, and 3) Evaluate generalization to longer sequences.

## Limitations
- Findings based on limited task types (arithmetic and Tic Tac Toe) may not generalize to all non-linguistic tasks
- Attention analysis methodology has inherent limitations in capturing 2D spatial relationships
- Results may be specific to BERT architecture and not applicable to other transformer models
- Entropy and embedding similarity metrics are supplementary and their robustness across different tasks is not fully established

## Confidence
- Structured hierarchical problem-solving approach: Medium
- Layer 10 as optimal for fine-tuning: Medium
- Generalization to longer sequences in ListOps: High

## Next Checks
1. Conduct similar analyses on a broader range of non-language tasks, including more complex spatial reasoning problems and tasks with different hierarchical structures, to test the generalizability of the findings.
2. Apply the same attention weight and hidden state analysis methodology to other pre-trained transformer architectures (e.g., RoBERTa, GPT variants) to determine if the observed patterns are specific to BERT or representative of transformer models in general.
3. Develop and implement enhanced visualization techniques or alternative analytical methods to better capture and interpret 2D spatial relationships in attention patterns, addressing the current limitations in analyzing vertical and diagonal line detection in the Tic Tac Toe task.
