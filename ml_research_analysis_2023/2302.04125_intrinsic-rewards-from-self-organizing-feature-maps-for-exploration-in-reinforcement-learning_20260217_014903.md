# Intrinsic Rewards from Self-Organizing Feature Maps for Exploration in Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.04125
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04125
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: ART-based novelty provides effective intrinsic rewards for exploration, matching human-level performance in Ordeal.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes using online self-organizing feature maps, specifically adaptive resonance theory (ART), to provide intrinsic rewards for exploration in reinforcement learning. The key idea is to use ART to cluster states online and quantify novelty based on the inverse square root of category counts, providing an exploration bonus added to extrinsic rewards. Tested on the Ordeal game environment, ART achieved human-level performance, matching ICM baseline and outperforming RND, though with high variance. ART-HL, a headless variant working directly on raw state space, also showed promise. The results demonstrate that ART-based novelty measures can provide effective intrinsic rewards for exploration, but tuning the intrinsic vs extrinsic reward balance remains challenging.

## Method Summary
The method uses Fuzzy ART to cluster states online during reinforcement learning. Each state observation is classified by ART into an existing cluster or a new one is created. The novelty reward is computed as the inverse square root of the cluster's visit count (k/√N), then added to the environment's extrinsic reward. A PPO agent learns a policy maximizing this combined reward. Two ART variants were tested: ART with a static feature encoding head, and ART-HL (headless) operating directly on raw states.

## Key Results
- ART achieved human-level performance in the Ordeal environment, matching the ICM baseline.
- ART outperformed RND in extrinsic reward accumulation.
- ART-HL showed promise but required careful tuning to avoid performance degradation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ART provides online, unsupervised clustering that quantifies novelty through category counts.
- Mechanism: The agent receives a state observation, ART classifies it into an existing cluster or creates a new one, and the novelty reward is computed as the inverse square root of how many times that cluster has been visited. This bonus is added to the environment reward, encouraging the agent to visit less frequently seen states.
- Core assumption: The number of times a cluster has been visited is a reliable proxy for state novelty in the Ordeal environment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "ART to cluster states online and quantify novelty based on the inverse square root of category counts"
  - [section] "the intrinsic reward it(xt) = k / √N(f(xt))"
  - [corpus] Weak: Related papers discuss novelty-based intrinsic rewards but do not specifically validate ART-based clustering in Ordeal-like environments.
- Break condition: If cluster granularity is too coarse, distinct states collapse into the same cluster, reducing novelty detection fidelity.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ART's online learning property allows immediate adaptation to new states without expensive retraining.
- Mechanism: Each new observation updates the ART weights incrementally via the learning step wJ ← (1 - β)wJ + β(x ∧ wJ), enabling the agent to quickly recognize new patterns and adjust exploration bonuses on the fly.
- Core assumption: Incremental updates preserve cluster quality without catastrophic forgetting in a small discrete state space.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "ART has performed best in class for anomaly detection" and "ART addresses the stability-plasticity dilemma"
  - [section] "Fuzzy ART ... accepts real-valued features by using operators from fuzzy set theory"
  - [corpus] Weak: No corpus neighbor explicitly validates ART's online stability in reinforcement learning settings.
- Break condition: Rapid drift in state distribution could cause unstable cluster formation, leading to noisy novelty signals.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Intrinsic reward scaling balances exploration vs. exploitation, preventing the agent from optimizing for intrinsic reward at the expense of extrinsic reward.
- Mechanism: The hyperparameter k in it(xt) = k / √N(f(xt)) controls the magnitude of the intrinsic bonus; tuning k ensures the agent still values extrinsic rewards like collecting the sword or killing the duck.
- Core assumption: The ratio of intrinsic to extrinsic reward magnitudes directly influences the agent's policy objective.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "the intrinsic reward is around 0.005 ... the total intrinsic reward is around 2.5, greater than the maximal extrinsic reward of 2"
  - [section] "None of the runs with ART with a dimension reducing head ... are able to achieve significant success"
  - [corpus] Weak: Related work mentions tuning but lacks specific empirical validation for ART-based systems.
- Break condition: If k is too large, the agent may ignore extrinsic goals and focus solely on maximizing intrinsic novelty.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: State encoder (optional static head or direct raw input) → Fuzzy ART classifier → Novelty calculator (inverse sqrt of category count) → Intrinsic reward scaler (k) → PPO policy learner → Environment interaction.
- Critical path: Observation → ART classification → Novelty reward → PPO update → Action.
- Design tradeoffs: Using a static feature encoder can reduce input dimensionality but may lose detail; headless ART preserves raw detail but may need more compute; intrinsic reward scaling must balance exploration incentives.
- Failure signatures: Agent gets stuck in loops (intrinsic reward too high), fails to explore (intrinsic reward too low), or clusters collapse (ART parameters poorly tuned).
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run ART with varying k values on Ordeal and plot extrinsic reward vs. intrinsic reward contribution.
  2. Compare ART-HL vs. ART with static head on same task to measure impact of input preprocessing.
  3. Fix k and vary ART vigilance ρ to observe effects on cluster granularity and exploration behavior.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of ART-based exploration compare to other state-of-the-art methods in more complex environments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that further work is needed to evaluate robustness and performance across more complex environments.
- Why unresolved: The experiments were conducted only on the Ordeal environment, which is relatively simple. More complex environments with different state spaces and reward structures could reveal limitations or advantages of the ART approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on a variety of complex environments (e.g., Atari games, robotic control tasks) and comparing the performance of ART-based exploration to other methods like ICM and RND.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between intrinsic and extrinsic rewards for ART-based exploration?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that tuning the intrinsic vs extrinsic reward balance remains challenging.
- Why unresolved: The experiments used a fixed ratio of intrinsic to extrinsic rewards, and the impact of varying this ratio was not explored. Finding the optimal balance could improve performance and stability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a systematic study of the impact of different intrinsic-to-extrinsic reward ratios on the performance of ART-based exploration in various environments.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the number of categories in Fuzzy ART affect the exploration performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that capping the number of classes in the head-less ART method performs better than the standard ART, suggesting that the number of categories is an important hyperparameter.
- Why unresolved: The experiments used a fixed number of categories, and the impact of varying this number was not explored. Understanding the relationship between the number of categories and exploration performance could lead to better tuning of the ART algorithm.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with different numbers of categories in Fuzzy ART and analyzing the impact on exploration performance and policy convergence.

## Limitations
- High variance in ART results suggests sensitivity to hyperparameters like k and ART vigilance parameters.
- Validation limited to a single, relatively simple game environment (Ordeal), raising questions about generalization.
- Missing specific hyperparameter values and exact implementation details for the static feature encoding head.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The theoretical mechanism of using ART clustering with inverse square root novelty scaling is sound and well-explained.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that ART achieves human-level performance is supported but requires verification due to high variance and missing implementation details.
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that ART-HL shows promise is based on limited evidence without quantitative comparison to ART with feature heads.
