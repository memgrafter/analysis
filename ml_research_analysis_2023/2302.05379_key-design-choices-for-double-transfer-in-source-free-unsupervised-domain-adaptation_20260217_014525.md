# Key Design Choices for Double-Transfer in Source-Free Unsupervised Domain Adaptation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.05379
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05379
- **Reference count**: 31
- **Primary result**: First large-scale empirical study of SF-UDA design choices across 500+ architectures and 74 domain pairs

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the first comprehensive empirical study of design choices in source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SF-UDA), evaluating over 500 architectures across 74 domain pairs. The study identifies critical factors affecting SF-UDA performance, including pre-training dataset choice, backbone architecture, and normalization strategy. The findings reveal that ImageNet-21K pre-training significantly improves performance by ~10% compared to ImageNet-1K, while normalization layers play a crucial role in determining whether fine-tuning on the source domain helps or hurts performance. The research also demonstrates that popular SF-UDA methods like SHOT and SCA can achieve competitive results with standard UDA approaches at lower computational cost.

## Method Summary
The study employs a two-stage evaluation framework for SF-UDA: first fine-tuning pre-trained models on source domain data, then adapting to target domains using unlabeled target data with methods like SHOT and SCA. The research systematically varies three key design choices: pre-training dataset (ImageNet-1K vs ImageNet-21K), backbone architecture (50+ different models), and normalization strategy (Batch Normalization vs Layer Normalization). Performance is evaluated across 74 domain pairs from multiple benchmark datasets including DomainNet, ImageClef-DA, Office-31, and others, measuring classification accuracy on target domains.

## Key Results
- ImageNet-21K pre-training yields ~10% accuracy improvement in SF-UDA compared to ImageNet-1K
- LN models show ~11% lower failure rate compared to BN models in SF-UDA
- SF-UDA methods (SHOT/SCA) achieve comparable performance to standard UDA approaches at reduced computational cost
- Fine-tuning on source domain degrades BN model performance by 4.6% while benefiting LN models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: ImageNet-21K pre-training yields a ~10% accuracy boost in SF-UDA compared to ImageNet-1K.
- **Mechanism**: The increased pre-training dataset size and diversity exposes the model to a broader distribution of visual concepts, leading to more generalizable feature representations that transfer better to unseen target domains.
- **Core assumption**: The feature extractor's quality (measured by ImageNet top-1 accuracy) is linearly correlated with downstream SF-UDA performance, and the ImageNet-21K pre-training provides additional benefits beyond this correlation.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "ImageNet-21K pre-training significantly boosts SF-UDA accuracy by ~10% compared to ImageNet-1K." [section] "Switching from ImageNet-1K to ImageNet-21K boosts the average performance up to ~10%."
- **Break condition**: The correlation between ImageNet accuracy and SF-UDA performance breaks down, or the additional benefit of ImageNet-21K is negated by other factors.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Fine-tuning on the source domain can degrade SF-UDA performance for models with Batch Normalization (BN).
- **Mechanism**: When source and target domain data distributions are significantly different, the Batch Normalization layers in the model can become misaligned, leading to poor generalization on the target domain.
- **Core assumption**: The statistics (mean and variance) of the source domain data used during fine-tuning are significantly different from those of the target domain, causing the BN layers to adapt suboptimally.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "fine-tuning on the source domain can degrade performance for models with Batch Normalization (BN), while models with Layer Normalization (LN) benefit from it." [section] "fine-tuning causes a performance degradation of 4.6% for models with Batch Normalization (BN)."
- **Break condition**: The source and target domain data distributions are similar, or the model uses Layer Normalization or other normalization techniques that are less sensitive to domain shifts.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: SF-UDA methods like SHOT and SCA are competitive with standard UDA approaches.
- **Mechanism**: These methods leverage the underlying structure of the feature space to overcome the lack of ground truth on the target domain, achieving comparable performance with less data and computational cost.
- **Core assumption**: The feature space learned during pre-training and fine-tuning on the source domain contains sufficient information to adapt to the target domain without requiring labeled data.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "SF-UDA methods like SHOT and SCA are competitive with standard UDA approaches, achieving comparable performance at a fraction of the data and computational cost." [section] "SF-UDA methods, like SHOT [Liang et al., 2020], SCA... and NRC [Yang et al., 2021a], perform well also with architectures and datasets different from the usual benchmark ones and are competitive with state-of-the-art UDA methods."
- **Break condition**: The feature space learned during pre-training and fine-tuning is not generalizable enough to the target domain, or the target domain is significantly different from the source domain in terms of visual appearance or class distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Pre-trained model -> Feature extractor -> Classifier -> SF-UDA method (SHOT/SCA)
- **Critical path**: 1. Load pre-trained model 2. Fine-tune on source domain (optional, depends on normalization) 3. Apply SF-UDA method on target domain 4. Evaluate performance
- **Design tradeoffs**: ImageNet-1K vs ImageNet-21K pre-training: Accuracy vs computational cost; Fine-tuning on source domain: Potential performance gain vs risk of degradation (especially with BN); SF-UDA method choice: SHOT (adapts feature extractor) vs SCA (adapts classifier)
- **Failure signatures**: Significant performance drop after fine-tuning (especially with BN); Poor performance on target domain despite good performance on source domain; High failure rate of SF-UDA methods
- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Compare SF-UDA performance of a model pre-trained on ImageNet-1K vs ImageNet-21K 2. Evaluate the impact of fine-tuning on source domain for a model with BN vs LN 3. Compare the performance of SHOT and SCA on a standard UDA benchmark dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does pre-training on multi-label datasets like ImageNet-21K affect SF-UDA performance compared to single-label datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that ImageNet-21K has 21,841 not mutually exclusive classes, while ImageNet-1K has 1000 mutually exclusive classes.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares ImageNet-1K and ImageNet-21K pre-training but does not explore the effect of multi-label vs single-label pre-training.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing SF-UDA performance using models pre-trained on single-label datasets (e.g., ImageNet-1K) vs multi-label datasets (e.g., ImageNet-21K).

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do different SF-UDA methods compare in terms of failure rate and performance degradation on real-world datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that SF-UDA methods like SHOT and SCA are competitive with UDA approaches but does not provide a comprehensive comparison on real-world datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on benchmark datasets and does not explore the performance of SF-UDA methods on diverse real-world datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the failure rate and performance degradation of various SF-UDA methods on a range of real-world datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of normalization layers (Batch Normalization vs Layer Normalization) affect the performance of SF-UDA methods in the presence of domain shifts?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper identifies normalization layers as a critical factor in SF-UDA performance and shows that models with Layer Normalization have lower failure rates compared to Batch Normalization.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the impact of normalization layers on SF-UDA performance under different domain shifts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of SF-UDA methods using models with different normalization layers (Batch Normalization vs Layer Normalization) under various domain shifts.

## Limitations
- Analysis focuses primarily on classification accuracy, potentially overlooking other important factors like model robustness or calibration
- Results may not generalize to all possible architecture and domain pair combinations despite extensive testing
- Impact of different pre-training objectives (e.g., contrastive learning) beyond standard classification is not explored

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: ImageNet-21K pre-training improves SF-UDA performance (~10% boost)
- **Medium confidence**: BN models degrade with source fine-tuning while LN models benefit
- **Medium confidence**: SF-UDA methods (SHOT/SCA) are competitive with standard UDA

## Next Checks
1. Cross-validation of normalization findings: Replicate the BN vs LN fine-tuning experiments across additional architecture families beyond those tested in the original study
2. Ablation on pre-training dataset size: Systematically vary pre-training dataset size between ImageNet-1K and ImageNet-21K to determine the minimum effective size for SF-UDA improvements
3. Failure case analysis: Conduct detailed analysis of the ~11% failure cases where SF-UDA methods underperform, examining domain shift characteristics and potential mitigation strategies
