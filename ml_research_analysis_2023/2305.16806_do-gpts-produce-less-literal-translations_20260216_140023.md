# Do GPTs Produce Less Literal Translations?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16806
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16806
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: GPT models produce less literal translations than NMT models when translating from English, with this tendency particularly pronounced for idiomatic expressions.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether GPT models produce less literal translations compared to traditional NMT models. Using word alignment and monotonicity metrics, the authors find that GPT translations from English are less literal while achieving similar or better quality scores. This tendency is particularly pronounced for sentences containing idiomatic expressions. Human evaluations confirm the findings. The authors hypothesize that GPTs' translation capability arises from language modeling bias rather than parallel data training, leading to greater abstractive abilities.

## Method Summary
The study compares GPT models (text-davinci-002, text-davinci-003) against NMT systems (WMT-21-SOTA, MS-Translator) using WMT test sets, idiom datasets (MAGPIE, EPIE, PIE), and synthetic datasets. Translations are generated and evaluated using COMET-QE+LID for quality, Unaligned Source Words (USW) and Translation Non-Monotonicity (NM) for literalness. Word alignments are computed using awesome-aligner. Human evaluation is conducted on a subset of translations to validate automatic metric findings.

## Key Results
- GPT translations from English consistently show higher unaligned source words and non-monotonicity scores than NMT translations
- GPT models achieve similar or better COMET-QE+LID scores while being less literal
- This tendency is particularly pronounced for sentences containing idiomatic expressions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT models produce less literal translations due to language modeling bias rather than parallel data training.
- Mechanism: GPT models are trained on raw text corpora without explicit alignment supervision, leading them to learn more natural phrasing patterns. This contrasts with NMT systems trained on parallel corpora containing literal web-crawled translations, which reinforces overly-literal translation patterns.
- Core assumption: The absence of parallel data training allows GPT models to avoid learning "translationese" artifacts and produce more natural target language outputs.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states "We hypothesize that GPTs' translation capability arises from language modeling bias rather than parallel data training, leading to greater abstractive abilities."
  - [section 4] discusses "Parallel Data Bias" and "Language Modeling Bias" as potential causes for GPTs' less literal translations.
  - [corpus] includes "Lost in Literalism: How Supervised Training Shapes Translationese in LLMs" which relates to the impact of supervised training on translationese.
- Break condition: If GPT models were fine-tuned on parallel data or if NMT systems were trained on more natural, less literal parallel corpora, this mechanism would weaken or break.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT models exhibit greater figurative compositionality, allowing them to better translate idiomatic expressions.
- Mechanism: GPT models can abstract the meaning of idiomatic expressions and express them naturally in the target language, rather than translating them literally. This ability is demonstrated through experiments showing GPT models produce better translations of sentences containing idioms, with higher COMET-QE-QE scores and more unaligned source words.
- Core assumption: Greater non-literalness in GPT translations stems from correct translation of idiomatic expressions rather than other forms of variation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] mentions "This tendency is particularly pronounced for sentences containing idiomatic expressions."
  - [section 3.1] presents results showing "text-davinci-002 produces better quality translations than the WMT'21 SOTA system, with greater number of unaligned words as well as with higher non-monotonicity" for idiomatic sentences.
  - [section 3.2] shows synthetic experiments where "the percentage of unaligned source words is highest in the case of idioms" when comparing GPT performance to NMT.
  - [corpus] includes "Evaluating LLMs on Chinese Idiom Translation" which directly relates to idiom translation capabilities.
- Break condition: If the improved translation quality for idiomatic expressions were due to factors other than figurative compositionality, or if GPT models performed similarly on non-idiomatic and idiomatic expressions, this mechanism would weaken.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Differences in word alignment and monotonicity metrics between GPT and NMT translations indicate less literal translation patterns in GPT models.
- Mechanism: GPT translations exhibit more unaligned source words and higher non-monotonicity scores compared to NMT translations of similar quality. This suggests GPT models are more likely to paraphrase or restructure source sentences rather than translating them literally.
- Core assumption: Higher unaligned source words and non-monotonicity scores correlate with less literal translation patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2] introduces "Unaligned Source Words (USW)" and "Translation Non-Monotonicity (NM)" as measures of literalness, finding that "GPT translations also consistently show higher non-monotonicity for E→X translations."
  - [section 2] states "translations from two LLMs from the GPT series of LLMs are indeed generally less literal than those of their NMT counterparts when translating out of English."
  - [section 4] discusses how "Differences in E →X and X →E" show consistent patterns in literalness measures.
- Break condition: If the alignment and monotonicity metrics were found to not correlate with literalness, or if GPT models showed similar literalness patterns to NMT models, this mechanism would break.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the tendency towards less literal translations in GPT models vary across different language pairs and domains?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the authors focus on English-German, English-Chinese, and English-Russian translations, but it is unclear how the findings generalize to other language pairs and domains.
- Why unresolved: The paper only investigates a limited set of language pairs and domains, and it is not clear if the findings hold for other language pairs or domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on a wider range of language pairs and domains, including both similar and distant language pairs, and various domains such as literature, technical documents, and social media, could provide insights into the generalizability of the findings.

- Why unresolved: The paper only investigates a limited set of prompting strategies and fine-tuning approaches, and it is not clear how different strategies would affect the literalness of translations.

## Limitations

- The paper relies on automatic metrics (USW and NM) to quantify translation literalness, as there is no well-established metric for this purpose, which may introduce measurement uncertainty.
- Human evaluation is limited in scale (500 sentence pairs) and may not capture the full spectrum of translation behaviors.
- The study focuses primarily on English as the source language, leaving open the question of whether these patterns hold for other language pairs.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The finding that GPT models produce less literal translations than NMT systems when translating from English, as evidenced by consistently higher USW and NM scores across multiple language pairs and validated by human evaluation.

**Medium Confidence**: The hypothesis that GPTs' less literal translations stem from language modeling bias rather than parallel data training, given the acknowledged uncertainty about the exact contribution of each factor and the lack of direct experimental isolation of these variables.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that GPT models demonstrate superior handling of idiomatic expressions, though the evidence is primarily based on automatic metrics and a limited human evaluation focused on this specific phenomenon.

## Next Checks

1. **Extended Language Pair Analysis**: Conduct the same comparative study using non-English source languages to determine whether the observed less-literal translation patterns in GPT models generalize beyond English-centric translation scenarios.

2. **Controlled Training Experiments**: Design experiments that isolate the effects of language modeling bias versus parallel data training by fine-tuning GPT models with varying amounts of parallel data and comparing literalness metrics against both standard GPT and NMT systems.

3. **Expanded Human Evaluation**: Scale up human evaluation to include at least 2,000 sentence pairs across different domains and language pairs, with specific attention to distinguishing between beneficial non-literalness (idiomatic translation) versus detrimental non-literalness (meaning loss).
