# Towards fully covariant machine learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13724
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13724
- Reference count: 20
- Key outcome: This paper introduces passive symmetries in machine learning, drawing parallels to their role in physics. Passive symmetries arise from arbitrary choices in data representation, like coordinate systems or units, and include coordinate freedom, gauge symmetry, and units covariance. The authors argue that enforcing these symmetries can improve generalization and model performance. They demonstrate this through toy examples, such as using units covariance to predict black-body radiation intensity and employing O(3)-equivariant models to learn the dynamics of a springy double pendulum. The paper also connects passive symmetries to causal inference and provides guidance on structuring ML models to respect these symmetries. The results show that enforcing passive symmetries can lead to the discovery of important hidden objects in data problems and improve out-of-sample generalization.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the concept of passive symmetries in machine learning, drawing parallels to their fundamental role in physics. Passive symmetries arise from arbitrary choices in data representation, such as coordinate systems or units, and include coordinate freedom, gauge symmetry, and units covariance. The authors argue that enforcing these symmetries can improve generalization and model performance by eliminating spurious model flexibility that doesn't respect the underlying data structure. Through toy examples, including predicting black-body radiation intensity and learning the dynamics of a springy double pendulum, the paper demonstrates how passive symmetries can lead to better out-of-sample generalization and even enable the discovery of missing physical constants or contextual features from data alone.

## Method Summary
The paper presents a framework for implementing passive symmetries in machine learning models through equivariant architectures and dimensional analysis. For the springy double pendulum example, three O(3)-equivariant models are implemented with different approaches to handling gravitational acceleration: known, learned, or not used. For black-body radiation prediction, units covariant regression is compared against standard MLP approaches. The methods involve constructing models that are invariant under passive symmetry transformations, with particular emphasis on units covariance and coordinate transformations. The framework also addresses the discovery of missing contextual parameters by allowing models to learn dimensional constants that restore symmetry when key information is absent.

## Key Results
- Enforcing passive symmetries (coordinate freedom, gauge symmetry, units covariance) improves generalization and model performance in physics-inspired ML tasks
- Units covariance can enable prediction without training data in some cases by constraining functional forms through dimensional analysis
- Missing contextual information (like physical constants) can be discovered from data when passive symmetries are enforced, as demonstrated by the recovery of Planck's constant in black-body radiation prediction
- O(3)-equivariant models outperform standard approaches in learning the dynamics of a springy double pendulum, especially when gravitational acceleration is unknown

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Enforcing passive symmetries improves generalization by eliminating spurious model flexibility that doesn't respect the underlying data structure
- Mechanism: When a model is constrained to be equivariant under passive symmetry transformations (like coordinate changes or unit conversions), it cannot "learn" arbitrary mappings that depend on these arbitrary representation choices. This forces the model to capture only the invariant relationships in the data, leading to better out-of-sample performance
- Core assumption: The data-generating process is truly invariant under the passive symmetry transformations being enforced
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "enforcing these symmetries can improve generalization and model performance"
  - [section] "enforcing passive symmetries can lead to the discovery of important hidden objects in data problems and improve out-of-sample generalization"
- Break condition: If the model is trained on data that doesn't fully capture all relevant contextual information needed for the symmetry to hold

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Passive symmetries enable discovery of missing physical constants or contextual features from data alone
- Mechanism: When a model is constrained to be equivariant under passive symmetries but is missing key contextual information (like Planck's constant in black-body radiation), the optimization process is forced to "discover" the missing quantities as learnable parameters that restore the symmetry
- Core assumption: The training data contains sufficient information about the underlying physical relationships
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "enforcing passive symmetries can lead to the discovery of important hidden objects in a data problem"
  - [section] "when the regression is permitted to introduce a new dimensional constant...it finds a constant with units (and, less precisely, magnitude) that is consistent with h"
- Break condition: Insufficient data richness or signal-to-noise ratio to infer the missing quantities

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Passive symmetries constrain the functional form of learned relationships through dimensional analysis
- Mechanism: Units covariance (a type of passive symmetry) enforces that functions must be dimensionally consistent, which severely constrains the possible functional forms. This can allow prediction without any training data in some cases, as the functional form is determined by dimensional requirements alone
- Core assumption: All relevant quantities with appropriate units are included in the input features
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Units covariance has been tremendously important in the development of contemporary physics"
  - [section] "the only possible combination of m,g,v,θ that has units of length is β(θ)v²/g"
- Break condition: Missing relevant input quantities or incorrect unit assignments

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can passive symmetries be effectively implemented in machine learning models when not all relevant contextual information is known or included in the learning problem?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the difficulty of implementing passive symmetries when relevant constants or features are missing, using examples like Planck's constant and the gravity vector.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges this as a practical difficulty but doesn't provide a concrete solution for identifying and incorporating missing contextual information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of methods that can automatically identify missing contextual information or learn unknown constants/features from data, along with empirical validation on complex real-world problems.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the success of convolutional neural networks in image tasks be attributed to their implicit exploitation of observer symmetries?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions observer symmetries as approximate symmetries arising from the independence of data content on minor observer choices, and suggests a potential link to CNN success.
- Why unresolved: While the paper suggests a connection, it doesn't provide empirical evidence or theoretical analysis to support this claim.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of CNN performance with and without observer symmetry constraints on image datasets, and theoretical analysis of how observer symmetries affect CNN architectures and learning dynamics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the concept of passive symmetries be extended to improve generalization in machine learning tasks beyond those with obvious physical symmetries?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper argues that passive symmetries could have big impacts in ML and suggests their potential for improving generalization, but provides limited examples beyond physical systems.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides some conceptual arguments and toy examples, but doesn't explore the broader applicability of passive symmetries to diverse ML tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of a framework for identifying and implementing passive symmetries in various ML domains, along with empirical studies demonstrating improved generalization across different types of tasks (e.g., natural language processing, reinforcement learning).

## Limitations

- The empirical validation is limited to relatively simple physics problems that may not represent the complexity of real-world ML applications
- The paper doesn't thoroughly analyze the computational overhead of enforcing exact symmetries
- Cases where passive symmetries are only approximately valid are not addressed
- The discovery of missing contextual parameters assumes clean, noise-free data
