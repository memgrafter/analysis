# XAI-based Comparison of Input Representations for Audio Event Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14019
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14019
- Reference count: 33
- Key outcome: XAI reveals that waveform-based models learn more separable, class-specific features and are more robust to pitch alterations than spectrogram-based models.

## Executive Summary
This paper compares two deep learning models for audio event classification using eXplainable AI (XAI). One model uses raw waveform input while the other uses spectrogram input. Through Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) and a DFT-based method, the authors analyze how each model makes decisions by generating relevance heatmaps in the time-frequency domain. The study demonstrates that XAI can reveal meaningful differences in model behavior beyond classification accuracy, enabling better-informed choices about input representations and improving alignment with human understanding of audio events.

## Method Summary
The authors train a 1DCNN on raw waveforms and YAMNet on mel-spectrograms using the UrbanSound8k dataset (10 classes, 8732 samples). They apply LRP with DFT-LRP to generate time-frequency relevance heatmaps and compute within-class and between-class cosine similarities to compare classification strategies. Model robustness is tested through high-pass, low-pass, and pitch-shift augmentations.

## Key Results
- The 1DCNN model based on raw waveforms shows higher within-class similarity and lower between-class similarity than YAMNet, indicating more separable class-specific features.
- YAMNet (spectrogram-based) suffers greater accuracy drops than 1DCNN when tested with pitch-shift and high-pass filtering augmentations.
- XAI analysis reveals that spectrogram models rely more on pitch information while waveform models capture broader temporal patterns.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
DFT-LRP transforms relevance from time domain to time-frequency domain, enabling comparison of models trained on different input representations. The mechanism assumes DFT and STDFT are linear transformations, allowing valid relevance propagation. Evidence shows this method provides uniform time-frequency heatmaps for cross-model comparison.

### Mechanism 2
Cosine similarity between relevance heatmaps quantifies classification strategy differences. Higher within-class similarity indicates models learn class-specific features. The assumption is that similarity metrics reflect meaningful strategy differences rather than noise.

### Mechanism 3
Pitch-based augmentations expose model reliance on frequency content. High-pass, low-pass, and pitch-shift modifications reveal which models are more sensitive to frequency manipulation. The core assumption is that spectrogram models depend more on pitch while waveform models capture temporal patterns.

## Foundational Learning

- Layer-wise Relevance Propagation
  - Why needed here: LRP quantifies feature importance for model decisions, enabling comparison of internal strategies between models.
  - Quick check question: Can you explain how LRP redistributes output relevance back to input features?

- Discrete Fourier Transform and Spectrogram Processing
  - Why needed here: Understanding DFT/STDFT is essential for applying DFT-LRP and interpreting frequency-domain relevance maps.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between DFT and STDFT in terms of time-frequency resolution?

- Cosine Similarity for Pattern Comparison
  - Why needed here: Cosine similarity quantifies heatmap alignment, revealing whether models learn separable class-specific features.
  - Quick check question: How does cosine similarity differ from Euclidean distance when comparing relevance distributions?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: UrbanSound8k -> 1DCNN/YAMNet -> LRP relevance maps -> DFT-LRP transformation -> Cosine similarity analysis -> Classification accuracy

- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess UrbanSound8k data
  2. Train 1DCNN on waveforms and YAMNet on spectrograms
  3. Compute LRP relevance maps for test samples
  4. Apply DFT-LRP to waveform model relevance
  5. Compare heatmaps via cosine similarity
  6. Test robustness with pitch-based augmentations

- Design tradeoffs:
  - DFT-LRP adds computational overhead but enables cross-representation comparison
  - Cosine similarity is efficient but may miss temporal misalignments
  - Using mel-scale aligns with human perception but reduces frequency resolution

- Failure signatures:
  - Uniform relevance maps indicate model instability or lack of feature learning
  - High between-class similarity suggests models rely on spurious correlations
  - Large accuracy drops on pitch shifts indicate over-reliance on frequency content

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare LRP heatmaps for a correctly classified "Siren" sample between models
  2. Compute within-class vs. between-class cosine similarity for all classes
  3. Measure accuracy drop after applying a 3kHz high-pass filter to test set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do the classification strategies of models trained on waveform inputs compare to those trained on spectrogram inputs across different audio event classification datasets beyond UrbanSound8k? The study is limited to one dataset, and different audio event datasets may have varying characteristics that affect model behavior.

### Open Question 2
Can the differences in classification robustness between waveform and spectrogram models be attributed to specific architectural features versus input representation choices? The study uses different architectures (1DCNN vs. YAMNet) that have varying depths and structures, making it difficult to isolate the effect of input representation alone.

### Open Question 3
How does the choice of window function and hop size in spectrogram computation affect the classification strategies revealed by XAI analysis? The paper mentions that previous work has investigated different window functions but does not explore this systematically in their XAI analysis.

## Limitations

- The DFT-LRP mechanism's validity remains unverified in the corpus, creating uncertainty about cross-representation relevance comparison.
- Lack of specific hyperparameter details for both the 1DCNN and YAMNet models creates potential reproducibility gaps.
- The study uses different architectures (1DCNN vs. YAMNet) that have varying depths and structures, making it difficult to isolate the effect of input representation alone.

## Confidence

- High Confidence: Classification accuracy comparisons between models are well-supported by the methodology.
- Medium Confidence: The interpretation of cosine similarity differences between within-class and between-class relevance maps is reasonable but lacks direct corpus validation.
- Low Confidence: The DFT-LRP transformation mechanism for comparing relevance across different input representations needs further empirical verification.
