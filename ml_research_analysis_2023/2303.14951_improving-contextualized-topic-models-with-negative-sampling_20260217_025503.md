# Improving Contextualized Topic Models with Negative Sampling

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.14951
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.14951
- Reference count: 13
- This paper proposes a negative sampling strategy to improve the quality of topics generated by a contextualized topic model (CTM)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CTM-Neg, a contextualized topic model that incorporates negative sampling to improve topic coherence and diversity. The key innovation is perturbing the document-topic vector during training and using triplet loss to create contrastive signals between original and perturbed reconstructions. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on topic coherence (NPMI and CV) and diversity (IRBO) metrics across three benchmark datasets.

## Method Summary
CTM-Neg builds on the contextualized topic model (CTM) architecture by adding a negative sampling mechanism. The model uses both SBERT embeddings and bag-of-words representations as input, encodes documents through a variational autoencoder framework, and introduces a perturbation module that zeroes out the top S topic probabilities to create negative samples. A triplet loss term encourages the model to produce reconstructions from correct topic vectors that are closer to the input than those from perturbed vectors. The method is evaluated on GoogleNews, 20NewsGroups, and M10 datasets using topic coherence, diversity, and document classification metrics.

## Key Results
- CTM-Neg achieves higher average topic coherence (NPMI and CV) than competitive topic models across most experiments
- The model attains very high topic diversity (IRBO) on all three datasets
- Manual evaluation confirms CTM-Neg generates coherent and diverse topics

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Negative sampling via perturbed document-topic vectors improves topic coherence by explicitly penalizing loss of salient words when top topics are removed
- Mechanism: The model generates a perturbed topic vector by zeroing out the top S topic probabilities, reconstructs a document from this vector, and uses triplet loss to ensure the reconstructed document from the correct vector is closer to the input than the one from the perturbed vector
- Core assumption: Removing top topics should significantly degrade reconstruction quality if those topics are truly salient
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we perturb the generated document-topic vector and use a triplet loss to encourage the document reconstructed from the correct document-topic vector to be similar to the input document and dissimilar to the document reconstructed from the perturbed vector"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Combining contextualized embeddings with bag-of-words representations provides richer semantic signal for topic modeling
- Mechanism: CTM-Neg uses both SBERT embeddings and BoW vectors, concatenated into a 2V-dimensional input, allowing the model to leverage both distributional semantics and local word co-occurrence patterns
- Core assumption: Contextualized embeddings capture semantic relationships that BoW alone misses, and combining both modalities improves topic quality
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.1] "CTM (Bianchi et al., 2021) augments ProdLDA by allowing in its input a contextualized representation (SBERT) of the documents"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Triplet loss with margin encourages the model to learn more discriminative topic distributions
- Mechanism: The triplet loss term compares the reconstruction from the correct topic vector (anchor) with the input document (positive) and the reconstruction from the perturbed vector (negative), pushing the anchor closer to positive than negative by margin m
- Core assumption: A margin-based contrastive loss will encourage the model to create topic distributions that are sensitive to the presence/absence of key topics
- Evidence anchors: [section 3.2] "We introduce a new term, triplet lossLTL, in Eq. (3) assuming the anchor is ˆx, the positive sample isxBoW (the original input document), and the negative sample is ˆxneg"

## Limitations

- The perturbation strategy (zeroing top S topics) may not always create meaningful negative samples if the top topics are not truly salient
- The effectiveness of triplet loss with margin m=1 may vary significantly across different datasets and topic counts, requiring dataset-specific tuning
- The combination of SBERT embeddings with BoW vectors may introduce representational redundancy or noise that could affect model performance

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core VAE architecture and CTM baseline implementation are well-established. The experimental results showing improved NPMI and CV scores are reproducible based on the provided methodology
- **Medium confidence**: The negative sampling mechanism's effectiveness depends on proper hyperparameter tuning (S values, λ weights) which may not generalize across all datasets without optimization
- **Low confidence**: The manual evaluation of topic quality is subjective and may not fully capture the model's limitations in real-world applications
