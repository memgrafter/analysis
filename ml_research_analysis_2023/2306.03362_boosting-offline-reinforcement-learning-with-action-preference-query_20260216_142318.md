# Boosting Offline Reinforcement Learning with Action Preference Query

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03362
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03362
- Reference count: 22
- One-line primary result: OAP achieves up to 115% higher scores than online fine-tuning on AntMaze tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Offline-with-Action-Preferences (OAP), a novel offline reinforcement learning scheme that improves policy performance without requiring online interactions. OAP periodically queries a blackbox expert model for action preferences between pre-collected and learned actions, using these preferences to adaptively adjust policy constraints during training. This mechanism allows the agent to distinguish beneficial policy improvements from overestimations of out-of-distribution data.

The approach demonstrates significant empirical improvements over online fine-tuning methods, particularly on challenging AntMaze tasks in the D4RL benchmark. OAP is positioned as a safer and more practical solution for offline RL in high-stakes scenarios where online interactions are costly or risky, achieving up to 115% higher scores compared to traditional fine-tuning approaches.

## Method Summary
OAP works by querying a blackbox expert policy for action preferences between pre-collected and learned actions at regular intervals. These preferences are used to train a RankNet model that learns preference patterns, which then pseudo-queries the remaining data. The policy constraint is adaptively adjusted based on these action preferences, allowing the agent to distinguish beneficial policy improvements from overestimations of out-of-distribution data. The agent is trained with this adjusted optimization objective, improving performance without requiring online interactions.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 115% higher scores than online fine-tuning on AntMaze tasks
- Significantly outperforms online fine-tuning methods on D4RL benchmark
- Demonstrates safer and more practical approach for high-stakes offline RL scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
OAP leverages action preferences to provide a safe signal for policy improvement without requiring online interactions. By periodically querying a blackbox expert for preferences between pre-collected and learned actions, the system can identify which policy updates are beneficial while avoiding dangerous out-of-distribution actions. The RankNet component generalizes these preferences across the dataset, enabling more efficient learning than individual queries alone.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Offline Reinforcement Learning** - Learning from static datasets without environment interactions
   - Why needed: Enables learning in high-stakes scenarios where online exploration is costly or dangerous
   - Quick check: Can the agent improve performance without additional data collection?

2. **Preference Learning** - Learning from relative comparisons rather than absolute labels
   - Why needed: Expert preferences provide richer information than binary accept/reject decisions
   - Quick check: Does the RankNet accurately predict preferences between actions?

3. **Policy Constraint Adjustment** - Dynamically modifying the optimization objective based on learned preferences
   - Why needed: Balances between staying close to the dataset and improving performance
   - Quick check: Does the policy constraint prevent catastrophic forgetting of safe behaviors?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Dataset -> RankNet -> Policy Constraint Adjustment -> Agent Training -> Improved Policy

**Critical Path**: The core innovation flows from preference queries through RankNet learning to policy constraint adjustment, which then directly influences the agent's optimization objective.

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- More frequent queries provide better guidance but increase computational cost
- Stricter policy constraints ensure safety but may limit performance gains
- RankNet complexity affects generalization capability vs. overfitting risk

**Failure Signatures**:
- Poor performance on challenging tasks (insufficient exploration or incorrect constraints)
- RankNet overfitting leading to poor pseudo-query results
- Action divergence increasing without corresponding value gains
