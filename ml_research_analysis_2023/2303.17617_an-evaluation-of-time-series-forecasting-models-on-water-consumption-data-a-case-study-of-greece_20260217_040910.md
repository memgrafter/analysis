# An evaluation of time series forecasting models on water consumption data: A case study of Greece

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.17617
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17617
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: LSTM and GRU neural networks outperform traditional statistical methods on quarterly water consumption data from Greece

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates multiple time series forecasting algorithms on water consumption data from Greece, focusing on a dataset with 218,588 quarterly time series from Attica spanning 2013-2019. The research compares traditional statistical methods (SARIMA, FB-Prophet) with advanced neural networks (LSTM, GRU, NeuralProphet) across different clustering configurations. The key finding is that neural networks, particularly LSTM and GRU, significantly outperform other methods, while clustering approaches did not improve accuracy due to averaging effects across heterogeneous series.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates forecasting algorithms on 218,588 quarterly water consumption time series from Greece's Attica region. The dataset spans 2013-2019 with measurements taken quarterly. Six forecasting approaches are compared: baseline (seasonal average), SARIMA, FB-Prophet, LSTM, GRU, and NeuralProphet. A modified DBSCAN algorithm with cosine similarity and Euclidean distance metrics creates four clustered datasets with varying parameters. Models are evaluated using MAE, MSE, and RMSE metrics, with KDE visualization of error distributions.

## Key Results
- LSTM and GRU neural networks achieved the best forecasting performance across all metrics
- Traditional statistical methods (SARIMA, FB-Prophet) performed moderately but were limited by data sparsity
- Clustering approaches did not improve neural network performance due to averaging effects across heterogeneous series
- NeuralProphet required more training data than quarterly observations could provide, resulting in poor performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LSTM and GRU neural networks outperform traditional statistical methods like SARIMA and FB-Prophet on water consumption data with quarterly sparsity and inhomogeneity.
- Mechanism: Neural networks with gated memory cells (LSTM, GRU) can capture complex nonlinear temporal dependencies and long-term seasonal patterns that traditional models struggle with when data is sparse (only four observations per year) and inhomogeneously aggregated from multiple users.
- Core assumption: The underlying water consumption patterns have nonlinear seasonal components that require adaptive memory mechanisms to learn effectively from limited quarterly data points.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The results show that LSTM and GRU neural networks perform the best, followed by the baseline approach and SARIMA."
  - [section]: "LSTM and GRU neural networks had the best forecasting performance with statistical methodologies such as SARIMA and FBprophet not falling further behind."
  - [corpus]: "Weak corpus support - no directly related papers on water consumption forecasting with LSTM/GRU on quarterly sparse data."
- Break condition: If water consumption patterns are primarily linear or if more frequent data (monthly or weekly) becomes available, traditional statistical methods may perform comparably or better.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Time series clustering before neural network training does not improve forecasting accuracy due to averaging effects across heterogeneous series.
- Mechanism: Clustering aims to group similar time series so neural networks can learn from more data per cluster, but when series within clusters have different consumption scales or patterns, the network learns an averaged representation that loses individual series characteristics.
- Core assumption: The benefits of increased training data from clustering outweigh the loss of individual series specificity when series within clusters are not sufficiently homogeneous.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Experimental results demonstrated that the clustering approach did not outperform other methodologies in the study due to the fact that an average time series out of all the time series per cluster was learned from the neural networks."
  - [section]: "This is due to the fact that when more than one time series are used for the training of the neural networks, the observation values of all the series are averaged out, thus creating an overall average time series per cluster."
  - [corpus]: "Weak corpus support - no directly related papers on clustering water consumption time series before neural network training."
- Break condition: If clusters can be formed with highly homogeneous time series (very similar consumption patterns and scales), clustering might provide benefits through increased training data.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: FB-Prophet and NeuralProphet require more training data than quarterly water consumption series can provide, leading to poor performance.
- Mechanism: These models have complex architectures with trend, seasonality, and holiday components that need sufficient observations to properly estimate parameters, which quarterly data (4 points/year) cannot provide adequately.
- Core assumption: The model complexity of FB-Prophet and NeuralProphet exceeds what can be reliably estimated from sparse quarterly observations over a 6-7 year period.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "FB-Prophet and NeuralProphet require more training data and do not perform as well on this type of data."
  - [section]: "SARIMA and FBprophet require more training data and that the sparsity of the time series (four observations per year) does not provide the volume of training required by these methodologies."
  - [corpus]: "Weak corpus support - no directly related papers on FB-Prophet performance degradation with quarterly sparse data."
- Break condition: If data frequency increases (monthly or weekly) or if longer historical periods become available, these models may perform better.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How do SARIMA and FBProphet perform on water consumption time series with different levels of sparsity (e.g., monthly vs. quarterly measurements)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that SARIMA and FBProphet "require more training data and that the sparsity of the time series (four observations per year) does not provide the volume of training required by these methodologies."
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated quarterly data, so it's unclear if the performance of these models would improve with more frequent measurements.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparing the performance of SARIMA and FBProphet on monthly or even daily water consumption data.

### Open Question 2

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper experimented with different DBSCAN parameters, resulting in four datasets with varying numbers of time series and clusters.
- Why unresolved: The study did not investigate the effect of clustering parameters on the forecasting accuracy of neural networks in detail.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic evaluation of different clustering parameter combinations and their impact on the forecasting performance of LSTM and GRU networks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do LSTM and GRU networks perform on water consumption time series from different regions or countries with diverse socio-economic and urbanization issues?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The study focused on water consumption data from Greece, a country with specific socio-economic and urbanization challenges.
- Why unresolved: The performance of neural networks may vary depending on the characteristics of the time series data, such as seasonality patterns and trends, which can differ across regions and countries.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating LSTM and GRU networks on water consumption datasets from other countries or regions with different socio-economic and urbanization profiles.

## Limitations

- Proprietary dataset from single Greek water company limits independent verification
- Quarterly frequency (4 observations/year) is extremely sparse for training complex models
- Clustering approach may have been improperly optimized, failing to show expected benefits
- No exploration of hybrid approaches combining statistical and neural methods

## Confidence

- High confidence in the relative ranking of LSTM and GRU performance over statistical methods
- Medium confidence in the conclusion that clustering did not improve performance, given potential parameter optimization issues
- Low confidence in the exact performance metrics due to lack of independent dataset verification
