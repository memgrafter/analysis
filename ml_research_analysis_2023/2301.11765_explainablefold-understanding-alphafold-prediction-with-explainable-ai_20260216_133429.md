# ExplainableFold: Understanding AlphaFold Prediction with Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11765
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11765
- Reference count: 29
- This paper introduces ExplainableFold, an explainable AI framework for understanding protein structure prediction models like AlphaFold.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ExplainableFold, an explainable AI framework for understanding protein structure prediction models like AlphaFold. The core idea is to generate counterfactual explanations that identify which amino acid residues are crucial for determining a protein's 3D structure, mimicking biochemical site-directed mutagenesis experiments in a dry-lab computational setting. The framework employs two approaches: residue deletion (identifying minimal residues whose removal changes structure) and residue substitution (identifying conservative vs. radical substitutions). Experiments on CASP14 proteins show ExplainableFold achieves 44% necessity score (PN) and 62% sufficiency score (PS) for residue deletion, significantly outperforming evolutionary baseline methods. For residue substitution, the generated exchangeability scores correlate positively with established biochemical indicators.

## Method Summary
ExplainableFold uses counterfactual reasoning to generate explanations for AlphaFold's predictions through two main approaches: residue deletion and residue substitution. The framework employs continuous relaxation optimization with sigmoid functions and L1 regularization to make the exponential search space tractable. A phased MSA re-alignment strategy is used to balance computational tractability with evolutionary context incorporation. The optimization minimizes/maximizes the number of perturbations needed to change/do not change the structure prediction based on TM-score thresholds, generating interpretable explanations about which residues are crucial for structure determination.

## Key Results
- ExplainableFold achieves 44% necessity score (PN) and 62% sufficiency score (PS) for residue deletion on CASP14 proteins
- Generated exchangeability scores correlate positively with biochemical indicators (Epstein's distance: r=0.388-0.494, Miyata's distance: r=0.602-0.796, evolutionary indicator: r=0.382-0.405)
- The framework significantly outperforms evolutionary baseline methods for identifying crucial residues

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The residue deletion approach identifies necessary residues by minimizing the set of deletions that change the protein structure prediction (TM-score ≤ 0.5).
- Mechanism: The framework learns a continuous perturbation vector σ(∆) that approximates which residues can be removed (treated as unknown) without affecting the predicted structure. Optimization uses a hinge loss on TM-score difference plus L1 regularization to enforce sparsity.
- Core assumption: A small set of residues contains the most necessary information for the structure prediction; their removal significantly alters the output.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Experiments on CASP14 proteins show ExplainableFold achieves 44% necessity score (PN)..."; [section] "We design the counterfactual explanation algorithm from these two views accordingly... find the minimal deletion that will alter the predicted structure..."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The residue substitution approach identifies radical vs conservative substitutions by minimizing/maximizing the set of substitutions that change/do not change the structure prediction.
- Mechanism: A continuous relaxation of the binary substitution matrix is optimized with hinge loss on TM-score and L1 regularization. Radical substitutions minimize the number needed to change structure; conservative substitutions maximize the number allowed without changing structure.
- Core assumption: Certain amino acid substitutions are inherently more destabilizing than others, and the optimization can rank them by effect size.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "... generated exchangeability scores correlate positively with established biochemical indicators (Epstein's distance: r=0.388-0.494...)"; [section] "Different replacements may have different effects on protein structures..."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Phased MSA re-alignment allows the perturbation learning to remain computationally tractable while incorporating evolutionary context.
- Mechanism: MSAs are fixed during each optimization phase (t=100 steps), then recomputed and used in the next phase. This reduces memory load and allows gradient flow through a manageable computational graph.
- Core assumption: Short optimization phases with fixed MSAs approximate the full joint optimization of sequence and MSA.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "It is impractical to re-compute MSAs in each training step... We propose a phased MSA re-alignment strategy."

  - Why needed here: The framework needs to generate explanations by observing how perturbations (deletions/substitutions) affect the output structure prediction.

  - Why needed here: TM-score is used as the primary measure of structural change to define necessity/sufficiency thresholds (≤0.5 for different folds, >0.5 for same fold).
  - Quick check question: What TM-score threshold distinguishes the same folding from different foldings?

- Concept: Continuous relaxation of discrete optimization
  - Why needed here: The search space of residue combinations is exponential; continuous relaxation with sigmoid/thresholding makes the problem tractable.
  - Quick check question: Why is L1 regularization added to the loss function in the relaxation approach?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Protein sequence embedding (P) -> MSA embedding (M(P)) -> AlphaFold model -> TM-score computation -> Perturbation optimization
- Critical path:
  1. Load AlphaFold weights and protein sequence/alignments
  2. Initialize perturbation vector/matrix
  3. For each phase (3 total): Optimize perturbation with fixed MSA, then recompute MSA
  4. Convert final continuous perturbation to binary explanation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Memory vs. fidelity: Gradient checkpointing reduces memory but requires recomputation
  - Speed vs. accuracy: Fixed MSA phases are faster but may miss joint effects
  - Sparsity vs. coverage: L1 regularization enforces minimal explanations but may exclude borderline residues
- Failure signatures:
  - Low necessity/sufficiency scores: Base model may not depend heavily on specific residues
  - High TM-score variance between phases: MSA re-alignment too disruptive
  - Optimization stuck at local minima: Learning rate too high or initialization poor
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run necessary deletion optimization on a single CASP14 protein, verify PN score and explanation size
  2. Run radical substitution optimization, compute exchangeability correlation with Epstein's distance
  3. Compare phased MSA re-alignment vs. full recomputation on a small protein for runtime and stability

## Limitations
- The approach relies heavily on the assumption that AlphaFold's predictions can be meaningfully perturbed through residue-level changes while maintaining structural coherence
- The phased MSA re-alignment strategy, while computationally necessary, introduces potential instability in the optimization landscape
- The evaluation metrics (necessity/sufficiency scores) are novel and their biological interpretability remains to be validated through independent experimental verification

## Confidence

- **Necessity/Sufficiency Claims**: Medium - The reported scores (PN=44%, PS=62%) are internally consistent with the framework design, but the biological significance of these specific thresholds requires further validation
- **Biochemical Correlation Claims**: High - The correlations with established biochemical indicators (Epstein: r=0.388-0.494, Miyata: r=0.602-0.796) provide strong evidence that the framework captures meaningful substitution patterns
- **Framework Generality**: Low - While demonstrated on CASP14 proteins, the approach's scalability to diverse protein families and its performance on proteins with varying structural complexity remains uncertain
