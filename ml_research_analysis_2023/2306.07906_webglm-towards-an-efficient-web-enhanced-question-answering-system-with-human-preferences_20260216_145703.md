# WebGLM: Towards An Efficient Web-Enhanced Question Answering System with Human Preferences

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.07906
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07906
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: WebGLM achieves accuracy close to WebGPT-175B while being faster and more cost-effective, with a 43% win rate against human answers in Turing tests.

## Executive Summary
WebGLM is a web-enhanced QA system designed to balance accuracy with efficiency. It combines an LLM-augmented retriever for fast web search, a bootstrapped generator trained on large-scale in-context learning, and a human preference-aware scorer learned from online QA feedback. The system achieves competitive performance with WebGPT-175B while being more efficient and cost-effective. WebGLM also outperforms Perplexity.ai on open QA benchmarks.

## Method Summary
WebGLM integrates three key components: an LLM-augmented retriever for efficient web search, a bootstrapped generator trained on large-scale in-context learning, and a human preference-aware scorer learned from online QA feedback. The LLM-augmented retriever uses in-context learning to label reference relevance, then fine-tunes a smaller dense retriever to mimic the LLM's selection behavior. The bootstrapped generator is trained on LLM-generated data filtered by citation accuracy and Rouge-1 scores. The human preference-aware scorer is trained on online QA forum feedback, with preprocessing steps to mitigate length bias and augment contrast.

## Key Results
- WebGLM achieves accuracy close to WebGPT-175B while being faster and more cost-effective.
- WebGLM achieves a 43% win rate against human answers in Turing tests.
- WebGLM surpasses Perplexity.ai on open QA benchmarks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** GLM-augmented retrieval improves accuracy while maintaining speed by leveraging LLM's ability to adopt relevant references.
- **Mechanism:** Uses LLM in-context learning to label reference relevance, then fine-tunes a smaller dense retriever (Contriever) on these labels to mimic the LLM's selection behavior.
- **Core assumption:** LLM's reference adoption accuracy (90.2%) is significantly higher than dense retriever's (68.6%), and this knowledge can be transferred effectively via fine-tuning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "We discover that instead of relying on expensive human expert writing in WebGPT, LLMs can be enabled to learn to generate high-quality data with proper citation-based filtering."
  - [section] "We find only 68.6% of them are related. However, when we provide the query with corresponding candidate references to GPT-3 for 1-shot in-context learning inference... we discover that the LLM would only adopt part of the references and the corresponding accuracy is 90.2%, far better than Contriever's."
- **Break condition:** If the LLM's reference adoption accuracy is not significantly higher than the dense retriever's, or if the fine-tuning fails to transfer the knowledge effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Bootstrapped generator trained on LLM-generated data achieves high quality without expensive expert annotations.
- **Mechanism:** Uses GPT-3 in-context learning to generate quoted long-formed QA data, then filters using citation accuracy and Rouge-1 scores to ensure high quality.
- **Core assumption:** LLM can generate high-quality quoted long-formed answers with proper citation-based filtering, and the filtering process can effectively remove low-quality samples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "We discover that instead of relying on expensive human expert writing in WebGPT, LLMs can be enabled to learn to generate high-quality data with proper citation-based filtering."
  - [section] "After all the filtering conditions mentioned above, the number of samples drops from 83k to 45k, which becomes a high quality quoted long-formed QA dataset for web-hanced QA system training."
- **Break condition:** If the LLM-generated data is consistently low quality, or if the filtering process removes too many samples, making the dataset too small to be effective.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Human preference-aware scorer trained on online QA forum feedback performs comparably to expert-labeled scorers.
- **Mechanism:** Collects QA pairs with user thumb-ups from online forums, preprocesses to mitigate length bias and augment contrast, then trains a GLM model to predict human preferences.
- **Core assumption:** Online forum feedback (thumb-ups) captures human majority preferences effectively, and the preprocessing steps (length bias mitigation, contrast augmentation) improve the quality of the training data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section] "We propose to build a human preference-aware scorer based on massive user feedback (e.g., thumb-ups) from online QA forums."
  - [section] "After our prepossessing, there are 93k questions and 249k comparison pairs in total, with 230k pairs as the training set and 19k pairs as the test set."
- **Break condition:** If the online forum feedback does not accurately represent human preferences, or if the preprocessing steps do not effectively improve the quality of the training data.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** LLM-augmented Retriever -> Bootstrapped Generator -> Human Preference-Aware Scorer
- **Critical path:** Retriever -> Generator -> Scorer
  - The retriever fetches relevant web pages and extracts paragraphs, the generator creates answers based on the retrieved information, and the scorer evaluates and selects the best answer.
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Speed vs. accuracy: WebGLM prioritizes speed by using a smaller dense retriever and LLM-augmented retrieval, while WebGPT prioritizes accuracy by using a larger model and expert annotations.
  - Cost vs. quality: WebGLM reduces costs by using LLM-generated data and online forum feedback, while WebGPT incurs higher costs with expert annotations.
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Retriever: Low recall (missing relevant information) or high latency (slow response times)
  - Generator: Hallucinations (generating incorrect information) or poor citation accuracy
  - Scorer: Incorrect preference predictions or overfitting to the training data
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Evaluate the accuracy and speed of the LLM-augmented retriever compared to the baseline dense retriever and traditional sparse methods.
  2. Assess the quality of the bootstrapped generator by comparing its outputs to those of WebGPT and human-written answers.
  3. Test the effectiveness of the human preference-aware scorer by comparing its predictions to those of human evaluators and expert-labeled scorers.

## Limitations

- The paper's claims about efficiency gains rest heavily on comparisons with WebGPT-175B, but direct runtime measurements or cost breakdowns are absent.
- The reported 43% win rate against humans in Turing tests is impressive but lacks statistical significance details and test conditions.
- The human preference-aware scorer's effectiveness depends on whether online forum thumb-ups truly reflect quality judgments rather than popularity bias, which isn't validated.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The architecture design combining LLM-augmented retrieval, bootstrapped generation, and preference-aware scoring is technically sound and follows established patterns in web-enhanced QA systems
- **Medium Confidence**: The efficiency improvements relative to WebGPT are plausible given the described architectural differences, though empirical verification requires more detailed benchmarking
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that GLM-augmented retrieval achieves 90.2% reference adoption accuracy needs independent verification, as this specific evaluation methodology isn't fully described

## Next Checks

1. **Runtime benchmarking**: Conduct controlled experiments measuring end-to-end inference time and cost per query for WebGLM versus WebGPT-175B and commercial systems like Perplexity.ai under identical conditions

2. **Generalization testing**: Evaluate WebGLM's performance on out-of-distribution questions and across different domains to assess whether the human preference-aware scorer maintains effectiveness beyond the training distribution

3. **Ablation study**: Remove each component (LLM-augmented retrieval, bootstrapped generator, human preference scorer) individually to quantify their marginal contributions to both accuracy and efficiency metrics
