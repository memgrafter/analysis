# Objectives Matter: Understanding the Impact of Self-Supervised Objectives on Vision Transformer Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13089
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13089
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: JE models show higher similarity in their representations compared to REC models, even across different architectures.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how different self-supervised learning objectives affect the representations learned by Vision Transformers. The authors compare joint-embedding (JE) methods like MoCo-V3 and DINO with reconstruction-based (REC) methods like MAE. They find that JE models learn more similar representations across architectures and concentrate class discriminative information in the final CLS token, leading to better linear probe transfer. REC models distribute information across layers and require fine-tuning to achieve comparable transfer performance. The differences arise primarily in attention and normalization layers.

## Method Summary
The authors compute Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) similarity across layers to compare representations between different pre-trained models. They evaluate transfer performance using linear and nonlinear probes on the final and intermediate CLS token features, as well as on frozen backbone features for object detection and segmentation tasks on MS-COCO. RCDM visualizations are used to analyze invariances in final layer features. The study compares ViT-Base models pre-trained with MoCo-V3, DINO (JE) and MAE (REC), along with CNN counterparts and MSN (a JE method with masking).

## Key Results
- JE models show consistently higher CKA similarity to each other than to REC models, even across different architectures
- JE models concentrate class discriminative information in the final CLS token, while REC models distribute it across layers
- Fine-tuning REC models reorganizes their representations to become more similar to pre-trained JE models
- REC features transfer better to tasks requiring spatial specificity compared to JE features

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: JE models learn representations that are more similar to each other than REC models, even across different architectures.
- Mechanism: JE training objectives encourage view invariance between image samples via joint-embedding frameworks, leading to representations that share similar invariances. REC methods, which reconstruct images from masked inputs, prioritize local feature reconstruction and do not enforce the same type of cross-view similarity.
- Core assumption: The SSL objective governs representational similarity more than the underlying architecture.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "JE models show high similarity, and are less similar to REC models. Furthermore, JE ViTs demonstrate strong layer-to-layer correspondence in similarity, but groups of JE layers correspond to groups of REC layers."
- Break condition: If the architecture introduces fundamentally different inductive biases (e.g., CNNs vs Transformers) that override the effect of the SSL objective.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: JE models concentrate class discriminative information in the final CLS token, while REC models distribute this information across layers.
- Mechanism: JE methods maximize view invariance, which aligns well with classification tasks and allows class information to be efficiently encoded in the final CLS token. REC methods focus on reconstructing local features, distributing class information across intermediate layers and requiring additional processing for classification.
- Core assumption: The final CLS token in JE models captures all relevant class discriminative information for linear probe transfer.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "JE models contain more linearly decodable representations because all relevant class discriminative information is available in final pre-projector layer CLS token. In contrast, REC models lack key invariances and distribute class discriminative information across layers, leading to poor downstream transfer without fine-tuning."
- Break condition: If the downstream task requires spatial specificity or local feature reconstruction, where REC models may perform better.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Fine-tuning REC models reorganizes the information to be more similar to pre-trained JE models.
- Mechanism: Fine-tuning with supervised signals provides a strong gradient towards concentrating class discriminative information in the final CLS token, aligning REC model representations with those learned by JE models. This reorganization occurs primarily in the attention and normalization layers.
- Core assumption: Fine-tuning with supervision can restructure REC model representations to match the information distribution of JE models.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Fine-tuning REC models makes them similar to JE models by re-organizing class information into the final layer."
- Break condition: If fine-tuning is performed with a task that requires spatial specificity, where REC models may retain some advantages.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Representation Similarity Metrics (e.g., CKA)
  - Why needed here: To compare the learned representations across different SSL methods and architectures in a quantitative and architecture-agnostic way.
  - Quick check question: What does a high CKA value between two representations indicate about their similarity?

- Concept: Self-Supervised Learning Objectives (JE vs REC)
  - Why needed here: To understand the fundamental differences in how JE and REC methods learn representations and how these differences impact downstream transfer.
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between the training objectives of JE methods (e.g., MoCo, DINO) and REC methods (e.g., MAE)?

- Concept: Linear Probe Transfer
  - Why needed here: To evaluate the quality and accessibility of class discriminative information in pre-trained representations without fine-tuning.
  - Quick check question: Why is linear probe transfer often used as a proxy for the quality of self-supervised representations?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: ViT Backbone -> SSL Pre-training (JE/REC) -> Linear/Nonlinear Probe -> Transfer Evaluation
- Critical path: SSL Pre-training -> Representation Analysis -> Transfer Evaluation (Linear Probe / Fine-tuning) -> Interpret Results
- Design tradeoffs:
  - JE methods: Require careful augmentation and additional components to avoid representational collapse but yield better linear probe transfer
  - REC methods: Do not require hand-crafted augmentations but require a decoder and do not transfer as well without fine-tuning
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor linear probe transfer from REC models: Indicates that class discriminative information is not concentrated in the final CLS token
  - High CKA similarity between JE and REC models: Suggests that the SSL objective may not be the dominant factor in determining representational structure
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train ViT models with JE (MoCo-V3) and REC (MAE) methods and compare their representations using CKA
  2. Evaluate linear probe transfer performance from pre-trained JE and REC models on ImageNet classification
  3. Fine-tune REC models and re-evaluate their transfer performance to see if representations become more similar to JE models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do the representations learned by self-supervised vision transformers scale with model size and dataset size and diversity?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that they focused on understanding the ViT-Base model representations and that exploring how different SSL pre-training methods scale with model size and dataset size and diversity is an interesting avenue for future research.
- Why unresolved: The paper only investigated the ViT-Base model, so the scaling behavior with larger models or different datasets is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with larger ViT models (e.g., ViT-Large) and on more diverse and larger datasets (e.g., ImageNet-21k, JFT-300M) to analyze how the representation structure and transfer performance change.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the mathematical definition of "information" in self-supervised learning representations, and how does it relate to downstream task performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that it would be interesting to explore the impact of SSL objectives on multi-modal representation learning methods and that it would also be interesting to see how both JE and REC representations transfer to other downstream tasks beyond classification, detection, and segmentation. Additionally, the paper mentions that the notion of "information" in SSL representations is treated as its impact on downstream transfer, but a mathematical perspective could be insightful.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the empirical analysis of representation similarity and transfer performance but does not provide a rigorous mathematical definition of "information" in SSL representations or its relationship to task performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a mathematical framework to quantify the information content in SSL representations, potentially using information theory or other relevant metrics, and correlating this with downstream task performance across various tasks and datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do self-supervised learning objectives impact multi-modal representation learning methods like CLIP and Omni-MAE?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that it would be interesting to explore the impact of SSL objectives on multi-modal representation learning methods such as CLIP and Omni-MAE.
- Why unresolved: The paper only investigates SSL objectives on vision transformers for image-based tasks and does not explore their effects on multi-modal models that learn from both images and text or other modalities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments to train multi-modal models like CLIP and Omni-MAE using different SSL objectives (JE and REC) and analyzing how the learned representations differ in terms of similarity, information distribution, and transfer performance across various multi-modal tasks (e.g., image-text retrieval, visual question answering).

## Limitations

- The architecture-independent effects of SSL objectives are based on weak evidence, as direct comparisons across architectures are limited
- The claim about information concentration in CLS tokens versus intermediate layers lacks direct measurement of information concentration
- The mechanism by which fine-tuning reorganizes REC representations to match JE patterns is supported by indirect evidence but lacks direct comparison of information distribution before and after fine-tuning

## Confidence

High confidence in: The basic finding that JE and REC models show different CKA similarity patterns, and that REC models generally require fine-tuning for strong transfer performance.

Medium confidence in: The mechanism by which JE objectives create view-invariant representations that transfer better via linear probes, and the specific claim about information concentration in CLS tokens.

Low confidence in: The architecture-independent nature of SSL objective effects, and the completeness of the explanation for why fine-tuning reorganizes REC representations to match JE patterns.

## Next Checks

1. Directly measure and compare class discriminative information concentration in final CLS tokens versus intermediate layers for both JE and REC models using information-theoretic metrics like mutual information.

2. Compare representation similarity between fine-tuned REC models and pre-trained JE models at multiple training stages to track the reorganization process and identify which layers change most.

3. Test the architecture-independence claim by comparing JE vs REC similarity patterns across additional architecture types (CNNs, MLP-Mixers) to determine if the SSL objective consistently dominates architectural effects.
