# Augmenting Large Language Model Translators via Translation Memories

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17367
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17367
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: LLM translators with TM-based prompts achieve 20-30 BLEU point improvements over baselines

## Executive Summary
This work investigates using translation memories (TMs) to enhance large language model (LLM) translators through prompting techniques. By designing tailored templates that incorporate TM sentence pairs, the approach enables LLMs to leverage translation hints for improved performance. Experiments demonstrate significant BLEU score improvements (20-30 points) and outperform strong NMT baselines across multiple translation tasks. The key insight is that LLMs' ability to understand prompts, rather than their translation capabilities, is crucial for effectively utilizing TM hints.

## Method Summary
The approach uses TMs as prompts by retrieving similar sentence pairs from TM databases and incorporating them into tailored templates. Apache Lucene retrieves top similar TMs based on fuzzy matching scores, and different prompt templates (code-style vs. instruction-style) are tested. The pre-trained LLM text-davinci-003 (175B parameters) generates translations with and without TM prompts, and performance is evaluated using BLEU scores across multiple language pairs.

## Key Results
- TM-based prompts achieve 20-30 BLEU point improvements over baseline LLM translators
- Code-style templates outperform instruction-style templates for TM incorporation
- LLM translators with TM prompts outperform strong NMT baselines on most translation tasks
- Language understanding ability, not translation ability, is key to leveraging TMs effectively

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LLMs can extract and apply translation patterns from TM sentences when provided in context
- Mechanism: TM sentences provide in-context demonstrations of translation patterns, allowing LLMs to learn how to handle similar linguistic structures
- Core assumption: LLMs can understand and apply patterns from TM examples without explicit training
- Evidence anchors: Abstract and section statements about TM-based prompts improving LLM results

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Code-style templates work better than instruction-style templates for TM-based prompting
- Mechanism: Simpler, structured templates provide clearer signals to LLMs about where to end generation
- Core assumption: Template complexity affects LLM's ability to properly utilize TM information
- Evidence anchors: Section observations about simpler templates working better

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LLM's language understanding ability, not translation ability, is the key factor in leveraging TMs
- Mechanism: LLMs can understand instructions and TM content, allowing them to extract relevant translation hints
- Core assumption: LLMs' language understanding is more important than translation accuracy for this task
- Evidence anchors: Section statements about translation ability not being important

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies entirely on BLEU scores, which may not fully capture translation quality improvements
- Single LLM (text-davinci-003) used without comparison across different model sizes or architectures
- Simple fuzzy matching retrieval may miss semantically similar but lexically different sentence pairs
- Template design shows significant variance, suggesting extensive tuning may be required

## Confidence
- High Confidence: TM-based prompts improve LLM translation performance by 20-30 BLEU points
- Medium Confidence: Language understanding, not translation ability, drives TM prompt effectiveness
- Low Confidence: Code-style templates universally outperform instruction-style templates
