# Joint embedding in Hierarchical distance and semantic representation learning for link prediction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15655
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15655
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: HIE model outperforms state-of-the-art KGE methods on WN18, WN18RR, FB15k-237, and YAGO3-10 in MRR and Hits@k metrics

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces HIE, a knowledge graph embedding model that jointly projects triplets into distance and semantic measurement spaces to capture both structural and semantic information. The model uses a flexible transformation matrix instead of translation or rotation operations to obtain tail entities, and incorporates hierarchical level information to learn more expressive embeddings. HIE achieves significant improvements over existing methods on four benchmark datasets, demonstrating robust performance on complex relation modeling tasks.

## Method Summary
HIE is a KGE model that embeds each triplet (h, r, t) into both distance and semantic measurement spaces simultaneously. The model splits entities and relations into geometric (h0/t0) and semantic (h1/t1) parts, then projects them separately using diagonal matrices. A flexible transformation matrix Ms_r captures geometric features between entities and relations, while hierarchical levels extract both shallow and deep features. The final score combines distance and semantic measurements with learnable weights across hierarchical levels.

## Key Results
- On WN18RR, HIE achieves MRR of 0.480 and Hits@10 of 0.499, significant improvements over existing methods
- Outperforms state-of-the-art KGE methods on four benchmark datasets (WN18, WN18RR, FB15k-237, YAGO3-10)
- Demonstrates robust performance on complex relation modeling tasks (1-to-N, N-to-1, N-to-N patterns)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Joint embedding into both distance and semantic measurement spaces captures complementary structural and semantic features that single-space models miss
- Core assumption: Structural and semantic information are sufficiently orthogonal and complementary
- Evidence anchors: Model projects triplets into both spaces; no direct corpus evidence comparing dual-space to single-space approaches
- Break condition: If geometric and semantic subspaces are highly correlated, joint embedding provides little benefit

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Flexible transformation matrix Ms_r captures more general movement patterns than rigid translation or rotation
- Core assumption: Real-world KG relations exhibit diverse geometric transformations not well-modeled by pure translation or rotation
- Evidence anchors: Uses learned transformation matrix from projected head entity and relation; no corpus papers validate flexible matrices over baselines
- Break condition: If transformation matrix overfits or becomes too general, it may fail to learn stable patterns

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hierarchical level embeddings capture both low-level geometric relations and high-level semantic abstractions
- Core assumption: KG information is naturally hierarchical with both shallow and deep relational patterns
- Evidence anchors: Extracts hierarchical geometric and semantic information across levels; no corpus papers confirm hierarchical embeddings outperform flat embeddings
- Break condition: If hierarchical levels add noise rather than useful abstraction, performance degrades

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input triplet (h, r, t) -> Embedding split into h0/h1, r0/r1, t0/t1 -> Distance space projection via diag(M1_hp), diag(M1_rp), diag(M1_tp) -> Semantic space projection via diag(M1_hs), diag(M1_rs), diag(M1_ts) -> Transformation matrix Ms_r = M0 * r1_p (distance), Ms_r = M0 * r1_s (semantic) -> Hierarchical extraction via Mpe, Mse -> Scoring with dp_k = ||h_k_p * M_k_r - t_k_p||, ds_k = ||h_k_s + r_k_s - t_k_s|| -> Final score ∑ λ_k * d_k -> Loss with negative sampling

- Critical path:
  1. Embedding split and projection into two spaces
  2. Transformation matrix computation
  3. Hierarchical feature extraction
  4. Scoring and loss computation
  5. Gradient backpropagation through all components

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Joint distance+semantic vs. single-space: More expressive but more parameters and complexity
  - Flexible transformation vs. translation/rotation: Greater modeling power but risk of overfitting
  - Hierarchical levels vs. flat: Better abstraction but diminishing returns and added noise risk

- Failure signatures:
  - Degraded performance if λ heavily biased toward one space
  - Overfitting if transformation matrices or hierarchical weights are too flexible
  - Poor convergence if embedding splits are poorly initialized

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation: Remove distance space → measure impact on MRR/Hits@k
  2. Ablation: Remove semantic space → measure impact on MRR/Hits@k
  3. Ablation: Remove hierarchical levels (use only level 1) → measure impact on performance and parameter efficiency

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of hierarchical levels in HIE affect its performance on link prediction tasks with different relation patterns?
- Basis in paper: Paper mentions number of hierarchical levels impacts performance and two levels were chosen based on empirical results
- Why unresolved: Paper does not provide detailed analysis of how different hierarchical levels affect performance across various relation patterns
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with varying hierarchical levels on datasets with different relation patterns and comparing results

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of using different transformation operations (translation, rotation, or proposed flexible transformation matrix) on KGE performance?
- Basis in paper: Paper introduces flexible transformation matrix as alternative to translation-based or rotation-based approaches
- Why unresolved: Paper does not compare HIE performance with models using different transformation operations
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing and evaluating KGE models with various transformation operations on same datasets and comparing performance

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does joint embedding of distance and semantic spaces in HIE contribute to improved performance compared to models using only one type of space?
- Basis in paper: Paper claims HIE projects triplets into both distance and semantic measurement spaces to capture more complex features
- Why unresolved: Paper does not provide detailed analysis of individual contributions of distance and semantic spaces to overall performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies evaluating HIE performance when using only distance or semantic spaces, compared to full model

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does the proposed HIE model handle entities and relations with varying degrees of complexity and hierarchical information?
- Basis in paper: Paper mentions HIE captures hierarchical information from different space levels and utilizes flexible transformation matrix for complex relations
- Why unresolved: Paper does not provide detailed analysis of how HIE handles entities and relations with varying complexity and hierarchical information
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing HIE performance on datasets with entities and relations of varying complexity and hierarchical information, compared with other models

## Limitations

- Performance gains may be dataset-specific rather than demonstrating universal superiority across knowledge graph types
- Dual-space architecture introduces significant complexity and parameter overhead with unclear scaling behavior for larger KGs
- Hierarchical levels lack empirical validation of their individual contributions versus simpler architectures

## Confidence

- High confidence: Core methodology and mathematical formulation of HIE are clearly specified and reproducible
- Medium confidence: Reported performance improvements on benchmark datasets are likely accurate but may not generalize to all KG domains
- Low confidence: Claims about superiority of joint distance-semantic embedding and hierarchical levels over simpler alternatives lack direct empirical support from ablation studies

## Next Checks

1. Ablation study: Remove the distance measurement space and retrain to quantify its contribution to overall performance across all datasets

2. Scalability test: Evaluate HIE's performance and training time on a larger knowledge graph (e.g., DBpedia or Freebase) to assess practical deployment viability

3. Generalization assessment: Test HIE on knowledge graphs from different domains (e.g., biomedical, social networks) to verify if performance gains transfer beyond original benchmark datasets
