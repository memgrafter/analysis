# A predictive physics-aware hybrid reduced order model for reacting flows

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.09860
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09860
- Reference count: 40
- One-line result: Hybrid ROM combining POD with deep learning predicts reacting flow temporal dynamics with LSTM outperforming CNN and physics-aware loss ensuring mass conservation

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a hybrid reduced order model (ROM) for reacting flows that combines Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) with deep learning architectures. The key idea is to reduce the dimensionality of the reacting flow data using POD, then use deep learning models (LSTM and CNN) to predict the temporal coefficients of the POD modes. The proposed method was tested on a laminar co-flow flame with methane as fuel. The LSTM model showed better prediction accuracy than the CNN model, with a relative root mean square error (RRMSE) close to the reconstruction error using SVD. The model was also found to be capable of predicting more complex simulations, showing transfer learning capabilities. The physics-aware loss function was introduced to ensure mass balance conservation during training.

## Method Summary
The method involves applying Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) via Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to reduce the dimensionality of high-dimensional reacting flow data, extracting spatial modes and temporal coefficients. Two deep learning architectures (LSTM and CNN) are then trained to predict future temporal coefficients based on previous time steps. The predicted coefficients are reconstructed with POD modes to obtain predicted flow snapshots. A physics-aware loss function enforces mass balance conservation during training. The approach is validated on a laminar co-flow flame simulation with methane fuel.

## Key Results
- LSTM outperformed CNN in predicting temporal coefficients with RRMSE close to SVD reconstruction error
- Physics-aware loss function improved physical consistency and extrapolation capabilities
- Model successfully predicted more complex laminar flame simulations through transfer learning
- POD with 18 modes captured 80% of total energy while reducing degrees of freedom from thousands to manageable dimensions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: POD reduces the dimensionality of reacting flow data from thousands of snapshots to a few spatial modes with corresponding temporal coefficients, enabling tractable deep learning modeling.
- Mechanism: Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) via SVD decomposes the original high-dimensional snapshot matrix into orthogonal spatial modes (POD modes) and temporal coefficients. The most energetic modes (largest singular values) are retained, preserving dominant flow dynamics while eliminating noise and small scales. This reduction from thousands of degrees of freedom to a few POD modes makes the data tractable for deep learning architectures.
- Core assumption: The dominant dynamics of the reacting flow can be captured by a small number of POD modes that contain the largest energy contributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The number of degrees of freedom is reduced from thousands of temporal points to a few POD modes with their corresponding temporal coefficients."
  - [section]: "This decomposition is then fed to two deep learning algorithms to discover the underlying physical dynamics of the system using the embedded information in the temporal coefficients."
- Break condition: If the reacting flow contains important dynamics in higher-order POD modes not captured by the reduced set, or if the flow is highly turbulent with energy distributed across many scales.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Deep learning architectures (LSTM and CNN) can learn the temporal dynamics of POD coefficients to predict future states of the reacting flow.
- Mechanism: The reduced temporal coefficients from POD serve as input sequences to deep learning models. LSTM networks use recurrent connections to maintain memory of previous states, making them suitable for learning temporal dependencies in the temporal coefficients. CNN models use convolutional filters to detect patterns and extract features from the temporal sequences. Both architectures predict future temporal coefficients, which are then reconstructed with POD modes to obtain predicted flow snapshots.
- Core assumption: The temporal evolution of POD coefficients can be modeled as a time series that follows patterns learnable by deep learning architectures.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Two different deep learning architectures have been tested to predict the temporal coefficients, based on recursive (RNN) and convolutional (CNN) neural networks."
  - [section]: "In the present work, two different deep learning models are used to act as time integrators to solve the temporal coefficients."
- Break condition: If the temporal dynamics of POD coefficients are too chaotic or non-Markovian for the chosen architectures to capture, or if the time horizon for prediction exceeds the models' capability.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Physics-aware loss functions that enforce mass balance conservation improve model generalization and extrapolation capabilities.
- Mechanism: A physics-aware mean-squared error (PA-MSE) loss function is introduced that combines the standard MSE between predicted and true temporal coefficients with an additional term that enforces mass balance conservation. This constraint ensures that the predicted mass fractions of chemical species sum to one at each time instant, embedding physical laws into the learning process.
- Core assumption: Enforcing physical constraints during training improves model performance, especially for extrapolation scenarios where mass conservation is critical.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "PA−MSE = 1/NK Σ_t (1/N ||T̂_t − T̂*_t||^2) + 1/NK Σ_t (||Σ_s Y_s(t) − Σ_s Y*_s(t)||^2)"
  - [section]: "The introduction of the physics-aware loss function might be crucial in an extrapolation scenario in which the mass balance must be conserved in each time instant."
- Break condition: If the physics-aware constraint conflicts with the primary prediction objective, potentially degrading overall prediction accuracy, or if the constraint is not properly weighted in the loss function.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data Preprocessing -> POD via SVD -> Temporal Coefficients -> Deep Learning Prediction (LSTM/CNN) -> Reconstructed Snapshots -> Physics Constraint (PA-MSE)
- Critical path: Data → POD → Temporal Coefficients → Deep Learning Prediction → Reconstructed Snapshots
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of POD modes vs. reconstruction error: More modes improve accuracy but increase computational cost
  - Model complexity vs. overfitting: More complex architectures may overfit the training data
  - Physics constraint weighting vs. prediction accuracy: Stronger physics constraints may improve generalization but potentially reduce pure prediction performance
- Failure signatures:
  - High reconstruction error despite good training performance indicates poor dimensionality reduction
  - Overfitting indicated by low training loss but high validation/test RRMSE
  - Physical inconsistency (e.g., mass not conserved) indicates physics-aware loss function issues

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the model's performance change if the learning rate was not reduced during training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors modified the learning rate from 0.001 to 0.005 with a decay of 0.8 every 10 epochs in case D and E, and found it improved performance.
- Why unresolved: The authors only tested this modification in combination with other changes, so the isolated effect of the learning rate schedule is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: A controlled experiment comparing the original learning rate schedule to a fixed learning rate of 0.005 or 0.001 would show the isolated impact on prediction accuracy and convergence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would using a different number of POD modes (N) than 18 improve the model's accuracy for this reacting flow problem?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors selected 18 POD modes to capture 80% of the total energy, but did not explore other values of N.
- Why unresolved: The optimal number of POD modes likely depends on the specific problem and could affect both the reconstruction error and the model's ability to learn the temporal dynamics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the model with different numbers of POD modes (e.g., 10, 15, 20, 25) and comparing the prediction accuracy and computational cost would reveal the optimal value of N for this problem.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How would the model perform on a turbulent reacting flow problem instead of the laminar flame studied here?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that the model shows promise for transfer learning to more complex laminar flames, but do not test it on turbulent flows.
- Why unresolved: Turbulent flows have much more complex dynamics and higher dimensionality, which could pose challenges for the POD and deep learning components of the model.
- What evidence would resolve it: Applying the model to a turbulent reacting flow simulation and evaluating its ability to predict the temporal coefficients and reconstruct the flow field would demonstrate its scalability and robustness.
