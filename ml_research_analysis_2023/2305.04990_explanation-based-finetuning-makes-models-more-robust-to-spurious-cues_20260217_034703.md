# Explanation-based Finetuning Makes Models More Robust to Spurious Cues

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.04990
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04990
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Explanation-based finetuning reduces accuracy drops and prediction-feature correlations across four classification tasks: ComVE (+1.2), CREAK (+9.1), e-SNLI (+15.4), and SBIC (+6.5)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces explanation-based finetuning as a novel approach to reduce large language models' reliance on spurious correlations. Unlike standard finetuning, which only trains models to predict the answer, explanation-based finetuning also requires the model to generate a free-text explanation supporting its answer. To evaluate this method, the authors construct training sets with various types of spurious cues, where the presence of the cue is perfectly correlated with a label. They then compare the performance of models finetuned with and without explanations on test sets without these cues. Results show that explanation-based finetuning makes models significantly more robust to spurious correlations, reducing accuracy drops and prediction-feature correlations across four classification tasks.

## Method Summary
The method involves finetuning pretrained language models using a dual-task approach where the model must generate both an answer and a free-text explanation supporting that answer. The training data is artificially constructed to contain spurious correlations - features that are perfectly correlated with labels but are not causally relevant. The model is then evaluated on test sets where these spurious cues are removed, measuring accuracy drops and prediction-feature correlations. The approach works with both human-written and model-generated explanations, making it applicable to datasets without human explanations.

## Key Results
- Explanation-based finetuning reduces accuracy drops when spurious cues are removed across all four tested datasets
- The method works across different model sizes and families (GPT-3, T5, BART)
- Benefits are most pronounced for larger models and stronger spurious correlations
- Model-generated explanations work nearly as well as human-written explanations
- Prediction-feature correlations decrease significantly when explanations are included during training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Explanations force models to focus on task-relevant features rather than spurious cues.
- Mechanism: By requiring the model to generate a free-text explanation supporting its answer during training, the model learns to prioritize human-relevant reasoning over simple feature correlations.
- Core assumption: Human-provided explanations capture task-relevant features that are causally linked to the correct label.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract]: "By forcing it to generate the explanation, we provide a signal that can allow the model to focus on features humans find relevant, instead of spurious features."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Explanation generation creates additional optimization pressure that regularizes the model.
- Mechanism: The dual task of generating both an explanation and a label forces the model to develop more robust internal representations that aren't easily fooled by spurious correlations.
- Core assumption: The explanation generation task is sufficiently different from label prediction to create orthogonal optimization pressure.
- Evidence anchors: [section 7]: "Surprisingly, even with permuted explanations, our method still provides a benefit over having no explanations at all."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Explanation-based finetuning works across different model sizes and families.
- Mechanism: The approach is model-agnostic and works by modifying training data rather than model architecture, making it applicable across different model types.
- Core assumption: The explanation generation task can be learned by different model architectures.
- Evidence anchors: [section 7]: "We run the same experiments in Section 6 with GPT-3 (Ada), T5, and BART."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Original datasets → Skewed training sets → Unskewed test sets → Model training (with/without explanations) → Evaluation (accuracy, accuracy drop, prediction-feature correlation)

- Critical path: 1) Construct skewed training set with perfect spurious correlation 2) Finetune model with and without explanations 3) Evaluate on unskewed test set 4) Measure accuracy drop and prediction-feature correlation 5) Compare results between finetuning methods

- Design tradeoffs: Using human-written vs model-generated explanations; perfect vs partial spurious correlation strength; model size vs explanation quality; training data size vs overfitting to spurious cues

- Failure signatures: High prediction-feature correlation indicates model reliance on spurious cues; small accuracy drops when spurious cues are removed; explanation quality degrades as model size decreases; standard finetuning outperforms explanation-based finetuning on absolute accuracy

- First 3 experiments: 1) Replicate CREAK experiments with different spurious cues to verify consistency 2) Test explanation-based finetuning on a new dataset without human explanations 3) Vary spurious correlation strength to find the threshold where explanation-based finetuning becomes effective

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- The paper doesn't explicitly call out open questions, but the limitations section suggests future work could include: extending to multi-class classification, exploring the method on naturally occurring spurious correlations rather than artificially constructed ones, and investigating the relationship between explanation quality and robustness benefits.

## Limitations

- The experimental setup uses artificially constructed spurious correlations rather than naturally occurring ones
- The method is only tested on binary classification tasks
- The quality of bootstrapped explanations is not thoroughly evaluated
- The exact mechanism by which explanations create robustness is not fully characterized

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core empirical finding that explanation-based finetuning reduces accuracy drops when spurious cues are removed across multiple datasets and model families.

**Medium Confidence**: The generalizability of this approach to real-world datasets with naturally occurring spurious correlations.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that explanation-based finetuning works equally well with human-written or model-generated explanations.

**Low Confidence**: The proposed mechanism explaining why explanations help - that they force models to focus on human-relevant features.

## Next Checks

1. Apply explanation-based finetuning to a dataset known to contain naturally occurring spurious correlations and measure whether the method reduces accuracy drops on carefully constructed out-of-distribution test sets.

2. Systematically evaluate the quality and relevance of bootstrapped explanations across different model sizes and families, measuring whether explanation quality correlates with robustness gains.

3. Test whether the free-text format is necessary by comparing against structured explanation formats (e.g., highlighting relevant features, multiple choice explanations) to determine which aspects of explanation generation are most critical for robustness.
