# Comparing Trajectory and Vision Modalities for Verb Representation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12737
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12737
- Reference count: 7
- Primary result: 2D visual modalities performed similarly well to 3D trajectories in learning verb semantics

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether 3D trajectory data is essential for learning verb representations compared to 2D visual data. The authors challenge the conventional assumption that richer 3D representations are necessary for capturing verb semantics by conducting controlled experiments comparing self-supervised encoders trained on both modalities. Their findings reveal that 2D visual modalities perform comparably to 3D trajectories in learning verb semantics, suggesting that the spatial information lost in dimensionality reduction does not significantly impact verb discrimination. This work has important implications for multimodal NLP systems that typically rely on 2D visual inputs.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted controlled experiments by training self-supervised encoders on both 2D visual data and 3D trajectory data to compare their effectiveness in learning verb representations. They evaluated the encoders' ability to differentiate verb concepts and found that 2D visual modalities performed similarly to 3D trajectories. Additionally, they tested whether 2D trajectories alone could reconstruct 3D trajectories reasonably well, providing insights into what information is preserved or lost during dimensionality reduction.

## Key Results
- 2D visual modalities performed similarly well to 3D trajectories in learning verb semantics
- 2D trajectories alone can reconstruct 3D trajectories reasonably well
- The differences lost when moving from 3D to 2D are not central for differentiating verb semantics
- Results challenge the conventional wisdom that richer environment representations necessarily translate into better representation learning for language

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper suggests that the essential semantic information for distinguishing verb meanings can be captured effectively in 2D representations without significant loss of discriminative power. This occurs because verb semantics primarily depend on action patterns, temporal dynamics, and spatial relationships that can be preserved in 2D projections, even though fine-grained 3D spatial details may be lost. The self-supervised learning framework appears robust enough to extract meaningful verb representations from the reduced dimensional space.

## Limitations
- Experiments conducted on specific subset of action verbs and synthetic environments
- May not represent complexity of real-world verb semantics across all domains
- Paper doesn't extensively validate 2D to 3D reconstruction across diverse action types
- Limited exploration of scenarios where 3D information might be crucial for specific semantic distinctions

## Confidence
- Core finding (2D vs 3D verb representation effectiveness): **High**
- 2D trajectory reconstruction of 3D data: **Medium**
- Generalizability to real-world applications: **Medium**
