# Causal Structural Learning from Time Series: A Convex Optimization Approach

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11336
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11336
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: Proposes a convex optimization approach for causal structural learning from time series using monotone operator variational inequalities

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel convex optimization framework for learning causal structures from time series data. The approach reformulates causal structure learning as a monotone operator variational inequality (VI) problem, using data-adaptive linear regularization to enforce directed acyclic graph (DAG) constraints. By leveraging well-established convex optimization techniques, the method provides non-asymptotic recovery guarantees even under general non-linear link functions.

The authors demonstrate through extensive numerical experiments that their VI-based approach outperforms existing methods in recovering causal structures, particularly in high-dimensional settings with limited data. The method shows superior performance compared to traditional DAG regularization, ℓ1 regularization, and adaptive ℓ1 regularization approaches.

## Method Summary
The proposed method formulates causal structure learning as a monotone operator variational inequality, which allows for efficient convex optimization techniques to be applied. A key innovation is the use of data-adaptive linear regularization to encourage DAG structure in the learned graph. This regularization term is designed to promote sparsity and acyclicity simultaneously. The authors establish theoretical guarantees for their approach under general non-linear link functions, providing non-asymptotic recovery bounds. The method is then validated through extensive numerical experiments comparing its performance to existing approaches in terms of structure recovery accuracy, especially in challenging high-dimensional and small-sample scenarios.

## Key Results
- VI-based approach outperforms DAG regularization, ℓ1 regularization, and adaptive ℓ1 regularization in structure recovery
- Superior performance demonstrated in high-dimensional settings and with limited data
- Theoretical non-asymptotic recovery guarantees established under general non-linear link functions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by transforming the causal structure learning problem into a convex optimization framework using monotone operator variational inequalities. The data-adaptive linear regularization term encourages DAG structure by simultaneously promoting sparsity and acyclicity. This allows the use of efficient convex optimization algorithms while maintaining theoretical guarantees for structure recovery under general non-linear relationships.

## Foundational Learning

1. **Monotone Operator Variational Inequalities**: A mathematical framework for solving optimization problems involving monotone operators; needed for formulating causal structure learning as a convex problem; quick check: verify the monotonicity condition holds for the chosen operators.

2. **Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) Constraints**: Mathematical properties ensuring the learned graph has no cycles; needed to represent valid causal structures; quick check: confirm the regularization term effectively enforces acyclicity.

3. **Data-Adaptive Regularization**: Techniques for adjusting regularization strength based on data characteristics; needed to balance structure recovery and model complexity; quick check: evaluate sensitivity of results to regularization parameter choices.

4. **Non-Asymptotic Recovery Guarantees**: Probabilistic bounds on structure recovery that hold for finite sample sizes; needed to establish theoretical performance without relying on asymptotic assumptions; quick check: verify the bound tightness in simulation studies.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Data -> Pre-processing -> VI Formulation -> Convex Optimization -> DAG Structure -> Structure Evaluation

Critical path: The most critical components are the VI formulation and the data-adaptive regularization, as they directly determine the quality of the learned causal structure. The convex optimization step is also crucial for computational efficiency.

Design tradeoffs: The main tradeoff is between computational efficiency (favoring convex formulations) and flexibility in modeling complex non-linear relationships. The choice of regularization strength represents another key tradeoff between structure recovery accuracy and computational tractability.

Failure signatures: Potential failures include convergence to local optima (mitigated by convex formulation), poor performance on highly non-linear relationships (partially addressed by theoretical guarantees), and sensitivity to regularization parameter choice (requiring careful tuning).
