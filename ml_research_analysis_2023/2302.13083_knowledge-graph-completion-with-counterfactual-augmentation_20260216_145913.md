# Knowledge Graph Completion with Counterfactual Augmentation

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces KGCF, a novel approach to Knowledge Graph Completion (KGC) that leverages counterfactual augmentation to improve learning. The authors address the issue of imbalanced relation distributions in KGs by incorporating a causal model to generate counterfactual relations, enhancing the representation learning of entity pairs. KGCF outperforms existing methods on KGC benchmarks, achieving new state-of-the-art results. Specifically, it improves the MRR metric on FB15k-237 and WN18RR datasets compared to the previous best model, NBFNet. The proposed method also enhances interpretability by providing path-based explanations for predictions.

## Method Summary
KGCF addresses knowledge graph completion by generating counterfactual relations to augment training data. The method defines treatments based on structural information using K-core clustering, then generates counterfactual relations by finding entity pairs with similar weighted node2vec embeddings but opposite structural treatments. These counterfactual examples are incorporated into training alongside factual examples. The model uses an NBFNet encoder with path-based message passing and an MLP decoder, trained with a joint loss combining factual loss, counterfactual loss, and discrepancy regularization to maintain alignment between factual and counterfactual representations.

## Key Results
- KGCF achieves new state-of-the-art MRR results on FB15k-237 and WN18RR datasets
- The method improves interpretability by providing path-based explanations for predictions
- Counterfactual augmentation demonstrates effectiveness in addressing imbalanced relation distributions in KGs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Counterfactual augmentation improves generalization by exposing the model to diverse structural contexts during training.
- Mechanism: The method generates counterfactual relations by finding entity pairs with similar embeddings but opposite structural treatments (same/different neighborhood membership). These counterfactual examples are incorporated into training alongside factual examples, expanding the training distribution.
- Core assumption: Similar embeddings with opposite structural treatments provide meaningful counterfactual information for improving generalization.

- Break condition: If the embedding space does not preserve structural similarity (i.e., similar embeddings don't correspond to similar structural roles), the counterfactual relations will be noisy and potentially harmful.

- Claim: Weighted node2vec embeddings account for imbalanced relation distributions when finding counterfactual substitutes.

- Core assumption: Weighting embeddings by relation type proportions helps maintain balance in the counterfactual distribution relative to the observed data.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input: Knowledge graph triplets (ℎ, 𝑟, 𝑡) -> Preprocessing: Compute factual treatments using K-core clustering, generate counterfactual relations via nearest neighbor search in weighted node2vec space -> Encoder: NBFNet (path-based GNN) producing entity pair representations -> Decoder: MLP scoring function producing tail entity probabilities -> Training: Joint loss combining factual loss, counterfactual loss, and discrepancy regularization -> Output: Ranked list of tail entities for each query

- Critical path:
  1. Compute factual treatments (T𝐹) using K-core clustering
  2. Generate counterfactual relations (A𝐶𝐹, T𝐶𝐹) via nearest neighbor search in weighted node2vec space
  3. Train encoder-decoder with joint loss L = L𝐹 + α·L𝐶𝐹 + β·L𝑑𝑖𝑠𝑐

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using weighted node2vec embeddings adds preprocessing overhead but helps address imbalanced relation distributions
  - The discrepancy regularization prevents distribution shift but may limit counterfactual learning if set too high
  - K-core clustering provides global structural information but may miss finer-grained patterns

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on rare relations suggests counterfactual generation isn't capturing useful patterns
  - High discrepancy loss during training indicates misalignment between factual and counterfactual representations
  - Degraded performance when removing discrepancy regularization suggests covariate shift is occurring

## Limitations

- The counterfactual generation mechanism relies heavily on node2vec embedding quality, which depends on hyperparameters not fully specified in the paper
- The weighted node2vec approach to address imbalanced relation distributions lacks empirical validation of its effectiveness
- The discrepancy regularization strength appears dataset-dependent and may require careful tuning to balance counterfactual learning with preventing distribution shift

## Confidence

- High confidence: The overall framework architecture and training procedure are clearly specified and reproducible
- Medium confidence: The improvement claims over NBFNet are supported by reported metrics, though exact hyperparameter settings are unclear
- Low confidence: The theoretical justification for weighted node2vec embeddings addressing imbalanced relation distributions lacks empirical validation
