# Neural Summarization of Electronic Health Records

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15222
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15222
- Reference count: 40
- This study investigates neural network models for automatic hospital discharge summary generation from nursing notes in MIMIC-III dataset

## Executive Summary
This study explores neural network approaches for generating hospital discharge summaries from nursing notes using the MIMIC-III dataset. The researchers tested five different data preparation setups with four state-of-the-art models (BART, T5, Longformer, FLAN-T5). The highest ROUGE scores were achieved using all nursing notes as input to generate full discharge summaries, with FLAN-T5 reaching 45.6 ROUGE F1. The study demonstrates that focused datasets and appropriate model selection can produce coherent discharge summary sections, potentially reducing documentation burden on healthcare providers.

## Method Summary
The study used the MIMIC-III dataset, extracting nursing notes and discharge summaries. Five training setups were created: (1) all nursing notes → full discharge summary, (2) earliest+latest nursing notes → Luhn-summarized discharge summary, (3) earliest+latest nursing notes → first three lines of each section, (4) latest nursing note → History of Present Illness section, and (5) latest nursing note → History of Present Illness + Discharge Instructions sections. Four models were fine-tuned using Hugging Face transformers with beam search (num_beams=10). Performance was evaluated using ROUGE metrics (ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, ROUGE-L-SUM).

## Key Results
- FLAN-T5 achieved the highest ROUGE score of 45.6 F1 using all nursing notes to generate full discharge summaries
- Generating specific sections (Setups 4-5) also showed strong performance
- BART and T5 achieved similar results in most setups
- Fine-tuning instruction-tuned models (FLAN-T5) showed better performance in summarizing entire reports

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Using all nursing notes as input and generating the full discharge summary (Setup 1) yields the highest ROUGE scores.
- **Mechanism**: Complete nursing note history provides richer contextual information, enabling the model to capture comprehensive patient stay representation for more accurate summary generation.
- **Core assumption**: Nursing notes contain substantial overlap with discharge summaries, and more input data improves model performance.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "The highest ROUGE scores were achieved using all nursing notes as input and generating the full discharge summary (Setup 1), with FLAN-T5 reaching 45.6 ROUGE F1."
  - [section]: "Setup 1 aims at using all the existing data, i.e., the entire history of nursing notes to generate a discharge summary report."
- **Break condition**: If nursing notes lack relevant information or the model cannot process long sequences effectively, performance may degrade.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Fine-tuning instruction-tuned models (FLAN-T5) shows better performance in summarizing entire reports.
- **Mechanism**: Instruction-tuned models have exposure to diverse tasks and formats, making them more adaptable to generating structured medical summaries that follow specific formats and include required sections.
- **Core assumption**: Models fine-tuned on diverse tasks can better generalize to new summarization tasks.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "We also observed that a fine-tuned T5 generally achieves higher ROUGE F1 scores than other fine-tuned models and a fine-tuned FLAN-T5 achieves the highest ROUGE score overall, i.e., 45.6."
  - [section]: "FLAN-T5 represents an enhanced version of T5 since it is fine-tuned on more than 1000 additional tasks covered in more languages compared to T5."
- **Break condition**: If instruction-tuning doesn't align with medical summarization needs, the performance advantage may disappear.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Generating specific sections of the discharge summary (Setups 4-5) also shows strong performance.
- **Mechanism**: Focusing on specific, well-defined sections reduces generation task complexity, allowing the model to specialize and produce higher quality outputs for those particular sections.
- **Core assumption**: Narrowing the scope of generation improves accuracy for targeted sections.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Generating specific sections (Setups 4-5) also showed strong performance."
  - [section]: "Setups 4 and 5 aim at narrowing their source texts to only the most recent nursing note... For Setup 4 the target output is one section of the discharge summary report, namely, 'History of Present Illness.'"
- **Break condition**: If the model cannot generalize from specific sections to full summaries, or if chosen sections aren't representative, performance may be limited.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: MIMIC-III data extraction -> Data preprocessing (5 setups) -> Model fine-tuning (BART, T5, Longformer, FLAN-T5) -> ROUGE evaluation -> Qualitative analysis

- **Critical path**: 1. Data preparation (creating source-target pairs for different setups) -> 2. Model selection and fine-tuning -> 3. Evaluation using ROUGE metrics -> 4. Qualitative analysis of results

- **Design tradeoffs**: Using all nursing notes vs. specific sections as input, fine-tuning vs. using pre-trained models, balancing model complexity vs. computational resources, tradeoff between generating full summaries vs. specific sections

- **Failure signatures**: Low ROUGE scores indicating poor summary quality, factual errors or hallucinations in generated text, inability to capture key information from nursing notes, model overfitting to training data

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Test Setup 1 with FLAN-T5 on a small subset of data to verify highest ROUGE score claim
  2. Compare Setup 4 and Setup 5 outputs qualitatively to assess section-specific generation quality
  3. Fine-tune BART on Setup 2 and compare performance with Setup 1 to validate data setup effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

1. How do the results change when using a combination of nursing notes and physician notes as input, rather than just nursing notes?
2. What is the impact of using instruction-fine-tuned models like FLAN-T5 versus standard fine-tuned models for summarizing entire discharge reports versus specific sections?
3. What is the accuracy rate of the models in preserving factual information when generating discharge summaries, beyond ROUGE scores?

## Limitations
- ROUGE scores may not fully capture clinical accuracy or coherence of generated summaries
- Study relies on MIMIC-III data which may not reflect diverse clinical settings or patient populations
- Fine-tuning process details are sparse, making it difficult to assess optimal hyperparameter usage

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The comparative performance ranking between models (FLAN-T5 > T5 ≈ BART > Longformer) across different setups, supported by explicit ROUGE score reporting and consistent patterns across multiple test configurations
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that using all nursing notes as input yields better performance than using only recent notes, though this assumes nursing notes contain relevant information
- **Low Confidence**: The practical clinical utility of generated summaries, as the study doesn't evaluate whether summaries meet clinical documentation standards or save time in practice

## Next Checks
1. Have medical professionals review a sample of generated summaries to assess factual accuracy, completeness, and adherence to clinical documentation standards, comparing against both reference summaries and original nursing notes
2. Test the best-performing models (FLAN-T5, Setup 1) on a different electronic health record dataset to evaluate whether performance gains transfer beyond MIMIC-III
3. Generate full discharge summaries and evaluate temporal consistency across sections, checking whether the model maintains coherent patient narratives when synthesizing information from multiple nursing notes spanning different time periods
