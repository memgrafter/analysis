# Incorporating Experts' Judgment into Machine Learning Models

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a novel framework for resolving conflicts between machine learning model predictions and domain experts' judgments by incorporating expert input when data points are poorly represented in training data. The method uses a generative adversarial network (GAN) to assess the degree of representation of new unlabeled data points and adjusts predictions accordingly. The framework is evaluated on synthetic data and two real-world case studies from IT services and financial industries, demonstrating significant improvements in aligning with expert judgment while maintaining high prediction accuracy.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a framework that leverages a GAN to assess how well new data points are represented in the training data. When a data point is deemed poorly represented, the framework adjusts the model's prediction by incorporating the expert's judgment, with the weight on expert input increasing as representation decreases. This approach addresses situations where training data may not fully capture the population, allowing for better integration of domain expertise into automated decision-making processes.

## Key Results
- Framework achieves significantly higher closeness to experts' judgment compared to baseline methods
- Minimal sacrifice in prediction accuracy when incorporating expert judgment
- New evaluation metric combining accuracy and closeness to expert judgment demonstrates framework effectiveness

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The framework works by using a GAN to measure how well new data points are represented in the training data. This representation score serves as a proxy for how much we should trust the model's prediction versus the expert's judgment. When data is well-represented, the model's prediction is trusted more; when data is poorly represented, expert judgment is weighted more heavily. This dynamic adjustment allows the system to leverage both data-driven and expert knowledge appropriately.

## Foundational Learning
- **GAN-based representation assessment**: Needed to quantify how similar new data points are to training data; quick check: verify GAN can distinguish in-distribution vs out-of-distribution samples
- **Dynamic weighting of expert input**: Required to balance model predictions and expert judgment based on data representation; quick check: test weighting scheme with synthetic data having known representation gaps
- **Combined evaluation metrics**: Necessary to assess both prediction accuracy and alignment with expert judgment simultaneously; quick check: ensure metric captures trade-off between accuracy and expert alignment

## Architecture Onboarding
- **Component map**: New data point -> GAN representation assessment -> Dynamic weighting module -> Final prediction (model + expert judgment)
- **Critical path**: The GAN assessment must complete before the dynamic weighting can occur, which then feeds into the final prediction adjustment
- **Design tradeoffs**: GAN complexity vs simpler distance-based methods; fixed vs adaptive expert weighting schemes; accuracy vs expert alignment optimization
- **Failure signatures**: Poor GAN representation assessment leading to incorrect expert weighting; overfitting to specific expert patterns; degradation in accuracy when expert input is incorporated
- **First experiments**: 1) Test GAN's ability to detect out-of-distribution samples, 2) Validate dynamic weighting produces expected behavior on synthetic data with known gaps, 3) Measure impact of different expert weight thresholds on accuracy-expert alignment trade-off

## Limitations
- Limited generalizability beyond the specific IT services and financial case studies examined
- Small number of real-world case studies (n=2) limits strength of generalization claims
- GAN-based representation assessment lacks comparison to simpler alternative methods

## Confidence
- Claims about framework effectiveness: Medium
- Claims about generalizability across domains: Low
- Claims about superiority of GAN-based representation assessment: Medium
