# Boosting Adversarial Robustness using Feature Level Stochastic Smoothing

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06462
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06462
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: FLSS achieves 3.15% improvement in robust accuracy after rejection on CIFAR-10 and 3.89% on CIFAR-100 compared to strongest baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Feature Level Stochastic Smoothing (FLSS), a method to improve adversarial robustness in deep neural networks while rejecting low-confidence samples. The approach enforces stochasticity in feature space by making one feature layer follow a predefined distribution, enabling sampling during training and inference. Combined with adversarial training and a rejection scheme based on prediction frequency, FLSS significantly outperforms existing methods on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets.

## Method Summary
FLSS introduces stochasticity in network predictions by enforcing a feature layer to follow a predefined distribution, typically standard normal. During training, the encoder network outputs mean and variance vectors, which are sampled using the reparameterization trick. The model is trained with adversarial examples (PGD and AWP) alongside KL divergence terms to enforce smoothness. During inference, multiple samples are drawn from the feature space, and predictions are made based on majority vote. Samples are rejected if the predicted class frequency falls below a threshold, improving overall accuracy by removing low-confidence predictions.

## Key Results
- FLSS achieves 3.15% improvement in robust accuracy after rejection on CIFAR-10
- FLSS achieves 3.89% improvement in robust accuracy after rejection on CIFAR-100
- The method also provides robustness gains without rejection, outperforming existing adversarial training methods
- Combined with CCAT, FLSS achieves the benefits of both approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Enforcing the feature layer to follow a fixed distribution enables smoother decision boundaries and more reliable rejection of low-confidence samples. The encoder network outputs mean and variance vectors for each input, sampled from a standard normal distribution. This stochasticity, controlled through the reparameterization trick, produces consistent predictions across multiple samples, making decision boundaries less sensitive to small perturbations.

### Mechanism 2
Adversarial training combined with stochastic smoothing improves robustness by making the model less sensitive to adversarial perturbations. The model is trained using adversarial samples generated by PGD and AWP, with a loss function that includes terms encouraging consistency between clean and adversarial sample predictions. KL divergence terms in the loss function help smooth decision boundaries.

### Mechanism 3
The rejection scheme based on the frequency of the predicted class improves overall accuracy by rejecting low-confidence samples. During inference, the model samples multiple times from the feature space and predicts the class based on majority vote. Samples are rejected if the frequency of the predicted class is below a predefined threshold, using frequency as a proxy for prediction confidence.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input image -> Encoder network -> Sample from feature space -> MLP head -> Output predictions
- Critical path: The encoder network and MLP head are the main components that need to be trained together, with a carefully designed loss function ensuring the model learns to be both robust and smooth
- Design tradeoffs: Number of samples during inference affects accuracy and computational cost (more samples = better accuracy but higher cost); rejection threshold must balance rejecting clean vs adversarial samples
- Failure signatures: Model not robust enough fails to classify adversarial examples correctly; ineffective rejection scheme either rejects too many clean samples or not enough adversarial samples
- First 3 experiments: 1) Evaluate performance on clean and adversarial samples without rejection to assess stochastic smoothing impact on robustness, 2) Test different rejection thresholds to find optimal balance, 3) Compare performance against other adversarial training methods and rejection schemes

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does FLSS perform against adaptive attacks that specifically target latent space features? While the paper presents results against several adaptive attacks, it's unclear how FLSS performs against comprehensive adaptive attacks targeting latent space features. Extensive experiments with such attacks and comparison with other state-of-the-art methods would provide insights into FLSS robustness.

### Open Question 2
How does FLSS performance change when combined with other adversarial training methods like TRADES or AWP? The paper combines FLSS with CCAT but doesn't explore combinations with other methods. Evaluating FLSS performance when combined with TRADES or AWP would reveal potential benefits and limitations of such combinations.

### Open Question 3
How does the proposed rejection scheme based on predicted class frequency compare to other schemes based on entropy of softmax predictions or distance between predicted and true classes? While the paper compares its scheme to one based on sample mean, comparison with entropy or distance-based schemes would reveal its effectiveness in terms of rejection accuracy and computational efficiency.

## Limitations
- Effectiveness heavily depends on proper tuning of feature distribution parameters and rejection threshold
- Current evaluation limited to CIFAR-10/100 datasets with ResNet-18 architecture, untested on larger-scale datasets or different architectures
- Computational overhead from multiple sampling rounds during inference could be prohibitive for real-time applications

## Confidence

### Mechanism 1 (Stochastic smoothing improves decision boundaries): Medium confidence
The theoretical foundation is sound, but empirical validation is limited to specific datasets and architectures.

### Mechanism 2 (Adversarial training with stochastic smoothing): High confidence
Follows established adversarial training practices with additional smoothing components.

### Mechanism 3 (Rejection scheme based on frequency): Medium confidence
The approach is intuitive but relies on frequency as a proxy for confidence, which may not always be reliable.
