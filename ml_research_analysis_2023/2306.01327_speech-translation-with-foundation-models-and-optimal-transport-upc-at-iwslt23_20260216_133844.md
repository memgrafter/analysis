# Speech Translation with Foundation Models and Optimal Transport: UPC at IWSLT23

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01327
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01327
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: UPC's submission to IWSLT 2023 Offline Speech Translation task, achieving 31.2 BLEU on MuST-C tst-COMMON, 29.8 on IWLST.tst2020, and 33.4 on IWSLT.ACLdev2023

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the UPC Machine Translation group's submission to the IWSLT 2023 Offline Speech Translation task. The approach leverages foundation models (wav2vec 2.0 for speech and mBART50 for text) with a novel Siamese pretraining step using CTC and Optimal Transport losses to align speech and text representations. After pretraining, the system is fine-tuned end-to-end with Cross Entropy and Knowledge Distillation losses. The method achieves state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks, particularly excelling on the newly released IWSLT.ACLdev2023 test set.

## Method Summary
The method builds upon foundation models by first pretraining speech and text encoders separately using CTC and Optimal Transport losses in a Siamese framework. This alignment step adapts speech representations to match the text model's learned space. The acoustic encoder (wav2vec 2.0) and semantic encoder (mBART50) are connected through coupling modules that include adapter layers and 1D convolutions. After pretraining, the system is fine-tuned end-to-end on speech translation data using Cross Entropy and Knowledge Distillation losses, with the text decoder (also from mBART50) providing supervision. Synthetic data is generated using SegAugment to adapt to the custom segmentations of IWSLT test sets.

## Key Results
- Single model achieves 31.2 BLEU on MuST-C tst-COMMON, outperforming many ensemble systems
- Best performance on IWSLT.ACLdev2023 with 33.4 BLEU points, demonstrating strong adaptation to new test sets
- Effective use of SegAugment synthetic data for domain adaptation to custom segmentations
- Siamese pretraining with CTC and OT losses enables effective transfer learning from pretrained MT foundation models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Pretraining speech and text encoders separately before joint fine-tuning aligns their internal representations, improving speech translation performance. The Siamese pretraining step uses CTC loss for speech-to-character alignment and Optimal Transport loss for speech-to-text representation alignment. By freezing the text encoder during this step, the text decoder's learned space is preserved while speech encoder representations are adapted to match.

### Mechanism 2
Decoupling acoustic and semantic modeling in the speech encoder, combined with CTC-based compression and coupling modules, reduces length mismatch between speech and text representations. Acoustic modeling generates raw speech features, which are compressed via CTC to remove repeated predictions and blanks, reducing sequence length. Coupling modules (adapter + 1D convolution) further transform the compressed representation to match sub-word level text representations.

### Mechanism 3
Knowledge distillation from a strong MT model during ST fine-tuning provides additional supervision that improves translation quality, especially for low-resource or out-of-domain conditions. The MT model's predictions are used as soft targets, with the KL divergence between MT and ST output distributions as part of the loss function. This guides the ST model to produce more fluent and accurate translations.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Transfer learning from foundation models
  - Why needed here: Foundation models (wav2vec 2.0, mBART50) have been pretrained on massive datasets and capture general speech and text representations that can be adapted to specific ST tasks with less data than training from scratch.
  - Quick check question: What are the key differences between fine-tuning a foundation model versus training a model from scratch on the same task?

- Concept: Optimal transport for representation alignment
  - Why needed here: OT provides a principled way to measure and minimize the distance between speech and text representations in a way that accounts for their different distributions and dimensionalities.
  - Quick check question: How does the Wasserstein distance (used in OT) differ from other distance metrics like Euclidean or cosine distance in the context of representation alignment?

- Concept: Knowledge distillation
  - Why needed here: KD allows a smaller or less capable model (the ST model) to learn from a larger, more capable model (the MT model), improving performance especially when data is limited.
  - Quick check question: What are the advantages and disadvantages of using hard targets (one-hot vectors) versus soft targets (probability distributions) in knowledge distillation?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Feature Extractor -> Acoustic Encoder -> CTC Compression -> Coupling Modules -> Semantic Encoder -> Text Decoder
- Critical path: Speech waveform → Feature Extractor → Acoustic Encoder → CTC Compression → Coupling Modules → Semantic Encoder → Text Decoder → Translation output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Freezing foundation model components vs. fine-tuning them: Freezing provides stability and leverages pretrained knowledge but may limit adaptation to task-specific patterns.
  - Length compression via CTC vs. other methods: CTC is efficient and aligns with ASR but may lose fine-grained temporal information.
  - Number and size of coupling modules: More parameters can improve adaptation but increase computational cost and risk of overfitting.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor translation quality: Check if speech and text representations are properly aligned (inspect OT loss during training).
  - Slow convergence: Verify that regularization parameters are appropriate and that the learning rate schedule matches the foundation model's characteristics.
  - Mode collapse: Ensure that the text decoder isn't being overwhelmed by the ST encoder's representations (monitor KL divergence during KD training).
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify Siamese pretraining alignment: Train a simple Siamese network with CTC and OT losses on a small speech-text dataset, visualize the learned representations, and confirm they become more similar over training.
  2. Test CTC compression effectiveness: Compare BLEU scores with and without CTC compression on a speech translation task, ensuring the compression doesn't degrade translation quality.
  3. Validate KD impact: Train two ST models with identical architectures but one with KD supervision, comparing their performance on a held-out validation set to quantify the benefit of KD.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the performance of the Siamese pretraining approach vary when applied to different foundation models for speech and text, such as HuBERT or SpeechLM, instead of wav2vec 2.0 and mBART50? The paper mentions that most encoders exhibited a comparable level of performance in preliminary experiments, but SpeechLM results were noticeably below expectations, leading to a suspicion of a bug in the implementation.

### Open Question 2
What is the impact of varying the hyperparameters, such as learning rate and regularization, on the performance of the Siamese pretraining approach and the overall speech translation system? The paper mentions that hyperparameters were optimized for a specific speech encoder, and a reduction in the learning rate might boost HuBERT's performance.

### Open Question 3
How does the Siamese pretraining approach perform in zero-shot settings, where the model is trained on one language pair and evaluated on another? The paper mentions that the authors plan to investigate the zero-shot capabilities of optimal transport in the context of foundation models in future work.

## Limitations
- Architecture details of coupling modules between acoustic and semantic encoders are underspecified
- Lack of ablation studies isolating the impact of each component (Siamese pretraining, KD, SegAugment)
- Results reported on only three test sets without comprehensive cross-validation across different domains or languages

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The core methodology of using foundation models with Siamese pretraining and knowledge distillation is well-established in the literature and the experimental setup is clearly described.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of Optimal Transport for speech-text alignment is supported by theoretical motivation and moderate empirical gains, but lacks detailed analysis of representation quality or convergence behavior.
- **Low Confidence**: The contribution of SegAugment synthetic data is difficult to evaluate since the paper doesn't report performance without this augmentation.

## Next Checks

1. **Component Ablation Study**: Retrain the system with Siamese pretraining disabled, then with KD disabled, then with SegAugment disabled, measuring the isolated contribution of each component to overall BLEU score.
2. **Representation Analysis**: Visualize the speech and text encoder representations before and after Siamese pretraining using t-SNE or UMAP to confirm that OT loss actually improves cross-modal alignment.
3. **Cross-Domain Evaluation**: Test the trained models on out-of-domain speech translation datasets (e.g., from different topics or recording conditions) to assess robustness beyond the IWSLT benchmarks.
