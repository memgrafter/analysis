# SummQA at MEDIQA-Chat 2023:In-Context Learning with GPT-4 for Medical Summarization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17384
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17384
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: 3rd place in Task A, 4th place in Task B, 2nd place overall in MEDIQA-Chat 2023 Shared Task

## Executive Summary
This paper presents SummQA, a system for medical dialogue summarization in the MEDIQA-Chat 2023 Shared Task. The approach leverages GPT-4's in-context learning capabilities with a two-stage process: semantic similarity-based example selection and few-shot prompting. For section-wise summarization (Task A), the system retrieves top-k similar dialogues as in-context examples, while for full-note summarization (Task B), it uses k=1. The system achieved strong results, placing 3rd in Task A, 4th in Task B, and 2nd place among all teams. The authors find that GPT-4 generates more abstractive and shorter summaries compared to fine-tuned models like T5 and BioBERT.

## Method Summary
SummQA uses GPT-4 with in-context learning for medical dialogue summarization. The approach involves encoding training dialogues with MiniLM, calculating cosine similarity to retrieve top-k similar examples, and constructing prompts with these examples for GPT-4. For Task A (section-wise summarization), k=7 examples are used, while for Task B (full-note summarization), k=1 is used due to context limits. An ensemble of BioBERT and GPT-4 is employed for section header classification, using BioBERT for three specific headers where it performs well and GPT-4 otherwise.

## Key Results
- Achieved 3rd place in Task A, 4th place in Task B, and 2nd place overall in MEDIQA-Chat 2023
- GPT-4 summaries are more abstractive and shorter than fine-tuned models (T5, BioBERT)
- Ensembled BioBERT and GPT-4 approach improves section header classification accuracy
- Outperformed several fine-tuned baselines across both tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: In-context learning with GPT-4 is effective for medical summarization due to the model's ability to leverage semantically similar examples from the training data.
- Mechanism: The system uses semantic similarity (via MiniLM embeddings and cosine similarity) to retrieve relevant dialogue-summary pairs from the training set. These examples are then used as few-shot demonstrations in the GPT-4 prompt, providing the model with context-specific guidance for generating summaries.
- Core assumption: GPT-4's performance is enhanced when provided with examples that are semantically similar to the input dialogue.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our approach for section-wise summarization (Task A) is a two-stage process of selecting semantically similar dialogues and using the top-k similar dialogues as in-context examples for GPT-4."
  - [section] "We encode each dialogue in the training data with MiniLM (Wang et al., 2020). For each dialogue to be summarized, we calculate cosine similarities with encoded dialogues from the training data. We retrieve k=7 similar examples from the training data based on the highest similarity."
- Break condition: If the training data lacks sufficient diversity or the input dialogue is too dissimilar from any training examples, the semantic similarity approach may fail to find relevant in-context examples.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GPT-4 generates more abstractive and shorter summaries compared to fine-tuned models like T5 and BioBERT.
- Mechanism: GPT-4, when prompted to summarize, tends to produce concise summaries that prioritize abstractive content over extractive content. This is in contrast to fine-tuned models which may produce longer, more extractive summaries.
- Core assumption: The summarization behavior of GPT-4 is influenced by its pre-training and the nature of few-shot prompting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We find that GPT-4 summaries are more abstractive and shorter."
  - [section] "We analyze the differences in outputs between the settings in output length and extractive ability. We find that the summaries generated by LLMs tend to be shorter and less extractive than human-generated summaries as well as SOTA fine-tuned biomedical summarization models."
- Break condition: If the task requires highly extractive summaries or if the input dialogues are very long, the abstractive and shorter nature of GPT-4's summaries may not be desirable.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Ensembling BioBERT and GPT-4 for section header classification improves accuracy by leveraging the strengths of both models.
- Mechanism: BioBERT is used for section headers where it performs well (ROS, GENHX, CC), while GPT-4 is used for other sections. This ensemble approach combines the domain-specific knowledge of BioBERT with the broader language understanding of GPT-4.
- Core assumption: Different models have varying strengths for different section headers, and an ensemble can leverage these strengths.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We observe empirically that our prompting-based approaches do not perform well on three of the section headers: ROS (Review of Systems), GENHX (History of the present illness), and CC (Chief Complaint), To create an ensemble classifier, we select BioBERT’s predictions when it classifies a dialogue as one of these three section headers, and we select the prediction of our GPT-4 based pipeline otherwise."
  - [section] "The overall accuracies are shown in Table 2."
- Break condition: If the performance gap between BioBERT and GPT-4 for specific section headers is not significant, the ensemble approach may not provide a substantial benefit.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: In-context learning
  - Why needed here: In-context learning allows GPT-4 to learn from a few examples without requiring fine-tuning, which is beneficial in low-resource settings like medical summarization where data is limited and privacy concerns restrict data sharing.
  - Quick check question: How does in-context learning differ from traditional fine-tuning in terms of model adaptation and data requirements?

- Concept: Semantic similarity and cosine similarity
  - Why needed here: Semantic similarity is used to retrieve relevant examples from the training data, which serve as in-context demonstrations for GPT-4. Cosine similarity is a common metric for measuring the similarity between vector representations of text.
  - Quick check question: Why is cosine similarity a suitable metric for comparing the semantic similarity of medical dialogues and summaries?

- Concept: Ensemble methods
  - Why needed here: Ensemble methods combine the predictions of multiple models to improve overall accuracy. In this case, an ensemble of BioBERT and GPT-4 is used for section header classification to leverage the strengths of both models.
  - Quick check question: What are the potential benefits and drawbacks of using an ensemble approach for classification tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input dialogue -> MiniLM encoder -> Cosine similarity calculation -> Top-k example retrieval -> GPT-4 prompt construction -> GPT-4 summary generation
  BioBERT model (for section header classification) -> Ensemble with GPT-4 predictions -> Output section headers

- Critical path:
  1. Encode input dialogue with MiniLM.
  2. Calculate cosine similarity with training dialogues.
  3. Retrieve top-k similar examples.
  4. Construct GPT-4 prompt with examples.
  5. Generate summary using GPT-4.
  6. Classify section headers using BioBERT/GPT-4 ensemble.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using GPT-4 vs. fine-tuned models: GPT-4 offers strong few-shot performance but may be less consistent and more expensive than fine-tuned models.
  - k value for in-context examples: Higher k provides more context but may exceed context limits and increase inference time.
  - Ensemble vs. single model: Ensemble can improve accuracy but adds complexity and inference time.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low semantic similarity scores for retrieved examples: May indicate poor example selection or insufficient diversity in training data.
  - Inconsistent summary quality across runs: Could be due to prompt sensitivity or model randomness.
  - Poor section header classification accuracy: May indicate model limitations or imbalanced training data.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary k (number of in-context examples) and measure impact on summary quality and inference time.
  2. Compare different semantic similarity metrics (e.g., cosine similarity vs. other distance measures) for example retrieval.
  3. Evaluate the impact of prompt template variations on GPT-4's summary generation quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal number of in-context examples (k) for medical dialogue summarization across different medical domains and dialogue lengths?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions evaluating the impact of varying k in their experiments, noting that "We observe a general improvement across all metrics as we increase k" for Task A, but couldn't perform the same ablation for Task B due to context limits.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests k values up to 7 for Task A and k=1 for Task B, without exploring the full range of possible values or considering different medical specialties that might require different k values.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments testing k values across 1-10 (or higher) for both tasks, across multiple medical specialties, measuring performance metrics and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the stability and reproducibility of few-shot prompting be improved for medical dialogue summarization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that "despite setting the temperature (T) as 0 for all decodings in our experiments, we observe the variance in the generated summaries across runs" and notes that "in-context learning has shown to be susceptible to changes in order of in-context examples."
- Why unresolved: The authors note this as a limitation but don't propose concrete solutions, and existing research shows that few-shot prompting is inherently unstable due to prompt sensitivity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of techniques (such as calibration methods, prompt combination strategies, or automated prompt generation) that consistently produce stable outputs across multiple runs and different model versions.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the trade-off between summary length, extractiveness, and clinical utility in medical dialogue summarization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper finds that "summaries generated by LLMs tend to be shorter and less extractive than human-generated summaries" and that "individual section summaries, when combined together to produce a full-length summary are closer to the original length rather than prompting GPT-4 to generate a complete summary together."
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't investigate whether the shorter, more abstractive summaries are actually more useful for clinical practice, or how extractiveness relates to downstream clinical decision-making quality.
- What evidence would resolve it: Clinical evaluation studies comparing the utility of shorter, more abstractive summaries versus longer, more extractive summaries in actual medical decision-making scenarios, measuring both efficiency and accuracy of clinical outcomes.

## Limitations
- Limited test samples (50 dialogues for Task A, 33 for Task B) may not generalize to broader medical summarization contexts
- Lack of ablation studies on prompt engineering variations and semantic similarity thresholds
- Acknowledged instability in few-shot prompting performance across different runs

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The overall ranking achievement (3rd in Task A, 4th in Task B, 2nd overall) is verifiable through competition results. The methodology of using semantic similarity for in-context example selection is clearly described and technically sound.

- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that GPT-4 generates shorter and more abstractive summaries than fine-tuned models is supported by metrics analysis, but the comparison lacks direct benchmarking against specific state-of-the-art biomedical summarization models beyond T5 and BioBERT.

- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of the BioBERT+GPT-4 ensemble for section header classification is demonstrated through accuracy metrics, but the paper doesn't provide detailed analysis of error cases or the specific conditions under which each model should be preferred.

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation Study on In-Context Examples**: Systematically vary k (number of examples) from 1 to 10 and measure the impact on ROUGE scores, summary length, and EFC/EFD metrics to determine the optimal trade-off between context richness and summary quality.

2. **Prompt Engineering Analysis**: Test multiple prompt templates with different instruction phrasings, example orderings, and context instructions to quantify the variance in GPT-4's summary generation quality and identify the most stable prompt configuration.

3. **Cross-Dataset Generalization**: Evaluate the trained ensemble classifier and GPT-4 summarization approach on an independent medical dialogue dataset (e.g., medical conversation datasets from MIMIC-III or other clinical sources) to assess robustness beyond the MEDIQA-Chat domain.
