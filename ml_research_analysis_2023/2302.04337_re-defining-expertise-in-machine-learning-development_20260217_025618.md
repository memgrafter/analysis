# (Re)Defining Expertise in Machine Learning Development

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04337
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04337
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Systematic review of 96 ML publications reveals expertise is frequently unspecified and experts are often limited to data labeling tasks rather than higher-level system design

## Executive Summary
This systematic literature review examines how expertise is defined and recognized in machine learning development. Analyzing 96 ML publications, the researchers found that expertise criteria are frequently unspecified, with 38 publications providing no explicit basis for selecting experts. Additionally, experts were primarily engaged as data workers (annotating data or providing term definitions) in 25 publications, while only 2 publications involved experts in higher-level system design decisions. The study highlights the need for explicit documentation of expertise criteria and broader engagement of experts beyond data labeling tasks to ensure diverse perspectives are incorporated and expertise is not deskilled in ML development.

## Method Summary
The study employed systematic literature review methodology, analyzing 96 peer-reviewed ML publications that involved system development. The researchers used open coding to examine three key aspects of each publication: the basis for defining expertise, the development phase in which experts contributed, and the role/participation of experts in the system. The analysis aimed to identify patterns in how expertise is defined, recognized, and utilized in ML development practices.

## Key Results
- 38 of 96 ML publications provided no explicit criteria for selecting experts
- Experts were primarily engaged as data workers (annotating data or providing term definitions) in 25 publications
- Only 2 publications involved experts in higher-level system design decisions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Explicitly defining expertise criteria improves scientific reproducibility and reduces representational harms
- Mechanism: When ML publications specify the basis for selecting domain experts, it creates a transparent chain of reasoning that allows others to evaluate whether the chosen experts represent the full spectrum of relevant knowledge and whether their perspectives are adequately captured
- Core assumption: Different experts may hold varying "ground truths" based on cultural practices or training, and failing to document these differences masks important variations in knowledge representation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Failing to document explicit reasoning for determining expertise introduces issues for scientific reproducibility by under-specifying methodological details, as well as masks the reality of multiple ground truths"
  - [section] "38 of the 96 publications did not provide any explicit criteria that motivated the inclusion of the experts or non-experts engaged in system development or evaluation"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus matches for reproducibility or ground truth concepts
- Break condition: If domain knowledge is truly universal and homogeneous, explicit documentation becomes less critical, though this is rarely the case in practice

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Current ML practice deskills expert knowledge by limiting experts to data labeling tasks
- Mechanism: When experts are primarily engaged as data annotators rather than decision-makers in system design, their tacit knowledge and higher-order expertise are not incorporated into the system, reducing their role to that of data providers
- Core assumption: Expert knowledge extends beyond explicit facts to include tacit understanding, contextual judgment, and strategic decision-making that cannot be captured through annotation alone
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experts were frequently engaged as data workers (25 publications), annotating data or providing term definitions, rather than participating in higher-level system design decisions (only 2 publications)"
  - [section] "This points to the use of experts as a 'repository of knowledge' that can regurgitate domain-specific facts, ignoring the breadth of skills that constitute expertise"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus focuses on ML libraries and platforms rather than expert engagement patterns
- Break condition: If the ML system's success depends primarily on labeled data quality rather than expert judgment in design decisions, this deskilling may be acceptable

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Participatory AI approaches require explicit expertise documentation to ensure equitable outcomes
- Mechanism: As ML development increasingly incorporates participatory approaches, explicitly documenting expertise criteria helps ensure that diverse perspectives are genuinely included rather than tokenized
- Core assumption: Expanded participation in ML does not automatically lead to improved equitable outcomes without careful attention to whose expertise is recognized and how it is incorporated
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "From a responsible AI perspective, should also recognize and evaluate against expertise rooted in different cultural practices and lived experiences"
  - [section] "On the backdrop of recent critiques pointing out that expanded participation in ML does not necessarily improve equitable outcomes"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus contains papers on AI in organizational settings and robotics but not specifically on participatory AI equity
- Break condition: If the ML system operates in a context where cultural diversity and lived experience are irrelevant to system performance

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Systematic literature review methodology
  - Why needed here: The study relies on systematic coding of 96 ML publications to identify patterns in how expertise is defined and used
  - Quick check question: What are the key inclusion criteria that determine which papers are analyzed in this systematic review?

- Concept: Expertise definition and recognition
  - Why needed here: Understanding how different fields define expertise is crucial for interpreting why ML publications may leave expertise criteria unspecified
  - Quick check question: What are the three distinct bases for defining expertise mentioned in the literature review?

- Concept: Deskilling in AI development
  - Why needed here: The paper critiques how expert knowledge is reduced to data labeling tasks, which requires understanding the concept of deskilling
  - Quick check question: How does treating experts primarily as data annotators potentially deskill their traditional expertise?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Search term generation from initial pre-search -> Publication filtering by relevance criteria -> Open coding of three aspects per paper (expertise definition basis, development phase contribution, role/participation) -> Thematic analysis to identify patterns
- Critical path: The most time-consuming step is the open coding phase where each publication must be analyzed for expertise criteria, development phase contributions, and participation roles
- Design tradeoffs: The choice to focus on ML publications that involve system development (rather than theoretical papers) means missing potentially relevant discussions of expertise in adjacent fields
- Failure signatures: Incomplete coding leads to inaccurate patterns; inconsistent application of inclusion criteria results in sampling bias; failure to capture implicit expertise definitions misses important cases
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate coding scheme by having two researchers independently code 10 sample papers and calculate inter-rater reliability
  2. Test search term effectiveness by running the pre-search with different term combinations and comparing result overlap
  3. Conduct a pilot analysis on 20 papers to refine the thematic categories before full-scale analysis

## Limitations
- The study focuses only on ML publications involving system development, potentially missing relevant expertise discussions in theoretical or methodological papers
- Analysis cannot definitively establish whether unspecified expertise criteria reflect actual methodological gaps versus implicit community standards
- Claims about deskilling assume that data annotation represents deskilling, though some practitioners may view annotation as legitimate expert contribution

## Confidence
- Descriptive findings about expertise definition patterns: High
- Causal claims about deskilling and representational harms: Medium
- Claims about reproducibility impacts: Medium

## Next Checks
