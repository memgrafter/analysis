# Efficient Online Processing with Deep Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.13474
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13474
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Novel frameworks for efficient online processing of time-series data with deep neural networks, achieving up to 108× FLOPs reduction and 26× throughput increase.

## Executive Summary
This dissertation addresses the computational and memory challenges of online processing with deep neural networks (DNNs), focusing on time-series data. The work introduces two key frameworks: Continual Inference Networks (CINs) for transforming state-of-the-art DNNs into efficient online architectures, and Structured Pruning Adapters (SPAs) for adapting large models to lightweight versions. The proposed methods demonstrate significant improvements in efficiency trade-offs while maintaining predictive performance across various tasks, including human activity recognition and image classification.

## Method Summary
The dissertation proposes two complementary approaches to efficient online processing. Continual Inference Networks (CINs) transform existing DNN architectures by reusing pre-trained weights and enabling efficient incremental processing, achieving dramatic reductions in computational complexity. Structured Pruning Adapters (SPAs) provide a method for adapting large source models to smaller derived models using fusible adapters and structured pruning, maintaining accuracy while significantly reducing parameters. Both frameworks are designed to work with existing DNN architectures and pre-trained models, enabling practical deployment without extensive retraining.

## Key Results
- CINs achieve up to 108× FLOPs reduction and 26× throughput increase on CPUs/GPUs compared to traditional sliding-window processing
- SPAs achieve 19× fewer learned parameters at 30% density with only 1.60% lower accuracy compared to fine-tuning with pruning
- The frameworks maintain predictive performance while significantly improving computational and memory efficiency for online processing tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The efficiency gains stem from CINs' ability to reuse pre-trained weights and process data incrementally rather than through full recomputation, eliminating redundant calculations inherent in sliding-window approaches. SPAs work by strategically pruning model components while maintaining critical pathways through fusible adapters, preserving essential learned representations while reducing parameter count and computational load.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses primarily on human activity recognition and image classification tasks, limiting generalizability
- Runtime performance on resource-constrained edge devices is not explicitly validated
- Long-term continual learning capabilities and handling of concept drift are not addressed

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Computational efficiency improvements for 3D CNNs and ST-GCNs under sliding-window processing
- Medium Confidence: Structured pruning adapter effectiveness for parameter reduction while maintaining accuracy
- Low Confidence: Generalizability of CINs to arbitrary DNN architectures and online processing domains

## Next Checks
1. Validate CINs and SPAs on diverse online processing tasks (e.g., speech recognition, multivariate time series forecasting) to assess generalizability beyond activity recognition and image classification

3. Extend the continual inference framework to include online adaptation mechanisms and evaluate performance under concept drift and catastrophic forgetting scenarios using benchmark datasets like CORe50 or OmniLog
