# Assessing Word Importance Using Models Trained for Semantic Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19689
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19689
- Reference count: 38
- Primary result: Proposed method computes word importance scores using attribution techniques and binary masks to explain predictions of models trained for semantic tasks

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel method for assessing word importance using models trained for semantic tasks like natural language inference (NLI) and paraphrase identification (PI). The approach leverages an underlying model and an interpreter that generates binary masks to identify the most critical words in a sentence. The interpreter minimizes a loss function that balances sparsity (L0 term) with preserving the underlying model's decision (divergence term). The resulting word importance scores are derived from the interpreter's output and are shown to be robust across tasks and correlated with syntactic patterns, offering a new perspective on semantic importance in text.

## Method Summary
The method involves training an interpreter to generate binary masks over input sentences, identifying the most important words for a given model's prediction. The interpreter works in conjunction with an underlying model (trained for NLI or PI tasks) and minimizes a loss function comprising an L0 term (to enforce sparsity) and a divergence term (to preserve the model's decision). The word importance scores are derived from the interpreter's output, reflecting the contribution of each word to the model's prediction. The approach is evaluated using a cross-task assessment, demonstrating robustness and alignment with syntactic patterns.

## Key Results
- Word importance scores are robust across different initial tasks (NLI and PI).
- Scores reflect semantic importance and correlate with syntactic patterns, revealing a relationship between semantic importance and syntactic position.
- The method provides interpretable binary masks, enhancing transparency in identifying critical words.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging attribution techniques to explain model predictions through binary masks. The interpreter identifies the most important words by minimizing a loss function that balances sparsity and decision preservation. This approach ensures that the identified words are both minimal and sufficient to maintain the model's prediction, capturing the essence of semantic importance.

## Foundational Learning

- **Binary Masking**: Simplifies word importance by reducing it to a binary decision. *Why needed*: Enhances interpretability by focusing on essential words. *Quick check*: Validate mask sparsity and decision preservation.
- **Cross-Task Evaluation**: Assesses robustness by testing across different semantic tasks. *Why needed*: Ensures generalizability of word importance scores. *Quick check*: Compare scores across tasks for consistency.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Model (NLI/PI) -> Interpreter (Binary Mask Generator) -> Word Importance Scores

### Critical Path
1. Input sentence processed by the underlying model.
2. Interpreter generates binary mask to identify important words.
3. Loss function (L0 + divergence) minimized to refine mask.
4. Word importance scores derived from refined mask.

### Design Tradeoffs
- **Binary vs. Soft Masks**: Binary masks enhance interpretability but may oversimplify word interactions.
- **Task Specificity**: Training on NLI/PI tasks may limit generalizability to other semantic domains.
- **Loss Function Balance**: Balancing sparsity and decision preservation is critical for accurate importance scores.

### Failure Signatures
- Overemphasis on sparsity leading to loss of critical context.
- Inconsistent scores across tasks indicating task dependency.
- Misalignment with syntactic patterns suggesting semantic-syntactic disconnect.

### 3 First Experiments
1. Test word importance scores on a held-out dataset to validate robustness.
2. Compare scores with human judgments to assess interpretability.
3. Evaluate the impact of varying mask granularity (binary vs. soft) on accuracy.

- Evaluation assumes chosen tasks adequately capture semantic importance, which may not hold universally.
