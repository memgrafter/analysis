# A Theory on Adam Instability in Large-Scale Machine Learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.09871
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09871
- **Reference count**: 5
- **Primary result**: Proposes theory explaining Adam optimizer instabilities in large-scale language model training through bimodal update distributions

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a theory explaining previously unexplained divergent behavior observed during training of large language models. The authors argue that Adam optimizer can enter a state where parameter update vectors have large norms but are uncorrelated with the loss landscape descent direction, causing divergence. This phenomenon is more likely to occur in deep models with large batch sizes, which is typical for large-scale language model training. The theory is supported by observations from training runs of models ranging from 7 billion to 546 billion parameters.

## Method Summary
The study investigates training instability in large-scale language models using AdamW optimizer with specific hyperparameters (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, ε=1e-8). Models of varying sizes (7B, 30B, 65B, 546B parameters) are trained on large datasets with different batch sizes, monitoring perplexity and gradient norms. The analysis focuses on the distribution of Adam updates (u[i,t]) and ratios (r[i,t]) for different layers during training to identify potential instabilities. The reproduction plan requires implementing transformer models with ReLU activation and absolute positional embeddings, training with specified hyperparameters, and analyzing update distributions across layers.

## Key Results
- Adam instability manifests as bimodal distribution of update components when gradient norms for certain layers approach the stability parameter ε
- Earlier layers in deep models are most susceptible to instability due to vanishing gradients
- Large batch sizes increase the probability of time-domain correlation in gradient estimates, exacerbating instability
- The phenomenon occurs across model scales from 7B to 546B parameters, with increasing severity in larger models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adam instability arises when the update vector's norm is large and its direction is uncorrelated with the loss landscape descent direction.
- Mechanism: When the ratio mt/√vt becomes bimodal (due to time-domain correlation in gradient estimates), the update vector aligns with the sign of the gradient rather than its magnitude, causing steps that can move away from the loss minimum.
- Core assumption: The time-domain independence between gradient estimation components is violated for certain layers, particularly earlier ones with vanishing gradients.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Adam can enter a state in which the parameter update vector has a relatively large norm and is essentially uncorrelated with the direction of descent on the training loss landscape"
  - [section] "The distribution of the components of the ratior[:,t ] can turn from bimodal to a uni-modal and back without a prominent spike in the model perplexity"
  - [corpus] Found related work on Adam instabilities, though specific evidence for this mechanism is weak
- Break condition: When the gradient norm for earlier layers becomes comparable to or larger than the stability parameter ε, causing the update distribution to shift from spiked at zero to bimodal.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Vanishing gradients in earlier layers create conditions where the Adam update distribution becomes concentrated around zero, setting up conditions for instability.
- Mechanism: As gradient norms for certain layers approach ε, the update components u[i,t] become extremely small, causing the optimizer state (mt, vt) to also shrink. This creates time-domain correlation in gradient estimates as parameters remain essentially unchanged.
- Core assumption: Larger models experience more severe vanishing gradient effects in earlier layers due to architectural properties.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "The gradients of a group of parameters of the model (e.g. a layer, let us denote it with G) are vanishing (≪ε) over the course of training"
  - [section] "From our observations, this is most likely to happen in the earlier layers of the model, where a good feature representation has been learned"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers exist but don't directly support this specific mechanism
- Break condition: When the gradient estimation norm for earlier layers increases above ε, triggering the bimodal distribution of the ratio r[i,t] and subsequent divergence.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Large batch sizes increase the probability of time-domain correlation in gradient estimates, making instability more likely in large-scale training.
- Mechanism: With large batch sizes, gradient estimates have low time-domain variance, increasing the chance that consecutive gradient estimates are highly correlated. This correlation, combined with vanishing gradients, leads to the bimodal distribution that causes instability.
- Core assumption: The relationship between batch size and gradient estimation variance is critical for maintaining time-domain independence.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "This artifact is more likely to be observed in the training of a deep model with a large batch size"
  - [section] "The batch size for a large model is usually also very large, thus the gradient evaluations have small time-domain variance"
  - [corpus] Limited direct evidence in corpus for this specific claim about batch size effects
- Break condition: When batch size is reduced or gradient estimation variance increases sufficiently to restore time-domain independence.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the specific mechanisms by which the time-domain correlation of gradient estimations leads to the formation of a bimodal distribution in the update components?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the formation of a bimodal distribution in the update components when there is a strong time-domain correlation between gradient estimations, but does not fully explain the underlying mechanisms.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides a theoretical framework and empirical observations but does not delve into the detailed mechanisms of how the correlation leads to the bimodal distribution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further theoretical analysis and empirical studies focusing on the specific steps in the Adam algorithm where the correlation affects the update components could provide insights into the mechanisms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the size of the batch influence the severity of the training instabilities observed in large-scale models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that a large batch size is more likely to cause training instabilities due to the small time-domain variance in gradient evaluations.
- Why unresolved: While the paper discusses the correlation between batch size and training instabilities, it does not provide a quantitative analysis of how different batch sizes affect the severity of instabilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with varying batch sizes and measuring the frequency and severity of instabilities could help establish a clearer relationship.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the long-term effects of training instabilities on the performance of large-scale models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper discusses the immediate effects of instabilities, such as loss spikes, but does not explore the long-term impact on model performance.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the immediate phenomenon of loss spikes and does not extend its analysis to the broader implications for model performance over time.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies comparing the performance of models trained with and without instabilities could provide insights into the long-term effects.

## Limitations
- The evidence primarily relies on observational correlations rather than controlled ablation studies
- The proposed mechanisms, while theoretically sound, lack direct experimental validation in the paper
- The role of batch size in inducing time-domain correlation is particularly weakly supported with limited empirical evidence

## Confidence
- **Medium**: The core hypothesis that Adam instability arises from bimodal distributions in update components when gradients vanish relative to ε
- **Medium**: The architectural claim that earlier layers are most susceptible to instability due to vanishing gradients
- **Low**: The specific mechanism linking large batch sizes to increased time-domain correlation and instability frequency

## Next Checks
1. Conduct controlled experiments varying ε across multiple orders of magnitude while measuring the distribution of update components and training stability, to establish the critical threshold where instability emerges.

2. Implement a diagnostic tool to measure time-domain correlation in gradient estimates during training, and systematically vary batch size to quantify its relationship with correlation strength and instability occurrence.

3. Design an intervention that explicitly decorrelates gradient estimates across time steps (e.g., by introducing gradient noise or reducing batch size) and measure whether this prevents the observed instabilities without compromising overall training efficiency.
