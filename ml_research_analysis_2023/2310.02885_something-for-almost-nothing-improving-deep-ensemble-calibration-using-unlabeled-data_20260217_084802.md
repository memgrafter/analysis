# Something for (almost) nothing: Improving deep ensemble calibration using unlabeled data

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2310.02885
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02885
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: ν-ensembles significantly improve calibration (lower ECE, TACE, Brier score, NLL) compared to standard ensembles for small to medium training sets while maintaining accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces ν-ensembles, a simple method to improve deep ensemble calibration using unlabeled data. The key innovation is assigning each ensemble member a different random label for each unlabeled data point, in addition to fitting true labels on the training set. This approach forces ensemble members to learn diverse features, which the authors show theoretically (via PAC-Bayes bounds) and empirically leads to better calibration and robustness to distribution shifts. The method is easy to implement and only requires tuning one additional hyperparameter.

## Method Summary
ν-ensembles train each ensemble member on both labeled training data (with true labels) and unlabeled data (with randomly assigned labels). For each unlabeled sample, K distinct random labels are drawn without replacement and assigned to different ensemble members. The training loss combines both tasks with a hyperparameter β controlling the weight of the unlabeled data loss. This enforces diversity among ensemble members, which the authors show improves calibration and generalization on test data.

## Key Results
- ν-ensembles achieve significantly better calibration (lower ECE, TACE, Brier score, NLL) than standard ensembles for small to medium training sets
- The method maintains comparable accuracy to standard ensembles while improving calibration
- ν-ensembles show robustness to various distribution shifts and improve ensemble diversity (lower mutual information between members)
- The approach is simple to implement and only requires an additional hyperparameter

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Randomizing labels for unlabeled data in ν-ensembles forces ensemble members to learn diverse features, which directly improves calibration and negative log-likelihood on test data.
- Mechanism: Each unlabeled sample is assigned K distinct random labels (one per ensemble member) without replacement. Because exactly one label matches the true class, the ensemble member with the correct label learns useful features, while the others learn spurious correlations. This enforced diversity reduces overconfidence and improves calibration.
- Core assumption: Deep neural networks can fit random labels perfectly, and the resulting feature diversity from random labels translates into better generalization on true labels.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our approach is extremely simple to implement: given an unlabeled set, for each unlabeled data point, we simply fit a different randomly selected label with each ensemble member."
  - [section] "We then show in the following proposition that it is actually not necessary to know the true label y. For each unlabeled sample x, it simply suffices to draw K labels randomly without replacement and assign each of them to a different member of the deep ensemble."
- Break condition: If the network cannot fit random labels well, or if the unlabeled data is too dissimilar from training data, the diversity gains may not translate to improved calibration.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The PAC-Bayes bound guarantees that optimizing for both low negative log-likelihood on training data and high diversity on unlabeled data yields a well-calibrated ensemble.
- Mechanism: The theoretical analysis shows that the expected test negative log-likelihood is bounded by the average training loss minus a diversity term (variance) plus complexity penalties. By maximizing variance on unlabeled data via random labeling, the bound becomes tighter, ensuring better generalization.
- Core assumption: The unlabeled data is drawn from the same distribution as the test data, and the PAC-Bayes bound holds with high probability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We provide a theoretical analysis based on a PAC-Bayes bound which guarantees that if we fit such a labeling on unlabeled data, and the true labels on the training data, we obtain low negative log-likelihood and high ensemble diversity on testing samples."
  - [section] "Theorem 1. With high probability over the training set Z and the unlabeled set U drawn from D, for an ensemble ..."
- Break condition: If the unlabeled data distribution differs significantly from the test distribution, the diversity term may not improve generalization.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: ν-ensembles retain standard ensemble accuracy while significantly improving calibration metrics (ECE, TACE, Brier score) for small to medium training sets.
- Mechanism: The empirical experiments show that ν-ensembles match standard ensemble accuracy but achieve lower calibration errors and higher ensemble diversity (lower mutual information between members). This is attributed to the forced diversity from random labeling.
- Core assumption: The improvements in calibration metrics are due to the increased diversity rather than overfitting to random labels.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Empirically, through detailed experiments, we find that for low to moderately-sized training sets, our ensembles are more diverse and provide better calibration than standard ensembles, sometimes significantly."
  - [section] "We see that ν-ensembles have comparable accuracy to standard ensembles but with significantly better calibration across all calibration metrics."
- Break condition: For large training sets, the random labeling may introduce noise that degrades performance, as seen in the "Limitations" section.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: PAC-Bayes generalization bounds
  - Why needed here: The paper relies on a PAC-Bayes bound to theoretically justify that diversity on unlabeled data improves test performance. Understanding how these bounds work is crucial for grasping the theoretical contribution.
  - Quick check question: What is the role of the variance term in the PAC-Bayes bound, and how does it relate to ensemble diversity?

- Concept: Ensemble diversity and its impact on calibration
  - Why needed here: The paper argues that diversity among ensemble members is key to reducing overconfidence and improving calibration. Knowing how diversity is measured (e.g., mutual information) and why it matters is essential.
  - Quick check question: How does increasing ensemble diversity typically affect calibration metrics like ECE or Brier score?

- Concept: Random labeling and memorization in deep networks
  - Why needed here: The method depends on the ability of deep networks to fit random labels. Understanding this phenomenon helps explain why the approach works and its limitations.
  - Quick check question: Why can deep neural networks fit random labels perfectly, and what are the implications for generalization?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data pipeline: Labeled training set (Z) -> Unlabeled set (U) -> Validation set (Zval) -> Test set (Ztest)
  Model: Ensemble of K neural networks (e.g., LeNet, MLP, ResNet)
  Training loop: For each ensemble member i: Train on Z (true labels) and Ui (random labels) with weighted loss
  Evaluation: Accuracy, ECE, TACE, Brier score, NLL, mutual information between ensemble members

- Critical path:
  1. Split data into Z, U, Zval, Ztest
  2. For each ensemble member i:
     - Create Ui by assigning random labels (without replacement) to each sample in U
     - Train member i on both Z (true labels) and Ui (random labels) with weighted loss
  3. Evaluate ensemble on Ztest for accuracy and calibration metrics

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Pros: Simple to implement, no extra hyperparameters beyond weight on random label loss, improves calibration for small/medium data
  - Cons: May degrade performance for large training sets, relies on unlabeled data availability, random labeling may introduce noise

- Failure signatures:
  - Calibration metrics (ECE, TACE) do not improve or worsen
  - Mutual information between ensemble members does not decrease
  - Accuracy drops significantly compared to standard ensembles
  - Training loss on random labels does not converge (network cannot fit random labels)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run standard ensemble on CIFAR-10 with 1000 training samples, record accuracy and ECE.
  2. Run ν-ensemble on same data, vary β (weight on random label loss), find best calibration improvement.
  3. Compare mutual information between ensemble members for both methods to confirm increased diversity.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper mentions that ν-ensembles could be combined with other ensemble diversity techniques like adversarial training or data augmentation to potentially further improve calibration and accuracy. However, the paper does not explore these combinations experimentally.

## Limitations

- The method relies on the assumption that unlabeled data shares the same distribution as test data, which may not hold in real-world scenarios with distribution shift
- Empirical validation focuses primarily on CIFAR datasets with relatively simple architectures, leaving open questions about scalability to more complex models and tasks
- For large training sets, the random labeling may introduce noise that degrades performance

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The mechanism of using random labels on unlabeled data to increase ensemble diversity is well-supported by empirical results and theoretical analysis
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that ν-ensembles significantly improve calibration for small to medium training sets is supported by experiments but limited to specific datasets and architectures
- **Medium confidence**: The assertion that the method works "for almost nothing" (minimal additional cost) holds for the experimental setup but may not generalize to all scenarios
