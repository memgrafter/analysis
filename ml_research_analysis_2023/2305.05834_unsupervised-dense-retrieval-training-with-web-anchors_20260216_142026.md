# Unsupervised Dense Retrieval Training with Web Anchors

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Anchor-DR, an unsupervised dense retrieval method that leverages web anchors for contrastive learning. The approach trains a dense retriever to match anchor text with its linked document, exploiting the similarity between anchor-document pairs and query-document pairs in search. A key challenge is filtering out uninformative anchors, which the authors address using heuristic rules and a query classifier. Anchor-DR significantly outperforms existing unsupervised dense retrievers on MSMARCO and BEIR datasets, demonstrating the potential of using web anchors as a rich source of training data for unsupervised dense retrieval.

## Method Summary
Anchor-DR uses web anchors as training data for unsupervised dense retrieval. The method trains a dense retriever to match anchor text with its linked document using contrastive learning. To address the challenge of uninformative anchors, the authors employ heuristic rules and a query classifier to filter out low-quality anchor-document pairs. The filtered anchors are then used to create positive and negative pairs for training the dense retriever. The approach leverages the similarity between anchor-document pairs and query-document pairs in search, allowing the model to learn effective representations for retrieval tasks.

## Key Results
- Anchor-DR achieves a 5.3% improvement in NDCG@10 on MSMARCO compared to state-of-the-art unsupervised dense retrievers
- The method significantly outperforms existing approaches on BEIR datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness across multiple retrieval tasks
- Analysis reveals that anchor-document pairs share similar patterns with query-document pairs, validating the approach

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper argues that anchor-document pairs share similar patterns with query-document pairs in search, making them suitable for training dense retrievers. Web anchors often serve as concise summaries or descriptions of the linked content, similar to how queries represent user information needs. By leveraging this similarity, Anchor-DR can effectively learn to match queries with relevant documents using anchor-document pairs as training data. The method's success hinges on the quality of the anchor-document pairs, which is ensured through careful filtering and selection.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
Anchor Text -> Query Classifier -> Filtered Anchors -> Dense Retriever -> Document Embeddings

### Critical Path
The critical path in Anchor-DR involves filtering anchors using the query classifier, creating positive and negative pairs, and training the dense retriever using contrastive learning. The quality of the filtered anchors directly impacts the effectiveness of the learned representations.

### Design Tradeoffs
- Tradeoff between the quantity and quality of anchors used for training
- Balance between the complexity of the query classifier and its filtering effectiveness
- Choice of contrastive learning objectives and their impact on retrieval performance

### Failure Signatures
- Poor filtering of anchors may lead to noisy training data and degraded retrieval performance
- Ineffective query classifier may fail to identify high-quality anchors, limiting the method's effectiveness
- Suboptimal contrastive learning objectives may result in representations that do not generalize well to downstream tasks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness may vary across different domains and languages due to the characteristics of web anchors
- The quality of the learned representations depends on the effectiveness of the anchor filtering and query classifier
- The approach may not generalize well to retrieval tasks that significantly differ from search and question answering
