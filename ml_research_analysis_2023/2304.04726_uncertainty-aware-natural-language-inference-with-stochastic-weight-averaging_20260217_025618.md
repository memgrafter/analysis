# Uncertainty-Aware Natural Language Inference with Stochastic Weight Averaging

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04726
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04726
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 11
- Key outcome: SWAG improves NLI accuracy and better matches human annotation uncertainty compared to standard training

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces uncertainty modeling using Stochastic Weight Averaging-Gaussian (SWAG) to natural language understanding tasks, specifically natural language inference (NLI). The approach uses Bayesian methods to capture model uncertainty by maintaining a Gaussian posterior over model parameters during training. Experiments on SNLI and MNLI datasets show that SWAG consistently improves both prediction accuracy and correlation with human annotation disagreements compared to standard training and Stochastic Weight Averaging alone. Cross-entropy analysis demonstrates that SWAG better reflects human uncertainty in annotation decisions, with improvements in capturing cases of annotator disagreement.

## Method Summary
The method fine-tunes RoBERTa-base on NLI datasets while tracking the optimization trajectory for SWAG. After fine-tuning, SWAG computes mean and covariance estimates from the trajectory, then samples multiple parameter sets from the Gaussian posterior during inference. Predictions are averaged across N samples (typically 20) to produce final outputs. The approach aims to capture epistemic uncertainty and find wider optima in the loss landscape for better generalization. Cross-entropy between predicted distributions and human annotation distributions serves as the primary metric for evaluating uncertainty calibration.

## Key Results
- SWAG consistently improves prediction accuracy on SNLI and MNLI benchmarks compared to baseline RoBERTa
- Cross-entropy analysis shows SWAG better reflects human uncertainty in annotation decisions than standard training
- SWAG improves correlation with human annotation disagreements, particularly in cases with multiple annotator labels

- Claim: SWAG captures epistemic uncertainty in NLU by maintaining a Gaussian posterior over model parameters during training.
- Mechanism: SWAG maintains a low-rank plus diagonal covariance approximation over the optimization trajectory, enabling sampling of multiple parameter sets to capture uncertainty.
- Core assumption: The optimization trajectory contains sufficient information to approximate the true posterior distribution over model parameters.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SWAG improves generalization by finding wider optima in the loss landscape.
- Mechanism: By averaging over multiple parameter sets from the optimization trajectory, SWAG avoids sharp minima that generalize poorly and instead finds flatter regions.
- Core assumption: Wider optima correspond to better generalization performance.
- Evidence anchors:

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: SWAG better reflects human annotation uncertainty by matching the predicted distribution to the observed annotation distribution.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Base model: Pre-trained RoBERTa-base
  - SWAG wrapper: Maintains parameter samples and covariance estimates
  - Sampling layer: Generates N samples from the posterior during inference
  - Aggregation: Averages predictions from N samples

- Critical path:
  1. Fine-tune base model while tracking optimization trajectory
  2. Compute SWAG mean and covariance estimates
  3. During inference, sample N parameter sets from the posterior
  4. Average predictions across all N samples

- Design tradeoffs:
  - More samples (N) → better uncertainty estimates but slower inference
  - Higher rank in covariance approximation → more accurate uncertainty but more parameters to track
  - Using SWAG during fine-tuning vs. only during inference

- Failure signatures:
  - Uncertainty estimates collapse to near-zero variance
  - Cross-entropy with human annotations doesn't improve despite accuracy gains
  - Performance degrades significantly on out-of-distribution data

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare baseline RoBERTa vs. SWAG on SNLI with fixed N=20 samples
  2. Sweep over different values of N to find the sweet spot between accuracy and uncertainty calibration
  3. Test cross-dataset generalization (SNLI→MNLI) to verify uncertainty generalizes

## Limitations
- The mechanism by which SWAG maintains a low-rank plus diagonal covariance approximation is described but not empirically verified through ablation studies
- The assumption that human annotation distributions represent true task uncertainty (rather than noise or inconsistent annotation guidelines) is never validated
- Claims about SWAG "better reflecting human uncertainty" lack analysis of whether the uncertainty estimates are calibrated or meaningful for practical applications

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The experimental results showing improved accuracy on SNLI and MNLI datasets with SWAG vs. baseline are straightforward to verify and well-documented
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that SWAG finds "wider optima" for better generalization is supported by accuracy improvements but lacks direct evidence showing the loss landscape geometry
- **Low confidence**: The assertion that SWAG "better reflects human uncertainty" is the weakest claim - while cross-entropy improves, there's no analysis of whether the uncertainty estimates are calibrated or meaningful for practical applications

## Next Checks
1. **Uncertainty calibration test**: Perform proper calibration analysis by binning predictions by confidence and measuring accuracy in each bin to reveal whether uncertainty estimates are meaningful
2. **Ablation on covariance rank**: Systematically vary the rank K parameter in the low-rank plus diagonal approximation and measure how it affects both accuracy and cross-entropy correlation
3. **Out-of-distribution robustness**: Evaluate the SWAG models on out-of-domain NLI datasets (like SICK) and measure both accuracy drops and uncertainty increases to validate whether SWAG captures epistemic uncertainty that generalizes beyond the training distribution
