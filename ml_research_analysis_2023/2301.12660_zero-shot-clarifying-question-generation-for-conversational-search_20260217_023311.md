# Zero-shot Clarifying Question Generation for Conversational Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12660
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12660
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a zero-shot method for generating clarifying questions for conversational search systems using facet-constrained decoding and question templates.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of generating clarifying questions for ambiguous search queries in conversational search systems. The proposed approach generates clarifying questions without any conversational search data by leveraging search facets as constraints during decoding. The method uses a facet-constrained decoding algorithm to ensure generated questions include facet-relevant terms, employs multiple question templates to guide proper question formation, and ranks candidates using a weighted sequential dependency model. The approach significantly outperforms both zero-shot and supervised baselines on a benchmark dataset, generating 25.2% more natural questions and 18.1% more useful questions compared to the best baseline.

## Method Summary
The method generates clarifying questions using facet-constrained decoding with NeuroLogic Decoding to promote facet word inclusion, multiple question templates to guide interrogative form, and Weighted Sequential Dependency Model (WSDM) ranking to select the most contextually coherent questions. The approach operates in a zero-shot setting, requiring no conversational search training data. It takes a search query and extracted facets as input, generates multiple question candidates using different templates, applies facet constraints during decoding to ensure facet words appear in the output, and ranks the candidates based on local term proximity using WSDM.

## Key Results
- The proposed approach generates 25.2% more natural questions than the best baseline
- The method produces 18.1% more useful questions compared to the best baseline
- The zero-shot approach outperforms supervised baselines including QF-GPT and Prompt finetuning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Facet-constrained decoding ensures generated clarifying questions include query-relevant terms.
- Mechanism: NeuroLogic Decoding ranks candidates by facet word inclusion and filters to top-α token probability and top-β facet coverage, forcing facet words into the beam.
- Core assumption: Facet words are semantically central to disambiguating the query; including them will improve question quality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The proposed method uses a facet-constrained decoding algorithm that promotes the inclusion of facet words in the generated questions"
  - [section 3.1] "we employ an algorithm called NeuroLogic Decoding... which incorporates constraints as Conjunctive Normal Forms (CNF) and estimates the viability of each beam to satisfy constraints by sampling their future generations"
  - [corpus] Weak. Corpus shows no direct evidence; needs empirical coverage analysis to validate facet-word inclusion rates.
- Break condition: If facets are irrelevant or noisy (e.g., overly generic or misspelled), the constraint will mislead generation, producing confusing or misleading questions.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prompt-based initialization biases generation toward interrogative form.
- Mechanism: Starting decoding with pre-defined question templates ("would you like to know...") constrains the output to be a question rather than narrative text.
- Core assumption: GPT-2's pre-training distribution is dominated by declarative text; priming with interrogative cues shifts the output distribution toward questions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "employs multiple question templates to guide the generation of questions in the appropriate form"
  - [section 3.2] "we use clarifying question templates as the starting text of the generation and let the decoder generate the rest of question body"
  - [corpus] Weak. Corpus neighbors do not explicitly confirm template efficacy; validation requires manual inspection of generated question types.
- Break condition: If templates are too rigid or mismatched to query intent, the system may generate stilted or off-topic questions regardless of facet constraints.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Weighted Sequential Dependency Model (WSDM) ranking selects the most contextually coherent question.
- Mechanism: WSDM scores candidates based on co-occurrence of facet and query terms within a sliding window, favoring questions where these terms appear close together.
- Core assumption: Semantic relevance is captured by local term proximity; closer co-occurrence indicates stronger topical alignment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "Questions like 'do you need information about the map of South Africa' will be ranked higher than 'do you want to buy a map that is made in South Africa' because 'South Africa' and 'map' are closer in the first question"
  - [section 4.1] "We implement our own Weighted Sequential Dependency Model using the intuitively adjusted parameters: 𝜆𝑡 = 𝜆𝑜 = 𝜆𝑢 = 1, 𝜇 = 25"
  - [corpus] Weak. No corpus evidence directly supports WSDM's effectiveness here; effectiveness must be inferred from ablation results in Table 2/3.
- Break condition: If query terms are polysemous or facets are multi-word phrases, WSDM's proximity metric may incorrectly favor irrelevant candidates.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Beam search decoding and its limitations for constrained generation.
  - Why needed here: The baseline GPT-2 decoder cannot enforce facet-word inclusion; understanding beam search is prerequisite to grasping NeuroLogic's modifications.
  - Quick check question: How does standard beam search select next tokens, and what happens when no constraint filtering is applied?

- Concept: Prompt engineering and its effect on language model output distribution.
  - Why needed here: The system relies on templates to bias toward interrogative form; knowing how prompts steer generation is critical.
  - Quick check question: What happens to GPT-2's generated text distribution when the input prefix is changed from declarative to interrogative?

- Concept: Evaluation metrics for natural language generation beyond BLEU/ROUGE.
  - Why needed here: The paper proposes question-body-only evaluation to avoid template bias; understanding why standard metrics fail here is key.
  - Quick check question: Why might word-overlap metrics overrate a question that repeats template words but has a poor body?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Query + Facet → Prompt generator (8 templates) → Decoder (GPT-2 with NeuroLogic Decoding) → Ranker (WSDM) → Output (clarifying question)
- Critical path: Query → Facet extraction → Prompt concatenation → NeuroLogic-constrained generation → WSDM ranking → Output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Facet constraint strength (α, β) vs. diversity of outputs
  - Number of templates vs. generation coherence
  - Ranking method (WSDM vs. AutoScore) vs. robustness to template noise
- Failure signatures:
  - Facet words missing from outputs → check NeuroLogic filtering thresholds
  - Generated text is narrative, not question → inspect prompt concatenation
  - Rankings ignore topical relevance → verify WSDM window size and term matching
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary α and β in NeuroLogic Decoding; measure facet coverage and BLEU
  2. Replace WSDM with AutoScore; compare question-body-only BLEU and human usefulness scores
  3. Remove templates; generate from raw query+facet; compare naturalness scores

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed zero-shot facet-constrained approach compare to finetuning methods in terms of performance and generalization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares the proposed zero-shot facet-constrained approach to finetuning baselines, including QF-GPT and Prompt finetuning, and shows that the zero-shot approach outperforms them in both automatic metrics and human evaluation.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates the superiority of the zero-shot approach over finetuning methods, it does not provide insights into the underlying reasons for this performance gap. Further investigation is needed to understand the strengths and limitations of each approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of the generated clarifying questions from both approaches, including qualitative evaluation and analysis of the underlying mechanisms.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of question templates affect the quality and diversity of the generated clarifying questions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses eight question templates derived from common patterns in clarifying question answers. However, it does not explore the impact of different template choices on the generated questions.
- Why unresolved: The choice of templates can significantly influence the generated questions' quality, diversity, and relevance to the user's query. Investigating the effects of different template sets could provide valuable insights for improving the system.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with different sets of question templates and analysis of the resulting generated questions' quality, diversity, and relevance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the proposed zero-shot facet-constrained approach be extended to handle more complex and ambiguous queries?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on generating clarifying questions for relatively straightforward ambiguous queries. However, it does not address the challenges posed by more complex and nuanced queries.
- Why unresolved: Real-world search scenarios often involve queries with multiple interpretations, implicit context, and domain-specific terminology. Extending the approach to handle such cases would be a significant contribution to conversational search.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluation of the system's performance on a dataset of complex and ambiguous queries, including analysis of the generated questions' quality and relevance.

## Limitations

- Evaluation relies entirely on a single benchmark dataset (ClariQ-FKw) with 425 examples, limiting generalizability to other domains or query types
- Human evaluation methodology is described but not fully specified - lacks details on annotator count, expertise level, and inter-annotator agreement statistics
- The "zero-shot" claim requires scrutiny since the approach depends on pre-trained GPT-2 and NeuroLogic Decoding, which were trained on large conversational datasets

## Confidence

- **High confidence** in the core technical contribution: The facet-constrained decoding algorithm and template-based generation are well-described and theoretically sound
- **Medium confidence** in effectiveness claims: While automatic metrics show strong improvements (25.2% more natural, 18.1% more useful), these rely on a single dataset and human evaluation details are sparse
- **Low confidence** in zero-shot purity: The dependence on pre-trained models with unknown conversational exposure weakens the zero-shot characterization

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on facet relevance**: Systematically remove facet words from test queries to measure degradation in question quality, validating that facets are genuinely necessary rather than merely correlated with better questions

2. **Cross-dataset evaluation**: Apply the method to a different clarifying question dataset (e.g., from a different domain like customer service or technical support) to assess generalizability beyond search queries

3. **Human evaluation replication**: Conduct a controlled human evaluation with detailed methodology documentation, including multiple annotators, agreement statistics, and comparison to supervised baselines on the same annotation task
