# DCMT: A Direct Entire-Space Causal Multi-Task Framework for Post-Click Conversion Estimation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06141
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06141
- Reference count: 40
- Improves state-of-the-art methods by 1.07% in CVR AUC offline

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses selection bias and data sparsity in post-click conversion rate (CVR) prediction through a Direct entire-space Causal Multi-Task framework (DCMT). The framework introduces a counterfactual mechanism that predicts both factual CVR and counterfactual CVR under a counterfactual prior knowledge constraint. By constructing a "counterfactual non-click space" and using a twin tower architecture, DCMT debiases selection bias in the entire exposure space. Extensive offline experiments on five public datasets demonstrate superior performance over state-of-the-art methods, with additional online A/B tests on Alipay Search platform confirming real-world effectiveness.

## Method Summary
DCMT employs a twin tower architecture with shared parameters for CTR and CVR tasks, and task-specific parameters for conversion and non-conversion decisions. The model predicts factual CVR in the click space and counterfactual CVR in a constructed non-click space, constrained by the prior knowledge that their sum should equal approximately 1. Inverse propensity weighting with self-normalization is applied to both factual and counterfactual spaces, creating a "debiasing circle" where corrections in one space affect the other. The framework is trained using Adam optimizer with a combined loss function incorporating counterfactual regularization.

## Key Results
- Offline: Improves state-of-the-art methods by an average of 1.07% in CVR AUC across five public datasets
- Online: A/B tests on Alipay Search platform show 0.75% improvement in PV-CVR with 95% confidence intervals
- Online: Translates to millions of additional conversions per week

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DCMT uses a counterfactual mechanism to predict both factual CVR and counterfactual CVR simultaneously, addressing selection bias in the entire exposure space.
- Mechanism: The model constructs a "counterfactual non-click space" N* that mirrors the factual non-click space N. For each sample in N, it generates a counterfactual sample with the opposite conversion label (0→1) and simulates what would happen if the user had clicked. The twin tower structure predicts both CVR and counterfactual CVR, constrained by the prior knowledge that their sum should be approximately 1.
- Core assumption: Users' conversion decisions follow a probabilistic structure where the probability of conversion plus the probability of non-conversion equals 1, and this structure can be captured by the model's parameters.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose a new counterfactual mechanism to debias the selection bias in D, which can predict the factual CVR and the counterfactual CVR under the soft constraint of a counterfactual prior knowledge."
  - [section III-C]: "Inspired by the decision process of conversion, we propose a concept of counterfactual sample space... the user ui purchases the item vj with a probability of pi,j (conversion rate), and thus ui does not purchase vj with a probability of 1− pi,j."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct citations of counterfactual mechanisms in related works, though the concept appears novel in the context of CVR estimation.
- Break condition: If the counterfactual prior knowledge (CVR + counterfactual CVR = 1) does not hold for real user behavior, or if the mirror space N* cannot be properly constructed due to insufficient negative samples.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DCMT eliminates both the causal effect of input features on click events and non-click events through a debiasing circle.
- Mechanism: The model applies inverse propensity weighting separately to both click space O (weight 1/ô) and counterfactual non-click space N* (weight 1/(1-ô)). These two debiasing strategies form a "debiasing circle" where debiasing in O affects predictions in N* and vice versa through shared parameters and the counterfactual constraint.
- Core assumption: The selection bias in both O and N* can be independently addressed through inverse propensity weighting, and these corrections will reinforce each other through the shared twin tower structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III-C]: "The debiasing strategy, i.e., e(ri,j,ˆri,j)/ôi,j, in O directly affects the predictions of factual CVR in N via the shared prediction function... and indirectly affects the predictions of counterfactual CVR in O* via the soft counterfactual constraint."
  - [section III-D]: Theorem proving unbiased estimation when ôi,j = oi,j and ˆri,j + ˆr*i,j = 1.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - while propensity-based debiasing is established in causal inference, the specific application to both factual and counterfactual spaces simultaneously is novel.
- Break condition: If propensity predictions are inaccurate (ôi,j ≠ oi,j), or if the counterfactual constraint cannot be properly enforced during training.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The twin tower architecture with shared and task-specific parameters effectively simulates users' decision process of conversion.
- Mechanism: The twin tower uses common parameters θc for shared reasoning across both factual and counterfactual predictions, while task-specific parameters θf and θcf capture divergent thinking for conversion vs non-conversion decisions. This structure allows the model to learn from both O and N* simultaneously.
- Core assumption: Users employ similar reasoning processes when making conversion decisions regardless of whether they clicked, but diverge in their final decision-making based on specific task characteristics.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III-E]: "The common parameters represent the same thoughts for decision making when receiving the same input, while the specific parameters represent the divergent thoughts to make the final decisions, i.e., conversion or non-conversion."
  - [section III-E]: "In the training process, the common parameters θc are updated by minimizing both the losses of the factual CVR task and the counterfactual CVR task."
  - [corpus]: Moderate evidence - multi-task learning with shared parameters is well-established, but the specific application to counterfactual CVR prediction is novel.
- Break condition: If the shared parameters cannot effectively capture common reasoning patterns, or if task-specific parameters lead to negative transfer between tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input layer: Shared features (deep + wide)
  - Embedding layer: Shared embeddings for CTR and CVR tasks
  - CTR task: Two towers (deep MLP + wide linear) predicting click propensity
  - CVR task: Twin towers predicting factual CVR and counterfactual CVR with shared/common parameters
  - CTCVR task: Element-wise multiplication of CTR and CVR predictions
  - Loss functions: Combined loss with counterfactual regularizer

- Critical path: Input features → Embedding layer → CTR tower → CVR twin tower → CTCVR prediction → Combined loss with propensity weighting and counterfactual regularization

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Wide&deep vs pure deep: The paper uses wide&deep to capture both memorization (wide) and generalization (deep), but could simplify to pure deep if wide features are unavailable
  - Hard vs soft counterfactual constraint: The paper chooses soft constraint (λ1 weighting) over hard constraint (ˆri,j + ˆr*i,j = 1) to avoid restricting predictions to narrow ranges
  - Self-normalization: Applied to reduce variance of inverse propensity weights, trading computational complexity for stability

- Failure signatures:
  - NaN loss values: Likely caused by propensity predictions approaching 0 or 1 without proper clipping
  - Overfitting: Indicated by large gap between training and validation performance, potentially from complex MLP structures or insufficient regularization
  - Poor counterfactual predictions: If counterfactual CVR predictions consistently fall outside reasonable ranges [0,1], suggesting issues with the twin tower architecture

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Validate embedding dimension impact: Test dimensions {4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128} on AE-ES dataset to find optimal balance between expressiveness and overfitting
  2. Validate MLP structure impact: Test different depths and widths on AE-ES to find optimal architecture for the twin towers
  3. Validate counterfactual regularizer weight: Test λ1 values {0.00001, 0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1} to find optimal balance between enforcing counterfactual constraint and allowing flexible predictions

## Limitations
- Limited ablation studies on counterfactual regularizer weight and twin tower architecture's specific contributions
- Online results only validated on a single platform (Alipay Search) without cross-platform validation
- Datasets primarily from e-commerce domains, limiting generalizability to other recommendation scenarios
