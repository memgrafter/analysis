# VR Facial Animation for Immersive Telepresence Avatars

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12051
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12051
- Reference count: 22
- Key outcome: Real-time VR facial animation system that adapts to new operators in 15 minutes, achieving 99/100 points and ranking first at ANA Avatar XPRIZE semifinals

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a real-time VR facial animation system for immersive telepresence avatars that can adapt to new operators in just 15 minutes. The method uses a source image and keypoint-based motion transfer to animate the face of an operator wearing a VR headset. Key innovations include a fast operator adaptation process, a training regime using the VoxCeleb dataset, a learned keypoint mapping to handle perspective differences between mouth camera and source images, an efficient eye tracking method trainable in under a minute, and temporal consistency filters. The system was tested at the ANA Avatar XPRIZE semifinals, where the authors' team achieved 99/100 points and ranked first out of 28 teams, demonstrating the effectiveness of the approach in real-world telepresence scenarios. The method runs at 33 fps on a single GPU and can generate realistic facial animations that convey the operator's expressions and emotions to remote recipients.

## Method Summary
The system uses a two-stage keypoint detector (global for head/eyes, specialized for lower face) combined with a motion network based on Siarohin et al. [4] but adapted for occluded driving frames. It learns a keypoint mapping ΠS for perspective alignment between mouth camera and source camera, uses image retrieval to select optimal expression frames from source video, and employs a generator network that can fuse information from source and expression frames. The eye tracking pipeline uses a lightweight hourglass network trainable in under a minute, and temporal consistency filters are applied to expression frames and eye keypoints to enhance temporal stability.

## Key Results
- Achieved 99/100 points and ranked first out of 28 teams at ANA Avatar XPRIZE semifinals
- System runs at 33 fps on a single RTX 3090 GPU
- Operator adaptation process completed in 15 minutes
- Effective facial animation that conveys expressions and emotions to remote recipients

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The pipeline achieves fast operator adaptation by capturing a short enrollment video sequence without the VR headset.
- Mechanism: The system records a sequence of source images during a quick enrollment step, which captures all operator-specific appearance information. During inference, this stored appearance is mapped to real-time driving frames captured by the mouth and eye cameras.
- Core assumption: A short video sequence of the operator's face without occlusion contains sufficient appearance variation to generalize to real-time expressions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "In a quick enrollment step, we capture a sequence of source images from the operator without the VR headset which contain all the important operator-specific appearance information."
  - [section III.A]: Describes the setup with cameras attached to the VR headset for capturing mouth and eye expressions during inference.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no directly related papers in the corpus discuss this specific enrollment approach.
- Break condition: If the enrollment video lacks sufficient expression variety, the system cannot accurately map new expressions during inference.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The learned keypoint mapping ΠS addresses perspective differences between mouth camera and source images.
- Mechanism: Instead of naïvely translating keypoints, the system learns a homogeneous transformation matrix TS(i) for each lower-face keypoint to map from the mouth camera space to the source image space. This accounts for perspective differences and facial distortions caused by the headset.
- Core assumption: Learning a separate transformation for each keypoint is more robust than estimating a single global transformation matrix.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III.D]: "Instead, we propose to learn a separate homogeneous transformation matrix TS(i) that maps from each lower-face keypoint kp(i) to the corresponding keypoint ˆkp(i) in the source frame head pose."
  - [section III.D]: Explains that this approach avoids the need to estimate exact depth in the mouth image, which is not very robust.
  - [corpus]: No corpus evidence; this is a novel contribution not found in related works.
- Break condition: If the optimization of the transformation matrices fails to converge or if the correspondence between mouth and source images is poor, the mapping will be inaccurate.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The extended inference pipeline with image retrieval enhances temporal consistency and mouth expression accuracy.
- Mechanism: By capturing multiple source frames in a video, the system dynamically selects the expression frame closest to the current projected lower-face keypoints. This reduces the need for large corrections and mitigates flickering effects during source frame changes.
- Core assumption: Temporally close frames exhibit similar expressions, allowing for accurate retrieval of matching expression frames.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III.E]: "To address this issue, we capture not just a single source image but multiple source frames in a video. This allows us to dynamically change the source image to the one which is closest to the projected lower-face keypoints of the current mouth image."
  - [section III.E]: Describes the training regime that selects expression frames from a small interval around the driving image to avoid the network learning to simply copy information.
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; no directly related papers in the corpus discuss this specific image retrieval approach for VR facial animation.
- Break condition: If the keypoint-based image retrieval fails to find a close match, or if the temporal consistency filters are not effective, the system may exhibit flickering or inaccurate expressions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Keypoint Detector (K) -> Motion Network (M) -> Generator Network (G)
  - Image Retrieval System (dynamic expression frame selection)
  - Eye Tracking Pipeline (gaze direction and eye openness estimation)
  - Temporal Consistency Filters (expression and eye keypoint smoothing)

- Critical path:
  1. Capture enrollment video of operator without headset
  2. Optimize keypoint transformations TS(i) between mouth camera and source images
  3. Calibrate and train eye tracking network
  4. During inference, extract keypoints from mouth and eye cameras
  5. Map mouth keypoints to source image space using ΠS
  6. Retrieve optimal expression frame using image retrieval
  7. Construct driving keypoints and estimate deformation grids
  8. Warp source and expression images and fuse them using the generator
  9. Apply temporal consistency filters to the output

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using a single source image vs. multiple source frames: Single image is simpler but less expressive; multiple frames enhance accuracy but increase complexity
  - Learning separate transformations for each keypoint vs. a global transformation: Separate transformations are more robust but require more computation
  - Real-time inference vs. offline training: Real-time is necessary for telepresence but limits the complexity of the models

- Failure signatures:
  - Inaccurate mouth expressions: Could indicate poor keypoint mapping or insufficient expression variety in the enrollment video
  - Flickering during source frame changes: May suggest the image retrieval system is not selecting appropriate frames or the temporal consistency filters are not effective
  - Eye tracking inaccuracies: Could be due to poor calibration or insufficient training data for the eye tracking network

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test keypoint detection on partially occluded faces to verify KVR's performance
  2. Validate the learned keypoint mapping ΠS by comparing mapped keypoints to ground truth correspondences
  3. Evaluate the image retrieval system's accuracy by measuring the similarity between retrieved and target expression frames

## Limitations

- Hardware requirements: System requires custom mouth camera and eye cameras mounted on HMD, limiting accessibility
- Multi-user scalability: 15-minute operator-specific adaptation process may not scale well for multi-user scenarios
- Enrollment quality dependency: System performance heavily depends on quality and expression variety of enrollment video
