# GFlowNet-EM for learning compositional latent variable models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.06576
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06576
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: GFlowNet-EM achieves lower negative log-likelihood and higher F1 scores than marginalization and Monte Carlo EM on grammar induction and discrete VAE tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes GFlowNet-EM, a method for training discrete latent variable models with intractable posterior distributions. The key innovation is using GFlowNets to learn an amortized sampler of the posterior during the E-step of expectation-maximization, enabling the M-step to perform gradient updates on the generative model. By training a conditional GFlowNet to sample latents proportional to the joint likelihood of latents and observed data, GFlowNet-EM avoids the need for conditional independence assumptions that limit existing methods like marginalization and Monte Carlo EM.

## Method Summary
GFlowNet-EM extends the EM algorithm by replacing the intractable E-step with a GFlowNet-based amortized inference mechanism. The method alternates between training a conditional GFlowNet to sample from the posterior distribution of latents given observed data (E-step), and updating the generative model parameters using samples from this approximate posterior (M-step). A key innovation is the use of trajectory balance (TB) to train the GFlowNet, ensuring it samples latents proportional to their joint likelihood with the data. The paper also introduces adaptive E-step scheduling via loss thresholding and a sleep phase to mitigate posterior collapse.

## Key Results
- GFlowNet-EM outperforms marginalization and Monte Carlo EM on hierarchical mixture models, achieving lower negative log-likelihood.
- On grammar induction tasks, GFlowNet-EM achieves higher F1 scores compared to existing methods while maintaining competitive NLL.
- Experiments with discrete VAEs show GFlowNet-EM can learn expressive latent representations without requiring conditional independence assumptions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GFlowNets can amortize posterior inference over discrete compositional latents without requiring tractable factorization.
- Mechanism: GFlowNets learn a stochastic policy that sequentially constructs samples, mapping the posterior estimation problem to a policy learning problem where the reward is proportional to the joint likelihood.
- Core assumption: The latent space can be represented as a DAG where states correspond to partial constructions and terminal states correspond to complete latents.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "By training GFlowNets to sample from the posterior over latents, we take advantage of their strengths as amortized variational inference algorithms for complex distributions over discrete structures."
  - [section 2.2] "GFlowNets (Bengio et al., 2021a) are a family of algorithms for training a stochastic policy to sample objects from a target distribution over a set of objects Z"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related papers focus on GFlowNet theory and streaming extensions, not compositional latent models.
- Break condition: If the latent structure cannot be represented as a DAG (e.g., cyclic dependencies), the sequential construction assumption fails.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adaptive E-step scheduling via loss thresholding prevents unnecessary updates and improves stability.
- Mechanism: GFlowNet training loss is used as a proxy for posterior approximation quality; M-step updates are triggered only when the loss falls below a threshold that decreases over training.
- Core assumption: GFlowNet loss correlates with approximation accuracy to the true posterior.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "We propose a method for adaptively choosing the number of updates to the GFlowNet that are performed in between successive M-step gradient updates."
  - [section 5.2] "In all experiments, GFlowNet-EM performs significantly better than VQ-VAE, which we attribute to the higher expressiveness of the posterior."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; neighboring papers discuss streaming and theoretical aspects but not loss-based scheduling.
- Break condition: If GFlowNet loss does not correlate with posterior quality (e.g., due to vanishing gradients), thresholding becomes ineffective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Sleep phase mitigates posterior collapse by training the GFlowNet to recover hallucinated latents from generated data.
- Mechanism: During sleep phase, latents and data are sampled from the generative model, and the GFlowNet is trained to maximize the likelihood of recovering the latents, which corresponds to minimizing KL(p||q) in the opposite direction.
- Core assumption: The generative model can produce plausible data and latents for training the GFlowNet.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "The sleep phase requires minimizing log q(z|x) as in Eq. 4 for z, x sampled ancestrally from the generative model."
  - [section 5.3] "During E-steps, we exploit the sleep phase for exploration, where we sample z from either the uniform or learned prior and x from the current p_θ(x|z)."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related work focuses on Bayes GFlowNets and latent tree extraction, not sleep phase training.
- Break condition: If the generative model produces unrealistic samples, the sleep phase gradient signal becomes useless.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Generative model (p_θ(z,x)) with parameters θ
  - Conditional GFlowNet (q_φ(z|x)) with parameters φ
  - State space DAG for sequential construction of latents
  - Flow estimator and backward policy for TB training
  - Reward function R(z|x) = p_θ(z) p_θ(x|z)

- Critical path:
  1. Sample data point x_i
  2. Train GFlowNet (E-step) to minimize TB loss with reward R(z|x_i)
  3. If loss threshold met, sample z from GFlowNet and update generative model (M-step)
  4. Optionally, perform sleep phase updates

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sequential vs. parallel construction of latents (sequential allows DAG representation but may be slower)
  - Off-policy vs. on-policy exploration (off-policy can improve mode coverage but requires more careful tuning)
  - Adaptive vs. fixed E-step scheduling (adaptive can improve efficiency but adds complexity)

- Failure signatures:
  - Posterior collapse: GFlowNet only models a few modes of the true posterior
  - Unstable training: Oscillations in generative model parameters due to poor posterior estimates
  - Mode dropping: GFlowNet fails to explore all relevant regions of the latent space

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Reproduce the hierarchical mixture experiment to validate basic GFlowNet-EM mechanics
  2. Implement the grammar induction experiment with a simple CFG to test scalability
  3. Apply to a small discrete VAE task to validate image domain applicability

## Limitations
- Scalability to high-dimensional latent spaces is unclear and may be limited by the sequential construction assumption.
- The correlation between GFlowNet loss and posterior quality is assumed but not empirically validated.
- The sleep phase may introduce instability if the generative model produces unrealistic samples.

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (amortized posterior inference): Medium - Theoretical framework is sound, but empirical validation is limited to small-scale experiments.
- Mechanism 2 (adaptive scheduling): Low - No ablation studies comparing adaptive vs. fixed scheduling; threshold choice appears heuristic.
- Mechanism 3 (sleep phase): Medium - Sleep phase is intuitively beneficial but lacks rigorous theoretical justification or ablation.
