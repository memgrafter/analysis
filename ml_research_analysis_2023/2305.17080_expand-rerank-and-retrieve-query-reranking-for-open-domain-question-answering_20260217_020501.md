# Expand, Rerank, and Retrieve: Query Reranking for Open-Domain Question Answering

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17080
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17080
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 30
- Primary result: EAR improves sparse retrieval for open-domain QA by reranking query expansions, outperforming GAR and DPR by 3-8 points in top-5 accuracy in-domain and 5-10 points out-of-domain.

## Executive Summary
EAR (Expand, Rerank, and Retrieve) addresses the limitation of greedy decoding in query expansion models by introducing a reranking step that selects the most promising expansions before retrieval. The method generates a diverse set of query expansions using models like T0-3B or GAR, then trains a reranker to predict which expansions will yield the best retrieval performance with BM25. This approach significantly improves both retrieval accuracy and end-to-end QA performance while being complementary to existing passage reranking techniques.

## Method Summary
EAR generates diverse query expansions using T0-3B or GAR, then trains a DeBERTa V3-based reranker to predict the retrieval quality of each expansion by estimating the rank of gold passages. At inference, the reranker selects the best expansion from 50 candidates, which is then used with BM25 for retrieval. The method includes two reranker variants: retrieval-independent (RI) that uses only the query and expansion, and retrieval-dependent (RD) that also incorporates top-1 retrieved passages. EAR is specifically designed for sparse retrieval and shows particular strength in out-of-domain scenarios compared to query generation alone.

## Key Results
- EAR improves top-5 accuracy by 3-8 points in-domain compared to GAR and DPR
- EAR shows 5-10 point improvements in top-5/20 accuracy out-of-domain
- EAR is complementary to passage reranking, with combined performance exceeding either method alone

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
EAR improves BM25 performance by selecting query expansions that have higher lexical overlap with relevant passages. The reranker estimates quality based on predicted retrieval rank of gold passages, leveraging the fact that better keyword overlap leads to improved BM25 results. This assumes lexical matching remains effective for many QA tasks.

### Mechanism 2
EAR's improvements are complementary to passage reranking because they operate at different pipeline stages. EAR reorders queries before retrieval to improve first-pass results, while passage reranking reorders retrieved passages afterward. Their independence allows for additive performance gains when combined.

### Mechanism 3
EAR generalizes better to out-of-domain data than GAR because query reranking is more domain-agnostic than query generation. While generation depends heavily on training domain, reranking learns to judge quality based on retrieval performance, a more universal skill transferable across domains.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Query → Expansion generation → Reranking → Retrieval → (Reranking) → Reader
- **Critical path**: Query → Expansion generation → Reranking → Retrieval → Reader
- **Design tradeoffs**: EAR-RD uses top-1 passage per expansion for better accuracy but higher latency; EAR-RI is faster but less accurate; both require 50 expansions per query
- **Failure signatures**: Poor expansions lead to no improvement; retriever changes degrade performance; reranker overfitting reduces cross-domain performance
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Compare EAR-RI vs EAR-RD on NQ dev set to understand latency vs accuracy tradeoff

  3. Combine EAR with passage reranking to confirm complementarity

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How can EAR be effectively adapted to work with dense retrievers like DPR? The paper mentions that EAR is heavily reliant on the retriever and its performance may be impacted when changing the retriever to DPR, but making EAR work with DPR would require retraining with DPR retrieval results and significantly more compute.

### Open Question 2
What mechanisms could be developed to exclude terrible query expansions generated by large language models like T0-3B during inference? The paper discusses the challenge of using T0-3B to generate query expansions during inference, as the quality of expansions is too diverse, leading to a higher chance of selecting terrible expansions.

### Open Question 3
How does EAR perform in cross-lingual settings, particularly when transferring to languages without supervised QA data? The paper acknowledges that EAR may face challenges when transferring to other languages without supervised QA data, which it is trained on.

## Limitations
- EAR's effectiveness is tightly coupled with specific retrieval paradigms, showing performance degradation when switching from BM25 to DPR
- The method relies on lexical matching assumptions that may not hold for domains requiring deep semantic understanding
- Cross-lingual applicability remains untested, raising questions about performance on non-English datasets

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: EAR's improvement over GAR in top-5 accuracy (3-8 points in-domain, 5-10 points out-of-domain) is well-supported by experimental results. The complementary relationship with passage reranking is demonstrated through combined performance metrics.

**Medium Confidence**: The mechanism explaining why EAR works better with BM25 than DPR is plausible but not rigorously tested. The paper assumes EAR's reranker is optimized for lexical matching without exploring adaptation for dense retrievers.

**Low Confidence**: The claim that query reranking is inherently more domain-agnostic than query generation lacks strong empirical support. The comparison between EAR and GAR across domains shows correlation but doesn't establish causation for domain adaptation differences.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Lingual Validation**: Test EAR on multilingual QA datasets (e.g., XOR-TyDi) to verify whether the lexical matching advantage holds across languages and whether the reranking mechanism generalizes to non-English queries.

2. **Dense Retriever Adaptation**: Implement an EAR variant optimized for DPR by retraining the reranker with dense retrieval features instead of BM25 scores, then compare performance against the original EAR to quantify the impact of retriever-specific optimization.

3. **Domain Transfer Experiment**: Conduct a controlled study where the query expansion model is trained on one domain and the reranker on another, measuring whether EAR's performance advantage persists when the expansion and reranking components are domain-disjoint.
