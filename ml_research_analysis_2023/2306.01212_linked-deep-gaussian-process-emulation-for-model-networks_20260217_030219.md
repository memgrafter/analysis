# Linked Deep Gaussian Process Emulation for Model Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01212
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01212
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 14
- One-line primary result: LDGP emulation achieves significantly lower NRMSE than LGP and composite DGP approaches for complex model networks.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of emulating complex networks of computer models that exhibit non-stationary behaviors, which limits the applicability of traditional linked Gaussian process (LGP) emulators. The authors propose a new approach, linked deep Gaussian process (LDGP) emulation, that conceptualizes a network of computer models as a deep Gaussian process with partial exposure of its hidden layers. This allows for the separate emulation of individual models using deep Gaussian processes (DGPs) and their subsequent linking, providing a divide-and-conquer strategy that retains the efficiency of LGP emulation while accommodating non-stationarity.

The authors demonstrate the superiority of LDGP emulators over both standard LGP emulators and single DGPs fitted to the network as a whole in terms of accuracy and uncertainty quantification. In a synthetic experiment, the LDGP emulator achieved a significantly lower normalized root mean squared error (NRMSE) of 0.18% compared to 6.56% for the LGP emulator. In an empirical application involving a delta-vega hedging decision network, the LDGP emulator provided the best predictive performance with NRMSE values of 1.09% and 0.82% for the two outputs of the network, outperforming the composite DGP and LGP emulators.

## Method Summary
The LDGP emulation approach involves training individual DGP emulators for each sub-model in the network separately, then linking them using a stochastic imputation (SI) framework. The SI approach alternates between imputing latent layers via elliptical slice sampling and updating link parameters using the LGP framework's closed-form updates. This allows the method to handle non-stationary behaviors while maintaining computational efficiency through the divide-and-conquer strategy.

## Key Results
- LDGP emulator achieved NRMSE of 0.18% in synthetic experiment vs 6.56% for LGP emulator
- In delta-vega hedging network, LDGP provided best predictive performance with NRMSE of 1.09% and 0.82% for two outputs
- LDGP outperforms both composite DGP and LGP emulators in accuracy and uncertainty quantification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Partial exposure of hidden layers in a deep Gaussian process allows separate DGP components to be trained and then linked analytically, preserving the efficiency of LGP emulation.
- Mechanism: The network is conceptualized as a DGP where outputs from intermediate models are treated as "observed" latent layers. This converts the problem into linking separately trained DGP emulators using the LGP framework's closed-form updates.
- Core assumption: The component models are independent conditional on the observed latent outputs, so their joint posterior factorizes into independent DGP posteriors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "conceptualize a network of computer models as a deep Gaussian process with partial exposure of its hidden layers"
  - [section 3]: "a natural extension is to replace GP emulators in an LGP structure with DGP emulators, leading to an LDGP emulator"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus does not mention partial exposure or LDGP-specific linking
- Break condition: If latent outputs are strongly correlated, the factorization assumption fails and the analytical linking becomes inaccurate.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Stochastic Imputation (SI) enables tractable inference in the partially observed DGP by alternating between imputing latent layers and updating link parameters.
- Mechanism: For each imputation iteration, hidden DGP outputs are sampled via elliptical slice sampling conditioned on neighboring layers, then the entire partially exposed DGP is treated as an LGP and updated using Algorithm 1.
- Core assumption: The latent layers can be adequately approximated by a finite number of imputations without collapsing uncertainty.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.3]: "Stochastic Imputation (SI), that provides a good balance between the computation and accuracy of DGP emulator constructions"
  - [section 3]: "a SI approach for LDGP inferences" using independent imputations of latent layers
  - [corpus]: Missing - corpus does not discuss imputation or elliptical slice sampling
- Break condition: Insufficient imputations or poor elliptical slice sampling mixing leads to biased posterior estimates.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Training individual DGP components separately before linking avoids the combinatorial explosion of joint design points while still capturing non-stationarity.
- Mechanism: Each sub-model is emulated with a DGP using its own training design, which can be tailored to its computational cost and functional complexity, then linked using the partial exposure framework.
- Core assumption: Component DGPs trained independently will generalize well to the joint input space implied by the network structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3]: "enabling us to obtain more runs and better component emulators for sub-models that are relatively inexpensive to run"
  - [section 4]: "we choose 2-layered DGPs for the 3 individual computer models in the chain"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus focuses on deep learning emulators rather than linked model networks
- Break condition: If interactions between components are highly nonlinear, independent training may miss important coupling effects.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Individual DGP emulators for each sub-model -> Elliptical slice sampling for latent layer imputation -> LGP linking equations for analytical propagation -> SI loop to aggregate imputations into final LDGP predictions
- Critical path:
  1. Train component DGP emulators independently
  2. Run SI loop: sample latent outputs, link via LGP updates, aggregate predictions
  3. Evaluate accuracy vs composite DGP and LGP baselines
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More imputations → higher accuracy but more computation
  - Deeper DGP layers → better non-stationarity modeling but harder training
  - Partial exposure → preserves LGP efficiency but may miss strong latent correlations
- Failure signatures:
  - High NRMSE relative to composite DGP indicates poor latent imputation
  - Large predictive uncertainty suggests insufficient imputations
  - Numerical instability in LGP updates signals ill-conditioned covariance matrices
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replicate synthetic 3-model chain and compare NRMSE for LDGP vs LGP vs composite DGP
  2. Vary number of imputations (N=5, 10, 20) and observe convergence of predictive mean/variance
  3. Replace one DGP component with a stationary GP and verify performance degradation matches expectations

## Limitations
- LDGP framework inherits computational complexity from DGP training, particularly for high-dimensional input spaces or deeply nested networks
- Partial exposure assumption may break down when latent outputs exhibit strong correlations
- Stochastic imputation approach requires careful tuning of the number of imputations to balance computational cost against uncertainty quantification

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core mechanism of linking separately trained DGP emulators using partial exposure is well-supported by the theoretical framework and demonstrated in synthetic experiments.
- **Medium Confidence**: The empirical application results show promising performance, but the limited scope (single decision network) and lack of comparison to alternative deep learning approaches reduce generalizability.
- **Low Confidence**: The claim that LDGP emulators consistently outperform all composite approaches across diverse network structures requires further validation on more varied real-world examples.
