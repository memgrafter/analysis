# The ADAIO System at the BEA-2023 Shared Task on Generating AI Teacher Responses in Educational Dialogues

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05360
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05360
- Reference count: 4
- Key outcome: ADAIO team achieved second place in BEA-2023 shared task using GPT-3 with few-shot prompt-based approach

## Executive Summary
This paper presents the ADAIO team's system for the BEA-2023 shared task on generating AI teacher responses in educational dialogues. The system employs OpenAI's GPT-3 text-davinci-003 model with a few-shot prompt-based approach, using carefully designed prompts and sample dialogues to elicit teacher-like responses from the TSCC dataset. The approach achieved second place in the competition, demonstrating the effectiveness of large language models for educational dialogue applications without requiring fine-tuning.

## Method Summary
The system uses OpenAI GPT-3 text-davinci-003 with few-shot prompting, where 5 dialogue examples are provided in-context to condition the model's output. The team designed five different prompts (A-E) testing various teaching approaches, student understanding probing, and learning goals. No preprocessing or NLP toolkit was used to correct student errors, relying instead on GPT-3's robustness to non-canonical language. The system was evaluated using BertScore and DialogRPT metrics, comparing performance against baseline models including fine-tuned GPT-2 variants and other GPT-3 models.

## Key Results
- Achieved second place ranking in BEA-2023 shared task
- Few-shot prompt-based approach outperformed fine-tuned models and other GPT-3 variants
- GPT-3 successfully generated appropriate responses despite not correcting student grammatical errors or non-canonical forms
- Prompt engineering significantly influenced the tone and instructional focus of generated responses

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
GPT-3 with few-shot prompting simulates teacher-like behaviors by conditioning on explicit role and pedagogical context. The model leverages its pre-trained knowledge of dialogue patterns and teacher-student dynamics, using in-context examples to align responses with teaching attributes. The approach assumes the TSCC corpus contains sufficient diversity to serve as effective examples. Break condition: if prompts fail to specify teacher role clearly or examples are unrepresentative, GPT-3 may generate generic responses.

### Mechanism 2
Prompt engineering steers GPT-3's tone and instructional focus without retraining. By altering prompt wording (e.g., emphasizing "probe understanding" vs. "be helpful"), the model adapts its output style while preserving coherence. This assumes GPT-3's internal representations encode flexible conversational roles that can be activated via explicit instructions. Break condition: if the model's alignment to the prompt is shallow, responses may revert to generic patterns.

### Mechanism 3
GPT-3 handles non-canonical language and grammatical errors in student input without explicit preprocessing. The model's pretraining on diverse web text gives it robustness to informal, error-prone, or code-switched language, allowing it to infer intent and generate appropriate corrections or clarifications. This assumes pretraining data includes sufficient low-quality and multilingual conversational samples. Break condition: if errors are too severe or structurally ambiguous, the model may fail to recover intended meaning.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: OpenAI GPT-3 API (text-davinci-003 model) → Prompt builder → Response generator → Evaluation (BertScore, DialogRPT)
- Critical path: Prompt construction → API call with few-shot examples → Response generation → Automated evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Few-shot vs. fine-tuning: Few-shot is faster and cheaper but less specialized; fine-tuning could yield better domain fit but at higher cost
  - Prompt specificity vs. generality: More specific prompts may yield better tone alignment but reduce flexibility; generic prompts allow broader applicability but risk off-topic output
  - Model choice (davinci-003 vs. ada-001): davinci-003 produces higher-quality responses but is costlier; ada-001 is cheaper but less capable
- Failure signatures:
  - Out-of-context or incoherent responses → Check prompt clarity and few-shot example quality
  - Repetitive or overly generic output → Verify prompt specificity and avoid overly short example sets
  - High API cost or slow response → Consider switching to a smaller model or reducing max_tokens
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Zero-shot baseline: Send a single prompt without examples to measure baseline GPT-3 performance
  2. Few-shot with varied prompts: Test Prompt A vs. Prompt B to observe tone differences
  3. Model comparison: Run few-shot with both davinci-003 and ada-001 on the same prompt to compare output quality and cost

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do different teaching approaches (e.g., constructivist learning vs. productive failure) impact the effectiveness of LLMs in educational dialogue systems? The paper briefly mentions these approaches and their potential for adapting LLM responses but provides no experimental evidence or detailed analysis of their impact on performance.

### Open Question 2
How do multilingual LLMs perform in educational dialogues compared to monolingual models, and what are the implications for language learning applications? While the paper highlights multilingual capabilities and potential to address language barriers, it lacks experimental data or detailed analysis of multilingual LLM performance in educational dialogues.

### Open Question 3
What are the limitations of relying solely on automatic evaluation metrics (e.g., BertScore, DialogRPT) for assessing the quality of AI teacher responses, and how can human evaluation be integrated into the assessment process? The paper acknowledges limitations of automatic metrics but does not provide a detailed analysis of these limitations or propose a comprehensive framework for integrating human evaluation.

## Limitations

- Performance heavily depends on quality and representativeness of few-shot examples, but exact selection criteria are not fully specified
- Evaluation metrics (BertScore, DialogRPT) provide quantitative measures but may not fully capture pedagogical effectiveness or nuanced aspects of teacher-student interaction quality
- Success with text-davinci-003 may not generalize to other GPT-3 variants or future models, and approach hasn't been tested on diverse educational domains beyond TSCC dataset

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The few-shot prompting mechanism with GPT-3 is technically sound and well-documented. Second-place ranking in BEA-2023 shared task provides strong empirical validation for the specific task and dataset.

**Medium Confidence**: Claim about GPT-3's ability to handle non-canonical language without preprocessing is supported by internal results but lacks direct comparison with preprocessing-augmented baselines. Evaluation methodology using automated metrics is standard but may not fully capture educational dialogue quality.

**Low Confidence**: Generalizability of prompt engineering strategies across different educational contexts and student populations is not well-established. Long-term pedagogical impact of AI-generated responses remains unassessed.

## Next Checks

1. **Sample Selection Validation**: Systematically vary the few-shot examples used in prompts (different teaching styles, dialogue lengths, and error types) and measure the impact on response quality to determine the sensitivity of performance to example selection.

2. **Human Evaluation Benchmark**: Conduct human expert evaluation comparing GPT-3-generated responses against baseline models and human-written responses across multiple pedagogical dimensions (correctness, helpfulness, engagement, and appropriateness).

3. **Cross-Domain Transfer Test**: Apply the same prompt engineering approach to a different educational dialogue dataset (e.g., STEM tutoring or language learning in different contexts) to assess generalizability beyond the TSCC corpus.
