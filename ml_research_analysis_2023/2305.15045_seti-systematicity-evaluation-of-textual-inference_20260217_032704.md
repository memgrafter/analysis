# SETI: Systematicity Evaluation of Textual Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.15045
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15045
- Reference count: 21
- SETI benchmark evaluates PLMs' systematicity in natural language inference through compositional reasoning tasks

## Executive Summary
SETI (Systematicity Evaluation of Textual Inference) introduces a novel benchmark to assess pre-trained language models' systematicity capabilities in natural language inference. The benchmark presents three NLI tasks that require models to perform compositional inference based on known primitive constituents, evaluating various types of systematicity in reasoning processes. Through experiments on six widely used PLMs, the study reveals that models can solve unseen compositional inferences when they have learned how to combine primitives, but show significant limitations (40-100% performance drops) when this compositional knowledge is unknown. The findings also demonstrate that PLMs can improve drastically with minimal exposure to crucial compositional knowledge through few-shot learning.

## Method Summary
SETI presents three NLI tasks with corresponding datasets to evaluate systematicity generalization in PLMs. The benchmark focuses on compositional inference, where models must combine primitive inference types (veridical and natural inference) using Boolean logic rules. The tasks are designed to test both trivial settings (where composition rules are known) and non-trivial settings (where composition rules must be inferred). The benchmark evaluates six widely used PLMs across these tasks, measuring performance differences between settings and testing few-shot learning capabilities.

## Key Results
- PLMs achieve good performance on unseen compositional inferences when they have learned how to combine primitive constituents
- Significant performance drops (40-100% points) occur when models lack explicit knowledge of composition rules
- Models show drastic improvement with minimal exposure to compositional knowledge through few-shot learning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: PLMs can perform compositional inference when they have learned both primitive constituents and the knowledge of how to combine them.
- Mechanism: The model stores separate representations for primitive inferences (veridical and natural inference) and applies Boolean logic rules to compose them into new inferences.
- Core assumption: The model has distinct and retrievable representations for primitive inference types that can be combined using learned composition rules.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Results show that various PLMs are able to solve unseen compositional inferences when having encountered the knowledge of how to combine primitives, with good performance."
  - [section 3.2] "This is a trivial setting since the challenge is restricted to classifying compositional inference from seen primitive inferences."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations about Boolean logic application in PLMs
- Break condition: If the model cannot maintain separate representations for primitive inferences or if composition rules are not properly learned.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PLMs show significant improvement with minimal exposure to compositional knowledge through few-shot learning.
- Mechanism: The model leverages its pre-existing knowledge of primitives and rapidly adapts to new composition rules when exposed to a small number of examples that demonstrate the rule.
- Core assumption: The model's architecture allows for rapid adaptation to new composition patterns without extensive retraining.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Furthermore, we find that PLMs can improve drastically once exposed to crucial compositional knowledge in minimalistic shots."
  - [section 5.2] "Across different tasks, we find that all evaluated PLMs benefit from few-shot samples that teach the model relevant compositional inference knowledge."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations about few-shot learning effectiveness in PLMs
- Break condition: If the model requires a large number of examples to learn new composition rules, contradicting the "minimalistic shots" claim.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The performance drop in non-trivial settings indicates a limitation in systematicity generalization when composition rules are not explicitly provided.
- Mechanism: Without explicit examples of how to combine primitives, the model fails to generalize composition rules, leading to significant performance decreases.
- Core assumption: The model's inability to infer composition rules from primitive knowledge alone indicates a gap in systematicity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "However, they are considerably limited when this knowledge is unknown to the model (40-100% points decrease)."
  - [section 5.1] "In Task2, the test accuracy of all generalization-tested models declines sharply to 50% in the non-trivial setting, no matter how well a model performs in the trivial setting."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations about systematicity limitations in PLMs
- Break condition: If models can infer composition rules from primitive knowledge without explicit examples, contradicting the performance drop observation.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Veridical and Natural Inference as Primitives
  - Why needed here: These primitive inference types form the building blocks for compositional inferences in the benchmark.
  - Quick check question: Can you explain the difference between veridical and non-veridical verbs in the context of inference?

- Concept: Boolean Logic Rules for Composition
  - Why needed here: These rules determine the outcome of compositional inferences based on the combination of primitive inference types.
  - Quick check question: Given fv(s) → s and s → s', what is the result of fv(s) → s' using Boolean logic?

- Concept: Systematicity in Language Models
  - Why needed here: Understanding systematicity is crucial for interpreting the benchmark's goals and results.
  - Quick check question: What is systematicity, and why is it considered a hallmark of human cognition?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input preprocessing -> Tokenization -> Transformer encoder layers -> Composition mechanism -> Output classification -> Few-shot adapter
- Critical path:
  1. Tokenize input premise and hypothesis
  2. Process through transformer layers
  3. Apply composition rules (if in trivial setting)
  4. Classify as entailment or non-entailment
  5. (Optional) Update with few-shot examples
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size vs. computational efficiency
  - Pre-training objectives (masked LM vs. denoising autoencoder)
  - Architecture type (encoder-only vs. encoder-decoder vs. decoder-only)
  - Training data diversity vs. task-specific performance
- Failure signatures:
  - Performance drop in non-trivial settings indicates systematicity limitations
  - Inconsistent results across different model architectures suggest architectural dependencies
  - Over-reliance on lexical overlap heuristics rather than compositional reasoning
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test model performance on trivial vs. non-trivial settings to identify systematicity gaps
  2. Evaluate few-shot learning effectiveness by gradually increasing the number of compositional examples
  3. Compare performance across different model architectures to understand architectural impacts on systematicity

## Limitations

- The benchmark focuses only on Boolean-composition patterns, potentially missing other forms of systematicity
- Evaluation relies on held-out compositional inferences rather than truly novel combinations
- Limited exploration of few-shot learning with different sample sizes and adaptation methods

## Confidence

- High confidence in observed performance gaps between trivial and non-trivial settings (40-100% point drops)
- Medium confidence in interpreting performance differences as systematicity limitations
- Medium confidence in few-shot learning effectiveness claims due to limited sample size exploration

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation study on primitive inference knowledge**: Remove access to primitive inference labels during training and test whether models can still learn composition rules from raw text alone, isolating the contribution of explicit primitive knowledge to compositional performance.

2. **Cross-dataset generalization test**: Evaluate models trained on SETI tasks on established NLI benchmarks (e.g., SNLI, MNLI) to determine whether systematicity gains transfer to naturalistic inference scenarios or remain confined to synthetic compositions.

3. **Complexity scaling experiment**: Systematically increase the complexity of compositional patterns (e.g., chains of three or more primitive inferences) to identify whether performance degradation follows predictable patterns or reveals fundamental limitations in the models' compositional reasoning capabilities.
