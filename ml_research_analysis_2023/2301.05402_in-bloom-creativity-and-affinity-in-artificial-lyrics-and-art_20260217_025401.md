# In BLOOM: Creativity and Affinity in Artificial Lyrics and Art

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.05402
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05402
- Reference count: 6
- This paper evaluates BLOOM-176B-generated Chinese song lyrics and album art, finding that while computational metrics like MAUVE improve with larger sampling probabilities, human creativity and affinity peak at moderate values.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the creative potential of BLOOM-176B for generating Chinese song lyrics and corresponding album artwork using stable diffusion. The study finds that human creativity and affinity peak at moderate sampling probabilities (0.90-0.95), while computational metrics like MAUVE continue improving at higher probabilities until coherence breaks down. Some LLM-generated lyrics scored higher than real lyrics by popular artists, though low inter-annotator agreement suggests inconsistent evaluation criteria. The research demonstrates that computational evaluation metrics alone are insufficient proxies for subjective human creativity assessment in creative writing tasks.

## Method Summary
The study generated Chinese song lyrics using BLOOM-176B with top-p nucleus sampling at p∈[0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99], then created album art using a Chinese-language Taiyi stable diffusion model with CFG∈[4.0, 7.0, 10.0]. The MojimLyrics dataset (39,747 songs, 230 artists, 14.4M tokens) served as the real lyrics reference, while a crowdsourced real album cover dataset (~4,000 images) provided the art comparison. MAUVE scores measured text quality, FID scores evaluated image quality, and human reviewers on Amazon Mechanical Turk assessed coherence, creativity, affinity, and recognition across 60 samples per condition.

## Key Results
- Human creativity and affinity ratings peak at moderate sampling probabilities (p=0.90-0.95) before declining at higher values where coherence decreases
- Some LLM-generated lyrics received higher human ratings than real lyrics from popular artists, though inter-annotator agreement was low
- Album artwork generated via stable diffusion achieved FID score of 90.21 (CFG=4.0) against real album covers
- MAUVE scores improve with larger sampling probabilities but break down at extreme values where coherence decreases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Top-p nucleus sampling at moderate probability mass produces lyrics that balance coherence and distinctiveness.
- Mechanism: When sampling at moderate p-values (0.90-0.95), the model selects more common words to maintain coherence while still occasionally choosing less common tokens to add distinctiveness, creating lyrics perceived as creative.
- Core assumption: Human creativity assessment requires both comprehensibility and uniqueness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We note that the human concept of creativity requires lyrics to be both comprehensible and distinctive"
  - [section] "Subjective quality measurements of creativity and affinity peak around p = [0.9, 0.95], and trend sharply downwards at p = 0.99 as the model selects less common words and becomes less coherent"

- Break condition: If human reviewers fundamentally change their criteria for creativity or if BLOOM-176B's token distribution changes significantly during sampling.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: MAUVE scores improve with larger sampling probabilities but break down at extreme values where coherence decreases.
- Mechanism: MAUVE measures distributional similarity between generated text and human text, improving as p increases until coherence drops below a threshold where MAUVE no longer correlates with human creativity assessment.
- Core assumption: MAUVE can partially reflect subjective human creativity assessment.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "MAUVE) have limitations in evaluation of creative writing"
  - [section] "While MAUVE rises alongside subjective creativity from p = 0.80 to p = 0.95, this relationship breaks down at p = 0.99"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found in corpus neighbors
- Break condition: If MAUVE's underlying computation changes or if human creativity criteria become completely uncorrelated with distributional similarity.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Stable diffusion with Chinese text encoder generates album art that correlates with lyric content and achieves reasonable FID scores against real album covers.
- Mechanism: The Chinese-language Taiyi stable diffusion model processes Chinese lyrics as prompts, generating images that visually represent the lyric content, with CFG parameter controlling guidance strength.
- Core assumption: Lyrics contain sufficient semantic information to guide meaningful image generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we leverage a Chinese-language stable diffusion model to produce high-quality lyric-guided album art"
  - [section] "We use this model to produce 1000 samples of album artwork, from seeds 0-999, using the prompt 'album art' in Chinese, followed by our generated lyrics"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence found in corpus neighbors
- Break condition: If the stable diffusion model's training data or architecture changes significantly, or if lyrics become too abstract for visual representation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: BLOOM-176B (text generation) → Human reviewers (subjective evaluation) → MAUVE (computational evaluation) → Taiyi Stable Diffusion (image generation) → FID (image evaluation)
- Critical path: Text generation → Human evaluation → Computational metric comparison
- Design tradeoffs: Using BLOOM-176B provides high-quality generation but requires significant computational resources; human evaluation provides subjective insights but introduces variability and requires careful experimental design
- Failure signatures: Low inter-annotator agreement indicates inconsistent evaluation criteria; poor MAUVE scores suggest distributional mismatch; high FID scores indicate image quality issues
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate lyrics at multiple p-values (0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99) and compute degeneration metrics and MAUVE scores
  2. Conduct human evaluation survey on Amazon Mechanical Turk for generated lyrics at different p-values
  3. Generate album art using stable diffusion with different CFG values and compute FID against real album dataset

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What specific criteria do human reviewers use when giving low scores to generated lyrics, and how can these be standardized across evaluators?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that while reviewer scores showed low variance, inter-annotator agreement was consistently low, particularly for low scores, suggesting inconsistent criteria across reviewers.
- Why unresolved: The study was relatively small with limited trained reviewers, making it difficult to identify consistent patterns in low-scoring criteria.
- What evidence would resolve it: A larger study with more trained reviewers using structured rubrics could identify common factors that lead to low scores and help standardize evaluation criteria.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the relationship between computational metrics (like MAUVE) and subjective human creativity assessment vary across different genres of creative writing beyond song lyrics?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper found that MAUVE scores correlated with human creativity assessment for lyrics up to p=0.95, but this relationship broke down at p=0.99, suggesting computational metrics have limitations.
- Why unresolved: The study focused specifically on Chinese song lyrics, leaving open whether these findings generalize to other creative writing forms like poetry, fiction, or dialogue.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the same computational metrics against human evaluations across multiple creative writing genres would reveal whether the relationship is genre-specific or universal.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal balance between sampling probability mass and generation length for producing high-quality creative text with LLMs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper varied top-p sampling probability but kept generation length fixed at 128 tokens, observing that creativity peaked around p=0.9-0.95 before coherence declined.
- Why unresolved: The study didn't explore how generation length might interact with sampling probability to affect quality, potentially missing an important dimension of control.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying both sampling probability and generation length across different tasks would reveal optimal combinations for various creative applications.

## Limitations
- Low inter-annotator agreement (Krippendorff's alpha 0.07-0.11) suggests inconsistent human evaluation criteria, particularly for low-scoring samples
- Computational metrics (MAUVE, FID) show limitations as proxies for human creativity assessment, especially at extreme sampling probabilities
- The study uses only one language model (BLOOM-176B) and one stable diffusion model, limiting generalizability across different architectures

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The finding that human creativity and affinity peak at moderate sampling probabilities (p=0.90-0.95) is well-supported by the data
- Medium Confidence: The claim that some LLM-generated lyrics scored higher than real lyrics from popular artists is supported but should be interpreted cautiously due to low inter-annotator agreement
- Medium Confidence: The relationship between computational metrics and human assessment, particularly MAUVE breakdown at extreme p-values, is demonstrated but requires further validation
- Low Confidence: Generalizability to other models, domains, or cultural contexts remains uncertain due to single-model, single-language approach

## Next Checks
1. **Inter-annotator Agreement Validation**: Conduct a follow-up study with enhanced training for human reviewers, including calibration exercises and detailed rubrics for creativity and affinity assessment to improve Krippendorff's alpha scores above 0.3 threshold.
2. **Cross-Model Generalization Test**: Repeat the entire experimental pipeline using different large language models (e.g., GPT-3, LLaMA) and stable diffusion variants to verify whether the observed patterns in creativity assessment and computational metric limitations are model-agnostic.
3. **Cultural Context Expansion**: Translate the methodology to evaluate lyrics in multiple languages and artistic traditions to assess whether the identified relationships between sampling probability, computational metrics, and human creativity assessment hold across diverse cultural contexts.
