# Increasing Performance And Sample Efficiency With Model-agnostic Interactive Feature Attributions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16431
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16431
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Novel interactive feature attribution method improves model performance and sample efficiency by over 50% across multiple datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a model-agnostic approach to improving machine learning models through interactive feature attributions, where domain experts can correct explanations of model decisions. The core innovation involves augmenting training data with corrected attributions using Occlusion and SHAP value-based techniques, addressing the limitation of existing interactive learning approaches that only correct irrelevant features. Experiments demonstrate significant performance improvements across linear models, logistic regression, and real-world datasets including Boston Housing and Titanic survival prediction.

## Method Summary
The proposed method enables domain experts to interactively correct feature attributions by providing feedback on which features should be more or less important in model decisions. This feedback is then used to augment the training data, forcing the model to align its explanations with the expert's domain knowledge. The approach implements two augmentation strategies: occlusion-based and SHAP-based methods. During training, corrected attributions are enforced by modifying the loss function or directly augmenting the training examples with attribution-based constraints. This creates a feedback loop where the model progressively learns to produce explanations that match expert expectations while maintaining or improving predictive performance.

## Key Results
- Achieved over 50% improvement in sample efficiency across all tested datasets
- Outperformed both baseline methods and state-of-the-art interactive learning techniques
- Validated effectiveness on multiple datasets including Boston Housing and Titanic survival prediction
- Demonstrated applicability across linear models and logistic regression architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by creating a direct feedback mechanism between domain experts and model explanations, allowing the correction of attribution errors that would otherwise propagate through the learning process. By enforcing corrected attributions during training, the model learns to align its decision-making process with expert knowledge, effectively transferring domain expertise into improved model behavior. This approach addresses the fundamental limitation of black-box models where explanations often conflict with domain expertise, leading to mistrust and reduced adoption. The augmentation strategies ensure that corrected attributions become part of the learning objective, creating a self-improving cycle where better explanations lead to better model performance.

## Foundational Learning
- Interactive Learning: The process where human experts provide feedback to improve model behavior, needed to incorporate domain knowledge that cannot be captured in training data alone
- Feature Attribution Methods: Techniques like SHAP and Occlusion that explain model predictions, needed to provide interpretable explanations for expert feedback
- Data Augmentation: The process of artificially expanding training datasets, needed to incorporate attribution corrections without requiring new labeled examples
- Loss Function Modification: Techniques for incorporating additional constraints into training objectives, needed to enforce attribution corrections during model optimization
- Model-Agnostic Methods: Approaches that work across different model architectures, needed for broad applicability across various ML models

## Architecture Onboarding
Component Map: Domain Expert -> Attribution Correction -> Data Augmentation -> Model Training -> Improved Model
Critical Path: Expert Feedback → Attribution Calculation → Training Data Augmentation → Model Retraining → Performance Validation
Design Tradeoffs: Balancing attribution correction strength vs. model stability, computational cost of attribution calculation vs. performance gains, frequency of expert interaction vs. training efficiency
Failure Signatures: Poor convergence when attribution corrections are too aggressive, reduced performance if expert feedback is inconsistent or noisy, increased training time due to additional attribution calculations

## Limitations
- Limited experimental validation to linear models and logistic regression
- Reliance on simulated expert feedback rather than real domain experts
- Computational overhead of generating SHAP values during training not fully addressed
- Uncertainty about effectiveness on complex neural network architectures
