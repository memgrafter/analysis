# User-defined Event Sampling and Uncertainty Quantification in Diffusion Models for Physical Dynamical Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07526
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07526
- Reference count: 35
- This paper demonstrates that diffusion models can be used for probabilistic modeling and uncertainty quantification of chaotic dynamical systems.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a method for sampling from user-defined nonlinear event constraints in diffusion models without retraining. The key innovation is a probabilistic approximation scheme for the conditional score function using moment matching, which provably converges to the true distribution as noise decreases. The approach enables conditional sampling on nonlinear equality and inequality constraints at inference time, allowing for uncertainty quantification and extreme event sampling in chaotic dynamical systems.

## Method Summary
The method involves training an unconditional diffusion model on trajectories from dynamical systems, then using moment-matching approximation to derive conditional score functions for nonlinear equality and inequality constraints. The approach combines Tweedie's formula for conditional moments with linearization of constraint functions to enable tractable conditional sampling. The diffusion model learns the score function of noised data, and the conditional scores are computed using the Gaussian approximation of the conditional distribution.

## Key Results
- The diffusion model captures dynamics and uncertainties in chaotic systems like Lorenz, FitzHugh-Nagumo, and double pendulum
- The method can sample from event tails and compute marginal likelihoods of extreme events
- Moment matching with linearized constraints enables conditional sampling without retraining

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Gaussian approximation via Tweedie's formula enables tractable conditional sampling on nonlinear constraints without retraining.
- Mechanism: Tweedie's formula provides exact expressions for the conditional mean and covariance of the denoised signal given a noised observation. These moments are then used to construct a Gaussian approximation to the intractable conditional distribution.
- Core assumption: The true conditional distribution of x0 given xt is sufficiently close to Gaussian for the approximation to be useful.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we develop a probabilistic approximation scheme for the conditional score function which provably converges to the true distribution as the noise level decreases"
  - [section 4.1]: "Using these two expressions for the conditional mean and covariance of x0, we can approximate p(x0|xt) with a Gaussian"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers focus on uncertainty quantification but not specifically on Gaussian moment matching for diffusion models.
- Break condition: When the noise level is high or the true conditional distribution is highly non-Gaussian, the approximation may be poor.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Moment matching with linearized constraints enables sampling from event tails and computing their marginal likelihood.
- Mechanism: The linearized constraint C(x0) ≈ C(ˆx0) + ∇C · (x0 − ˆx0) combined with the Gaussian approximation allows computing the conditional scores for both equality and inequality constraints.
- Core assumption: The constraint function C varies slowly over the scale of the conditional covariance, making the linearization accurate.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we are able to sample conditionally on nonlinear userdefined events at inference time, and matches data statistics even when sampling from the tails of the distribution"
  - [section 4.4]: "With the Gaussian approximations of Equation 10 and Equation 12 for linear and nonlinear equality constraints, we can also handle inequality constraints such as E = {x0 : C(x0) > y }"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss rare event simulation but not specifically the combination of moment matching with linearized constraints for diffusion models.
- Break condition: When the constraint function has high curvature relative to the conditional covariance, the linearization becomes inaccurate.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The approximation becomes increasingly accurate as the noise level decreases, converging to the true conditional distribution.
- Mechanism: As the noise scale σt approaches zero, the signal-to-noise ratio s2t/σ2t approaches infinity, causing the conditional covariance to shrink and the conditional distribution to concentrate around the true conditional distribution.
- Core assumption: The score function and constraint function are smooth enough for the convergence arguments to hold.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "provably converges to the true distribution as the noise level decreases"
  - [section 5.3]: "Theorem 1... the random variable (∇C ⊤ ˆΣ∇C)−1/2(C(x0) − C(ˆx0)) (conditioned on xt) converges in distribution to a multivariate normal as t → 0"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence - corpus papers don't discuss the convergence properties of moment matching approximations for diffusion models.
- Break condition: When the noise level is high or the score function or constraint function is not smooth, the convergence may be slow or not hold.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Forward SDE -> Score network training -> Reverse SDE with conditional scores -> Moment matching -> Constraint linearization -> Conditional sampling

- Critical path: Forward SDE → Score network training → Reverse SDE with conditional scores → Moment matching → Constraint linearization → Conditional sampling

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Moment matching vs. other approximations: Moment matching provides a principled approximation with convergence guarantees, but requires computing Jacobians which can be expensive.
  - Linearization vs. exact constraints: Linearization makes the problem tractable but introduces approximation error, especially for highly nonlinear constraints.
  - Fixed vs. adaptive step size: Fixed step size is simpler and more stable with the conditional scores, but adaptive step size could be more efficient for smooth regions.

- Failure signatures:
  - Samples don't satisfy constraints: Could indicate poor approximation of the conditional distribution, especially for high noise levels or highly nonlinear constraints.
  - Samples have incorrect statistics: Could indicate the linearization error is too large or the constraint function varies rapidly over the conditional covariance.
  - Integration fails to converge: Could indicate numerical issues with the conditional scores, especially with adaptive step size integrators and the scaling factors in the scores.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train unconditional diffusion model on simple 1D dynamical system (e.g., logistic map) and evaluate point predictions and uncertainty calibration.
  2. Implement moment matching with Tweedie's formula and verify the conditional mean and covariance are computed correctly on a simple Gaussian example.
  3. Implement constraint linearization and conditional sampling on a simple 1D constraint (e.g., x > 0) and verify samples satisfy the constraint and have correct statistics.

## Limitations
- The moment-matching approximation introduces errors that can accumulate, particularly for high noise levels or highly nonlinear constraint functions.
- The linearization of constraint functions may fail when the constraints have high curvature relative to the conditional covariance.
- The computational cost of computing Jacobians for each constraint evaluation can be prohibitive for high-dimensional problems.

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The theoretical foundation of Tweedie's formula and the convergence properties of the Gaussian approximation as noise decreases.
- **Medium confidence**: The practical performance of the linearization approximation for nonlinear constraints, as this depends heavily on the specific problem structure.
- **Medium confidence**: The numerical stability of the conditional sampling scheme, particularly when computing marginal likelihoods via change of variables.
