# SAM on Medical Images: A Comprehensive Study on Three Prompt Modes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00035
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00035
- Reference count: 35

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) on 12 diverse medical image segmentation datasets, examining three prompt modes: auto-prompt, box-prompt, and point-prompt. The study reveals that prompt design significantly impacts SAM's zero-shot performance, with box-prompt mode achieving the highest average Dice scores when minimal jitter is applied. The research demonstrates that SAM's performance is highly sensitive to prompt size and number, with increased point prompts approaching box-prompt results. While SAM's zero-shot performance generally lags behind supervised models, it occasionally matches or exceeds state-of-the-art results on specific datasets, highlighting its potential as a foundation model for medical imaging with further refinement.

## Method Summary
The study evaluated SAM across 12 medical image datasets spanning multiple modalities and anatomical structures. Researchers tested three prompt modes: auto-prompt (default SAM behavior), box-prompt (using bounding boxes with varying jitter), and point-prompt (using multiple points per object). They systematically varied the number of prompts and jitter levels for box-prompt mode, and the number of points for point-prompt mode. Performance was measured using Dice scores, comparing zero-shot SAM results against both state-of-the-art supervised models and human expert performance where available. The experiments aimed to identify optimal prompt strategies for medical image segmentation tasks.

## Key Results
- Box-prompt mode with minimal jitter achieved the highest average Dice scores across all datasets
- Adding jitter to bounding boxes significantly reduced segmentation accuracy, indicating sensitivity to prompt size
- Increasing the number of point prompts improved performance, approaching box-prompt results
- SAM's zero-shot performance generally lagged behind supervised models but occasionally matched or exceeded state-of-the-art results on specific datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SAM's transformer-based architecture enables effective zero-shot generalization through its promptable design, allowing the model to segment objects without task-specific fine-tuning. The model's decoder uses prompts as guidance to generate segmentation masks, making prompt design critical for performance. In medical images, where anatomical structures have consistent shapes and locations, well-designed prompts can effectively guide SAM to accurate segmentations without requiring domain-specific adaptation.

## Limitations
- Sample sizes per dataset not specified, raising questions about statistical power
- Results reported as averages without confidence intervals, making consistency assessment difficult
- Comparison limited to Dice scores without other metrics like precision or recall
- Computational efficiency and memory requirements not addressed
