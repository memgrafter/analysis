# Summarizing, Simplifying, and Synthesizing Medical Evidence Using GPT-3 (with Varying Success)

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06299
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06299
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: GPT-3 accurately summarizes and simplifies single biomedical documents but struggles to synthesize findings across multiple sources

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates GPT-3's performance in summarizing biomedical literature across single-document and multi-document settings. Medical experts annotated 300 summaries of randomized controlled trials and 150 multi-document syntheses. Results demonstrate that GPT-3 excels at single-document summarization and simplification tasks, producing coherent and useful outputs, though occasionally omitting details. However, in multi-document settings, while GPT-3 generates summaries supported by input articles, it frequently disagrees with expert-written target summaries and struggles to effectively synthesize findings across multiple sources.

## Method Summary
The evaluation framework involved medical experts annotating GPT-3-generated summaries of randomized controlled trials and multi-document syntheses. The study examined both single-document summarization tasks and multi-document synthesis scenarios, comparing GPT-3 outputs against expert-written target summaries. The annotation process focused on assessing coherence, usefulness, accuracy, and the ability to synthesize information across multiple sources.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 accurately summarizes and simplifies single biomedical documents with high coherence and usefulness
- In multi-document settings, GPT-3 generates summaries supported by input articles but frequently disagrees with expert-written target summaries
- Most errors in multi-document synthesis involve underspecification rather than inaccuracies
