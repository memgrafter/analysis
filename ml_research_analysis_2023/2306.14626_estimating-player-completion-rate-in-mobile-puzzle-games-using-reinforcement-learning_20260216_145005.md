# Estimating player completion rate in mobile puzzle games using reinforcement learning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.14626
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Key outcome**: Reinforcement learning agents can estimate level difficulty in mobile puzzle games by correlating their completion behavior with player success rates, with move counts in best runs showing strongest correlation.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores whether reinforcement learning agents can estimate level difficulty in mobile puzzle games by analyzing their behavior patterns relative to human players. Using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agents in Lily's Garden, the authors demonstrate that agent performance metrics, particularly move counts in best runs, correlate with actual player completion rates. Notably, even though agents perform below human-level across all levels, the relative differences in their behavior between levels closely match human patterns. The study proposes a curriculum-based approach where agents train on earlier levels and then estimate difficulty of new levels without further training, offering a practical tool for level designers to evaluate content before release.

## Method Summary
The authors trained PPO-based reinforcement learning agents on Lily's Garden puzzle levels and analyzed their completion behavior across different difficulty tiers. They examined multiple performance metrics including success rates, average moves, and best-run moves, finding that the number of moves taken in the agent's best ~5% runs showed the strongest correlation with human completion rates. The agents were trained on a curriculum of earlier levels, then used to estimate difficulty of new levels without additional training. The study compared agent-human correlations across different metrics and training approaches to identify the most effective method for difficulty estimation.

## Key Results
- Agent move counts in best ~5% runs show strongest correlation with human completion rates
- Even with subhuman performance across all levels, relative behavior differences between levels match human patterns
- Curriculum-based approach allows difficulty estimation of new levels without additional training
- Single game testing (Lily's Garden) limits generalizability to other puzzle game genres

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The correlation between agent and human behavior likely stems from shared problem-solving patterns when facing puzzle constraints, despite differences in cognitive processes. The agents learn to optimize for level completion through repeated attempts, developing strategies that, while not necessarily human-like, produce measurable behavior patterns that reflect underlying level difficulty. The curriculum-based approach works because early levels teach transferable problem-solving skills that generalize to new challenges, similar to how human players develop strategies through progressive difficulty.

## Foundational Learning

**Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**: A reinforcement learning algorithm that balances exploration and exploitation while maintaining training stability through clipped objective functions. Needed for stable agent training on puzzle environments; quick check: verify reward clipping parameters.

**Curriculum Learning**: Training agents on progressively harder tasks to build foundational skills before tackling complex problems. Required for developing transferable problem-solving strategies; quick check: validate level ordering maintains monotonic difficulty increase.

**Correlation Analysis**: Statistical methods for measuring relationships between agent metrics and human completion rates. Essential for validating the difficulty estimation approach; quick check: confirm Pearson correlation coefficients with p-values.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Game Environment -> PPO Agent -> Performance Metrics -> Correlation Analysis -> Difficulty Estimation

**Critical Path**: Environment state → Agent policy network → Action selection → Reward accumulation → Episode termination → Metric calculation → Correlation computation

**Design Tradeoffs**: The study prioritizes correlation strength over agent performance quality, accepting subhuman agent performance to maintain behavioral patterns that match human difficulty perception. This tradeoff enables practical difficulty estimation but limits the approach's applicability to human-level performance analysis.

**Failure Signatures**: Weak correlations may indicate level design flaws, agent training instability, or fundamental mismatches between agent and human problem-solving approaches. Sudden drops in correlation across difficulty tiers suggest curriculum design issues.

**First Experiments**: 1) Validate correlation stability across different PPO hyperparameters, 2) Test curriculum effectiveness with randomized level orderings, 3) Compare multiple performance metrics beyond move counts for correlation strength.

## Limitations
- Single game testing limits generalizability to other puzzle game genres
- Subhuman agent performance raises questions about whether correlations reflect meaningful difficulty patterns or algorithmic biases
- Curriculum approach requires validation across different game progression patterns
- No investigation of environmental factors like network latency or device performance effects

## Confidence
- High confidence: The correlation between agent move counts and human completion rates is empirically demonstrated within the tested game
- Medium confidence: The curriculum-based approach for estimating new level difficulty without retraining
- Low confidence: Generalization of findings to different puzzle game genres and more complex mechanics
