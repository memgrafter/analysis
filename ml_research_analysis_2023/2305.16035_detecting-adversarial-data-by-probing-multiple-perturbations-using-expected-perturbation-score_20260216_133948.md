# Detecting Adversarial Data by Probing Multiple Perturbations Using Expected Perturbation Score

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16035
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16035
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: EPS-AD achieves near-perfect AUROC scores across different network architectures and attack scenarios, consistently outperforming existing baselines.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes EPS-AD, a novel adversarial detection method that leverages expected perturbation scores from diffusion models. By computing scores across multiple perturbations and timesteps, EPS-AD captures multi-view observations that distinguish natural from adversarial data. The method employs EPS-based MMD to measure distributional discrepancies, achieving superior performance against various attacks including unseen and transferable ones on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets.

## Method Summary
The method computes Expected Perturbation Scores (EPS) by perturbing samples with multiple noise levels and estimating scores using pre-trained diffusion models. It then applies EPS-based Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) to measure distributional differences between natural and adversarial samples. The approach uses Score SDE for CIFAR-10 and Guided Diffusion for ImageNet, with deep kernel MMD for detection. Training involves computing EPS for natural and adversarial samples, then training the MMD metric to distinguish between them based on distributional distance.

## Key Results
- EPS-AD consistently outperforms existing baselines with near-perfect AUROC scores across different network architectures
- The method demonstrates strong performance against unseen attacks and transferable attacks
- EPS-AD achieves superior results on both CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets at 256×256 resolution

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Expected Perturbation Score (EPS) captures multi-view information that single-sample scores miss
- Mechanism: EPS computes the expected score over multiple timesteps and perturbations, creating a richer statistical representation
- Core assumption: Adversarial distributions differ systematically from natural distributions in score evolution across perturbations
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the score is sensitive in identifying adversarial samples due to insufficient information with one sample only"
  - [section 3.1]: "we consider multiple levels of noise perturbations to diversify one sample, allowing us to capture multi-view observations of one sample"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus focuses on perturbation-based detection but not EPS specifically
- Break condition: If adversarial perturbations create the same score evolution pattern as natural samples across perturbations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: EPS-based MMD creates a robust distributional distance metric for adversarial detection
- Mechanism: Uses EPS vectors as input features and computes maximum mean discrepancy between test sample EPSs and natural sample EPSs
- Core assumption: Distributional distance between natural and adversarial EPSs is consistently larger than among natural EPSs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2]: "we adopt the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) between the test sample and natural samples relying on EPS"
  - [section 3.3]: "we theoretically show that the EPS-based MMD between natural and adversarial samples is larger than that among natural samples"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus mentions perturbation discrepancy but not MMD-based approaches
- Break condition: If adversarial samples can be crafted to match natural EPS distributions

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pre-trained diffusion models provide accurate score estimation for EPS computation
- Mechanism: Leverages existing score-based diffusion models trained via score matching to estimate ∇x log pt(x) for any sample
- Core assumption: Pre-trained diffusion models generalize well to adversarial samples even without adversarial training
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "we use a pre-trained diffusion model to estimate EPS for each sample"
  - [section 2]: "By optimizing Eq. (4), with sufficient data and model capacity, score matching ensures that the optimal solution to Eq. (4) equals ∇x log pt(x)"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus mentions diffusion models but not for adversarial detection specifically
- Break condition: If diffusion model score estimates become unreliable for out-of-distribution adversarial samples

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Pre-trained diffusion model -> EPS computation module -> MMD computation module -> Detection threshold module

- Critical path:
  1. Load pre-trained diffusion model
  2. For each test sample, generate perturbations at multiple timesteps
  3. Compute EPS for test sample and natural samples
  4. Calculate deep kernel MMD between test EPS and natural EPS distribution
  5. Apply threshold to classify as adversarial/natural

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Resolution vs speed: Higher resolution inputs yield better detection but slower inference
  - Timestep count vs accuracy: More timesteps provide better EPS estimation but increase computation
  - Kernel choice vs robustness: Deep kernel MMD is more expressive but has more hyperparameters

- Failure signatures:
  - Low AUROC across multiple attacks suggests EPS doesn't capture sufficient distributional differences
  - High sensitivity to timestep indicates score estimation instability
  - Poor transferability performance suggests over-reliance on specific dataset statistics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare EPS-AD with S-N baseline on CIFAR-10 under PGD attack with varying ϵ
  2. Test sensitivity to timestep by varying T and measuring AUROC degradation
  3. Evaluate cross-dataset transferability by training on ImageNet, testing on CIFAR-10 adversarial samples

## Limitations
- The method's reliance on pre-trained diffusion models raises concerns about generalization to out-of-distribution adversarial samples
- Computational cost of generating multiple perturbations and computing EPS may limit scalability to larger datasets
- Limited ablation studies leave uncertainty about hyperparameter sensitivity

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The theoretical framework for EPS-based MMD is well-established with solid mathematical foundations
- Medium Confidence: Experimental results on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet show strong performance but limited ablation studies
- Medium Confidence: Claims of transferability detection are supported but not extensively validated across diverse attack scenarios
