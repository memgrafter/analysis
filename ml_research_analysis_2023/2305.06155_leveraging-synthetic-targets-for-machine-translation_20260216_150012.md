# Leveraging Synthetic Targets for Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.06155
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06155
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 18
- Key outcome: Synthetic target data from a large pre-trained model consistently improves machine translation performance, especially for smaller student models and low-resource settings.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a method to improve machine translation in resource-constrained settings by training student models on synthetic target data generated from a large pre-trained teacher model, rather than using ground-truth translations. The approach consistently outperforms traditional training on real data across bilingual, multilingual, and speech translation tasks, with improvements attributed to the more deterministic nature of synthetic targets. Experiments demonstrate benefits particularly for smaller models and limited data regimes, suggesting this paradigm could be valuable for low-resource language pairs.

## Method Summary
The method involves training a large pre-trained NMT model (teacher) to generate synthetic translations for source sentences via beam search, then training smaller student models on these synthetic targets instead of ground-truth data. The approach is evaluated across multiple settings: bilingual translation with various student model sizes, multilingual translation in both directions, and speech translation. Models are trained using standard Transformer architectures with pre-layer normalization, label smoothing, and dropout, with synthetic data generation using beam size 4.

## Key Results
- Synthetic target training consistently outperforms real data training, with gains up to +3.1 BLEU for small student models
- Benefits increase with smaller student model sizes and lower-resource settings
- Improvements hold across bilingual, multilingual, and speech translation tasks
- Synthetic targets show lower predictive entropy, indicating more deterministic outputs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Training on synthetic targets is easier to optimize than training on ground-truth data.
- Mechanism: Synthetic targets from a large teacher model are more deterministic and have lower entropy, making the optimization landscape smoother and easier for the student model to learn.
- Core assumption: The teacher model's outputs are sufficiently deterministic and close to optimal translations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The improvements are attributed to the more deterministic nature of the synthetic targets compared to the ground-truth data."
  - [section]: "Our findings in Table 8 highlight that indeed the predictive entropy of models trained with synthetic targets is lower than the models trained with ground-truth targets, implying more deterministic nature of the former translation systems."

- Break condition: If the teacher model is not sufficiently accurate or deterministic, the synthetic targets may not provide a smoother optimization landscape.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Synthetic targets provide better generalization to out-of-distribution domains.
- Mechanism: The deterministic nature of synthetic targets allows the student model to learn more robust and generalizable features, improving performance on unseen domains.
- Core assumption: The teacher model is trained on a diverse and large dataset, providing synthetic targets that capture general translation patterns.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "We also provide preliminary analysis into whether this boost in performance is linked to ease of optimization or more deterministic nature of the predictions, and whether this paradigm leads to better out-of-distribution performance across different testing domains."
  - [section]: "Our experiments on multilingual translation in both directions reveal that when we use bilingual experts for each language pair as teachers, we see consistent improvements when training solely on synthetic data."

- Break condition: If the teacher model is not diverse enough or overfits to a specific domain, the synthetic targets may not provide good generalization.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Synthetic targets enable efficient knowledge transfer from large to small models.
- Mechanism: By using the outputs of a large pre-trained teacher model as targets, the student model can learn to mimic the teacher's translation abilities without requiring the same amount of training data or computational resources.
- Core assumption: The teacher model is sufficiently large and well-trained to provide high-quality synthetic targets.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "In this work, we provide a recipe for training machine translation models in a limited resource setting by leveraging synthetic target data generated using a large pre-trained model."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Teacher model (24×6 Transformer) -> Beam search (beam size 4) -> Synthetic targets -> Student model (2×2, 6×6, or 24×6 Transformer)

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-train a large NMT model (teacher)
  2. Use the teacher model to generate synthetic targets for source sentences
  3. Train a smaller NMT model (student) on the synthetic targets

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Model size: Larger teacher models may provide better synthetic targets but require more computational resources
  - Data quality: The quality of the synthetic targets depends on the teacher model's performance and the diversity of the training data
  - Training time: Generating synthetic targets and training the student model can be time-consuming

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on the target task
  - Slow convergence during training
  - High variance in the student model's predictions

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a small NMT model on real data and evaluate its performance
  2. Train a small NMT model on synthetic targets generated by a large pre-trained model and compare its performance to the model trained on real data
  3. Vary the size of the teacher model and the amount of synthetic data to study their impact on the student model's performance

## Limitations
- Teacher Model Quality Dependency: The approach's success critically depends on the teacher model's translation quality and its ability to produce diverse yet deterministic outputs.
- Domain Generalization Evidence: While the paper claims synthetic targets may improve out-of-domain performance, the evidence is preliminary and not systematically tested across diverse domains.
- Optimization Landscape Claims: The assertion that synthetic targets create a "smoother optimization landscape" is supported by entropy measurements but lacks direct empirical validation of optimization dynamics.

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The core empirical finding that synthetic targets consistently outperform real data for student training is well-supported by extensive experiments across multiple language pairs, model sizes, and data regimes.
- Medium Confidence: The explanation that deterministic synthetic targets lead to better optimization and knowledge transfer is plausible given the entropy measurements, but the mechanistic link between determinism and performance could be more rigorously established.
- Low Confidence: Claims about superior out-of-domain generalization and the specific optimization benefits require more systematic validation across diverse test domains and optimization trajectory analyses.

## Next Checks
1. Teacher Quality Ablation: Systematically vary teacher model quality (smaller models, different architectures, varying training data sizes) to determine the minimum requirements for effective synthetic target generation.

2. Domain Transfer Experiment: Design a controlled experiment testing student models trained on synthetic targets across multiple distinct domains (e.g., news, subtitles, technical documentation) to validate generalization claims.

3. Optimization Dynamics Analysis: Compare training curves, gradient norms, and loss landscapes between synthetic and real data training to provide direct evidence for the "smoother optimization" hypothesis.
