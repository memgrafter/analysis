# On the Soundness of XAI in Prognostics and Health Management (PHM)

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.05517
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05517
- Reference count: 7
- Key outcome: Grad-CAM outperforms other XAI methods for explaining regression models on time series data, particularly when applied to intermediate convolutional layers rather than the final layer.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effectiveness of various Explainable AI (XAI) methods for interpreting deep learning models used in Prognostics and Health Management (PHM), specifically for predicting Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of aircraft engines. The authors compare five XAI methods (LIME, SHAP, LRP, Saliency Maps, and Grad-CAM) using eight quantitative proxies that measure desirable properties of explanations. Their findings challenge the common assumption in image processing that the last convolutional layer is optimal for explanations, showing instead that intermediate layers perform better for time series regression tasks. The study also proposes an extension to Grad-CAM that accounts for both time and feature dimensions, improving interpretability for temporal data.

## Method Summary
The study employs a Deep Convolutional Neural Network (DCNN) trained on the N-CMAPSS dataset to predict RUL of aircraft engines. Five XAI methods are applied to the trained model and evaluated using eight quantitative proxies measuring properties like identity, separability, stability, selectivity, coherence, completeness, congruency, and acumen. The authors propose an extension to Grad-CAM that incorporates time and feature dimension contributions through weighting factors β and σ. The evaluation is performed on 256 held-out test samples with 20 time series each of 160 time units, comparing proxy scores across all methods to determine the most effective approach for time series regression in PHM applications.

## Key Results
- Grad-CAM outperforms other XAI methods, achieving the highest values for five out of eight proxies and being close to maximum in two others.
- The optimal layer for Grad-CAM is not the final convolutional layer but rather an intermediate layer (layer 7 in this architecture), contradicting common image processing assumptions.
- The proposed extension to Grad-CAM that accounts for both time and feature dimensions significantly improves interpretability for time series data.
- The weighting factors β and σ in the extended Grad-CAM formulation show direct correlation with most proxies, with optimal values typically between 0.2 and 0.4.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Grad-CAM outperforms other XAI methods for regression tasks on time series data by leveraging gradient information from intermediate convolutional layers.
- Mechanism: Grad-CAM computes neuron importance scores by aggregating gradients from the output layer back to feature maps in convolutional layers. By using intermediate layers instead of the final layer, Grad-CAM captures more localized and relevant features for time series analysis.
- Core assumption: The importance of features in time series regression problems is better captured by intermediate convolutional layers rather than the final layer.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The results show that GRAD-CAM is the most robust method, and that the best layer is not the bottom one, as is commonly seen within the context of Image Processing."
  - [section] "Results showed that Grad-CAM was the most effective XAI method among the ones tested, achieving the highest values for five out of eight proxies and being close to the maximum in two others."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on PHM but not specifically on Grad-CAM's performance in regression tasks.
- Break condition: If the relationship between feature importance and layer depth is not consistent across different datasets or if intermediate layers do not provide meaningful feature maps.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The proposed extension to Grad-CAM that accounts for both time and feature dimensions improves the interpretability of explanations.
- Mechanism: The extension introduces additional components to the Grad-CAM heat map calculation, namely the time component contribution and the individual time series contribution. These components are weighted by factors β and σ respectively, allowing for modulation of their influence.
- Core assumption: Time series data contains both temporal and feature-based patterns that contribute to the model's predictions, and these patterns can be effectively captured by the extended Grad-CAM formulation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "an alternative version of Grad-CAM is proposed, which takes into account both the time and time series dimensions of the input, improving its interpretability."
  - [section] "The factor being discussed shows a direct correlation with all proxies excepting congruency and coherence, which present an inverse correlation."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers do not discuss time series extensions to Grad-CAM.
- Break condition: If the additional components do not improve the interpretability across different types of time series data or if the weighting factors do not generalize well.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Quantitative proxies are effective in evaluating and comparing the quality of explanations provided by different XAI methods.
- Mechanism: The proxies measure desirable properties of explanations, such as identity, separability, stability, selectivity, coherence, completeness, congruency, and acumen. By computing these proxies for each XAI method, the study provides a quantitative basis for comparison.
- Core assumption: The defined proxies accurately capture the desirable properties of explanations and are applicable across different XAI methods and regression tasks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "This study uses the most relevant quantitative proxies found in the literature [...] to evaluate and compare each method."
  - [section] "Results showed that Grad-CAM was the most effective XAI method among the ones tested, achieving the highest values for five out of eight proxies and being close to the maximum in two others."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - related papers focus on PHM but do not discuss the use of quantitative proxies for XAI method comparison.
- Break condition: If the proxies do not correlate with human judgment of explanation quality or if they fail to differentiate between XAI methods in other contexts.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different layer choices in Grad-CAM affect the explanation quality for time series regression tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explicitly states that Grad-CAM was applied to different convolutional layers, and results showed that the best layer was not the last one as commonly assumed in image processing.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that the second layer performed best, it does not provide a systematic analysis of why certain layers work better than others or how this varies across different types of time series data.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study testing Grad-CAM across multiple time series datasets with varying characteristics (frequency, amplitude, noise levels) and systematically analyzing which layers provide optimal explanations for different data types.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the proposed time-dependence component in Grad-CAM perform compared to other XAI methods specifically designed for time series?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper introduces a time component to Grad-CAM but does not compare it to XAI methods specifically designed for time series data.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares traditional XAI methods without evaluating whether the time-dependence modification makes Grad-CAM competitive with methods specifically designed for time series analysis.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative study testing the modified Grad-CAM against XAI methods specifically designed for time series data on multiple benchmark datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust are the proposed quantitative proxies for evaluating XAI methods in time series regression tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses eight quantitative proxies to evaluate XAI methods but acknowledges that these proxies approximate desirable properties rather than directly measuring explanation quality.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not validate whether these proxies are reliable indicators of true explanation quality or whether they might be sensitive to specific dataset characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: A validation study using synthetic time series data with known ground truth explanations to test whether the proxies accurately identify the most accurate XAI methods.

## Limitations
- The study's findings are based on a single dataset (N-CMAPSS) and one specific regression task, limiting generalizability.
- The quantitative proxies used have not been validated against human judgment of explanation quality.
- The optimal layer selection for Grad-CAM was determined empirically through grid search, which may not generalize across different model architectures or datasets.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The methodological framework for comparing XAI methods using quantitative proxies is well-established and reproducible. The empirical observation that intermediate layers outperform final layers for Grad-CAM is consistent across multiple experiments within the study.
- **Medium Confidence**: The extension of Grad-CAM to account for time and feature dimensions shows promising results, but the weighting parameters (β and σ) were optimized for this specific dataset and may not generalize.
- **Low Confidence**: The study's conclusion that Grad-CAM is the "most robust" XAI method overall is limited by the narrow scope of tested methods and the specific PHM context.
