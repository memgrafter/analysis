# Goniometers are a Powerful Acoustic Feature for Music Information Retrieval Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01090
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01090
- Reference count: 14
- Primary result: Goniometer features effectively separate and cluster music genres in MIR tasks

## Executive Summary
This study introduces goniometer-based acoustic features for music information retrieval, motivated by their established use in audio production for monitoring spatial and dynamic aspects of mixes. The proposed features include phase scope (quantifying point cloud distribution via box counting) and channel correlation (measuring normalized correlation between stereo channels). These features demonstrate strong performance in distinguishing between contrasting music genres and clustering broader genre categories, offering causal interpretability advantages over traditional features like MFCCs.

## Method Summary
The research extracts two goniometer-based features from audio signals: phase scope and channel correlation. Phase scope measures the distribution of audio samples in the stereo field using box counting methods, while channel correlation quantifies the normalized correlation between left and right audio channels. The features were evaluated through genre comparison and clustering using self-organizing maps, demonstrating their ability to separate contrasting genres (e.g., drum & bass vs. meditation music) and nonlinearly cluster broader categories (e.g., classical vs. hip hop). The study also showed potential for clustering individual albums.

## Key Results
- Goniometer features successfully linearly separate contrasting music genres
- Features effectively nonlinearly cluster broader genre categories and individual albums
- Two features demonstrate orthogonality, providing complementary information with low computational cost

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Goniometers capture spatial and dynamic properties of audio that directly relate to production choices and genre characteristics. The phase scope feature measures how audio energy is distributed in the stereo field, while channel correlation quantifies the relationship between channels. These physical properties of the audio signal provide interpretable features that correlate with musical characteristics, enabling effective genre discrimination and clustering.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Audio Signal -> Goniometer Analysis -> Phase Scope + Channel Correlation -> Feature Vector -> SOM Clustering

Critical Path: The core workflow involves converting stereo audio to goniometer representation, extracting phase scope and channel correlation features, and using these for clustering analysis. The goniometer analysis serves as the central processing step that transforms raw audio into interpretable spatial features.

Design Tradeoffs: The approach trades computational simplicity for potentially missing fine-grained spectral information captured by traditional features. However, this simplicity enables real-time processing and provides causal interpretability that deep learning features often lack.

Failure Signatures: Poor performance may occur with mono recordings (no stereo information), heavily compressed audio (reduced dynamic range), or genres with unconventional production techniques. The features may also struggle with complex polyphonic music where spatial cues are less prominent.
