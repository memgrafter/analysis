# Obeying the Order: Introducing Ordered Transfer Hyperparameter Optimisation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.16916
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16916
- Reference count: 40
- Introduces Ordered Transfer Hyperparameter Optimisation (OTHPO) that exploits sequential task correlation for improved hyperparameter tuning efficiency

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Ordered Transfer Hyperparameter Optimisation (OTHPO), a novel approach that leverages the sequential nature of tasks in hyperparameter optimisation by transferring knowledge from the most recent tasks. Unlike standard transfer HPO methods, OTHPO assumes each task is most correlated to those immediately before it, matching practical settings where models are retuned as data accumulates. The authors provide a formal definition, ten benchmarks spanning diverse algorithms (XGBoost, random forest, approximate k-nearest neighbor, elastic net, SVM, and a real-world simulation problem), and demonstrate that simple OTHPO methods outperform state-of-the-art transfer HPO approaches.

## Method Summary
OTHPO formalizes the idea that in sequential hyperparameter optimisation, each new task is most similar to its immediate predecessors rather than all previous tasks equally. The method maintains and updates surrogate models that prioritize information from the most recent tasks when searching for optimal hyperparameters in the current task. This ordered transfer approach is particularly suited to scenarios where models are retuned as new data arrives or where hyperparameter tuning is performed sequentially across related problems. The authors implement several baseline OTHPO methods and compare them against traditional transfer HPO methods across ten benchmarks covering various ML algorithms and a real-world NewsVendor simulation.

## Key Results
- OTHPO methods achieve better performance with fewer hyperparameter evaluations compared to state-of-the-art transfer HPO
- Mean improvements include 21.7% in profit for NewsVendor, 22.5% in misclassified points for XGBoost, and 5.8% in AUC for SVM 1220
- OTHPO shows significantly reduced variance in results compared to standard transfer methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
OTHPO works by exploiting the natural ordering and correlation structure present in sequential hyperparameter optimisation tasks. By recognizing that each new task is most similar to its immediate predecessors, the method can build more accurate surrogate models that focus on the most relevant historical information. This targeted transfer of knowledge reduces the search space more effectively than traditional methods that treat all previous tasks equally, leading to faster convergence and better final performance with fewer evaluations.

## Foundational Learning
- **Sequential Task Correlation**: Understanding that consecutive hyperparameter optimisation tasks often share similar optimal regions in hyperparameter space - needed to justify the ordered transfer assumption; quick check: visualize performance similarity across consecutive tasks
- **Surrogate Model Updating**: Knowledge of how Bayesian optimisation surrogates can be efficiently updated with new task information - needed to implement OTHPO's incremental learning; quick check: verify surrogate prediction accuracy improves with ordered task additions
- **Transfer Learning in HPO**: Familiarity with how knowledge from previous optimisation runs can accelerate new runs - needed as foundation for OTHPO's approach; quick check: compare transfer vs. non-transfer baseline performance

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Task Sequence -> Surrogate Model Updates -> Hyperparameter Search -> Evaluation -> Next Task

**Critical Path**: The core workflow involves receiving a new task, updating the surrogate model with information weighted by recency, performing Bayesian optimisation using the updated surrogate, evaluating the new configuration, and repeating for the next task in sequence.

**Design Tradeoffs**: OTHPO trades computational overhead of maintaining and updating surrogate models across ordered tasks for improved sample efficiency and final performance. The method assumes monotonic correlation decay with task distance, which may not hold in all sequential settings.

**Failure Signatures**: Performance degradation occurs when task correlations don't follow the assumed ordered structure, when hyperparameter spaces change significantly between tasks, or when the sequence contains outlier tasks that violate the local similarity assumption.

**First Experiments**: 1) Validate correlation decay assumption by measuring task similarity as a function of sequence distance; 2) Benchmark against non-transfer HPO on synthetic sequential tasks with known correlation structure; 3) Test sensitivity to task re-ordering on simple benchmark problems.

## Limitations
- Assumes monotonic correlation decay with task distance, which may not hold in all sequential settings
- Computational overhead of maintaining and updating surrogate models across ordered tasks not fully characterized
- Evaluation limited to synthetic and simulated sequential tasks; real-world data streams with concept drift not addressed

## Confidence
- **High**: OTHPO outperforms standard transfer HPO in evaluated sequential settings; method is novel in its ordered-task assumption
- **Medium**: Stability and robustness across broader, more heterogeneous, or noisy task sequences; scalability to very large hyperparameter spaces
- **Low**: Handling task re-ordering, non-sequential dependencies, or abrupt distribution shifts

## Next Checks
1. Evaluate OTHPO on real-world data streams with known concept drift or non-stationary distributions to test robustness of the ordered correlation assumption
2. Conduct ablation studies comparing OTHPO to methods that allow flexible inter-task dependencies, and assess performance under task re-ordering or noisy correlations
3. Benchmark OTHPO's computational overhead and memory usage across many tasks and high-dimensional hyperparameter spaces, and characterize scaling behavior
