# Learning to Relate to Previous Turns in Conversational Search

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02553
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02553
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Up to 19.1% MRR and 20.6% NDCG@3 improvement over expanding with all historical queries

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of query expansion in conversational search by proposing a method to automatically select useful historical queries for expanding the current query. The authors introduce a pseudo-labeling approach that identifies relevant historical queries based on their impact on retrieval performance, then train a selector model using these labels. They further enhance the method with a multi-task learning framework that jointly optimizes both the selector and retriever during fine-tuning, achieving significant improvements across four conversational search datasets.

## Method Summary
The method consists of two main components: a pseudo-labeling approach to annotate useful historical queries, and a multi-task learning framework for joint optimization. First, pseudo-relevance labels (PRL) are generated by measuring whether adding each historical query to the current query improves retrieval performance using MRR as the metric. A selector model is then trained to classify historical queries as relevant or not for expansion. Finally, the selector and retriever are fine-tuned jointly using a multi-task learning framework with contrastive loss, where the balance between tasks is controlled by a parameter α.

## Key Results
- Up to 19.1% MRR improvement and 20.6% NDCG@3 improvement over expanding with all historical queries
- BERT-based selector outperforms ANCE-based selector on all datasets
- Weight assignment strategy improves retrieval but reduces selector precision
- Multi-task learning framework provides additional gains over single-task training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Pseudo-labeling based on retrieval impact effectively identifies useful historical queries for expansion
- **Mechanism:** The method generates pseudo-labels by measuring whether adding a historical query to the current query improves retrieval performance (MRR). Queries that cause improvement are labeled positive, others negative
- **Core assumption:** A relevant historical query should improve retrieval performance when used for expansion
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "we use a pseudo-labeling approach to annotate useful historical queries based on their impact on the retrieval results"
  - [section 4.1] "We assume that a relevant historical turn/term should make a positive impact when the current query is expanded with it"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no direct corpus measurements of pseudo-label accuracy
- **Break condition:** If the retriever baseline is poor, the pseudo-labels may not accurately reflect true relevance, leading to incorrect training signals

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Multi-task learning jointly optimizes selector and retriever, improving conversational search performance
- **Mechanism:** The method trains a selector to assign weights to historical queries while simultaneously optimizing the retriever using a contrastive learning loss, with a weighting parameter α controlling the balance
- **Core assumption:** The selector and retriever are interdependent - changes in one affect the optimal behavior of the other
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We further propose a multi-task learning framework to jointly train the selector and the retriever during fine-tuning"
  - [section 5.3] "We propose a multi-task learning method to jointly fine-tune the selector and retriever using conversational search data"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no detailed analysis of how selector and retriever weights co-evolve during training
- **Break condition:** If α is poorly chosen, one task may dominate, preventing effective joint optimization

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Weight assignment strategy improves selector training by focusing on positive examples
- **Mechanism:** During training, positive queries receive higher weights than negative ones, calculated as w[y] = |negative class|/|class y|, to force the selector to prioritize identifying useful expansions
- **Core assumption:** The class imbalance (more negative than positive examples) requires weighting to ensure the model learns to identify useful expansions
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 5.2.1] "we assign higher weights to positive queries than to negative ones" and "the weight assignment regularizes the model to focus more on the positive category"
  - [section 7.1] "By exploiting the weight assignment (W.A.) strategy, all the metric scores except the recall of the selector drop a lot"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - the paper shows improved retrieval performance with weight assignment, though at the cost of lower precision
- **Break condition:** If weighting is too aggressive, the model may become overly conservative and miss potentially useful expansions

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Current query → Selector (with historical queries) → Selected expansions → Expanded query → Retriever → Ranked passages

- **Critical path:** Current query → Selector (with historical queries) → Selected expansions → Expanded query → Retriever → Ranked passages

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Static vs. dynamic selection: The method uses static pseudo-labels vs. updating them during fine-tuning
  - Explicit vs. implicit selection: Weight assignment provides "soft" selection vs. hard filtering
  - Single-task vs. multi-task: Joint optimization vs. separate training

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor pseudo-labels: Selector accuracy drops, retrieval performance degrades
  - Weight imbalance: Selector overfits to either positive or negative examples
  - Multi-task instability: Retriever performance fluctuates during joint training

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Baseline comparison:** Run with No expand, All expand, and our method on TopiOCQA to verify improvement claims
  2. **Selector ablation:** Compare BERT PRL, ANCE PRL, and + Weight Assignment variants to understand contribution of each component
  3. **Multi-task sensitivity:** Test different α values in the multi-task loss to find optimal balance between selector and retriever training

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the quality of pseudo-relevance labels (PRL) change over multiple iterations of selector training and retriever fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that "a potentially better strategy is to update the gold PRL based on the fine-tuned retriever in every epoch" but leave this for future work.
- Why unresolved: The current method uses static PRL generated from the base retriever, which may become outdated as the retriever changes during fine-tuning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing retrieval performance using static PRL vs. iteratively updated PRL throughout the fine-tuning process.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different weighting strategies for positive vs. negative historical queries during selector training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors use a weight assignment strategy where positive queries are assigned higher weights than negative ones, but they note this affects precision and recall differently.
- Why unresolved: The optimal weighting strategy for balancing precision and recall in selector training is not explored beyond the simple inverse class frequency approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing various weighting schemes (e.g., different ratios, dynamic weights based on query difficulty) and their impact on final retrieval performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does incorporating retrieval results from historical queries affect the selection of useful expansion queries?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note in the conclusion that "we have not explored all aspects of the method" and specifically mention that "the latter [retrieval results] could also be used" alongside historical queries.
- Why unresolved: The current approach only considers historical queries themselves, not their retrieval results, which could provide additional signals for relevance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the proposed method with variants that incorporate historical retrieval results (e.g., using top retrieved passages from previous queries as additional context).

## Limitations
- Potential for noisy pseudo-labels if baseline retriever is suboptimal
- Sensitivity to weight assignment parameter α which could lead to imbalanced optimization
- Limited analysis of computational overhead during inference

## Confidence
- **Medium** for pseudo-labeling and query selection effectiveness (strong experimental results across datasets)
- **Low** for multi-task learning framework claims (limited ablation studies and no detailed analysis of co-evolution)

## Next Checks
1. **Pseudo-label quality analysis**: Evaluate the accuracy of the pseudo-labels by comparing them against human annotations on a subset of queries, and test how label noise affects final retrieval performance.

2. **Selector ablation under different retriever qualities**: Test the method with weaker retriever baselines to understand when pseudo-labeling becomes unreliable and whether the selector can adapt to different retrieval contexts.

3. **Multi-task learning sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary the α parameter across a wider range and measure both selector accuracy and retrieval performance to identify optimal weighting strategies and potential instability regions.
