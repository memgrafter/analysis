# Self-Agreement: A Framework for Fine-tuning Language Models to Find Agreement among Diverse Opinions

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.11460
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11460
- **Reference count**: 22
- **Primary result**: 7B parameter LLaMA model fine-tuned with Self-Agreement achieves comparable performance to GPT-3 (175B parameters) in finding consensus among conflicting opinions

## Executive Summary
Self-Agreement is a novel framework for fine-tuning large language models to autonomously find agreement among diverse opinions without requiring human-annotated data. The method leverages GPT-3 to generate multiple opinions and potential agreement statements for each question, then uses a BERT-based model to select the highest-scoring agreement. This creates a synthetic question-opinion-agreement dataset that is used to fine-tune a pre-trained LLM. The framework demonstrates that a 7-billion-parameter LLaMA model can achieve performance comparable to GPT-3 (175B parameters) in consensus finding tasks, representing a 25x reduction in model size while maintaining effectiveness.

## Method Summary
The Self-Agreement framework operates in three main stages: first, it uses GPT-3 to generate multiple diverse opinions for each input question, along with candidate agreement statements; second, a BERT-based model evaluates and selects the highest-scoring agreement from the generated candidates; and third, the resulting question-opinion-agreement triplets form a synthetic dataset that fine-tunes a pre-trained LLM. This approach eliminates the need for human-annotated consensus data by creating synthetic training examples through self-generation and selection processes. The framework is designed to handle conflicting opinions and extract meaningful agreement statements that represent common ground among diverse perspectives.

## Key Results
- A 7-billion-parameter LLaMA model fine-tuned with Self-Agreement achieves comparable performance to GPT-3 (175B parameters) on the MultiOpinQA dataset
- The framework demonstrates effectiveness in finding consensus among conflicting opinions without human-annotated data
- Self-Agreement uses only 1/25 of GPT-3's parameters while maintaining competitive performance

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The Self-Agreement framework works by leveraging the generative capabilities of large language models to create synthetic training data that captures the essence of finding agreement among diverse opinions. GPT-3's ability to generate multiple perspectives on a given question, combined with BERT's discriminative power to select the most representative agreement, creates a powerful data synthesis pipeline. The fine-tuning process then allows a smaller LLM to learn the underlying patterns of consensus-finding without requiring the massive parameter count of GPT-3 during inference. This approach effectively transfers knowledge from the large generative model to a more compact, deployable model while preserving the ability to identify common ground among conflicting viewpoints.

## Foundational Learning
- **Synthetic Data Generation**: Creating training data through model-generated examples rather than human annotation. Needed because consensus-finding datasets are expensive to create manually. Quick check: Verify generated opinions are diverse and cover different perspectives on the same question.
- **BERT-based Agreement Selection**: Using discriminative models to evaluate and rank candidate agreements. Needed to identify the most representative consensus statement from multiple options. Quick check: Ensure selected agreements capture shared elements across diverse opinions.
- **Knowledge Distillation**: Transferring capabilities from large models to smaller, more efficient ones. Needed to make consensus-finding practical for deployment. Quick check: Compare performance of fine-tuned small model against large teacher model.
- **Multi-opinion Consensus Finding**: The task of identifying agreement points among conflicting viewpoints. Needed for applications requiring synthesis of diverse perspectives. Quick check: Evaluate whether found agreements represent genuine common ground.
- **Parameter-efficient Fine-tuning**: Adapting large pre-trained models with limited computational resources. Needed to make the approach accessible. Quick check: Monitor fine-tuning stability and avoid catastrophic forgetting.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: GPT-3 (opinion generation) -> BERT (agreement selection) -> LLM fine-tuning pipeline -> Consensus-finding model

**Critical Path**: The most important sequence is GPT-3 generating diverse opinions → BERT selecting agreement → LLM fine-tuning on synthetic data. This path determines the quality of the final consensus-finding capability.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The framework trades computational cost during training (generating multiple opinions per question using GPT-3) for reduced inference costs (smaller 7B model vs 175B). Alternative approaches could use few-shot prompting or smaller generative models, but this would likely compromise the quality and diversity of generated opinions.

**Failure Signatures**: The system may fail when opinions are too polarized to find meaningful agreement, when GPT-3 generates opinions that are too similar (reducing diversity), or when BERT consistently selects suboptimal agreements due to training data biases. Performance degradation would be most visible on highly contentious topics or specialized domains.

**First 3 Experiments**:
1. Measure agreement diversity by computing pairwise similarity scores between GPT-3 generated opinions
2. Compare BERT-selected agreements against human-curated consensus on a validation subset
3. Ablation study removing either opinion generation or agreement selection stages to quantify their individual contributions

## Limitations
- Evaluation primarily focuses on the MultiOpinQA dataset, raising questions about generalizability to other opinion diversity scenarios
- Performance on truly polarizing or highly nuanced opinions remains unclear
- Computational cost of generating multiple opinions and agreement candidates using GPT-3 is substantial but not thoroughly discussed for practical deployment

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core methodology and comparative performance between the 7B parameter fine-tuned model and GPT-3 (175B) on MultiOpinQA are well-supported by experimental results.

**Medium Confidence**: Generalizability to other domains and opinion types beyond MultiOpinQA requires further validation. The assumption that agreement among opinions is always desirable may not hold in all contexts.

**Low Confidence**: Insufficient analysis of failure cases or edge scenarios where Self-Agreement might produce misleading consensus. Quality of GPT-3 generated agreements is assumed rather than independently verified.

## Next Checks

1. Evaluate Self-Agreement performance on datasets with different opinion distributions and domains (e.g., highly technical topics, emotionally charged issues) to assess generalizability beyond MultiOpinQA.

2. Conduct ablation studies to quantify the contribution of each component: GPT-3 opinion generation, BERT agreement selection, and fine-tuning of the target LLM.

3. Perform human evaluation studies comparing agreements generated by Self-Agreement against human-curated consensus on the same opinion sets to assess alignment with human judgment.
