# Investigating Catastrophic Overfitting in Fast Adversarial Training: A Self-fitting Perspective

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.11963
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.11963
- Reference count: 36
- Primary result: This paper reveals that catastrophic overfitting occurs when networks learn to recognize self-information embedded in single-step adversarial perturbations, leading to overfitting to specific attacks.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates catastrophic overfitting (CO) in fast adversarial training (FAT), where multi-step robust accuracy suddenly drops to zero while single-step robust accuracy increases rapidly. The authors propose a novel "self-fitting" perspective, revealing that adversarial perturbations contain both data-information and self-information (model-related information). After CO, networks learn to recognize self-information embedded in single-step perturbations, leading to overfitting to specific attacks. The authors demonstrate this through experiments showing that networks with CO can still achieve high accuracy on random-label FGSM examples, indicating reliance on self-information. They also observe a "channel differentiation" phenomenon where certain channels become dominant in recognizing self-information while others are suppressed. Based on these insights, the authors provide new understanding of existing CO mitigation techniques and extend the concept to multi-step adversarial training.

## Method Summary
The authors reproduce catastrophic overfitting by training ResNet18 and WideResNet28-10 on CIFAR-10 using FGSM adversarial training with epsilon values of 8/255 and 16/255. They use zero initialization for adversarial perturbations, SGD optimizer, learning rate decay, and no weight decay to reproduce CO stably. The training monitors clean accuracy, FGSM accuracy, and PGD accuracy to detect CO occurrence. Post-training analysis includes measuring random-label FGSM accuracy, analyzing channel variance in convolutional layers, and performing pruning experiments to validate the self-fitting mechanism.

## Key Results
- Networks with catastrophic overfitting can achieve over 10% accuracy on random-label FGSM examples, indicating recognition of self-information rather than data-information
- Channel differentiation occurs after CO, with high-variance channels becoming dominant for recognizing adversarial perturbations while low-variance channels become suppressed
- The self-fitting phenomenon extends to multi-step adversarial training, explaining why CO can occur even in more robust training methods

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: After catastrophic overfitting, the network learns to recognize self-information embedded in single-step adversarial perturbations, leading to loss of robustness against multi-step attacks.
- Mechanism: The network develops a shortcut by prioritizing recognition of adversarial perturbations (self-information) over the original data features, causing it to only classify inputs that contain sufficient self-information.
- Core assumption: Adversarial perturbations contain both data-information and self-information that the network can learn to distinguish.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "After CO, networks learn to recognize self-information embedded in single-step perturbations, leading to overfitting to specific attacks."
- Break condition: The mechanism breaks if the network cannot effectively decouple data-information from self-information in the adversarial perturbations, or if regularization prevents the network from developing this shortcut.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Channel differentiation occurs after catastrophic overfitting, where certain channels become specialized in recognizing self-information while others are suppressed.
- Mechanism: The network reorganizes its feature extraction hierarchy, with high-variance channels becoming dominant for recognizing adversarial perturbations while low-variance channels become "dead" for data-information processing.
- Core assumption: Different network channels can specialize in different types of information processing.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "We find that this is also true for self-fitting such that there are special channels for fitting self-information."
- Break condition: The mechanism breaks if the network maintains balanced channel activity across all channels, or if pruning experiments don't show the predicted effects on FGSM accuracy.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Random-label FGSM accuracy increases after catastrophic overfitting because the network learns to recognize the self-information rather than the data-information.
- Mechanism: When the network is trained to recognize self-information, it can still correctly classify adversarial examples even when the labels are randomized, because it's recognizing the perturbation pattern rather than the original data features.
- Core assumption: The self-information embedded in adversarial perturbations is sufficient for the network to make correct classifications independent of the original data labels.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "The random FGSM accuracy of a network with CO is more than 10%."
- Break condition: The mechanism breaks if the network cannot achieve high accuracy on random-label FGSM examples after CO, or if the accuracy doesn't increase with larger perturbation budgets.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: CIFAR-10 dataset (padding, random crop, horizontal flip) -> ResNet18/WideResNet28-10 -> FGSM adversarial training loop -> Channel variance analysis -> Random-label accuracy testing -> Pruning experiments

- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess training data
  2. Initialize model and optimizer
  3. For each epoch:
     - Generate FGSM adversarial examples
     - Train model on adversarial examples
     - Check for catastrophic overfitting (PGD accuracy drop)
     - If CO detected, analyze channel behavior and random-label accuracy
  4. Post-training analysis: pruning experiments and feature visualization

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using zero initialization vs. adversarial initialization for perturbation generation
  - Choosing between ResNet18 and WideResNet28-10 for different learning capabilities
  - Deciding perturbation budget (epsilon) that balances between CO occurrence and training stability

- Failure signatures:
  - Sudden drop in PGD accuracy while FGSM accuracy increases
  - High random-label FGSM accuracy (>10%) after CO
  - Channel differentiation with high-variance channels dominating and low-variance channels becoming "dead"
  - Network can still classify random-label FGSM examples correctly

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train ResNet18 on CIFAR-10 with FGSM-AT using epsilon=8/255 and monitor accuracy curves for catastrophic overfitting detection
  2. After CO occurs, test random-label FGSM accuracy to verify self-information recognition
  3. Analyze channel variance in the first convolutional layer to observe channel differentiation phenomenon

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- What is the exact mechanism by which channels with high variance dominate the recognition of FGSM examples after catastrophic overfitting?
- Can the self-fitting phenomenon be prevented or mitigated by architectural changes to neural networks rather than just training modifications?
- How does self-fitting manifest in other types of adversarial training beyond FGSM, such as Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) with multiple steps?

## Limitations
- The "self-fitting" mechanism is primarily supported by empirical observations rather than rigorous mathematical proof
- The channel differentiation phenomenon lacks quantitative metrics to measure the degree of channel specialization
- The random-label FGSM accuracy experiments don't conclusively prove exclusive self-information recognition

## Confidence
- Self-fitting mechanism: Medium - Supported by empirical observations but lacks formal proof
- Channel differentiation phenomenon: Medium - Observed in experiments but not quantified rigorously
- Random-label accuracy as evidence: Medium - Suggests the mechanism but doesn't conclusively prove exclusive self-information recognition

## Next Checks
1. Develop metrics to measure the degree of channel differentiation before and after catastrophic overfitting, including statistical tests to confirm significance
2. Systematically vary the amount of self-information in perturbations to determine the threshold at which catastrophic overfitting occurs
3. Test the self-fitting hypothesis across multiple network architectures to determine if the mechanism is architecture-dependent or universal
