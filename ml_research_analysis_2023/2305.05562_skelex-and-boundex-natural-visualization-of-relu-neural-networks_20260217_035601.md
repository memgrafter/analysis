# SkelEx and BoundEx: Natural Visualization of ReLU Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.05562
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05562
- **Reference count:** 0
- **Primary result:** Novel methods for visualizing ReLU neural networks by extracting membership function skeletons and decision boundaries

## Executive Summary
SkelEx and BoundEx are novel visualization methods that transform ReLU neural network weights and biases into interpretable geometric representations. SkelEx extracts the skeleton of membership functions through iterative ReLU application and activation merging, revealing critical points and linear regions. BoundEx then analytically computes decision boundaries by finding intersections of membership polygons. Experiments on 2D datasets demonstrate that these methods produce interpretable visualizations showing how ReLU networks partition input space for classification.

## Method Summary
The approach involves two main components: SkelEx extracts function skeletons by applying ReLU activation to pre-activations and merging resulting activations layer-by-layer, producing a tessellation of linear regions and critical points. BoundEx uses these skeletons to analytically compute decision boundaries by identifying membership polygons where different classes intersect. The methods transform traditional weight-based representations into geometric skeletons that reveal parallel line segments and activation regions, providing insights into network behavior.

## Key Results
- SkelEx successfully extracts interpretable skeletons of ReLU network membership functions on 2D datasets
- BoundEx analytically computes decision boundaries from skeleton representations with high accuracy
- Visualizations reveal parallel line segments and activation regions that show how networks partition input space
- The skeleton encoding provides natural interpretability of learned functions beyond traditional weight analysis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SkelEx reveals interpretable function structure by converting weight-based representations into critical-point skeletons
- Mechanism: Applies ReLU activation to pre-activations and merges activations layer-by-layer, producing tessellation highlighting linear regions and critical points where gradient changes occur
- Core assumption: ReLU NN membership functions are piecewise linear, and their skeletons can be constructed by analyzing critical points and gradients
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "SkelEx extracts the skeleton of ReLU NN membership functions through a two-step iterative process: applying ReLU to pre-activations and merging resulting activations"
  - [section 3.3] "We know that, just like the membership function, gj_i(x) is a PL function. This means that its domain is divided into linear regions"
- Break condition: If network contains non-linear activations or functions are not piecewise linear, skeleton extraction would fail

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: BoundEx analytically computes decision boundaries using skeleton representation from SkelEx
- Mechanism: Uses membership function skeletons to perform argmax-like operation identifying where different class membership regions intersect, producing membership polygons that define decision boundary
- Core assumption: Decision boundary lies at intersection of membership functions, identifiable through skeleton representation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "BoundEx analytically extracts the decision boundary by finding membership polygons where different classes intersect"
  - [section 4.1] "When applying BoundEx to lr we find two cases, and we are only interested in the second case as it indicates the change of membership"
- Break condition: If membership functions are not PL or decision boundary is highly complex, analytical extraction may become computationally prohibitive

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Visualization provides insights into how ReLU NNs partition input space for classification
- Mechanism: Transforms learned weights and biases into skeletons revealing parallel line segments and activation regions showing network's linear region creation for classification
- Core assumption: Skeleton representation captures essential structural information about network's input space partitioning
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Experiments on 2D datasets show that these methods produce interpretable visualizations of learned functions and decision boundaries, with the skeleton encoding revealing parallel line segments and activation regions"
  - [section 3.5] "We also find that the learned functions tend to produce a significant amount of parallel and almost-parallel line segments visible in Fig. 4, and 7"
- Break condition: If input space dimensionality is too high, visualization becomes impractical despite method still working

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Pre-trained ReLU NN weights/biases -> SkelEx (ApplyReLU + MergeActivations) -> Function skeletons -> BoundEx (Polygon intersection analysis) -> Decision boundaries -> 2D visualization

- Critical path:
  1. Load pre-trained ReLU NN weights and biases
  2. Apply SkelEx to extract membership function skeletons
  3. Apply BoundEx to extract decision boundaries
  4. Visualize results using 2D projections

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Accuracy vs computational complexity: Method is exact but can be expensive for high-dimensional data
  - Dimensionality limitation: Currently limited to 2D due to GEOS library constraints
  - Interpretability vs performance: Skeleton representation is more interpretable but may lose some fine-grained details

- Failure signatures:
  - Incorrect decision boundaries: May indicate errors in skeleton extraction process
  - Excessive computational time: May indicate high-dimensional input space
  - Empty or malformed polygons: May indicate numerical instability or incorrect assumptions about PL structure

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train simple 2-2-1 ReLU NN on XOR dataset and visualize extracted skeleton
  2. Compare decision boundaries extracted by BoundEx vs numerical methods on toy dataset
  3. Test method on 2D projection of Balance Scale dataset as described in paper

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does computational complexity of SkelEx scale with increasing input dimensionality beyond 2D?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper mentions experiments performed only on 2D data due to GEOS library limitations, notes algorithm becomes expensive for high-dimensional data
- Why unresolved: Authors did not provide theoretical analysis or empirical results for higher-dimensional cases, GEOS library prevented practical testing
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical complexity measurements or theoretical bounds for SkelEx on 3D+ datasets, along with alternative implementations supporting higher dimensions

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can unnecessary linear regions identified in skeleton be systematically removed while preserving decision boundary?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors observe skeleton contains faces with no impact on decision boundary production, suggest should be able to separate faces passing through decision boundary vertices
- Why unresolved: Paper only mentions this as possibility but does not provide algorithm or proof for identifying and removing these regions
- What evidence would resolve it: Concrete algorithm for identifying and removing unnecessary linear regions, along with validation that decision boundary remains unchanged

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is relationship between number of parallel line segments in skeleton and network's generalization performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Authors note learned functions produce significant amount of parallel and almost-parallel line segments, hypothesize this occurs because activations of first hidden layer play pivotal role in forming skeleton
- Why unresolved: Paper presents this as observation but does not investigate whether these parallel segments are beneficial, detrimental, or neutral for generalization
- What evidence would resolve it: Correlation studies between parallel segment density and generalization metrics (accuracy, robustness) across multiple trained networks

## Limitations
- Computational complexity scales exponentially with network depth and input dimensionality
- Current implementation limited to 2D datasets due to GEOS library constraints
- Assumption of piecewise linear structure may not hold for networks with non-standard activation functions

## Confidence
- SkelEx skeleton extraction mechanism: High - Well-supported by theoretical analysis and experimental validation on toy datasets
- BoundEx decision boundary extraction: Medium - Analytical approach appears sound but limited validation on diverse datasets
- Interpretability claims: Medium - Visual results are compelling but subjective interpretation remains a challenge
