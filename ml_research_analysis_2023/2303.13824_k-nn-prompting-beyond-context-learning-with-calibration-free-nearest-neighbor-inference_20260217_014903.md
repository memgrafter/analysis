# $k$NN Prompting: Beyond-Context Learning with Calibration-Free Nearest Neighbor Inference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13824
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13824
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: kNN Prompting achieves 5-10% accuracy improvements over standard ICL and state-of-the-art calibration methods across 10 datasets, scaling effectively from 1 to 1024 training examples.

## Executive Summary
kNN Prompting addresses two critical limitations of In-Context Learning (ICL) in large language models: context length restrictions and bias in output distributions. Rather than using training examples directly in prompts, kNN Prompting queries the LLM with training data to obtain distributed representations (LM distributions), which are cached in a datastore. During inference, test instances are matched against this datastore using nearest neighbor retrieval, and predictions are made based on the labels of similar training examples. This calibration-free approach scales effectively beyond context length restrictions and demonstrates consistent performance improvements across 10 orders of magnitude of training data, from 1 to 1024 examples.

## Method Summary
kNN Prompting is a novel approach that caches LLM-generated distributions for training data and uses nearest neighbor retrieval for predictions. The method involves splitting training data into demonstration and anchor sets, querying the LLM with anchor examples to obtain LM distributions, and caching these distributions with their labels. During inference, test instances are processed similarly, and their distributions are matched against the cached datastore using KL divergence to find k nearest neighbors. The labels of these neighbors are then aggregated to make predictions. This approach is calibration-free, scales beyond context length restrictions, and demonstrates improved robustness compared to standard ICL methods.

## Key Results
- kNN Prompting achieves 5-10% accuracy improvements over standard ICL and state-of-the-art calibration-based methods across 10 text classification datasets
- Performance continues to improve with increased training data across 10 orders of magnitude (1 to 1024 examples)
- The method demonstrates robustness to different random seeds and permutations of training examples
- kNN Prompting shows consistent improvements across various LLM scales (0.8B to 30B parameters)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: kNN Prompting enables effective scaling of training data beyond the context length restriction by caching and retrieving LM distribution representations.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of using training examples directly in the prompt, kNN Prompting first queries the LLM with training data to obtain distributed representations (LM distributions), which are cached into a datastore. During inference, the test instance's LM distribution is matched against this datastore to find nearest neighbors, and their associated labels are used for prediction.
- **Core assumption**: The LM distribution captures sufficient information about the input instance to allow effective nearest neighbor matching for prediction.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "kNN Prompting, which first queries LLM with training data for distributed representations, then predicts test instances by simply referring to nearest neighbors."

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: kNN Prompting achieves calibration-free optimization by leveraging the LM distribution for matching rather than directly aligning with task-specific label space.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of mapping the LLM's output distribution directly to labels (which can introduce bias and requires calibration), kNN Prompting uses the LM distribution to find similar training instances and then uses their known labels for prediction. This avoids the need for delicate calibration treatment.
- **Core assumption**: The LM distribution is a better representation for matching similar instances than for direct label prediction.
- **Evidence anchors**: [abstract] "kNN Prompting does not directly align LLM output distribution with task-specific label space, instead leverages such distribution to align test and training instances."

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: kNN Prompting improves robustness by reducing sensitivity to the specific choice and permutation of training examples.
- **Mechanism**: By using a larger set of training examples (anchors) and finding nearest neighbors, kNN Prompting reduces the impact of any single training example on the final prediction, leading to more stable performance across different random seeds.
- **Core assumption**: Averaging predictions from multiple nearest neighbors leads to more robust and stable predictions than relying on a single in-context demonstration.
- **Evidence anchors**: [section 4.3.1] "We show that kNN Prompting significantly improves the robustness. Besides, the performance becomes more robust with increasing anchor set size."

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Demonstration set (D) -> Anchor set (A) -> LLM queries -> Datastore (LM distribution, label) -> Inference pipeline (test instance) -> Nearest neighbor search -> Label aggregation

- **Critical path**:
  1. Split training data into D and A
  2. For each anchor in A, construct prompt with D, query LLM, cache (LM distribution, label)
  3. At inference, for each test instance, construct prompt with D, query LLM, obtain LM distribution
  4. Find k nearest neighbors in datastore using KL divergence
  5. Aggregate neighbor labels to make prediction

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Larger anchor set improves data scaling but increases datastore size and inference time
  - Smaller k reduces computation but may decrease robustness
  - Using whole LM distribution vs. only label words: whole distribution provides more information but increases computation

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor performance: Check if LM distributions are capturing meaningful features; verify nearest neighbor search is working correctly
  - High variance: Increase anchor set size or adjust k
  - Slow inference: Reduce anchor set size or use more efficient distance metrics

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify kNN Prompting works on a simple dataset (e.g., SST2) with a small LLM (e.g., GPT2 small) and compare to standard ICL
  2. Test the impact of anchor set size on performance by varying |A| while keeping |D| fixed
  3. Compare using whole LM distribution vs. only label words for prediction on a held-out test set

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of kNN Prompting compare to traditional fine-tuning methods when scaling up to extremely large datasets beyond the tested range?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that kNN Prompting bridges data scaling into model scaling and that its data utility should still be inferior to specialized fine-tuning of LLMs in an ideal setting.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests up to 1024 shots and compares to fine-tuning within that range. It acknowledges that kNN Prompting's data utility factor is smaller than fine-tuning but does not explore performance at scales far beyond the tested range.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with kNN Prompting on datasets with thousands or millions of examples, comparing performance to fine-tuned LLMs of similar scale, and analyzing the scaling laws in this extended range.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different distance metrics (e.g., Euclidean, cosine similarity) on the performance of kNN Prompting, and is there an optimal choice for different types of tasks or datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper briefly mentions investigating Euclidean distance as an alternative to KL divergence and finding KL divergence to be superior. It also notes that the LM distribution is a projection of contextual representations through word embeddings, which may provide more disentangled representations.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides a brief comparison between KL divergence and Euclidean distance. It does not explore other distance metrics or analyze the impact of different choices on various tasks or datasets.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting extensive experiments with different distance metrics (e.g., cosine similarity, Manhattan distance) on various tasks and datasets, analyzing the performance differences, and identifying optimal choices for specific scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does kNN Prompting perform in multilingual settings, and what are the challenges and potential solutions for adapting it to languages other than English?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on English text classification tasks and does not discuss multilingual capabilities or challenges.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not address multilingual settings or the potential challenges of adapting kNN Prompting to other languages. It also does not explore potential solutions for handling language-specific issues.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with kNN Prompting on multilingual datasets, analyzing performance across different languages, identifying language-specific challenges (e.g., tokenization, vocabulary size), and proposing solutions to address these issues.

## Limitations

- The optimal size of demonstration and anchor sets remains empirical and dataset-dependent
- Computational overhead increases with larger anchor sets and LLM scales, potentially limiting real-world deployment
- Performance comparisons to traditional fine-tuning methods are limited to few-shot scenarios, with uncertain behavior at extreme scales

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core mechanism of using LM distributions for nearest neighbor matching is well-supported by experimental results. The paper demonstrates clear improvements over standard ICL across multiple datasets and model scales, with robust performance gains (typically 5-10% accuracy improvement) and consistent scaling behavior with increased training data.

**Medium Confidence**: The calibration-free optimization claim is plausible given the experimental evidence, but the theoretical justification could be stronger. While the paper shows that LM distributions are superior for matching versus direct prediction, the underlying reasons why this holds across diverse datasets and model architectures warrant further investigation.

**Low Confidence**: The robustness claims regarding reduced sensitivity to training example permutations are supported by experiments but rely heavily on controlled conditions. Real-world deployment scenarios with noisy or adversarial data may reveal limitations not captured in the current evaluation.
