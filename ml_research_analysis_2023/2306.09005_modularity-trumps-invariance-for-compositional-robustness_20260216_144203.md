# Modularity Trumps Invariance for Compositional Robustness

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.09005
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09005
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Modular architectures outperform invariance-promoting methods for compositional robustness to image corruptions

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the prevailing assumption that invariant representations are key to compositional robustness by introducing a new compositional robustness task. The framework tests whether models can generalize from a small set of elemental image corruptions to their compositions, requiring true compositional understanding rather than just memorization of corruption types. Through systematic comparisons, the authors demonstrate that modular architectures consistently outperform standard empirical risk minimization and contrastive training approaches, even when the latter explicitly encourages invariance between elemental corruptions. Surprisingly, the degree of invariance between elemental corruptions fails to correlate with robustness to their compositions, suggesting that modularity provides a more effective inductive bias for handling compositional distribution shifts.

## Method Summary
The authors introduce a compositional robustness task where models must generalize from a limited set of elemental image corruptions to all possible compositions of those corruptions. They evaluate three approaches: standard empirical risk minimization, contrastive training to encourage invariant representations between elemental corruptions, and a modular architecture that processes elemental corruptions separately before combining them. The modular architecture consists of separate modules for each corruption type that are then composed to handle multi-corruption scenarios. Performance is measured by accuracy on held-out compositions, testing the model's ability to extrapolate compositional knowledge beyond its training distribution.

## Key Results
- The modular architecture consistently outperforms both empirical risk minimization and contrastive training across all tested composition spaces
- Contrastive training, despite explicitly encouraging invariance between elemental corruptions, fails to match modular performance
- No correlation exists between the degree of invariance between elemental corruptions and robustness to their compositions
- The modular approach shows particular advantage as composition complexity increases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper doesn't provide a definitive mechanistic explanation for why modularity outperforms invariance. The empirical results suggest that modular architectures may better capture the compositional structure of corruption interactions, but the underlying reasons remain unclear. The failure of invariance metrics to predict compositional robustness indicates that simply making representations similar across corruptions doesn't provide the right inductive bias for handling their compositions. The modular approach may succeed by explicitly encoding compositional structure into the architecture rather than trying to learn it implicitly through representation similarity.

## Foundational Learning
- **Compositional robustness**: Ability to handle combinations of distribution shifts, not just individual ones - needed to evaluate real-world robustness where multiple corruptions co-occur
- **Invariance learning**: Training models to produce similar representations across different input variations - traditionally thought to improve generalization to unseen corruptions
- **Modular architectures**: Networks with separate processing pathways that can be composed - may better capture compositional structure in data
- **Contrastive learning**: Method for encouraging similar representations for related inputs - used here to promote invariance between corruptions
- **Distribution shift generalization**: Ability to maintain performance when test data differs from training data - the fundamental challenge being addressed
- **Corruption composability**: How different image corruptions interact when combined - critical for understanding real-world robustness

- Trade-off between architectural constraints (modularity) and flexibility (end-to-end learning)
- Balance between separate processing of corruptions versus joint processing

**Failure signatures**: 
- Poor performance on complex compositions despite good performance on individual corruptions
- Inability to generalize compositional knowledge beyond training compositions
- Invariance between corruptions doesn't translate to compositional robustness

## Limitations
- The analysis of why modularity succeeds remains correlational rather than mechanistic
- Results may be specific to the chosen corruption types and composition spaces
- Limited evaluation of whether findings generalize to more diverse or complex corruption sets
- The paper doesn't explore alternative modularity designs that might further improve performance

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The experimental comparison between modular and non-modular architectures, showing consistent performance differences
- **Medium confidence**: The conclusion that invariance metrics don't correlate with compositional robustness (methodologically sound but potentially limited by measure choice)
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that modular architectures have inherent advantages for compositional robustness (supported empirically but lacking mechanistic explanation)

## Next Checks
1. Test whether the modular architecture's advantage persists when trained with stronger invariance-promoting objectives or different regularization techniques
2. Evaluate performance on larger composition spaces with more diverse corruption types to assess generalizability beyond the current experimental setup
3. Conduct ablation studies isolating which aspects of the modular design (separation of corruption processing vs other architectural choices) drive the performance gains
