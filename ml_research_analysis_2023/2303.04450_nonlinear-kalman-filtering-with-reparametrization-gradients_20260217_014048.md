# Nonlinear Kalman Filtering with Reparametrization Gradients

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04450
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04450
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Energy Function Kalman Filter (EFKF) outperforms traditional filters like EKF, UKF, and particle filters, particularly in scenarios with parameter uncertainty, achieving lower RMSE in tracking problems.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel nonlinear Kalman filter based on energy function optimization, addressing convergence issues of previous alpha divergence minimization approaches. The proposed method leverages reparametrization gradients from deep learning to optimize an energy function formulation of the alpha divergence, allowing direct optimization without the convergence problems associated with moment matching methods. The authors demonstrate that EFKF achieves superior performance compared to traditional filters like EKF, UKF, and particle filters, particularly in scenarios with parameter uncertainty. Numerical results on a tracking problem show that EFKF with an optimal alpha value of 0.7 consistently outperforms competing methods, achieving lower root mean square errors (RMSE) in both matching and mismatching parameter scenarios.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes a nonlinear Kalman filter that minimizes alpha divergence between the true posterior and a Gaussian approximating distribution by optimizing an energy function using reparametrization gradients. The method reformulates the alpha divergence stationary points as an energy function that can be optimized directly, avoiding the convergence issues of moment matching approaches. By expressing Gaussian samples as deterministic transformations of standard normal variables, the method enables stable gradient estimation through automatic differentiation. The algorithm employs natural gradients for covariance updates, providing curvature-aware optimization suitable for full covariance matrices. The approach is demonstrated on a constant-velocity tracking problem with both matching and mismatching parameter scenarios.

## Key Results
- EFKF with alpha = 0.7 achieves lower RMSE than EKF, UKF, and particle filters in tracking experiments
- EFKF demonstrates superior robustness in mismatching parameter scenarios compared to competing methods
- The method provides more stable and accurate estimates of the target state compared to other filters

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Reparametrization gradients avoid numerical instability in alpha divergence optimization. By expressing the Gaussian sample as $x_s = C\epsilon_s + \mu_q$ with $\epsilon_s \sim N(0,I)$, gradients are computed through the reparameterization path rather than direct differentiation of expectations that depend on parameters. Core assumption: The gradient estimator remains stable under this transformation and converges to true gradients as sample size increases. Break condition: If the reparameterization path becomes ill-conditioned (e.g., very small eigenvalues in $\Sigma_q$), numerical instability may reappear.

### Mechanism 2
Energy function reformulation converts alpha divergence stationary points into a tractable optimization problem. The energy function $E(\lambda_q)$ is designed so that $\nabla_{\lambda_q}E(\lambda_q) = -(1 - 1/\alpha)[E_q[s(x)] - E_{\tilde{p}}[s(x)]]$, making its stationary points coincide with those of the alpha divergence. Core assumption: The exponential family form of the prior and approximating distributions is preserved, and the natural parameters are correctly mapped. Break condition: If the natural parameter mapping is incorrect or the prior is not in exponential family form, the stationary point equivalence fails.

### Mechanism 3
Natural gradients provide curvature-aware updates for covariance matrices, improving convergence. The update rule $\Sigma_{i+1}^q \leftarrow \Sigma_i^q - \rho_i C_i^\Sigma \nabla_{\Sigma_q} \hat{E}(\mu_i^q, \Sigma_i^q) C_i^\Sigma$ incorporates the Fisher information matrix via $C_i^\Sigma = \Sigma_i^q$, adjusting step size according to local geometry. Core assumption: The approximation of the Fisher metric by the covariance matrix is valid in the neighborhood of the current estimate. Break condition: If the parameter space curvature changes rapidly or the step size is too large, the natural gradient update may overshoot or diverge.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Prior $p_0(x_t) \sim N(\mu_0, \Sigma_0)$ -> Likelihood $p(y_t|x_t) \sim N(h(x_t), \Sigma)$ -> Approximate posterior $q(x_t) \sim N(\mu_q, \Sigma_q)$ -> Energy function $E(\mu_q, \Sigma_q)$ -> Gradient estimator (reparametrized Monte Carlo with $S$ samples) -> Natural gradient descent updates

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize $\mu_q \leftarrow \mu_0$, $\Sigma_q \leftarrow \Sigma_0$
  2. For each iteration:
     - Sample $\epsilon_s \sim N(0,I)$, transform to $x_s = C\epsilon_s + \mu_q$
     - Compute energy function estimate using $h(x_s)$
     - Backpropagate through reparameterization to get gradients
     - Apply natural gradient updates to $\mu_q$ and $\Sigma_q$
  3. Return final $\mu_q$, $\Sigma_q$ as posterior estimate

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sample size $S$ vs. gradient variance: Larger $S$ reduces variance but increases computation
  - Alpha value: Controls divergence type; $\alpha=0.7$ empirically best but may vary by problem
  - Full vs. diagonal covariance: Full covariance more expressive but computationally heavier

- Failure signatures:
  - Diverging covariance: Step size too large or ill-conditioned $\Sigma_q$
  - Poor tracking: Alpha value poorly chosen or insufficient samples
  - Numerical instability: Cholesky decomposition fails due to non-positive definite $\Sigma_q$

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run EFKF with $\alpha=0.7$ on synthetic constant-velocity tracking with known parameters; compare RMSE to EKF and UKF
  2. Vary $\alpha$ from 0.01 to 0.99; plot RMSE vs. $\alpha$ to identify optimal value
  3. Introduce parameter mismatch (e.g., incorrect process noise covariance); compare robustness of EFKF to particle filter

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the proposed Energy Function Kalman Filter (EFKF) perform on different types of nonlinear functions in the state-space model, beyond the constant-velocity model used in the experiments? The authors state that the EFKF is applicable to the more general case of exponential families, including but not limited to the case of Gaussian, and mention that in the full version of the paper they will show how the more general problem can also be addressed. This remains unresolved as the paper only demonstrates the EFKF on a specific constant-velocity model.

### Open Question 2
How sensitive is the EFKF to the choice of the alpha parameter, and is there a systematic way to select the optimal alpha value for a given problem? The authors experiment with five different values of alpha (0.01, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7) and find that alpha = 0.7 yields the best performance in most cases. However, they do not provide a systematic method for choosing the optimal alpha value, leaving this question unresolved.

### Open Question 3
How does the EFKF scale with the dimensionality of the state-space model, and what are the computational challenges associated with high-dimensional problems? The paper does not explicitly discuss the scalability of the EFKF with respect to the dimensionality of the state-space model. The use of reparametrization gradients and the need to compute gradients with respect to the mean and covariance of the Gaussian approximation suggest that computational challenges may arise in high-dimensional settings.

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation: The method is only tested on a single tracking problem, which may not capture the full range of scenarios where EFKF excels or fails
- Problem-specific optimal parameters: The optimal alpha value of 0.7 is problem-specific and may not generalize across different dynamical systems
- Incomplete numerical stability details: The numerical stability claims depend on implementation details of the scaling trick that are not fully specified

## Confidence

- High confidence in the theoretical equivalence between alpha divergence stationary points and the energy function formulation
- Medium confidence in the practical advantages of EFKF over competing methods, given limited empirical validation
- Medium confidence in the numerical stability of the reparametrization gradient approach, pending implementation details
