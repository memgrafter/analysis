# Political corpus creation through automatic speech recognition on EU debates

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.08137
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08137
- Reference count: 36
- 3.6 million word transcribed corpus of EU LIBE committee meetings

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive approach to creating a transcribed corpus of EU LIBE committee meetings using automatic speech recognition. The authors address the challenge of making parliamentary committee recordings accessible for research by developing an ASR pipeline that can handle non-native English speakers and interpreter speech. Through domain adaptation of Wav2vec2.0-based models, they achieved significant improvements in transcription accuracy, reducing word error rate from 28.22 to 17.95. The resulting corpus and analysis pipeline are made available for future political science research.

## Method Summary
The authors investigated unsupervised domain adaptation of ASR pipelines for EU parliamentary committee meetings. Building on Wav2vec2.0, they experimented with multiple acoustic models, language models, and domain-specific terms. The pipeline involved adapting existing ASR models to the specific characteristics of LIBE committee speech, including non-native English accents and interpreter output. Domain-specific acoustic and language models were developed and evaluated against baseline models to measure improvements in transcription accuracy.

## Key Results
- Created 3.6 million word transcribed corpus of LIBE committee meetings
- Word error rate reduced from 28.22 to 17.95 through domain adaptation
- Domain-specific acoustic and language models provided substantial improvements
- Domain-specific terms in decoding stage showed no positive effect on WER
- Initial topic modeling results indicated corpus utility for downstream analysis

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The domain adaptation approach works because parliamentary committee speech has distinct acoustic and linguistic patterns compared to general speech. Non-native speakers and interpreters create pronunciation patterns and vocabulary usage that differ from native speech. By training acoustic models on committee-specific data and language models on domain-specific text, the system learns these patterns. The transformer-based Wav2vec2.0 architecture provides a strong foundation for fine-tuning on specialized domains.

## Foundational Learning
**Automatic Speech Recognition**: Converts audio to text; needed for making spoken content searchable and analyzable; quick check: WER measurement
**Domain Adaptation**: Tailoring models to specific contexts; needed because general ASR models underperform on specialized domains; quick check: compare WER on domain vs general data
**Transformer Models**: Attention-based neural networks; needed for capturing long-range dependencies in speech; quick check: fine-tuning stability
**Word Error Rate**: Standard ASR evaluation metric; needed for quantifying transcription accuracy; quick check: human transcription comparison
**Topic Modeling**: Unsupervised document clustering; needed for initial corpus analysis; quick check: coherence scores
**Unsupervised Learning**: Training without labeled data; needed when manual transcriptions are expensive; quick check: reconstruction loss

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Audio -> Wav2vec2.0 Encoder -> Acoustic Model -> Language Model -> Decoder -> Text Output

Critical Path: Audio input flows through feature extraction, domain-adapted acoustic modeling, language modeling, and decoding to produce final transcriptions. The acoustic and language models are the primary adaptation points.

Design Tradeoffs: The choice between general vs domain-specific models involves balancing coverage breadth against accuracy depth. Using domain-specific terms in decoding didn't improve WER, suggesting vocabulary augmentation may need different integration strategies.

Failure Signatures: High WER indicates poor model adaptation to speaker characteristics. If domain-specific terms don't improve results, the term selection or integration method may be flawed. Topic modeling difficulties could indicate transcription quality issues.

## Limitations
- Results are specific to LIBE committee English-language content, limiting generalizability
- No statistical significance testing or confidence intervals provided for WER improvements
- Domain-specific terms experiment showed no improvement, but reasons for failure were not explored
- Initial topic modeling results lack comprehensive validation for downstream task performance

## Confidence
- High confidence: Domain-specific acoustic and language models improve ASR accuracy for parliamentary committee meetings
- Medium confidence: Specific WER improvements (28.22 to 17.95) are reproducible with LIBE committee data
- Medium confidence: Corpus creation methodology is sound for similar political speech contexts
