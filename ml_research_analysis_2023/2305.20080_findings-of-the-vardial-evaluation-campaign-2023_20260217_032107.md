# Findings of the VarDial Evaluation Campaign 2023

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.20080
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20080
- Reference count: 20
- This report presents results from three shared tasks in the VarDial 2023 evaluation campaign

## Executive Summary
This report presents results from three shared tasks in the VarDial 2023 evaluation campaign. The tasks include slot and intent detection for low-resource language varieties (SID4LR), discriminating between similar languages with true labels (DSL-TL), and discriminating between similar languages from speech (DSL-S). For SID4LR, UBC achieved best intent detection scores (0.89-0.94 accuracy), while Notre Dame achieved best results using monolingual models with noise-induced data. DSL-TL showed the challenge of distinguishing similar language varieties, with UnibucNLP achieving the best closed-track F1 score of 0.53, and VaidyaKane achieving 0.86 in the open track. DSL-S had no submissions, but baseline results showed transcription-based approaches outperformed direct speech classification. The tasks demonstrated the difficulty of low-resource and closely-related language processing, with effective approaches including multilingual fine-tuning, monolingual noise induction, and two-stage classification pipelines.

## Method Summary
The VarDial 2023 evaluation campaign featured three shared tasks addressing different aspects of similar language and dialect identification. SID4LR focused on slot and intent detection for low-resource language varieties, requiring participants to process short utterances across four languages. DSL-TL involved discriminating between similar languages using true labels rather than automatic predictions, creating a more controlled experimental setting. DSL-S presented the challenge of language discrimination directly from speech data without intermediate transcription. Participants employed various approaches including multilingual fine-tuning, monolingual noise-induced data augmentation, cross-task and multitask learning, and two-stage classification pipelines combining acoustic and linguistic features.

## Key Results
- UBC achieved best intent detection scores (0.89-0.94 accuracy) in SID4LR using multilingual fine-tuning
- UnibucNLP achieved best closed-track F1 score of 0.53 in DSL-TL, while VaidyaKane achieved 0.86 in open track
- DSL-S had no submissions, but baseline results showed transcription-based approaches outperformed direct speech classification

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: The effectiveness of multilingual fine-tuning in SID4LR likely stems from leveraging shared linguistic patterns across language varieties, enabling better generalization when training data is limited. The success of noise-induced data augmentation suggests that controlled perturbations help models become more robust to real-world variations. For DSL tasks, the superior performance of transcription-based approaches in DSL-S indicates that text representations may capture language-specific patterns more reliably than acoustic features alone, though this requires further investigation.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map:
Speech/audio processing -> Feature extraction -> Language classification
Text processing -> Intent detection -> Slot filling
Multilingual model -> Fine-tuning -> Low-resource adaptation

Critical path:
For SID4LR: Data preprocessing → Model fine-tuning → Intent detection → Slot filling
For DSL-TL: Feature extraction → Language discrimination → Confidence calibration
For DSL-S: Speech processing → Transcription (optional) → Language classification

Design tradeoffs:
Multilingual fine-tuning vs. monolingual noise induction for low-resource adaptation
Direct speech classification vs. transcription-based approaches for language discrimination
Closed-track controlled conditions vs. open-track real-world complexity

Failure signatures:
Overfitting to training language varieties in DSL tasks
Insufficient generalization across similar language varieties
Performance degradation when moving from true labels to automatic predictions

## Limitations
- SID4LR task limited to four languages with only two varieties each, constraining generalizability
- Lack of baseline results for open track in DSL-TL prevents assessment of relative effectiveness
- No participant submissions for DSL-S means findings based solely on organizer-provided baselines
- Limited ablation studies make it difficult to isolate the impact of specific methodological choices
- Short duration of shared tasks may not capture long-term model stability and performance

## Confidence
High: Standardized evaluation protocols for DSL-TL and DSL-S
Medium: Direct comparison challenging in SID4LR due to different participant approaches
Low: Limited ablation studies and detailed methodology descriptions
