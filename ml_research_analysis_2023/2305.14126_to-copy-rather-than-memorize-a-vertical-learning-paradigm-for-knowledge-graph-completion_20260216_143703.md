# To Copy Rather Than Memorize: A Vertical Learning Paradigm for Knowledge Graph Completion

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.14126
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14126
- **Reference count:** 16
- **Primary result:** VLP-based models achieve consistent and significant improvements over baseline embedding models, especially for distant link prediction on WN18RR and FB15k-237.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of multi-hop relation rule memorization in knowledge graph completion (KGC). Traditional embedding models struggle with distant link prediction because implicit memorization through single-edge constraints accumulates bias and exceeds model capacity. The authors propose a Vertical Learning Paradigm (VLP) that explicitly copies target information from related factual triples, improving generalization. Additionally, they introduce a Relative Distance-based negative sampling technique (ReD) that generates more informative negatives and reduces false negatives. Experiments demonstrate VLP's effectiveness across multiple embedding models and datasets.

## Method Summary
VLP extends existing embedding models by introducing explicit reference copying through reference query selection based on graph distance, reference graph construction, and similarity-weighted aggregation of ground-truth answers. The method integrates with any embedding model's score function by adding a vertical component alongside the horizontal score. ReD enhances negative sampling by using graph distance for pre-sampling to favor informative negatives and relative latent distance for post-sampling to downweight false negatives. The combined approach is trained using a two-component loss function and evaluated on standard KGC benchmarks.

## Key Results
- VLP-based models consistently outperform baseline embedding models on WN18RR and FB15k-237 datasets
- The method shows particular strength in distant link prediction, where multi-hop relation rules are most challenging
- VLP demonstrates generality across multiple embedding architectures including DistMult, ComplEx, and RotatE
- ReD negative sampling provides effective optimization by reducing false negatives and improving training efficiency

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Embedding models struggle with multi-hop relation rules because implicit memorization accumulates bias and requires high memory capacity
- Mechanism: Implicit memorization via single-triple constraints leads to bias accumulation across hops and exponential growth in path diversity that exceeds model capacity
- Core assumption: Bias accumulates multiplicatively across relation hops and model capacity is insufficient for storing all possible multi-hop rules
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "multi-hop relation rules are hard to be reliably memorized due to the inherent deficiencies of such implicit memorization strategy"
  - [section]: "The HLP-based embedding models implicitly perceive the multi-hop relation rules by constraining each training edge... the single-edge constraints inevitably have biases during the optimization, which will accumulate with the increase of relation hops"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for bias accumulation mechanism
- Break condition: If relation paths have low variability or model capacity scales exponentially with graph size

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Explicit reference copying from related factual triples improves distant link prediction by providing additional cues
- Mechanism: VLP selects reference queries based on graph distance and aggregates their answers using similarity-weighted combination to provide explicit contextual information
- Core assumption: Related factual triples contain relevant information that can guide prediction for distant entity pairs
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "VLP directly provides additional cues to improve the generalization ability of embedding models"
  - [section]: "VLP-based models learn to explicitly gather target information from neighbor answers for prediction" and "VLP can be widely applied to various embedding models"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for reference copying mechanism
- Break condition: If selected references are irrelevant or similarity weighting fails to properly adjust reference answers

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Relative distance-based negative sampling generates more informative negatives and reduces false negative toxicity
- Mechanism: ReD uses graph distance for pre-sampling to favor informative negatives and relative latent distance for post-sampling to downweight false negatives
- Core assumption: Distant entities are more likely to be easy negatives, while high-scoring negatives are more likely to be false
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we further propose a novel relative distance based negative sampling technique (ReD) for more effective optimization"
  - [section]: "ReD defines the post-sampling weights as a distribution that first rises and then falls as the negative score increases"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct corpus support for ReD sampling strategy
- Break condition: If graph distance doesn't correlate with sample difficulty or relative latent distance fails to identify false negatives

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the specific challenges in extending the Vertical Learning Paradigm (VLP) to inductive settings where new entities are introduced after the model is trained?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that VLP cannot directly generalize to the inductive setting since it is defined based on the score functions of transductive embedding models. They suggest that designing an inductive reference selector for emerging entities is a potential direction.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore or propose concrete methods for adapting VLP to handle unseen entities during inference.
- What evidence would resolve it: A proposed and experimentally validated method for adapting VLP to the inductive setting, demonstrating improved performance on link prediction tasks with unseen entities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can the efficiency of reference query selection in VLP be improved to reduce computational costs, especially for large knowledge graphs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the current graph distance-based approach for reference selection incurs no additional computational cost during training and inference. However, the scalability of this approach for very large graphs is not explored.
- Why unresolved: The authors do not provide an analysis of the scalability of their reference selection method or propose alternative approaches for more efficient reference selection.
- What evidence would resolve it: An analysis of the computational complexity of the current reference selection method and a comparison with alternative approaches, demonstrating improved efficiency without compromising performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the potential benefits and challenges of integrating the Relative Distance-based negative sampling (ReD) technique with other graph neural network (GNN) based knowledge graph embedding models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state that their proposals are orthogonal to other techniques like graph encoders and regularizers, and one can integrate them for better performance. They also mention that ReD generates and processes higher quality negatives for optimization.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the integration of ReD with GNN-based models or provide experimental results on the performance of such integrations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the performance of GNN-based models with and without ReD, demonstrating the benefits and challenges of the integration.

## Limitations
- The bias accumulation mechanism in implicit memorization remains largely theoretical with weak corpus support
- Reference copying effectiveness is asserted but not rigorously validated against alternative information aggregation methods
- ReD sampling superiority over existing methods is claimed but not systematically compared against the full range of negative sampling strategies

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The empirical results showing VLP improves KGC performance on benchmark datasets
- **Medium confidence**: The theoretical motivation for why implicit memorization fails on multi-hop relations
- **Low confidence**: The specific mechanisms of bias accumulation and the claim that VLP "copies" rather than memorizes
