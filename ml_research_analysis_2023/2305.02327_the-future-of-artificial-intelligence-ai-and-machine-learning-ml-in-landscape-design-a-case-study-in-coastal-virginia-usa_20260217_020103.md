# The Future of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) in Landscape Design: A Case Study in Coastal Virginia, USA

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.02327
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02327
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Storm-event-specific LSTM models outperformed full-dataset models for groundwater prediction in coastal Norfolk, Virginia

## Executive Summary
This interdisciplinary case study applies machine learning techniques to predict groundwater table levels during storm events in coastal Norfolk, Virginia. Using hourly tide, rainfall, and groundwater data from 2010-2018 across seven monitoring wells, the research compares LSTM model performance when trained on full datasets versus storm-only data. The study demonstrates that models trained specifically on storm events show higher sensitivity to storm-related patterns and better predictive performance. These forecasts can be interpolated into city-wide maps, providing decision-makers with critical flood management insights and highlighting the growing role of machine intelligence in ecological design.

## Method Summary
The study collected hourly tide, rainfall, and groundwater table data from seven monitoring wells in Norfolk, Virginia spanning 2010-2018. Two LSTM models were developed per well: one trained on the complete dataset and another trained exclusively on storm event data. Storm events were identified through specific criteria (not fully detailed in the paper). Models were evaluated on their ability to forecast groundwater responses based on weather forecasts, with performance metrics comparing predicted versus observed groundwater levels. The trained models' predictions were then interpolated to create city-wide flood risk maps.

## Key Results
- LSTM models trained on storm-only data outperformed those trained on full datasets
- Storm-specific training improved model sensitivity to storm-related groundwater patterns
- Model predictions can be interpolated into city-wide flood risk maps for decision support

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Storm-event-specific training improves model sensitivity by focusing learning on distinct groundwater dynamics during storms.
- **Mechanism:** Filtering training data to storm events reduces noise from baseline periods, allowing models to learn specific nonlinear patterns between rainfall, tides, and groundwater changes during storms.
- **Core assumption:** Storm events are statistically distinct from baseline groundwater behavior.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - "Results showed that models trained specifically on storm event data outperformed those trained on full datasets, demonstrating higher sensitivity to storm-related patterns."
  - "This study found that training models with storm event data resulted in better model performance in a simulated real-time forecasting scenario."
- **Break condition:** If storm events are not statistically separable from baseline periods.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** LSTM networks capture complex, nonlinear hydrological responses to weather inputs that simpler models miss.
- **Mechanism:** LSTM's hidden states across time steps enable learning temporal dependencies between weather inputs and delayed groundwater responses, including lagged effects.
- **Core assumption:** Groundwater dynamics are inherently nonlinear with memory effects requiring recurrent architectures.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - "Using hourly tide, rainfall, and groundwater data from 2010-2018, the study trained models on seven monitoring wells to forecast groundwater responses based on weather forecasts."
- **Break condition:** If the relationship between inputs and groundwater is linear or temporal dependencies are too short.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Interpolation of well-specific predictions enables city-wide flood risk mapping.
- **Mechanism:** Point predictions from trained models can be spatially interpolated using geostatistical methods to create continuous groundwater surface maps.
- **Core assumption:** Spatial patterns in groundwater response are smooth enough to interpolate accurately between wells.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - "This approach enables real-time flood prediction by interpolating groundwater forecasts into city-wide maps, providing decision-makers with critical insights for flood management and urban planning in coastal environments."
- **Break condition:** If groundwater behavior is too heterogeneous spatially.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data ingestion -> Preprocessing (event detection) -> Model training (LSTM per well) -> Prediction engine -> Interpolation module -> Visualization/dashboard
- **Critical path:** Data → Preprocessing → Model Training → Prediction → Interpolation → Visualization
- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Model complexity vs interpretability: LSTM provides accuracy but reduces transparency
  - Event-specific vs general models: Storm-only improves sensitivity but limits baseline prediction capability
  - Computational cost vs real-time requirements: GPU-accelerated training vs on-device inference
- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor generalization when applying storm-trained models to non-storm conditions
  - Interpolation artifacts in areas between monitoring wells with complex groundwater dynamics
  - Model drift when environmental conditions change significantly from training period
- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Compare model performance on held-out storm events using storm-only vs full training data
  2. Test interpolation accuracy by withholding one well and predicting its values from surrounding wells
  3. Validate model predictions against observed groundwater levels during a real storm event

## Limitations
- Lack of detailed methodological specifications including model hyperparameters and storm event detection criteria
- Single geographic location limits generalizability to other coastal environments
- Internal validation approach without external storm event testing or different time period validation

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The core finding that event-specific training data improves model sensitivity is well-supported by reported results and aligns with established ML principles
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of LSTM networks for capturing groundwater dynamics is plausible given their established capability for time series forecasting
- **Low confidence**: The extrapolation from point predictions to city-wide flood risk maps via interpolation is presented without rigorous validation

## Next Checks
1. **Model reproducibility check**: Replicate the study using publicly available groundwater and weather data from a different coastal city to test generalizability of the storm-event training approach.
2. **Interpolation validation**: Hold out one monitoring well entirely, train models on the remaining six, interpolate predictions to the held-out location, and compare against actual observations to quantify interpolation error.
3. **Storm event detection verification**: Implement multiple storm detection criteria (different rainfall thresholds, duration requirements) and test whether performance improvements persist across different event definitions.
