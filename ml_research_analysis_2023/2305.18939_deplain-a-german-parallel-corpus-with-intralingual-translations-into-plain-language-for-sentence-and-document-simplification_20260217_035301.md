# DEPLAIN: A German Parallel Corpus with Intralingual Translations into Plain Language for Sentence and Document Simplification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18939
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18939
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: DEPLAIN provides 1,239 document pairs and 16,562 sentence pairs for German text simplification, with both manual and automatic alignments.

## Executive Summary
DEPLAIN is a new German corpus for text simplification containing news and web documents with manual and automatic alignments on both document and sentence levels. The corpus includes approximately 1,239 document pairs and 16,562 sentence pairs, with 14,968 manual and 1,594 automatic alignments. DEPLAIN features both mild and strong simplifications and was used to train and evaluate transformer-based text simplification models. Experiments showed promising results, particularly when combining APA and web domain data. The corpus and associated tools are publicly available, with some licensing restrictions.

## Method Summary
DEPLAIN was constructed by crawling plain language news articles from APA and web content from leichte-sprache.org, then aligning documents and sentences at both levels. Manual alignments were performed by two annotators for 630 document pairs, achieving inter-annotator agreement of approximately 0.85. Automatic alignment was performed using various methods including MASSAlign and CATS, with evaluation against the manually aligned subset. The corpus was used to train transformer-based text simplification models (mBART and long-mBART) at both sentence and document levels.

## Key Results
- DEPLAIN contains 1,239 document pairs and 16,562 sentence pairs
- 14,968 sentence pairs were manually aligned with 0.85 inter-annotator agreement
- Combining APA and WEB domain data in training yielded the best model performance
- Document-level models showed improved results over sentence-level models when using DEPLAIN-WEB

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: DEPLAIN provides both document-level and sentence-level alignments, enabling training and evaluation of simplification models at multiple granularities.
- Mechanism: Document-level pairs give global context for simplification; sentence-level pairs enable fine-grained modeling of rewriting operations.
- Core assumption: Models can generalize from sentence-level pairs to handle document-level simplification if both are provided.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The corpus includes approximately 1,239 document pairs and 16,562 sentence pairs"
  - [section] "We present two new TS corpora on the document level, DEPLAIN -APA and DEPLAIN -WEB"
- Break condition: If the document-level alignments do not preserve discourse coherence, the benefit of document-level training is lost.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Manual alignments improve quality over automatic alignments, especially for n:m transformations like splitting and merging sentences.
- Mechanism: Human annotators can identify and annotate complex simplification operations (e.g., sentence splitting, merging, fusion) that automatic aligners miss.
- Core assumption: Manual alignment accuracy is sufficient to train models on rare but important operations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "14,968 sentence pairs of 630 document pairs are manually aligned"
  - [section] "The inter-annotator-agreement (measured with Cohen's κ) for these documents is equal to approx. 0.85"
- Break condition: If manual alignment introduces bias or inconsistency, model performance degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Mixing mild and strong simplifications (APA vs WEB) allows models to learn a spectrum of simplification strategies.
- Mechanism: Mild simplifications (APA) teach subtle rephrasing; strong simplifications (WEB) teach more aggressive rewriting (splitting, lexical substitution).
- Core assumption: Exposure to both styles during training improves generalization across target audiences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "DEPLAIN -APA focuses more on mild simplifications" and "DEPLAIN -WEB contains rather strong simplifications"
  - [section] "This supports that adding training data from different contexts leads to a better generalization of the model"
- Break condition: If the two styles conflict in training signals, model performance may degrade.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can n:m sentence alignments be improved for German text simplification corpora?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that current automatic alignment methods, including MASSAlign, primarily identify 1:1 alignments and struggle with n:m alignments (where n or m > 1). The authors note this is a significant limitation for text simplification.
- Why unresolved: Despite evaluating multiple alignment methods (LHA, SentenceTransformer, VecAlign, BertAlign, MASSAlign, CATS), the paper shows that n:m alignments remain challenging. The authors explicitly state this as a direction for future work.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments showing improved n:m alignment accuracy using enhanced algorithms, particularly those incorporating paragraph-level information or new similarity metrics tailored to simplification tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How well do automatic evaluation metrics for text simplification translate to German, and what are their limitations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that standard metrics like SARI, BLEU, and BERTScore were primarily developed for English and may not work optimally for German. The authors note that "automatic metrics do not perfectly correlate with human judgments" and that German FRE scores sometimes produce anomalous results (e.g., extremely high or low scores for short sentences).
- Why unresolved: The paper relies on these automatic metrics for evaluation but explicitly states that manual evaluation would be more reliable. The authors call for verification of automatic metric validity for German simplification.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic human evaluation studies comparing automatic metric scores against human judgments for German simplification outputs, with correlation analysis to determine which metrics best align with human preferences.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal training data composition for German text simplification models targeting different audience levels?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper contrasts "mild" simplifications (DEPLAIN-APA, targeting B1→A2) with "strong" simplifications (DEPLAIN-WEB, targeting C2→A2/B1/B2). Experiments show different performance patterns when training on each corpus separately versus combined, with the combined approach achieving highest scores across metrics.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that combining both mild and strong simplifications yields the best overall performance, it doesn't explore the optimal ratio or whether different target audiences (e.g., children vs. adults with reading difficulties) would benefit from different training data compositions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments varying the proportion of mild versus strong simplifications in training data, evaluated on specific target audience test sets, with manual evaluation to determine which combinations produce the most appropriate simplifications for each audience type.

## Limitations

- The corpus contains only 14,968 manually aligned sentence pairs, which is relatively small compared to English simplification corpora.
- Limited diversity in domains, focusing primarily on news and web content from two organizations, may restrict model generalization.
- The corpus shows mixed licensing for WEB content, limiting its use for commercial applications.
- Evaluation relies on SARI scores calculated against a single reference per sentence, which may not fully capture simplification quality.

## Confidence

**High confidence:** The corpus construction methodology is well-documented and follows established practices in parallel corpus creation. The claim that DEPLAIN contains approximately 1,239 document pairs and 16,562 sentence pairs is directly supported by the corpus statistics presented in the paper. The observation that combining APA and WEB domain data improves model generalization is well-supported by the experimental results showing improved SARI scores.

**Medium confidence:** The assertion that manual alignments significantly outperform automatic alignments for complex simplification operations is supported by the evaluation metrics, but the actual impact on downstream model performance could vary depending on the specific model architecture and training setup. The claim that the corpus supports both mild and strong simplifications is based on the source domain characteristics, but the actual simplification operations present in the corpus would need independent verification.

**Low confidence:** The claim that the corpus is "sufficiently large to train and evaluate TS models" is questionable given that only 14,968 sentence pairs are manually aligned. The assertion that the corpus "is also valuable for studies on the relation between TS operations and discourse phenomena" is speculative and not directly supported by the paper's experiments.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-validation of automatic alignment quality:** Extract a random sample of 100 automatically aligned sentence pairs from the WEB domain and manually verify their accuracy to quantify the actual quality of the automatic alignment method beyond the precision/recall metrics reported.

2. **Domain transfer evaluation:** Train a text simplification model on the APA portion of DEPLAIN and evaluate its performance on an external German simplification corpus (if available) or on simplified web content from different sources to assess domain generalization.

3. **Human evaluation study:** Conduct a human evaluation study comparing outputs from models trained on: (a) only manually aligned data, (b) only automatically aligned data, and (c) combined data, to determine the actual impact of automatic alignment quality on simplification output quality.
