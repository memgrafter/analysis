# adSformers: Personalization from Short-Term Sequences and Diversity of Representations in Etsy Ads

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01255
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01255
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: adSformer CTR and PCCVR models achieved +2.66% and +2.42% lift in offline ROC-AUC respectively, and drove significant online gains in ROAS and GMS in A/B tests.

## Executive Summary
Etsy Ads personalized sponsored search ranking by encoding short-term user sequences using a three-component module called the adSformer diversifiable personalization module (ADPM). The ADPM uses a transformer-based encoder, pretrained representations (visual, multimodal, text), and learned on-the-fly pooled representations to derive dynamic user representations. These are concatenated to the input layer of CTR and PCCVR models. The resulting adSformer CTR and PCCVR models achieved +2.66% and +2.42% lift in offline ROC-AUC respectively, and drove significant online gains in ROAS and GMS in A/B tests. The approach improves personalization by capturing short-term user intent while maintaining scalability and flexibility for future use cases.

## Method Summary
The ADPM encodes one-hour user action sequences (views, favorites, searches, etc.) capped at 50 actions, truncating to recent window and encoding actions with vocabularies limited to top K frequent entities. It combines three components: adSformer encoder (transformer block with multi-head attention, global max pooling), pretrained representations (visual, AIR, skip-gram, text), and learned pooled representations. These are concatenated for dynamic user representation, which is then combined with query/listing/context features and fed into DCN interaction layers for CTR/PCCVR prediction, trained with Adam/LazyAdam optimizer and cosine annealing.

## Key Results
- adSformer CTR model achieved +2.66% lift in offline ROC-AUC
- adSformer PCCVR model achieved +2.42% lift in offline ROC-AUC
- Significant online gains in ROAS and GMS in A/B tests

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Short-term user sequences within a one-hour window capture high-intent, recency-sensitive user actions.
- **Mechanism:** The ADPM encodes sequences of user actions (views, favorites, searches, etc.) from the most recent one-hour session. By truncating to a one-hour window, the model prioritizes recent actions over older ones, which is especially important for logged-out users who lack persistent identity. The sliding window approach allows more or less personalization depending on the available signal per user.
- **Core assumption:** Recency is more predictive of immediate user intent than long-term behavior in the context of sponsored search ads.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "model's effectiveness and flexibility by personalizing the Click-Through Rate (CTR) and Post-Click Conversion Rate (PCCVR) models used in sponsored search (ad) ranking"
  - [section 1.1.2]: "by truncating interaction sequences to a one hour window, we apply more or less personalization, depending on the per-user available relevant short-term personalization signal"
  - [corpus]: Weak - the cited papers focus on long-term or sequential modeling, not specifically one-hour recency windows.
- **Break condition:** If user actions are sparse or if the one-hour window does not align with typical shopping decision cycles, the signal may be too weak to improve personalization.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Diversity of representations (visual, multimodal, text, learned) enriches the user representation and improves downstream model performance.
- **Mechanism:** The ADPM combines three components: (1) adSformer encoder learns deep structure from sequences, (2) pretrained representations (visual, AIR, skip-gram) encode different modalities of the same entities, and (3) learned on-the-fly pooled representations add flexibility. This diversity mitigates correlated signals and captures complementary aspects of user intent.
- **Core assumption:** Different representation types encode complementary information; combining them yields better performance than any single type.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "enrich the signal through visual, multimodal and other pretrained representations"
  - [section 1.2.3]: "Without diversity we would have no need for personalization. When users’ interests are homogeneous a simple popularity recommender caters to all."
  - [section 4]: "we find that different pretrained listing ID representations perform differently in downstream tasks"
- **Break condition:** If representations are highly redundant or if one representation dominates, the diversity benefit diminishes.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The adSformer encoder block with global max pooling and multiple attention heads learns more salient sequence signals than standard transformer blocks.
- **Mechanism:** The adSformer encoder uses a modified transformer block with no dropout, a global max pooling layer, and multi-head attention to capture the most important actions in the sequence. It is trained end-to-end with the downstream models, allowing it to specialize for the CTR and PCCVR tasks.
- **Core assumption:** Removing dropout and adding global max pooling improves signal diversity and reduces overfitting in the context of short sequences.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [section 2.1]: "The adSformer encoder component uses one or more custom adSformer blocks with several attention heads, no dropout and a global max pooling layer"
  - [section A.2]: "Different from [48], we exclude the dropout layer for enhanced performance (+0.04% in ROC AUC in adSformer CTR case)"
  - [corpus]: Weak - cited papers use standard transformer architectures without the specific modifications described here.
- **Break condition:** If the sequence length or complexity increases significantly, the current adSformer block design may become insufficient.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Transformer encoder with multi-head self-attention
  - **Why needed here:** To learn contextual representations of user action sequences where order and relative importance matter.
  - **Quick check question:** What is the difference between self-attention and cross-attention in a transformer block?

- **Concept:** Pretrained representation learning (visual, multimodal, text)
  - **Why needed here:** To provide rich, off-the-shelf embeddings that capture different aspects of listings (e.g., visual style, semantic meaning) without training from scratch.
  - **Quick check question:** How does a skip-gram model differ from a visual representation model in terms of what it captures about listings?

- **Concept:** Position bias and debiasing in ranking systems
  - **Why needed here:** To prevent the model from favoring higher-ranked ads simply due to their position, which can create a feedback loop and hurt personalization.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might a model trained without position bias correction show higher CTR for higher positions even if personalization is poor?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** User action sequences -> ADPM (adSformer encoder + pretrained representations + learned pooled representations) -> Concatenated with query/listing features -> DCN interaction module -> CTR/PCCVR prediction

- **Critical path:**
  1. Stream user actions → feature store
  2. ADPM encodes short-term sequence + pretrained embeddings
  3. Concatenate with query/listing features
  4. Feed into DCN + fully connected layers
  5. Output calibrated probability
  6. Combine with bid to compute value score for ranking

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Vocabulary size vs memory: capping at top K listings reduces memory but may lose rare but relevant items.
  - Dropout in adSformer block: removing dropout improved performance here but may increase overfitting risk in other settings.
  - Training dataset size: adSformer PCCVR benefits from more data (3 weeks vs 2) due to higher model capacity.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Poor offline AUC lift despite online gains → possible data leakage or offline/online distribution shift.
  - High latency in serving → ADPM or feature lookup bottlenecks.
  - Calibration error spikes → mismatch between training and serving feature distributions.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. **Ablation study:** Remove ADPM from CTR model and measure AUC drop to confirm contribution.
  2. **Hyperparameter sweep:** Vary adSformer encoder heads and pooling type to find optimal configuration.
  3. **Position bias test:** Compare CTR of top-ranked ads vs personalized rank order to detect position bias effects.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several areas remain unexplored based on the methodology described. The paper mentions that future work includes experimenting with different adSformer encoder architectures and exploring the impact of different combinations of pretrained representations on model performance.

## Limitations
- Offline evaluation shows modest AUC improvements without statistical significance testing
- Online A/B test results lack detailed metrics, confidence intervals, or sample sizes
- One-hour recency window is justified but not empirically validated against other durations
- Diversity benefit claims are theoretical without systematic ablation studies of representation types

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The technical architecture and implementation details are clearly specified and reproducible. The modular design of ADPM and its integration with existing CTR/PCCVR models is well-documented.
- **Medium confidence**: The offline AUC improvements are measurable and the methodology is sound, though the practical significance and statistical robustness are unclear. The three-component design rationale is logical but not empirically validated through systematic ablation studies.
- **Low confidence**: The online A/B test results are insufficiently detailed to assess real-world impact. Claims about diversity benefits and recency window optimization lack empirical support or sensitivity analysis.

## Next Checks
1. **Statistical significance testing**: Recompute offline AUC improvements with confidence intervals and p-values to determine if the +2.66% and +2.42% gains are statistically meaningful, then design online A/B tests with pre-registered sample sizes to detect practical significance.
2. **Recency window sensitivity analysis**: Conduct experiments varying the one-hour window to 30 minutes, 2 hours, and 4 hours, measuring both offline AUC and online performance to identify optimal recency duration for different user segments.
3. **Representation diversity quantification**: Systematically ablate each pretrained representation type (visual, AIR, skip-gram, text) from ADPM and measure marginal AUC contributions to quantify the actual diversity benefit versus individual representation performance.
