# ChatGPT Beyond English: Towards a Comprehensive Evaluation of Large Language Models in Multilingual Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.05613
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05613
- Reference count: 39
- ChatGPT underperforms supervised state-of-the-art models on all evaluated multilingual NLP tasks except POS tagging, with large gaps in complex reasoning tasks and low-resource languages.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of ChatGPT's zero-shot multilingual capabilities across 37 languages and 7 NLP tasks. The study compares ChatGPT against supervised state-of-the-art models, revealing that while ChatGPT shows promise for low-level syntactic tasks like POS tagging, it consistently underperforms on more complex reasoning tasks. English prompts consistently outperform language-specific ones, highlighting a significant bias in the model's multilingual understanding. The results indicate that task-specific models remain necessary for reliable multilingual NLP applications.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates ChatGPT using zero-shot prompting across seven NLP tasks: POS tagging, named entity recognition, relation extraction, natural language inference, question answering, common sense reasoning, and summarization. For each task, the researchers constructed prompts with both English and language-specific task descriptions, sent them to the ChatGPT API, and parsed the responses using predefined rules. Performance was compared against supervised models (primarily XLM-R) across 37 languages representing various resource levels, using standardized multilingual datasets.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT underperforms supervised state-of-the-art models across all tasks except POS tagging
- English prompts consistently outperform language-specific prompts, even for non-English tasks
- Performance gaps are largest for complex reasoning tasks and low-resource languages
- POS tagging is an exception where ChatGPT matches or exceeds supervised models, especially for low-resource languages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT's zero-shot learning performance is significantly worse than supervised state-of-the-art models across most NLP tasks in multiple languages.
- Mechanism: Zero-shot learning relies on task descriptions and prompts to elicit the correct behavior from the model without any fine-tuning or task-specific examples. The model's knowledge is purely derived from pre-training data, which may not be sufficient for complex downstream tasks.
- Core assumption: The prompt engineering approach can fully specify the task requirements to the model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Compared to the performance of previous models, our extensive experimental results demonstrate a worse performance of ChatGPT for different NLP tasks and languages..."
  - [section 4]: "Table 2 presents the performance of ChatGPT (zero-shot learning with both English and language-specific task descriptions) and the fully supervised XLM-R model... ChatGPT outperforms XLM-R over 13 out 17 languages for multilingual POS tagging." (Note: This is an exception, but the general trend across other tasks is underperformance.)
- Break condition: If task descriptions are insufficient or ambiguous, or if the model's pre-training data lacks relevant examples for the task.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ChatGPT exhibits a clear bias towards English, performing better on English tasks than on other languages, especially for higher-level reasoning tasks.
- Mechanism: The model's training data is predominantly English, leading to better representation and understanding of English language patterns and structures. This bias translates into better performance on English tasks compared to other languages.
- Core assumption: The distribution of languages in the training data directly impacts the model's performance on tasks in those languages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "ChatGPT's performance is generally better for English than for other languages, especially for higher-level tasks that require more complex reasoning abilities..."
  - [section 4]: "Different from XLM-R where English has the best POS tagging performance, ChatGPT seems to have better accuracy than English with some other languages (e.g., French, Spanish)." (Note: This is an exception, but the general trend across other tasks is a bias towards English.)
- Break condition: If the model is trained on a more balanced multilingual dataset, or if the task requires less complex reasoning.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: English prompts consistently outperform language-specific prompts for ChatGPT, even when the task and input text are intended for other languages.
- Mechanism: English prompts may help shift the representations of language-specific inputs towards the English space, which the model is better equipped to process due to the dominance of English in its training data.
- Core assumption: The model's internal representations are more aligned with English than with other languages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "ChatGPT can perform better with English prompts even though the task and input texts are intended for other languages..."
  - [section 4]: "We observe that English prompts tend to perform better or at lest competitively with language-specific prompts for ChatGPT across different languages for POS tagging."
- Break condition: If the task is highly language-specific and requires deep understanding of the target language's nuances.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Task description + Input text + Entity mentions/context → Prompt generation → ChatGPT model → Generated response → Parsing → Evaluation
- Critical path: Prompt generation → ChatGPT model → Output parsing → Evaluation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Prompt engineering: Balancing between providing sufficient information and avoiding ambiguity
  - Model selection: Choosing between zero-shot, few-shot, or fine-tuning approaches based on available resources and task requirements
  - Language selection: Prioritizing languages based on resource availability and task relevance
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance: Indicates issues with prompt engineering, model limitations, or task complexity
  - Inconsistent outputs: Suggests sensitivity to prompt variations or model instability
  - Bias towards English: Highlights the need for more balanced multilingual training data
- First 3 experiments:
  1. POS tagging on a high-resource language (e.g., English) to establish a baseline
  2. NER on a low-resource language (e.g., Bengali) to test the model's ability to generalize
  3. QA on a medium-resource language (e.g., Vietnamese) to evaluate the model's reasoning capabilities

## Limitations

- The zero-shot prompting approach may not fully leverage ChatGPT's capabilities, potentially underestimating its true performance.
- The study focuses on a single ChatGPT model version without exploring performance variations across different model sizes or configurations.
- The comparison with supervised models assumes access to high-quality annotated data, which may not be available for all languages or tasks.
- The study does not explore the reasons behind ChatGPT's superior performance on POS tagging compared to other tasks.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: ChatGPT's consistent underperformance compared to supervised models across most tasks and languages, particularly for complex reasoning tasks.
- **Medium Confidence**: The observed bias towards English performance and the effectiveness of English prompts for non-English tasks, though exceptions exist.
- **Medium Confidence**: The superior performance on POS tagging, especially for low-resource languages, though the reasons for this exception require further investigation.
