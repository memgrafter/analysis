# Exploring the Benefits of Training Expert Language Models over Instruction Tuning

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.03202
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03202
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Expert LMs trained on single tasks can outperform multitask LMs trained on 300+ tasks on unseen tasks by 3.20% mean accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the conventional wisdom that scaling the number of tasks in multitask instruction tuning leads to better performance on unseen tasks. The authors demonstrate that expert language models trained on individual tasks can outperform multitask models on unseen tasks, while offering additional benefits such as avoiding negative task transfer, enabling continual learning, and supporting compositional capabilities through model merging.

## Method Summary
The approach involves training individual expert models (either full fine-tuned or adapter-based) for each of 296 prompts from 36 datasets, while keeping the base T5-3B model frozen. For inference on unseen tasks, a Retrieval-of-Experts mechanism uses a dense retriever to select the most relevant expert model from the Expert Library. The paper also explores merging experts for compositional tasks through weighted parameter averaging, and demonstrates continual learning by adding new experts without retraining on previous tasks.

## Key Results
- Expert LMs outperform multitask LMs on 11 unseen datasets by 3.20% mean accuracy
- Expert LMs outperform multitask LMs on 13 BIG-bench tasks by 1.29% mean accuracy
- Merged expert models achieve +2.71 ROUGE-L improvement on 5 compositional tasks compared to multitask counterparts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Single expert models outperform multitask models on unseen tasks
- **Mechanism**: Multitask models suffer from negative task transfer, while expert models avoid this by training independently
- **Core assumption**: Single task training provides sufficient generalization capability
- **Evidence anchors**: Expert outperforms multitask by 3.20% on unseen datasets and 1.29% on BIG-bench
- **Break condition**: If single task is not representative of broader task distribution

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Retrieval-based expert selection improves performance
- **Mechanism**: Dense retriever maps unseen tasks to most relevant expert model
- **Core assumption**: Dense retriever can effectively match tasks to experts
- **Evidence anchors**: Retrieval-of-Experts approach enables dynamic expert selection
- **Break condition**: If dense retriever fails to accurately map tasks to experts

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Merged experts outperform multitask models on compositional tasks
- **Mechanism**: Weighted parameter averaging combines complementary expert capabilities
- **Core assumption**: Individual experts have complementary skills for composition
- **Evidence anchors**: Merged experts achieve +2.71 ROUGE-L on compositional tasks
- **Break condition**: If experts lack complementary skills or merging fails

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept**: Negative task transfer
  - **Why needed here**: Explains why multitask training can harm individual task performance
  - **Quick check question**: What is negative task transfer, and how does it affect multitask model performance?

- **Concept**: Catastrophic forgetting
  - **Why needed here**: Highlights expert models' advantage in continual learning scenarios
  - **Quick check question**: What is catastrophic forgetting, and how do expert models mitigate this compared to multitask models?

- **Concept**: Compositional instructions
  - **Why needed here**: Essential for understanding expert merging benefits
  - **Quick check question**: What are compositional instructions, and how do they differ from regular instructions in terms of model performance?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Expert models -> Expert Library -> Dense retriever -> Retrieval-of-Experts mechanism -> Inference

- **Critical path**:
  1. Train expert models on individual tasks
  2. Construct Expert Library with training instances and expert IDs
  3. Use dense retriever to map unseen tasks to relevant experts
  4. Retrieve corresponding expert model from library
  5. Perform inference with retrieved expert

- **Design tradeoffs**: Training multiple experts requires more resources but avoids negative transfer and enables continual learning

- **Failure signatures**: Poor unseen task performance may indicate insufficient expert diversity or retrieval mechanism failure

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Train experts on diverse tasks and evaluate on unseen datasets
  2. Implement and benchmark different dense retriever models
  3. Experiment with merging strategies and assess compositional task performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question**: How do expert LMs compare to MT LMs on zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks?
- **Basis in paper**: Expert LM outperforms MT LM trained on 300+ tasks by 3.20% on unseen datasets
- **Why unresolved**: Limited task diversity and lack of comparison to other state-of-the-art MT LMs
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extensive comparison across diverse tasks and multiple MT LM architectures

### Open Question 2
- **Question**: How does the distributed expert approach compare to traditional MT LM training in efficiency and effectiveness?
- **Basis in paper**: Expert approach offers benefits like avoiding negative transfer and enabling continual learning
- **Why unresolved**: Lack of comprehensive comparison of computational cost, training time, and broad task performance
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Thorough efficiency and effectiveness comparison across multiple dimensions

### Open Question 3
- **Question**: How does expert merging affect unseen task performance and what are optimal merging methods?
- **Basis in paper**: Merging fully fine-tuned experts shows promise but adapter merging has inconsistent results
- **Why unresolved**: Limited exploration of merging methods and their effects on performance
- **What evidence would resolve it**: Extensive experiments testing different merging strategies and their impact on various unseen tasks

## Limitations
- Limited task diversity in expert training (36 datasets across 8 categories)
- Sparse hyperparameter reporting making precise replication challenging
- Dense retriever implementation details insufficiently specified
- Limited evaluation scope for compositional task merging

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Core finding that expert models outperform multitask models on unseen tasks
- **Medium confidence**: Benefits of avoiding negative transfer and enabling continual learning
- **Low confidence**: Specific implementation details for retrieval and merging strategies
