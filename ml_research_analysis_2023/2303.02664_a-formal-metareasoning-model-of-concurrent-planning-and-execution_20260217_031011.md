# A Formal Metareasoning Model of Concurrent Planning and Execution

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02664
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02664
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 11
- Key outcome: Formal MDP model of concurrent planning and execution under time pressure, with algorithms that outperform traditional planning-only methods by up to 85% success probability

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a formal model for concurrent planning and execution (CoPE) under time pressure, extending prior work on situated temporal planning. The model allows agents to interleave base-level actions with search, trading off opportunity (gaining more time to plan) against risk (executing premature or irreversible actions). The authors cast CoPE as a Markov decision process and prove it is NP-hard, but identify tractable special cases (known induced deadlines, bounded plan prefixes, equal slack) solvable in pseudo-polynomial time via dynamic programming. They propose several greedy and MCTS-based algorithms for the general case, and evaluate them empirically using 15-puzzle instances. Results show that execution-aware algorithms significantly outperform traditional planning-only methods under high time pressure, achieving up to 85% success probability where non-concurrent methods fail entirely.

## Method Summary
The authors formulate CoPE as a Markov decision process where states represent search frontier configurations, actions include both base-level actions and search expansions, and rewards capture the value of finding timely solutions. For known induced deadlines, they develop a dynamic programming algorithm that computes optimal policies in pseudo-polynomial time. For the general case with unknown deadlines, they propose greedy algorithms based on diminishing returns and delay-damage awareness, as well as Monte Carlo Tree Search variants. The empirical evaluation uses 15-puzzle instances with Manhattan distance heuristic, generating 10,000 solved instances to create CDF histograms for expansions and solution lengths. They compare success probability and runtime across different numbers of processes and base-action durations.

## Key Results
- CoPE algorithms achieve up to 85% success probability under high time pressure where traditional planning-only methods fail entirely
- Greedy algorithms based on diminishing returns and delay-damage awareness effectively allocate computation time among processes
- Pseudo-polynomial time algorithms solve CoPE optimally for special cases (known induced deadlines, bounded plan prefixes, equal slack)
- Empirical evaluation demonstrates significant performance improvements of execution-aware algorithms over planning-only approaches

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The CoPE model improves success probability by allowing execution to begin before planning is complete, trading off opportunity for risk.
- Mechanism: Execution-aware algorithms can start acting on partial plans while continuing to search, gaining time to find a complete solution. This is especially beneficial when time pressure is high and traditional planning-only methods fail.
- Core assumption: The world is deterministic, and uncertainty only exists in planning time and plan duration.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "agents can begin execution of actions before a complete plan to a goal has been found... This risk is most significant when actions are irreversible."
  - [section]: "Our main contribution is to formally define this setting as an abstract metareasoning problem... through tests on instances derived from search problems, find several methods that achieve promising practical performance."
- Break condition: If actions are highly reversible or time pressure is low, the risk of premature execution may outweigh the benefits.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Greedy algorithms based on diminishing returns and delay-damage awareness can efficiently allocate computation time among processes.
- Mechanism: The basic greedy scheme (BGS) and delay-damage aware (DDA) scheme prioritize processes based on a combination of urgency (expected deadlines) and utility per time unit. This allows for effective scheduling of limited computational resources.
- Core assumption: The processes are independent, and the performance profiles and deadline distributions are known.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Shperberg et al. (2019) used insights from a diminishing returns result to develop greedy schemes... the delay-damage aware (DDA) greedy scheme was to assign, at each processing allocation round, tu time to the process i that maximizes Q'(i)."
  - [corpus]: "The DDA scheme was then adapted and integrated into the OPTIC temporal planner (Benton, Coles, and Coles 2012), providing state-of-the-art results on situated problems where external deadlines play a significant role (Shperberg et al. 2021)."
- Break condition: If the deadline distributions have high variance or the processes are not independent, the greedy schemes may not perform optimally.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pseudo-polynomial time algorithms can solve CoPE optimally for special cases, such as known induced deadlines and bounded plan prefixes.
- Mechanism: For known induced deadlines, a dynamic programming approach can find the optimal policy by considering all possible initiation functions for the base-level actions. For bounded plan prefixes, the number of initiation functions is limited, making the problem tractable.
- Core assumption: The induced deadlines are known, and the action durations are bounded.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We can state the CoPE optimization problem as the solution to an MDP... We denote this known-induced-deadline restriction of CoPE by KID-CoPE and analyze this case, trying to get a pseudo-polynomial time algorithm for computing the optimal policy."
  - [section]: "Bounded Length Preﬁxes: If the length of all the Hi is bounded by a constant K, we get a pseudo-polynomial time algorithm, because the number of initiation functions is at most |dom(T )||Hi|."
- Break condition: If the induced deadlines are unknown or the plan prefixes are not bounded, the pseudo-polynomial time algorithms may not apply.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: MDP formulation -> Solution algorithms -> Empirical evaluation
- Critical path:
  1. Formulate the CoPE problem as an MDP
  2. Analyze the MDP to identify tractable special cases
  3. Develop solution algorithms for the general case
  4. Evaluate the algorithms empirically using benchmark instances
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Optimality vs. tractability: Exact solutions are intractable, so approximate algorithms are necessary
  - Execution vs. planning: Starting execution early can save time but also carries risks
  - Computation vs. memory: More complex algorithms may require more memory but can find better solutions
- Failure signatures:
  - Low success probability: The algorithms may not be allocating computation time effectively
  - High runtime: The algorithms may be too complex or the problem instances may be too large
  - Inconsistent results: The algorithms may be sensitive to the initial conditions or the random sampling
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the MDP formulation and verify that it captures the key aspects of the CoPE problem
  2. Develop a simple greedy algorithm based on the diminishing returns result and evaluate its performance on small instances
  3. Extend the greedy algorithm to handle the general case with unknown deadlines and evaluate its performance on larger instances

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
1. Extending the model to parallel durative actions rather than sequential non-preemptible ones
2. Adapting and integrating CoPE schemes into a situated online planner while keeping metareasoning overhead manageable
3. Developing methods to improve robustness to estimation errors in deadline distributions and performance profiles

## Limitations
- Theoretical complexity analysis assumes deterministic action durations, which may not hold in real-world domains with execution uncertainty
- Empirical evaluation uses 15-puzzle instances, which may not generalize to domains with more complex state spaces or partial observability
- Proposed algorithms require knowledge of deadline distributions and performance profiles, which may be difficult to obtain in practice

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The MDP formulation of CoPE is theoretically sound and the proof of NP-hardness is valid
- **Medium confidence**: The greedy algorithms and MCTS variants provide practical solutions for the general CoPE problem, but their performance may degrade in domains with high uncertainty
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical results demonstrate the effectiveness of execution-aware algorithms under time pressure, but the generalizability to other domains requires further validation
