# ChatGPT versus Traditional Question Answering for Knowledge Graphs: Current Status and Future Directions Towards Knowledge Graph Chatbots

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.06466
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.06466
- **Reference count**: 8
- **Primary result**: ChatGPT excels in explainability and robustness but struggles with academic KGs and lacks ability to incorporate recent information or generalize across domains.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a comparative evaluation framework for assessing conversational AI models like ChatGPT against traditional Question-Answering Systems (QAS) for Knowledge Graphs (KGs). The study evaluates two representative conversational models (ChatGPT and Galactica) against KGQA N, the state-of-the-art QAS, using four real KGs across different domains. The framework measures seven criteria: correctness, determinism, robustness, explainability, question understanding, incorporating recent information, and generality across domains. Results show that ChatGPT excels in explainability and robustness but struggles with academic KGs and lacks the ability to incorporate recent information or generalize across domains. KGQA N performs consistently across different KGs but lacks explainability and robustness. The study identifies open research challenges for developing advanced KG chatbots that combine the strengths of both approaches.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates ChatGPT and Galactica against KGQA N using four real KGs (DBpedia, YAGO, DBLP, MAG) and benchmarks containing 450 English questions. KGQA N is a pipeline-based QAS using just-in-time linking with pre-trained Seq2Seq models and word embedding models. ChatGPT is tested with three variations (default, follow-up, Excel). The evaluation framework measures seven criteria: correctness (micro-F1 score), determinism (repeated runs), robustness (spelling/grammar errors), explainability (manual assessment), question understanding (intermediate representation analysis), incorporating recent information (update checking), and generality (cross-domain performance). Results are compared across the four KG domains to assess system capabilities.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT excels in explainability and robustness but struggles with academic KGs and lacks ability to incorporate recent information or generalize across domains
- KGQA N performs consistently across different KGs but lacks explainability and robustness
- A combination of ChatGPT's strengths and KGQA N's strengths could lead to advanced KG chatbots
- The study identifies open research challenges for developing advanced KG chatbots

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can KG chatbots be developed that combine the explainability and robustness of conversational AI models with the generality and information recency of QASs?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes this as a key research challenge, stating "Advanced KG Chatbots could be developed by incorporating capabilities of language models, such as ChatGPT, in question understanding, robustness, and explainability, with the outstanding capabilities of a QA system, such as KGQA N, in incorporating recent information and generality across different domains."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as an open challenge but does not provide a solution or framework for combining these capabilities.
- What evidence would resolve it: A working prototype or system that successfully integrates both conversational AI and QAS capabilities, demonstrating improved performance across all seven criteria (correctness, determinism, robustness, explainability, question understanding, incorporating recent information, and generality).

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can question-answering systems be enhanced to support dialogue-based interactions with users while maintaining accuracy and efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the need to modify the traditional QAS pipeline to handle dialogue, stating "We need to modify question understanding and linking steps to handle the dialogue."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a challenge but does not provide a solution for how to modify the pipeline or what changes are needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: A new QAS architecture that supports dialogue interactions, with benchmarks showing improved performance in conversational settings compared to traditional QASs.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What methods can be developed to enable KG chatbots to identify and handle unanswerable questions, providing users with alternative resources or suggestions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions this as an open research challenge, stating "The KG chatbot should be able to identify unanswerable questions. It may also need to suggest where to find answers as that shows the user the question is understood, but the KG has no related facts."
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a challenge but does not propose any methods for implementing this capability.
- What evidence would resolve it: A KG chatbot system that can accurately identify unanswerable questions and provide helpful suggestions or alternative resources to users.

## Limitations
- Manual evaluation for explainability and question understanding introduces subjectivity and affects reproducibility
- Evaluation focuses primarily on English questions, limiting generalizability to other languages
- Does not account for potential variations in KG schema or completeness across different domains

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The comparison framework and evaluation methodology are clearly defined and systematic. The identification of ChatGPT's strengths in explainability and robustness versus KGQA N's consistency across domains is well-supported by the evidence.

- **Medium Confidence**: The conclusion that KGQA N lacks explainability and robustness is based on manual evaluation criteria that could be subjective. The assessment of generality across domains relies on performance across four specific KGs, which may not represent all possible domains.

- **Low Confidence**: The paper's assertion about ChatGPT's inability to incorporate recent information is not directly tested but rather inferred from its training characteristics. The claim that combining both approaches would create superior KG chatbots remains theoretical without empirical validation.

## Next Checks

1. **Automated Explainability Metrics**: Develop automated metrics for explainability assessment to reduce subjectivity in evaluation and enable more consistent comparisons across different KG QA systems.

2. **Multi-turn Dialogue Benchmark**: Create a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate conversational capabilities, including context maintenance and follow-up question handling, to better assess real-world KG chatbot performance.

3. **Cross-linguistic Evaluation**: Extend the evaluation framework to include questions in multiple languages to assess the cross-linguistic capabilities of both ChatGPT and KGQA N approaches.
