# InheritSumm: A General, Versatile and Compact Summarizer by Distilling from GPT

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.13083
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13083
- **Reference count**: 10
- **Primary result**: InheritSumm achieves the impossible triangle of moderate model size, strong zero/few-shot learning, and excellent supervised learning by distilling from GPT-3.5

## Executive Summary
InheritSumm is a compact summarization model that achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple settings by distilling knowledge from GPT-3.5. The model is trained on a curated corpus of 7M document-summary pairs (GPTSumm) generated by GPT-3.5, enabling it to match or outperform GPT-3.5 in zero/few-shot settings while being significantly smaller. The model demonstrates strong performance in prefix-tuning and full-data fine-tuning scenarios, achieving the highest average performance across all settings compared to previous small models.

## Method Summary
InheritSumm uses knowledge distillation to transfer summarization capabilities from GPT-3.5 to a smaller ZCode++ seq2seq transformer model. The model is trained on GPTSumm, a corpus of 7M document-summary pairs generated by GPT-3.5 in various zero/few-shot settings with different prompts and in-context demonstrations. The training process employs succinct demonstrations to include more examples within input length limits by truncating documents and adding length suffixes. The model is then adapted to four summarization settings: zero-shot, few-shot with instructions, few-shot with prefix-tuning, and fully supervised learning.

## Key Results
- InheritSumm matches or outperforms GPT-3.5 in zero/few-shot settings despite being much smaller
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance in prefix-tuning and full-data fine-tuning scenarios
- Demonstrates strong generalization across five diverse summarization datasets (MultiNews, XWiki, SAMSum, Reddit-TIFU, BigPatent)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: InheritSumm can match or outperform GPT-3.5 in zero/few-shot settings despite being much smaller.
- Mechanism: Knowledge distillation transfers summarization capability from GPT-3.5 to a smaller model by training on document-summary pairs generated by GPT-3.5.
- Core assumption: GPT-3.5-generated summaries capture generalizable summarization knowledge learnable by smaller models.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states InheritSumm "closely matches or outperforms GPT-3.5 in zero/few-shot settings"; section 3.2 describes using GPT-3.5 to generate summaries in various settings.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: InheritSumm achieves strong performance in prefix-tuning and full-data fine-tuning scenarios.
- Mechanism: Training on diverse GPTSumm corpus allows model to adapt well to different fine-tuning scenarios through broad knowledge acquired during distillation.
- Core assumption: Models trained on diverse, large datasets have better generalization and adaptability to new tasks.
- Evidence anchors: Abstract states it "outperforms the previously established best small models in both prefix-tuning and full-data fine-tuning scenarios"; section 4.1 describes adapting to three different summarization settings.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: InheritSumm's use of succinct demonstrations improves few-shot learning by allowing more in-context examples within input length limit.
- Mechanism: Truncating documents and summaries while adding length suffixes enables inclusion of more examples without exceeding input limits.
- Core assumption: Including more in-context examples improves model performance in few-shot learning.
- Evidence anchors: Section 3.4 describes truncating documents and adding "omitted X words in total" suffixes; section 5.2.1 shows performance improves from 1-shot to 4-shots with succinct demonstrations.

## Foundational Learning

- **Knowledge Distillation**: Why needed - To transfer GPT-3.5's summarization capabilities to a smaller, more efficient model. Quick check - What is the primary goal of using knowledge distillation in this context?
- **Few-shot Learning**: Why needed - To enable model to perform well with minimal training data by leveraging in-context examples. Quick check - How does few-shot learning differ from zero-shot learning in terms of model performance?
- **Prefix-tuning**: Why needed - To adapt model to specific tasks by tuning task-specific prefix vectors without modifying entire model. Quick check - What is the advantage of prefix-tuning over full fine-tuning in terms of computational efficiency?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Documents → GPT-3.5 (knowledge source) → ZCode++ model (distilled model) → Summarization outputs across four settings
- **Critical path**: Train ZCode++ on GPTSumm corpus → Adapt to zero/few-shot, prefix-tuning, and supervised settings → Evaluate performance on benchmark datasets
- **Design tradeoffs**: Balances model size reduction against maintaining performance across multiple settings, aiming for "impossible triangle" of moderate size, strong few-shot learning, and excellent supervised learning
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance in any of three settings (zero/few-shot, prefix-tuning, supervised) indicates failure in distillation process or fine-tuning adaptation
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Evaluate zero-shot performance on diverse summarization tasks to ensure coherent summary generation without examples
  2. Test few-shot learning with succinct demonstrations to confirm improved performance with more examples
  3. Assess prefix-tuning and supervised learning to verify adaptability to different fine-tuning scenarios

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal number of in-context examples to use for few-shot summarization?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions increasing in-context examples can enhance quality but documents are lengthy, leading to reduced usable input length when including numerous examples.
- Why unresolved: Paper only used up to 4 examples in experiments without determining optimal number.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing performance with varying numbers of in-context examples (1-6) on diverse summarization tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does InheritSumm's performance compare to other large language models like GPT-4 or Claude?
- Basis in paper: Paper compares to GPT-3.5 and several baselines but doesn't mention GPT-4 or Claude.
- Why unresolved: No comparison with newer large language models released after paper was written.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing InheritSumm to GPT-4 and Claude on same summarization tasks and datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does InheritSumm's performance vary across different domains?
- Basis in paper: Tested on 5 summarization datasets from diverse domains (MultiNews, XWiki, SAMSum, Reddit-TIFU, BigPatent).
- Why unresolved: Only limited number of domains tested; unclear how model would perform on other domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing InheritSumm on wider range of summarization datasets from various domains.

## Limitations

- Evaluation relies heavily on automatic metrics (ROUGE) without human evaluation to validate summary quality and coherence
- Claims of achieving "impossible triangle" are based on benchmark comparisons that may not generalize to real-world summarization tasks
- Training corpus derived from GPT-3.5 outputs could lead to memorization rather than genuine understanding of summarization principles

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Model architecture and training pipeline clearly specified with well-established techniques (ZCode++ transformer, knowledge distillation from GPT-3.5). Claim of outperforming previous small models in supervised learning supported by empirical results on standard benchmarks.

**Medium Confidence**: Effectiveness of succinct demonstrations in improving few-shot learning demonstrated through ablation studies, but impact may vary by domain and document length distribution. Claim of matching GPT-3.5 in zero/few-shot settings based on controlled experiments but may not hold for all summarization domains.

**Low Confidence**: Generalization to domains not represented in training corpus (Pile, arXiv, CNN/DM, WikiHow) is uncertain. No evidence provided for model's ability to handle specialized domains like legal or medical documents.

## Next Checks

1. **Human Evaluation Study**: Conduct human evaluation comparing InheritSumm's summaries against GPT-3.5 and baselines on coherence, informativeness, and factual accuracy to validate automatic metric results.

2. **Domain Generalization Test**: Evaluate InheritSumm on summarization tasks from domains not included in GPTSumm corpus (e.g., legal documents, medical literature) to assess generalization beyond training data distribution.

3. **Computational Cost Analysis**: Measure inference latency and memory usage on resource-constrained devices, comparing with other compact summarization models to assess real-world deployment viability.
