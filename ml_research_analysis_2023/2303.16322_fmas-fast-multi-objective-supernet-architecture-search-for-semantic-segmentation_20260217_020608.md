# FMAS: Fast Multi-Objective SuperNet Architecture Search for Semantic Segmentation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16322
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16322
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 36
- Key outcome: FMAS discovers efficient semantic segmentation networks by sub-sampling DeepLabV3+ weights and evaluating on validation subsets, achieving 99% faster search than training from scratch while maintaining accuracy

## Executive Summary
FMAS is a fast multi-objective neural architecture search (NAS) framework designed for semantic segmentation. It leverages pre-trained DeepLabV3+ weights as a supernet, allowing candidate models to inherit parameters without fine-tuning during search. By evaluating candidates on only 20% of the validation set and using NSGA-II to find Pareto-optimal models balancing accuracy and computational cost, FMAS achieves dramatic speedups—discovering efficient models in just 0.5 GPU days compared to 50 days for traditional methods.

## Method Summary
FMAS uses NSGA-II genetic algorithm with DeepLabV3+ as a supernet backbone. Candidate models are generated by sub-sampling the supernet structure, inheriting pre-trained weights without fine-tuning. Models are evaluated on 20% of the validation set to estimate MIoU error quickly. The algorithm searches for Pareto-optimal models balancing accuracy (MIoU) with FLOPs, parameters, or latency. Only final Pareto-optimal candidates undergo full training on the complete dataset.

## Key Results
- 99% reduction in search time compared to training from scratch (0.5 vs 50 GPU days)
- Achieves up to 10% reduction in FLOPs and 20% in parameters with less than 3% MIoU error increase
- Deploys efficient models to GAP8 hardware, achieving 2.2× speedup with only 7.61% MIoU loss

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Sub-sampling DeepLabV3+ pre-trained weights eliminates retraining during search. Candidates inherit parameters from matching layers of the supernet, and since atrous rates don't alter parameter counts, weights remain valid without fine-tuning.

### Mechanism 2
Evaluating candidates on 20% of validation set provides reliable MIoU estimates. Error stabilizes after this subset evaluation, enabling fast proxy assessment without full validation.

### Mechanism 3
NSGA-II effectively identifies Pareto-optimal models by balancing accuracy and computational cost through elitism and non-dominated sorting, using weight-sharing performance estimates.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: DeepLabV3+ supernet -> NSGA-II optimizer -> Subset validation evaluator -> Fine-tuning pipeline -> Deployment target (GAP8)
- **Critical path**: 1) Initialize population with DeepLabV3+ variants, 2) Evaluate on validation subset, 3) Select parents via NSGA-II, 4) Crossover/mutation to generate next generation, 5) Repeat until convergence, 6) Fine-tune Pareto-optimal models
- **Design tradeoffs**: Search speed vs accuracy (subset validation reduces time but may introduce error), Model size vs performance (reducing blocks improves speed but may hurt accuracy), Backbone choice (Modified Xception better accuracy, MobileNetV2 lighter)
- **Failure signatures**: Pareto front stagnates (diversity too low or search space too constrained), Accuracy drop during fine-tuning (inherited weights diverge), Slow convergence (too many generations or small population)
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Run FMAS with default parameters on PASCAL VOC using Modified Xception, 2) Modify validation subset size and measure impact on search time and accuracy, 3) Switch backbone to MobileNetV2 and target latency on GAP8

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Generalization to other backbones**: How does FMAS perform with MobileNetV3, EfficientNet beyond Modified Xception and MobileNetV2? The paper evaluates only two backbones, leaving others unexplored.
- **Validation subset size impact**: What effect does using different validation subset sizes have on search efficiency and accuracy? The study uses fixed 20% without exploring variations.
- **Performance on other datasets**: How does FMAS perform on Cityscapes or COCO beyond PASCAL VOC 2012? Results are specific to one dataset.
- **Additional optimization objectives**: Can FMAS optimize for energy consumption or memory usage beyond FLOPs, parameters, and latency? The study focuses on three specific objectives.

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation of weight-sharing approach beyond DeepLabV3+ backbone
- Subset validation introduces ±1.3% MIoU uncertainty that may affect Pareto-optimal selections
- Generalizability to other segmentation architectures or datasets remains untested

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: 99% reduction in search time supported by clear computational analysis
- **Medium confidence**: 10% FLOPs and 20% parameter reductions with <3% MIoU increase, but with subset validation uncertainty
- **Low confidence**: Generalizability of weight-sharing approach to architectures beyond DeepLabV3+
