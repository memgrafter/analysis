# BatchGFN: Generative Flow Networks for Batch Active Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.15058
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15058
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: BatchGFN matches performance of BatchBALD on synthetic tasks using a single forward pass per point

## Executive Summary
BatchGFN introduces a generative flow network (GFlowNet) approach to batch active learning, addressing the challenge of efficiently selecting diverse and informative query batches from large unlabelled pools. The method trains a GFlowNet to sample batches proportional to a batch reward function, such as joint mutual information, enabling principled batch construction without greedy approximations. Experiments on toy regression tasks show that BatchGFN achieves performance on par with computationally expensive batch-aware methods like BatchBALD while requiring only a single forward pass per point during inference.

## Method Summary
BatchGFN leverages a generative flow network trained to sample query batches from an unlabelled pool such that the probability of selecting a batch is proportional to a scalar reward function, such as joint mutual information (JMI). This allows principled construction of diverse, high-reward batches without the need for expensive greedy approximations or myopic acquisition strategies. The GFlowNet is trained on batches sampled from the unlabelled pool and can then be used at inference time to quickly generate new query batches in a single forward pass per data point. The method also introduces a lookahead training strategy, where the GFlowNet is trained across multiple acquisition steps, amortizing adaptation cost and improving sample efficiency.

## Key Results
- BatchGFN achieves performance comparable to BatchBALD on synthetic regression tasks
- Single forward pass per point during inference, versus multiple forward passes for BatchBALD
- Lookahead training reduces the number of iterations needed to adapt to changing reward distributions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
BatchGFN works by training a generative flow network to model the distribution over query batches proportional to a reward function. By using a GFlowNet, the method avoids greedy approximations and can efficiently sample diverse, high-reward batches in a single pass. The lookahead training strategy further improves efficiency by amortizing the cost of adapting the GFlowNet across multiple acquisition steps.

## Foundational Learning
- Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets): Stochastic policies for generating compositional objects; needed to model batch sampling distributions; quick check: can generate diverse, high-reward batches in a single forward pass
- Joint Mutual Information (JMI): Batch acquisition heuristic; needed to quantify batch informativeness and diversity; quick check: correlates with improved model performance
- Amortized Inference: Training over multiple acquisition steps; needed to reduce per-step adaptation cost; quick check: fewer training iterations required for stable performance

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Unlabelled pool -> GFlowNet -> Sampled batch -> Reward function (e.g., JMI) -> GFlowNet training loss

**Critical Path**
GFlowNet sampling -> Reward evaluation -> Policy update -> Next batch generation

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Reward function choice (JMI vs alternatives) affects both performance and computational cost
- Temperature hyperparameter controls exploration vs exploitation; too low causes training instability
- Amortized lookahead training trades off per-step adaptation cost for overall sample efficiency

**Failure Signatures**
- Low batch diversity despite high reward (overfitting to narrow reward modes)
- Training instability at low reward temperatures (vanishing gradients or mode collapse)
- Slow adaptation to changing reward distributions (insufficient lookahead training)
