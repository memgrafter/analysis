# OTOV2: Automatic, Generic, User-Friendly

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2303.06862
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06862
- **Reference count:** 40
- **Primary result:** Fully automated one-shot DNN training and compression framework achieving up to 83% FLOPs and 96% parameter reduction without fine-tuning

## Executive Summary
OTOv2 is a fully automated framework for training and compressing deep neural networks from scratch in one shot, without requiring fine-tuning. It introduces automated Zero-Invariant Group (ZIG) partitioning to identify minimal removal structures and Dual Half-Space Projected Gradient (DHSPG) optimization to reliably control sparsity. The framework achieves competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods across various DNN architectures including VGG, ResNet, CARN, ConvNeXt, DenseNet, and StackedUnets.

## Method Summary
OTOv2 automatically trains and compresses DNNs through three key components: automated ZIG partition that identifies minimal removal structures by analyzing computational dependencies in the network graph, DHSPG optimizer that enforces structured sparsity during training through dual half-space projection, and automated compressed model construction that removes zeroed structures and cleans up dependent parameters. The method works on various architectures from scratch without fine-tuning, requiring only dataset and target metrics as inputs.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 83% FLOPs reduction and 96% parameter reduction while maintaining accuracy
- Outperforms state-of-the-art methods on CIFAR10/100, DIV2K, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, and ImageNet
- Demonstrates effectiveness across diverse architectures including CNNs, super-resolution networks, and vision transformers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Automated ZIG partition eliminates manual dependency analysis by constructing a trace graph and growing connected components based on accessory, shape-dependent, and unknown vertices.
- Mechanism: The algorithm builds a graph where vertices represent operators, edges capture connectivity, and dependency is inferred by traversing and merging connected components. Accessory vertices are grouped with their dependent stem vertices; shape-dependent joints force mutual dependency; unknown vertices are conservatively excluded to maintain generality.
- Core assumption: The trace graph accurately represents the computational dependency of a DNN, and removing a connected component corresponds to a valid minimal removal structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "automatically exploits the dependency of general DNNs, partitions the trainable variables into Zero-Invariant Groups (ZIGs)"
  - [section] "we establish the trace graph (E, V) of the target DNN, wherein each vertex in V refers to a specific operator, and the edges in E describe how they connect"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related papers discuss automated partitioning but not the specific ZIG graph algorithm.
- Break condition: If a DNN uses custom operators not in the known list or has non-standard shapes, the dependency inference may miss valid ZIGs or incorrectly group unrelated variables.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Dual Half-Space Projected Gradient (DHSPG) reliably controls sparsity exploration by partitioning groups into penalized and non-penalized sets and enforcing descent in both objective and magnitude.
- Mechanism: Groups are split into Gp (top-K salience scores) and Gnp. For Gnp, standard SGD/Adam is used. For Gp, a subproblem with group-specific λg is solved, with λg chosen to ensure descent in both the objective and group norms. Half-space projection enforces sparsity without degrading performance.
- Core assumption: The salience score (e.g., cosine similarity) accurately predicts which groups can be zeroed without harming performance, and the dual-half-space constraint ensures progress toward both objectives.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "a novel optimizer to more reliably solve structured-sparsity problems"
  - [section] "we compute a negative subgradient of ψ as the search direction... To ensure... falls into the intersection between the dual half-spaces"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; similar constrained sparsity methods exist but not this dual-half-space formulation.
- Break condition: If salience scores are unreliable or λg tuning fails, the method may either prune too aggressively or not enough, harming accuracy or compression.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Automated compressed model construction reconstructs a valid DNN by removing structures corresponding to zeroed ZIGs and cleaning up dependent accessory parameters.
- Mechanism: After training yields x* with certain ZIGs zero, a depth-first traversal removes entire operator structures for zeroed groups and prunes accessory parameters that depend on them, preserving valid connectivity.
- Core assumption: The zeroed ZIGs correspond exactly to removable structures, and removing them does not invalidate subsequent layers or residual connections.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "constructs the compressed model; and (ii) Dual Half-Space Projected Gradient (DHSPG)"
  - [section] "we traverse all vertices with trainable parameters, then remove the structures in accordance with ZIGs being zero"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no direct citation of automated model reconstruction after pruning.
- Break condition: If ZIG identification is incorrect, removing structures may break the network topology, leading to runtime errors or incorrect outputs.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Trace Graph Extractor -> ZIG Partitioner -> DHSPG Optimizer -> Model Constructor -> ONNX/PyTorch Exporter

- Critical path:
  1. Input model → Extract trace graph via ONNX
  2. Partition into ZIGs → Train with DHSPG → Identify zeroed groups
  3. Remove zeroed structures → Export compressed ONNX model
  4. Optional: Convert back to PyTorch/TensorFlow

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Excluding unknown operators increases generality but may miss pruning opportunities
  - Salience-based Gp/Gnp split balances exploration and stability but requires careful λ tuning
  - Half-space projection adds runtime overhead but ensures sparsity without fine-tuning

- Failure signatures:
  - Model export fails → Trace graph missing required operators
  - Accuracy drops sharply → ZIGs incorrectly identified or λg poorly tuned
  - Compression ratio too low → Target group sparsity not achieved

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run on a small CNN (e.g., DemoNet) and verify automated ZIG partition matches manual analysis
  2. Train with DHSPG on VGG16-CIFAR10, vary K, and observe trade-off between sparsity and accuracy
  3. Export compressed model and confirm it runs correctly in ONNX runtime without fine-tuning

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can OTOv2 handle dynamic neural network architectures that change during training?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on static DNN architectures with predefined structures. It mentions Transformers have compatibility issues with PyTorch's ONNX optimization API, suggesting limitations with dynamic architectures.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not test or discuss dynamic architectures where the network structure evolves during training, such as neural architecture search during training or networks with adaptive connectivity.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments demonstrating OTOv2's effectiveness on networks with dynamic architectures, such as progressive neural networks or networks with learnable connectivity patterns.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does OTOv2's performance scale with extremely large models (e.g., GPT-scale)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The experiments include ResNet50 on ImageNet and BERT on Squad, but these are moderate-sized models. The paper mentions Transformers have compatibility issues but doesn't explore extremely large-scale models.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't test OTOv2 on models with billions of parameters or the computational challenges that arise at such scales.
- What evidence would resolve it: Scaling experiments showing OTOv2's performance, memory usage, and training time on models with 100B+ parameters, including comparisons with existing pruning methods at scale.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the group sparsity level K and the final model accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows empirical results of accuracy vs. group sparsity (Figures 6 and 5) but doesn't provide theoretical analysis of this relationship.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates the empirical Pareto frontier but doesn't explain why certain sparsity levels yield specific accuracy drops or whether this relationship is universal across different architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis or empirical studies showing how the group sparsity level K affects accuracy across different model families and whether there are universal principles governing this trade-off.

## Limitations
- Automated ZIG partition relies on ONNX operator registry completeness and may fail with custom operators
- DHSPG optimization requires careful tuning of salience threshold K and group sparsity levels
- Model reconstruction depends on accurate dependency tracking that may break for complex architectures

## Confidence
- **High confidence** in the overall framework's validity and the theoretical foundation of ZIGs and DHSPG
- **Medium confidence** in the automated aspects, particularly ZIG partition and model reconstruction, due to potential edge cases with custom operators
- **Medium confidence** in the DHSPG optimization's robustness across diverse architectures, as salience scoring and λg tuning are critical yet underspecified
