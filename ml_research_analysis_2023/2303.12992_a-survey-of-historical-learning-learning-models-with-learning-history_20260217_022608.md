# A Survey of Historical Learning: Learning Models with Learning History

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12992
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12992
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: First systematic review of historical learning in deep neural networks, categorizing approaches by data type, usage location, and storage form

## Executive Summary
This survey provides the first systematic review of "Historical Learning: Learning Models with Learning History," a paradigm that improves deep neural network performance by leveraging historical statistics during optimization. The paper comprehensively categorizes existing approaches into three dimensions: Historical Type (what data is used), Functional Part (where the data is used), and Storage Form (how the data is stored). The survey discusses relationships with related topics like ensemble learning and reinforcement learning, and identifies future challenges in the field, providing a valuable framework for understanding and advancing the use of historical information in deep learning.

## Method Summary
The paper systematically reviews and formulates the topic of historical learning by analyzing existing literature through a structured framework. It identifies three key aspects: Historical Type (gradients, model parameters, feature representations, predictions, and loss values), Functional Part (where historical data is applied during optimization), and Storage Form (moving averages or discrete elements). The survey synthesizes findings from various domains, particularly computer vision, to create a comprehensive taxonomy of historical learning approaches and their applications.

## Key Results
- Systematic categorization of historical learning approaches into three aspects: Historical Type, Functional Part, and Storage Form
- Comprehensive review of five types of historical data (gradients, parameters, features, predictions, loss) and their applications
- Discussion of relationships between historical learning and related fields including ensemble learning and reinforcement learning
- Identification of future challenges and opportunities in applying historical learning to NLP and other domains

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Historical statistics improve deep learning model performance through ensemble effects and gradient optimization.
- Mechanism: Historical data (predictions, features, parameters, gradients, loss values) is leveraged during optimization to enhance model performance through self-distillation, data augmentation, and optimization guidance.
- Core assumption: Historical statistics contain valuable information that can guide the learning process and improve model generalization.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "This survey systematically reviews and formulates the topic of 'Historical Learning: Learning Models with Learning History,' which aims to improve deep neural network performance by leveraging historical statistics during optimization."
- Break Condition: If the historical data becomes stale or irrelevant to the current training context, the benefits may diminish or become negative.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Moving averages and discrete elements are effective ways to store and utilize historical information.
- Mechanism: Two primary storage methods are discussed: Moving Average (MA) with momentum updates, and Discrete Elements (DE) with limited historical checkpoints, allowing efficient use of historical data.
- Core assumption: These storage methods effectively capture relevant historical information without excessive computational overhead.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The paper comprehensively categorizes existing approaches into three aspects: Historical Type (what data is used), Functional Part (where the data is used), and Storage Form (how the data is stored, either as discrete elements or moving averages)."
- Break Condition: If the storage method is inappropriate for the specific historical data type or computational resources, it may lead to suboptimal performance or infeasibility.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Historical learning is related to and can benefit from connections with recurrent networks, memory networks, ensemble learning, and reinforcement learning.
- Mechanism: The paper discusses how historical learning relates to these fields, noting differences in sequential vs. iterative information use and exploring potential integration opportunities.
- Core assumption: Related fields have developed techniques and insights that can be adapted to historical learning.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The discussions with related topics like recurrent/memory networks, ensemble learning, and reinforcement learning are demonstrated."
- Break Condition: If connections between historical learning and these fields are not properly understood or applied, it may lead to ineffective or counterproductive integration.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data Input Layer: Receives current training data
  - Historical Data Manager: Stores and retrieves historical statistics
  - Feature Extractor: Processes both current and historical data
  - Model Trainer: Updates model parameters using historical information
  - Performance Evaluator: Assesses model performance with and without historical data

- Critical path:
  1. Receive current training data
  2. Retrieve relevant historical statistics
  3. Process and combine current and historical data
  4. Update model parameters
  5. Evaluate performance and store new historical data

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Storage vs. Performance: More historical data can improve performance but requires more storage
  - Complexity vs. Benefit: More sophisticated historical learning techniques may offer marginal gains at the cost of increased complexity
  - Real-time vs. Batch Processing: Some historical learning techniques may require real-time processing, while others can be applied in batch mode

- Failure signatures:
  - Performance degradation when historical data is noisy or irrelevant
  - Increased training time without corresponding performance gains
  - Memory overflow due to excessive historical data storage

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can historical learning mechanisms be effectively adapted and applied to natural language processing tasks, given the significant differences in data format between images and text?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that most existing historical learning approaches focus on computer vision tasks, while natural language processing tasks have yet to heavily incorporate historical mechanisms. It also notes that the data format of language differs significantly from that of images, making it challenging to apply similar ideas from CV to NLP tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a promising opportunity but does not provide concrete solutions or methods for adapting historical learning mechanisms to NLP tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementation and evaluation of historical learning mechanisms in NLP tasks, demonstrating improved performance compared to existing methods.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective ways to utilize historical gradients of feature maps in deep learning models, beyond the current focus on model parameters?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the potential of historical gradients of feature maps, particularly in addressing long-tailed categories or abnormal samples. However, it notes that most studies have primarily focused on using historical gradients of model parameters through optimizers, leaving the potential of historical gradients of features largely unexplored.
- Why unresolved: While the paper acknowledges the potential of historical gradients of feature maps, it does not provide specific methods or techniques for effectively utilizing this information.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and evaluation of new methods that effectively incorporate historical gradients of feature maps into the training process.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can task-specific elements generated during training and inference in downstream vision tasks be effectively combined with learning history to improve model performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the potential benefits of incorporating task-specific elements, such as bounding boxes, keypoints, uncertainty, or other statistics, with learning history in downstream vision tasks like visual tracking, object detection, and segmentation. However, it notes that this area has not been widely explored.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a promising direction but does not provide concrete methods or techniques for combining task-specific elements with learning history.
- What evidence would resolve it: Successful implementation and evaluation of methods that combine task-specific elements with learning history in downstream vision tasks.

## Limitations

- Theoretical framework for relationships with other learning paradigms without concrete integration examples
- Focus primarily on vision tasks with limited discussion of NLP and other domains
