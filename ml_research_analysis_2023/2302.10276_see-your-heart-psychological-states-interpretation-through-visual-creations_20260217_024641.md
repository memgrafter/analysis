# See Your Heart: Psychological states Interpretation through Visual Creations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10276
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10276
- Reference count: 17
- One-line primary result: Proposed visual-semantic model achieves 81.3% improvement in CIDEr-D and 4.7% in BERTScore for interpreting psychological states in sandplay images

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Visual Emotion Interpretation Task (VEIT), a novel challenge requiring AI to generate interpretations of creator's psychological states embedded in visual creations. The authors present SpyIn, a professional-annotated multimodal dataset based on sandplay therapy works, and propose a visual-semantic combined model that achieves state-of-the-art results. The research demonstrates that VEIT requires multimodal information beyond visual features alone, including scene graph information and psychological knowledge, to accurately interpret psychological states in visual creations.

## Method Summary
The approach combines image features from a pretrained ResNet-101 with scene graph information and psychological knowledge embeddings using LSTM encoders. These multimodal features are concatenated and fed into an LSTM-based caption decoder with attention mechanism. The model is trained on the SpyIn dataset using standard captioning losses and evaluated using multiple metrics including CIDEr-D and BERTScore. The visual-semantic combined model outperforms baseline SAT (Show-Attend-Tell) models by leveraging the additional multimodal information.

## Key Results
- Proposed visual-semantic model achieves 81.3% improvement in CIDEr-D score and 4.7% improvement in BERTScore compared to baseline models
- VEIT is demonstrated to be a challenging task requiring scene graph information and psychological knowledge beyond visual features alone
- SpyIn dataset shows sandplay works are richer and more abstract on language, more diverse on visual content and emotion compared to existing datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: VEIT relies on multimodal information beyond visual features alone to interpret psychological states
- Mechanism: The model combines image features with scene graph information (location, orientation, relationships) and psychological knowledge (theme categories, miniature attributes) to capture context-dependent psychological meaning
- Core assumption: Psychological states in sandplay works depend on compositional and contextual relationships rather than isolated visual entities
- Evidence anchors: "The results indicate that VEIT is a more challenging task requiring scene graph information and psychological knowledge" and "VEIT is not only a simple visual-pattern-relied task, but need further information or external knowledge to assist"

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Abstract language and complex scene composition make VEIT harder than standard captioning
- Mechanism: Sandplay works contain more visual entities (31.9 vs 7.36), diverse categories (494 vs 80), and require abstract interpretation rather than concrete description
- Core assumption: Psychological interpretation requires abstract language and understanding of symbolic relationships
- Evidence anchors: "Sandplay works are widely used in psychotherapy" and "VEIT is a more challenging task" with analysis showing SpyIn is "richer and more abstract on language, more diverse on visual content and emotion"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Expert psychological annotation ensures ground truth quality and validity
- Mechanism: Professional psychotherapists frame expressive regions, interpret psychological states, and categorize themes according to psychological theory standards
- Core assumption: Expert interpretation captures creator's psychological state more accurately than creator self-report or non-expert annotation
- Evidence anchors: "We engage expert psychotherapists to annotate the collected sandplay works" and annotations meet "psychological assessment standards" with >70% accuracy and <25% false alarm rates

  - Why needed here: The task relies on relationships between visual entities (location, orientation) for psychological interpretation
  - Quick check question: What scene graph features would indicate a "wounding theme" versus a "healing theme"?

- Concept: Psychological theory basics
  - Why needed here: Understanding theme categories and miniature attributes is crucial for interpreting annotations
  - Quick check question: What distinguishes "wounding themes" from "healing themes" in sandplay therapy?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Image encoder (ResNet-101) → Scene graph encoder (LSTM) → Psychological knowledge encoder (LSTM) → Feature concatenation → Caption decoder (LSTM with attention)
- Critical path: Image features + Scene graph information + Psychological knowledge → Combined encoding → Caption generation
- Design tradeoffs: Complex multimodal fusion vs. simplicity; psychological accuracy vs. computational efficiency; expert annotation cost vs. dataset size
- Failure signatures: Poor performance on scenes with multiple miniatures; inability to distinguish psychological themes; over-reliance on visual features alone
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test baseline image captioning performance without any additional information
  2. Add scene graph information only and measure improvement
  3. Add psychological knowledge information only and measure improvement

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can AI systems be developed to interpret psychological states through visual creations more accurately?
- Basis in paper: The paper introduces VEIT and emphasizes the need for AI to generate reasonable interpretations of creators' psychological states through visual creations.
- Why unresolved: The current methods, including image captioning approaches, struggle to accurately interpret psychological states due to the complexity and abstractness of the task.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing new models that incorporate multimodal information, such as scene graph information and psychological knowledge, and evaluating their performance on datasets like SpyIn could provide insights into more accurate interpretations.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the key factors that contribute to the accuracy of psychological state interpretation in visual creations?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests that both scene graph related information and psychological knowledge information contribute to the performance of interpretation models.
- Why unresolved: The exact impact of different types of information on the accuracy of interpretations is not fully understood.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting ablation studies to isolate the effects of various types of information on model performance could help identify the key factors.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the challenge of interpreting psychological states in visual creations be addressed for different types of visual media, such as photographs and artworks?
- Basis in paper: The paper discusses the use of sandplay works and mentions the diversity of visual entities in SpyIn compared to other datasets.
- Why unresolved: Different types of visual media may require different approaches for accurate interpretation of psychological states.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing models on diverse datasets that include various types of visual media could provide insights into effective approaches for different media.

## Limitations

- Dataset Generalization: SpyIn is based on sandplay therapy, limiting generalizability to other visual creation types and cultural contexts
- Ground Truth Validity: Expert annotations follow psychological standards but lack inter-rater reliability scores and may contain expert bias
- Scene Graph Dependency: Model effectiveness depends on accurate scene graph extraction, which is not detailed in the paper

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Introducing VEIT as novel task and creating SpyIn dataset is well-supported
- **Medium confidence**: Multimodal fusion approach and performance improvements are demonstrated but depend on scene graph quality
- **Medium confidence**: Claim about multimodal requirements is supported by ablation studies but exact modality contributions need more precise quantification

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study Extension**: Conduct granular ablation study isolating scene graph versus psychological knowledge contributions, including individual psychological attribute analysis

2. **Cross-Domain Evaluation**: Test model on visual creations from different therapeutic modalities (art therapy, music therapy visualizations) to assess generalizability beyond sandplay works

3. **Human Evaluation**: Perform human studies comparing model interpretations with expert assessments across different cultural contexts and creator demographics to validate practical utility in real-world psychological assessment scenarios
