# Improving Adversarial Robustness of DEQs with Explicit Regulations Along the Neural Dynamics

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2306.01435
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01435
- **Reference count:** 36
- **One-line primary result:** Proposed methods significantly improve DEQ adversarial robustness, outperforming previous baselines and even surpassing strong deep network baselines under TRADES.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the problem of adversarial robustness for Deep Equilibrium (DEQ) models, which traditionally lag behind deep networks in this area. The authors propose two methods to improve robustness by exploiting DEQs' unique structural properties: reducing prediction entropy along neural dynamics and computing loss with random intermediate states during adversarial training. These methods explicitly regulate the entire neural dynamics rather than just the final equilibrium state. Extensive experiments on CIFAR-10 demonstrate significant improvements in adversarial robustness, with the combined approach achieving state-of-the-art results under both PGD-AT and TRADES frameworks.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two methods to improve DEQ adversarial robustness by regulating neural dynamics. First, during inference, prediction entropy is reduced by progressively updating inputs along the neural dynamics, stabilizing intermediate states against perturbations. Second, during adversarial training, loss is computed using random intermediate states rather than only the equilibrium state, forcing the model to maintain robust predictions throughout the entire trajectory. These methods leverage DEQs' unique structure where inputs are involved in each iteration and intermediate states can be seamlessly sent to the classification head. The approach significantly improves robustness on CIFAR-10 while maintaining DEQ's memory efficiency.

## Key Results
- Entropy reduction along neural dynamics improves DEQ robustness by stabilizing intermediate states
- Random intermediate state loss computation during adversarial training explicitly regulates entire dynamics
- Methods achieve state-of-the-art DEQ robustness, surpassing strong deep network baselines under TRADES
- Combined approach shows 2.99% improvement in AutoAttack accuracy over previous best DEQ baseline

## Limitations

- The proposed methods require additional computational overhead during both training and inference, which may limit practical applicability in resource-constrained scenarios.
- The effectiveness of entropy reduction depends on the specific correlation between prediction entropy and system stability, which may not hold uniformly across different architectures or datasets.
- The paper only validates the methods on CIFAR-10 with a specific DEQ architecture, leaving questions about scalability to larger datasets and more complex models.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The experimental results showing improved adversarial robustness under TRADES (e.g., 47.03% → 50.02% on AA) are well-documented and reproducible.
