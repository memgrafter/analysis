# Data Interpolants -- That's What Discriminators in Higher-order Gradient-regularized GANs Are

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00785
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00785
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Optimal discriminators in higher-order gradient-regularized GANs are polyharmonic RBF interpolants that improve convergence and stability

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes that optimal discriminators in higher-order gradient-regularized GANs (Poly-LSGAN and Poly-WGAN) solve polyharmonic RBF interpolation problems. The theoretical framework shows these discriminators satisfy iterated Laplacian PDEs with closed-form solutions, providing both theoretical insight and practical improvements in training stability and convergence speed. Experiments demonstrate significant gains on synthetic distributions and latent-space matching in WAEs, with PolyGAN-WAE achieving ~20% better FID scores than baselines.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes optimal discriminators under infinite-capacity assumptions, deriving that higher-order gradient penalties lead to polyharmonic RBF interpolation solutions. For Poly-LSGAN, weights require matrix inversion of potentially singular matrices, while Poly-WGAN weights simplify to ±1/(λ*N), avoiding singularity issues. The theoretical framework connects GAN training with polyharmonic interpolation theory, showing that optimal discriminators can be expressed as combinations of radial basis functions with specific smoothness properties determined by the gradient penalty order.

## Key Results
- Poly-WGAN with m≈⌈n/2⌉ converges 2-10× faster than WGAN variants and GMMNs on multivariate Gaussians
- Achieves Wasserstein-2 distances orders of magnitude lower than baseline methods
- PolyGAN-WAE improves FID by ~20% over baselines on image datasets (e.g., MNIST: 17.23 vs 21.65-25.05)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The theoretical analysis reveals that gradient-regularized GAN discriminators naturally learn to interpolate between data points in high-dimensional space. Higher-order gradient penalties enforce progressively smoother solutions, with the optimal discriminator satisfying polyharmonic PDEs whose solutions are known to be polyharmonic RBF interpolants. This mathematical structure provides both theoretical guarantees and practical benefits in training dynamics.

## Limitations
- Theoretical framework assumes infinite-capacity discriminators, which may not hold in practice
- Closed-form solutions assume perfect interpolation, but real training involves finite networks
- Experimental validation focuses on simple synthetic distributions rather than complex real-world data
- Improvements demonstrated primarily through indirect metrics rather than direct perceptual quality
