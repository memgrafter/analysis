# Delphic Offline Reinforcement Learning under Nonidentifiable Hidden Confounding

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01157
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01157
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Introduces delphic uncertainty to capture hidden confounding bias in offline RL, showing improved performance and expert preference over baselines when confounding strength increases

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of hidden confounding in offline reinforcement learning, where unobserved variables influence both actions and outcomes. The authors introduce delphic uncertainty as a new uncertainty measure that captures the variation across world models compatible with the observed data distribution. By training multiple world models with different confounder priors and measuring disagreement in their Q-value predictions, the method can identify actions affected by hidden confounders. This uncertainty estimate is then used to implement pessimism in offline RL, penalizing actions with high delphic uncertainty to reduce reliance on spurious correlations. Experiments on sepsis simulation and real ICU data demonstrate the approach outperforms standard offline RL methods when confounding is present.

## Method Summary
The method trains multiple world models with different confounder priors and architectures to estimate delphic uncertainty as the variance in Q-value predictions across these models. This uncertainty is combined with epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty estimates to form a complete uncertainty decomposition. The offline RL algorithm then applies pessimism by subtracting λ times the delphic uncertainty from Q-function Bellman targets, making the agent avoid actions with high model disagreement. The approach requires observational data with sufficient state-action coverage and access to the hidden confounders for evaluation purposes.

## Key Results
- Delphic uncertainty increases with confounding strength and cannot be reduced by more data, distinguishing it from epistemic uncertainty
- Expert clinicians prefer policies learned with delphic uncertainty in confounded settings compared to standard offline RL methods
- The method outperforms baselines like CQL and behavior cloning when hidden confounders affect the relationship between actions and outcomes
- Delphic uncertainty is highest for actions affected by hidden confounders, validating its role in capturing bias

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: World model training -> Uncertainty estimation -> Pessimistic RL -> Evaluation
- Critical path: Train world models → Estimate delphic uncertainty → Apply pessimism in RL algorithm → Evaluate policy performance
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More world models → Better delphic uncertainty estimate but higher computational cost
  - Stronger pessimism penalty → More robust to confounding but potentially overly conservative
  - Complex world model architecture → Better capture of confounding but harder to train
- Failure signatures:
  - Delphic uncertainty remains low despite known confounding → World models not capturing confounder effects
  - Performance degrades with delphic penalty → Uncertainty estimates not calibrated or penalty too strong
  - High variance across runs → Instability in world model training or uncertainty estimation

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we determine the optimal number of world models W needed to accurately estimate delphic uncertainty in practice?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that only a small number of world models (around 10 for the sepsis dataset) was sufficient to obtain an estimate of delphic uncertainty consistent with using a large number of world models.
- Why unresolved: While the paper found empirically that a small number of world models was sufficient, it does not provide theoretical guarantees or a principled method for determining the optimal number of world models needed for accurate uncertainty estimation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Theoretical analysis establishing bounds on the error in delphic uncertainty estimates as a function of the number of world models, or experimental results showing the convergence of delphic uncertainty estimates with increasing W.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does delphic uncertainty behave in settings with time-varying confounders, as opposed to the fixed confounders considered in the paper?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper's framework can theoretically accommodate time-varying confounders by treating them as part of the overall episode context, but the paper focuses on fixed confounders in its experiments.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide empirical results or theoretical analysis of delphic uncertainty in the presence of time-varying confounders, which are more realistic in many real-world applications.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing delphic uncertainty estimates and offline RL performance in settings with fixed versus time-varying confounders, or theoretical analysis of how delphic uncertainty captures bias from time-varying confounders.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the most effective way to implement pessimism with respect to delphic uncertainty in offline RL algorithms?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper proposes penalizing the Q-function Bellman target by subtracting a factor of the delphic uncertainty, but also discusses alternative approaches such as constraining value function updates or weighting samples in the objective function.
- Why unresolved: The paper's ablation study found that the proposed approach of penalizing the Bellman target performed best, but did not extensively compare all possible approaches to implementing pessimism with respect to delphic uncertainty.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive comparison of different approaches to implementing pessimism with respect to delphic uncertainty, including their performance on a range of offline RL tasks and their computational efficiency.
