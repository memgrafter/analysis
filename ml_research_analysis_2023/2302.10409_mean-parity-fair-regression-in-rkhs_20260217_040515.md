# Mean Parity Fair Regression in RKHS

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.10409
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10409
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposes a closed-form fair regression solution using RKHS that guarantees Mean Parity fairness and provides interpretable fairness-accuracy tradeoffs.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel approach to fair regression under Mean Parity (MP) fairness constraints by leveraging reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHS). The authors construct a functional space where all members inherently satisfy fairness requirements, enabling the derivation of a closed-form solution that naturally handles multiple sensitive attributes. The method is extended to address fairness-accuracy tradeoffs through a relaxed formulation, providing interpretable parameter tuning. Under mild assumptions, the approach also generalizes to covariance-based fairness criteria. Experimental results demonstrate competitive performance against state-of-the-art fair regression methods across benchmark datasets.

## Method Summary
The proposed method addresses fair regression by constructing a constrained functional space in RKHS where all functions satisfy Mean Parity fairness. The authors derive a closed-form solution for the optimal fair regressor by projecting the standard regression solution onto this fairness-constrained subspace. For the fairness-accuracy tradeoff, they introduce a relaxed formulation that interpolates between pure accuracy and pure fairness objectives. The method handles multiple sensitive attributes naturally through the RKHS construction. Additionally, the authors show that under certain conditions, the approach extends to covariance-based fairness criteria. The entire framework provides interpretable solutions where the fairness-accuracy tradeoff is controlled by a single regularization parameter.

## Key Results
- The closed-form solution guarantees Mean Parity fairness by construction in the RKHS framework
- The method naturally handles multiple sensitive attributes without modification
- Experimental results show competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art fair regression methods
- The relaxed formulation provides interpretable fairness-accuracy tradeoffs through a single parameter

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by constructing a functional space where fairness is guaranteed by design. In RKHS, functions are represented as linear combinations of kernel evaluations, and the fairness constraint (constant conditional mean with respect to sensitive attributes) translates to a linear constraint on the coefficient space. By projecting the unconstrained regression solution onto this constrained subspace, the method obtains a fair solution that minimizes the original loss within the fairness-restricted function class. The RKHS framework provides the mathematical machinery to perform this projection analytically, yielding the closed-form solution. The extension to multiple sensitive attributes follows naturally from the multivariate nature of RKHS, while the relaxed formulation adds a penalty term that balances fairness and accuracy through a tunable parameter.

## Foundational Learning

**Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS)**: A complete inner product space of functions where point evaluation is a continuous linear functional. Needed to provide the mathematical framework for representing and manipulating functions with built-in fairness constraints. Quick check: Verify that the kernel function used is positive definite.

**Mean Parity Fairness**: Requires the conditional expectation of the learned function to be constant across sensitive attribute values. Needed as the fairness criterion that drives the construction of the constrained functional space. Quick check: Confirm that the empirical mean difference across sensitive groups is near zero.

**Projection Theorem**: States that the closest point in a closed convex set to a given point is unique and satisfies certain orthogonality conditions. Needed to derive the closed-form solution by projecting the unconstrained solution onto the fairness-constrained subspace. Quick check: Validate that the residual is orthogonal to the constraint space.

**Dual Space Representation**: The relationship between a function space and its dual allows translating constraints on functions to constraints on coefficients. Needed to convert the functional fairness constraint into a tractable linear constraint in coefficient space. Quick check: Ensure the dual representation correctly captures the original constraint.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Data → Kernel Matrix Construction → Unconstrained Regression → Fairness Constraint Matrix → Closed-form Projection → Fair Regression Output

**Critical Path**: The critical computational path involves constructing the kernel matrix from training data, computing the unconstrained regression solution, forming the fairness constraint matrix from sensitive attribute information, and applying the projection operation to obtain the final fair regressor.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The method trades computational complexity (matrix inversions) for analytical tractability and guaranteed fairness. The choice of kernel function affects both fairness guarantee strength and predictive accuracy. The single regularization parameter simplifies tuning but may not capture more complex fairness-accuracy relationships.

**Failure Signatures**: Performance degradation occurs when the fairness constraint is too restrictive relative to data structure, leading to underfitting. Computational failure may arise with very large datasets due to kernel matrix size. The method may fail to capture complex fairness relationships if the kernel choice is inappropriate for the data structure.

## Limitations

- The approach is restricted to Mean Parity fairness and may not capture all fairness requirements in real-world applications
- Scalability with high-dimensional sensitive attribute spaces and large-scale datasets is not thoroughly analyzed
- The extension to covariance-based fairness relies on assumptions that are not fully specified or empirically validated
- Computational complexity may become prohibitive for very large datasets due to kernel matrix operations

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The theoretical derivation of the closed-form solution for MP fairness in RKHS is mathematically rigorous and well-established. The connection between MP fairness and functional space constraints is clearly proven.

**Medium Confidence**: The extension to covariance-based fairness and the relaxed fair regression framework are logically consistent but depend on assumptions that require more extensive empirical validation. Performance claims are based on benchmark comparisons but may not reflect all practical scenarios.

**Low Confidence**: The scalability analysis and computational complexity characterization for large-scale applications are insufficient, limiting confidence in practical deployment for big data scenarios.
