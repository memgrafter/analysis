# Multimodal Event Transformer for Image-guided Story Ending Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.11357
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11357
- Reference count: 22
- Key outcome: Achieves state-of-the-art performance on VIST-E and LSMDC-E datasets for image-guided story ending generation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a multimodal event transformer (MET) framework for image-guided story ending generation (IgSEG), which aims to generate coherent story endings based on given plots and an ending image. The MET framework leverages event-based reasoning through visual and semantic event graphs constructed from SRL parsing and scene graph parsing, respectively. Cross-modal fusion and a multimodal injector adaptively integrate features before passing them to the decoder. Experimental results demonstrate superior performance compared to strong competitors on benchmark datasets.

## Method Summary
The MET framework processes story plots through semantic role labeling to construct semantic event graphs, and processes ending images through scene graph parsing to construct visual event graphs. Separate relational graph convolutional networks (RGCNs) reason over each modality to mine implicit information. The whole image node from the visual graph connects to sentence nodes in the semantic graph, enabling cross-modal fusion via another RGCN layer. A multimodal injector with selective attention and gating then adaptively passes essential visual and semantic information to the decoder for story ending generation.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on VIST-E and LSMDC-E datasets
- Outperforms strong baseline models in automatic evaluation metrics
- Demonstrates effectiveness of event-based reasoning for multimodal story ending generation

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Event-based reasoning using semantic and visual event graphs captures implicit story context that improves story ending coherence.
- Mechanism: The model constructs semantic event graphs from SRL parsing of story plots and visual event graphs from scene graph parsing of the ending image. Relational Graph Convolutional Networks (RGCNs) then reason over these graphs to mine implicit information from each modality before fusion.
- Core assumption: Event graphs with relational edges preserve semantic dependencies and enable meaningful implicit information extraction through graph convolution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we construct visual and semantic event graphs from story plots and ending image, and leverage event-based reasoning to reason and mine implicit information in a single modality"
  - [section]: "we use semantic role labeling (SRL) to parse the story and extract events... For an ending image, we utilize scene graph parser to capture visual concepts and their relation to construct visual event graphs"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - related works focus on cross-modal fusion but not event-based reasoning in single modalities
- Break condition: If event extraction fails (e.g., SRL or scene graph parsing produces poor results), the graph reasoning cannot extract meaningful implicit information, leading to degraded performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Cross-modal fusion via connected event graphs enables better integration of visual and semantic features than simple concatenation.
- Mechanism: The model connects the whole image node from the visual event graph to the sentence nodes in the semantic event graph, then applies RGCN to fuse features across modalities while preserving graph structure.
- Core assumption: The whole image node contains sufficient summary information to bridge visual and semantic modalities when connected to sentence nodes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we connect visual and semantic event graphs and utilize cross-modal fusion to integrate different-modality features"
  - [section]: "we maintain graph structure for visual and semantic features and connect nodes that represent whole image and sentences... we utilize RGCN as Eq.2 to integrate cross-modal features"
  - [corpus]: Limited evidence - related works use attention-based models for fusion, not graph-based methods
- Break condition: If the connection between whole image and sentence nodes is too coarse-grained, fine-grained cross-modal dependencies may be lost, reducing the effectiveness of the fusion.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Multimodal injector with selective attention and gating adaptively combines visual and semantic features based on decoder needs.
- Mechanism: The injector uses selective attention to extract key information from visual and semantic features, then a gating mechanism controlled by a learned parameter λ determines how much of each modality to pass to the decoder.
- Core assumption: The gating parameter λ can effectively learn the optimal balance between visual and semantic information for each decoding step.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose a multimodal injector to adaptive pass essential information to decoder"
  - [section]: "we design a multimodal injector to integrate relevant features into the decoder... λ controls how much visual information is attended"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no clear corpus examples of similar multimodal gating mechanisms
- Break condition: If the gating mechanism fails to learn appropriate weights, the model may either ignore crucial information from one modality or become overly reliant on one source, degrading generation quality.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Semantic Role Labeling (SRL)
  - Why needed here: SRL extracts predicate-argument structures from story plots to construct semantic event graphs, providing the foundation for event-based reasoning
  - Quick check question: What are the main components extracted by SRL that form the nodes in the semantic event graph?

- Concept: Scene Graph Parsing
  - Why needed here: Scene graph parsing identifies objects and their relationships in the ending image to construct visual event graphs, enabling visual reasoning
  - Quick check question: How does the scene graph represent both objects and their relationships as edges in the visual event graph?

- Concept: Relational Graph Convolutional Networks (RGCN)
  - Why needed here: RGCN processes event graphs with multiple relation types, allowing the model to reason over complex event structures and capture implicit information
  - Quick check question: How does RGCN handle different relation types differently compared to standard graph convolution?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Story plots -> SRL parser -> Semantic event graph -> RGCN reasoning -> Cross-modal fusion -> Multimodal injector -> Decoder -> Story ending
  Ending image -> Scene graph parser -> Visual event graph -> RGCN reasoning -> Cross-modal fusion

- Critical path: Story plots → SRL → Semantic event graph → RGCN reasoning → Cross-modal fusion → Multimodal injector → Decoder → Story ending generation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Graph construction vs. direct feature extraction: Using event graphs adds complexity but captures richer implicit information
  - Separate vs. joint reasoning: Processing each modality separately before fusion allows modality-specific reasoning
  - Gating mechanism: Adds adaptive control but introduces additional parameters to learn

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor SRL or scene graph parsing results in sparse or incorrect event graphs
  - Ineffective cross-modal fusion if the whole image to sentence node connections are too coarse
  - Suboptimal gating leading to over-reliance on one modality
  - Training instability due to multiple loss components

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test event graph construction by visualizing semantic and visual event graphs on sample inputs
  2. Evaluate RGCN reasoning by comparing feature representations before and after reasoning on each modality separately
  3. Test cross-modal fusion by ablating the connection between whole image and sentence nodes to measure performance impact

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can the proposed model be extended to handle more complex story plots with multiple events and relationships between them?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the effectiveness of event-based reasoning for context understanding and subsequent event prediction. However, it does not explore the model's ability to handle complex story plots with multiple events and relationships.
- Why unresolved: The current model focuses on a single modality and does not explicitly address the challenge of handling complex story plots with multiple events and relationships.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further experiments and evaluations on datasets with more complex story plots, as well as comparisons with other models that can handle such complexity, would provide insights into the model's scalability and effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the multimodal injector be improved to better handle domain inconsistencies between visual and semantic features?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that features from different modalities suffer from domain inconsistency and proposes a multimodal injector to adaptively pass essential information to the decoder. However, it does not explore ways to improve the injector's ability to handle domain inconsistencies.
- Why unresolved: The current multimodal injector may not be optimal in handling domain inconsistencies, and further improvements could lead to better integration of cross-modal features.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies between the current multimodal injector and alternative methods that address domain inconsistency issues would provide insights into potential improvements.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the proposed method be adapted to handle different types of visual content, such as videos or interactive media?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on image-guided story ending generation and does not explore the model's ability to handle different types of visual content.
- Why unresolved: The current model is designed specifically for image-guided story ending generation and may not be directly applicable to other types of visual content.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments and evaluations on datasets with different types of visual content, such as videos or interactive media, would provide insights into the model's adaptability and effectiveness in handling diverse visual inputs.

## Limitations

- Performance bottlenecked by SRL and scene graph parsing accuracy
- Limited ablation studies to isolate component contributions
- Lack of detailed analysis on failure cases or edge scenarios
- Evaluation relies primarily on automatic metrics without comprehensive human assessment

## Confidence

- Event graph construction and reasoning: Medium - depends heavily on parsing quality
- Cross-modal fusion effectiveness: Medium - theoretical benefits demonstrated but limited ablation analysis
- Multimodal injector performance: Low - limited evidence of how the gating mechanism specifically contributes to improvements
