# Borrowing Human Senses: Comment-Aware Self-Training for Social Media Multimodal Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15016
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15016
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Comment-aware self-training with joint visual-lingual similarity significantly improves multimodal classification across four social media benchmarks

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of multimodal classification in social media, where image-text pairs often exhibit implicit relationships that hinder cross-modal understanding. To address this, the authors propose a novel approach called "comment-aware self-training" that leverages user comments from similar posts to provide hints and bridge the semantic gap between images and text. The method involves retrieving similar posts and their comments using a joint visual and lingual similarity measure, and then incorporating these comments into a self-training framework with a teacher-student model. The student model is trained to learn from both the pseudo-labels generated by the teacher and the hinting features offered by the retrieved comments. Extensive experiments on four multimodal social media benchmarks demonstrate that this approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art models, achieving improvements in accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score across various classification tasks. The results highlight the effectiveness of utilizing user comments and self-training to enhance cross-modal understanding in social media contexts.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a comment-aware self-training framework for multimodal classification in social media. The approach leverages user comments from similar posts to bridge the semantic gap between images and text. It involves retrieving similar posts and their consensus comments using a joint visual and lingual similarity measure, and then incorporating these comments into a self-training framework with a teacher-student model. The teacher model is trained on labeled data and generates pseudo-labels for similar unlabeled posts, while the student model learns from both the pseudo-labels and the hinting features provided by the retrieved comments. This framework enhances cross-modal understanding and improves classification performance, particularly when labeled data is scarce.

## Key Results
- Comment-aware self-training achieves significant improvements in accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score across four multimodal social media benchmarks
- The method outperforms state-of-the-art models by leveraging user comments to bridge implicit image-text relations
- Balancing visual and lingual similarity in post retrieval yields better results than using either modality alone

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Comments from similar posts provide context that bridges implicit image-text relations.
- Mechanism: User comments often mention key visual elements (e.g., "snow" in Figure 1), which helps models align weak cross-modal semantics by highlighting shared concepts.
- Core assumption: Human commenters naturally echo or emphasize important visual or textual elements, creating useful hints for models.

- Break condition: If comments become generic (e.g., "thank you") or unrelated due to poor retrieval, the hinting effect diminishes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Self-training with retrieved unlabeled posts improves robustness when labeled data is scarce.
- Mechanism: A teacher model trained on limited labeled data generates pseudo-labels for similar unlabeled posts; the student model learns from both labeled data and pseudo-labeled data, improving generalization.
- Core assumption: Similar posts share enough semantic structure that pseudo-labels are reliable for semi-supervised learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we explore how to leverage the retrieved comments in multimodal classification and exploit a self-training framework to identify comments' hints which shape the cross-modal understanding."
  - [section 3.4] "The teacher model is first trained with the labeled data and pseudo-label the similar posts with comments retrieved from the wild dataset."
  - [corpus] Weak; neighbor papers mention self-training but not in multimodal context with comment-aware retrieval.
- Break condition: If pseudo-labels are noisy or dissimilar posts are retrieved, student learning degrades.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Balancing visual and lingual similarity in retrieval yields better post selection than using either modality alone.
- Mechanism: The similarity score s_i = αs_Ii + (1-α)s_Ti combines image and text similarity, optimizing the trade-off based on dataset statistics.
- Core assumption: Both visual and textual cues contribute to semantic relevance; neither alone is sufficient.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2] "Post similarity is measured via balancing the effects of the image and text features."
  - [section 5.3] "We examine two ablations relying on the similarity in image (Only Image) and text (Only Text) in comparison to the full model trading-off image and text similarities (Image+Text)."
  - [corpus] Weak; neighbor papers discuss multimodal retrieval but not specifically the joint visual-lingual similarity trade-off.
- Break condition: If one modality is consistently more discriminative for a task, the balance may need adjustment.
