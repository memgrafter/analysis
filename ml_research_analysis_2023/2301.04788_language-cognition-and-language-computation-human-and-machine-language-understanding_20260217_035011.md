## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive survey of the relationship between language cognition and computation. It outlines how the two fields differ in their approaches: cognitive science focuses on the mechanisms of brain language processing using neuroimaging and behavioral methods, while computer science emphasizes building efficient models for practical language applications. The paper reviews research questions, methods, and challenges in both domains, and highlights the potential for integrating insights from both to advance human and machine language understanding. It proposes future research directions, including data-driven cognitive experiments, new cognitive mechanism hypotheses, and building more intelligent language models inspired by brain processes.

## Method Summary
This survey paper reviews existing research in both cognitive science and computer science, including neuroimaging data, behavioral experiments, and computational models. It synthesizes findings from these domains to identify opportunities for integration and proposes future research directions. The paper analyzes the differences between hypothesis-driven cognitive research and data-driven computational approaches, and suggests how combining these methods could lead to more human-like language understanding models.

## Key Results
- Language cognition and computation are distinct fields with different research approaches and goals
- Integrating cognitive science insights with computational modeling could lead to more human-like language understanding
- Current computational models lack interpretability and human-like learning capabilities compared to human cognition

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can computational models directly simulate the underlying neural mechanisms of brain language understanding, particularly at the single-neuron level?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that while computational models can predict brain activity, they don't directly simulate the underlying neural mechanisms at the single-neuron level.
- Why unresolved: Current methods lack the resolution to capture individual neuron activity and the computational models don't directly model this level of detail.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing new computational models that explicitly simulate single neuron activity and testing their predictions against high-resolution neural recordings would be needed.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do cognitive functions like attention and working memory interact with language processing in the brain, and can this be modeled computationally?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the importance of cognitive functions in language understanding but notes the difficulty in studying their interaction with language processing.
- Why unresolved: While behavioral and neural data exist, the complex interplay between cognitive functions and language processing is not fully understood, making it challenging to model computationally.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments that manipulate cognitive functions while measuring language processing, combined with computational models that integrate these functions, would be needed.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can computational models be made more interpretable and human-like, bridging the gap between machine and human language understanding?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the lack of interpretability in current computational models and the need for more human-like features.
- Why unresolved: Current models prioritize performance over interpretability, and the mechanisms behind their success are not well understood.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing models that can explain their decisions and learning from cognitive science research on human language understanding would be needed.
