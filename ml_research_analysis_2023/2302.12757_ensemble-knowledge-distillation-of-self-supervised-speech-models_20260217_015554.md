# Ensemble knowledge distillation of self-supervised speech models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12757
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12757
- Reference count: 0
- This work proposes multiple prediction heads for ensemble knowledge distillation of self-supervised speech models, improving performance and robustness

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an ensemble knowledge distillation (EKD) approach for self-supervised speech models using multiple prediction heads. Instead of averaging or concatenating teacher representations, the method assigns separate prediction heads to each teacher model (HuBERT, RobustHuBERT, WavLM). Experiments on SUPERB hidden-set tasks demonstrate that this approach outperforms single-teacher distillation baselines and improves robustness to unseen noisy environments while using one-third the parameters of an ensemble of three distilled models.

## Method Summary
The method uses three teacher models (HuBERT, RobustHuBERT, WavLM) to distill knowledge into a student model through multiple prediction heads. Each teacher's layer outputs are predicted by separate heads using L1 loss and cosine similarity. The approach is downstream-independent, meaning the distilled student can be applied to any task without retraining. The best configuration with all three teachers achieved top overall rank on SUPERB tasks while maintaining robustness to unseen noise.

## Key Results
- Multiple prediction heads outperform averaging or concatenating teacher representations
- Three-teacher ensemble achieved top overall rank (1st) on SUPERB hidden-set tasks
- Method improved robustness to unseen noisy environments while using one-third the parameters of an ensemble of three distilled models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Multiple prediction heads preserve complementary strengths of each teacher by learning distinct feature spaces without forcing them into a single averaged representation. This allows the student to effectively leverage HuBERT's general SSL performance alongside RobustHuBERT's noise robustness.

### Mechanism 2
Joint training on clean and noise-robust teachers enables the student to interpolate between clean and noisy feature representations, gaining generalized noise resilience without requiring additional noisy training data.

### Mechanism 3
Downstream-independent distillation works because the objective is based on teacher outputs rather than task-specific labels, allowing the student to retain all relevant teacher knowledge applicable to any downstream task.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Teacher models → Student encoder → Multiple prediction heads → Teacher-specific losses → Backward pass
- **Critical path**: Teacher → Student encoder → Multiple prediction heads → Teacher-specific losses → Backward pass
- **Design tradeoffs**: Multiple prediction heads vs. single averaged head (better performance but more parameters); Number of teachers (more improve robustness but increase complexity); Layer selection (layers 4,8,12 balances depth and cost)
- **Failure signatures**: Performance worse than single-teacher baseline (teacher conflicts or insufficient capacity); No robustness gain (teachers' strengths not complementary); High training instability (conflicting gradients)
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Baseline: Single teacher (HuBERT) distillation; 2) Two-teacher EKD: HuBERT + RobustHuBERT, test clean/noisy performance; 3) Three-teacher EKD: HuBERT + RobustHuBERT + WavLM, verify robustness gain

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How does the proposed multiple prediction heads approach compare to more sophisticated ensemble aggregation techniques (e.g., learned weighted averaging) in terms of performance and computational efficiency? The paper only compares basic averaging/concatenation methods to multiple prediction heads without exploring advanced aggregation techniques.

### Open Question 2
How does the proposed ensemble knowledge distillation method generalize to other self-supervised speech models beyond HuBERT, RobustHuBERT, and WavLM? Experiments are limited to these three specific models, leaving open questions about applicability to other SSL architectures.

### Open Question 3
What is the optimal number of prediction heads for the multiple prediction heads approach, and how does it scale with the number of teacher models? The paper uses one head per teacher but doesn't investigate whether this is optimal or how the approach scales with larger ensembles.

## Limitations

- Lack of detailed experimental hyperparameters (learning rate, batch size, training epochs)
- Claims about robustness improvements rely on comparisons to baselines without ablation studies isolating each teacher's contribution
- Downstream-independent nature stated but not empirically validated across diverse task types beyond SUPERB benchmark

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Multiple prediction heads outperform averaging/concatenation methods (supported by SUPERB results showing improved overall rank)
- **Medium Confidence**: Ensemble distillation improves robustness to unseen noisy environments (supported by clean/noisy performance comparisons but lacking detailed noise-type analysis)
- **Medium Confidence**: Downstream-independent distillation without retraining (theoretically sound but not experimentally validated across diverse task types)

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study**: Train students with each teacher individually and in pairs to quantify the marginal contribution of each model to overall performance and robustness gains

2. **Noise Characterization**: Test robustness across a broader spectrum of noise types and signal-to-noise ratios to validate generalization beyond the specific datasets used (Musan, WHAM!, DNS, CHiME3)

3. **Capacity Analysis**: Systematically vary student model size to determine the minimum capacity required for effective multiple prediction head learning without overfitting
