# Interactive System-wise Anomaly Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10704
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10704
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: InterSAD achieves ROC-AUC scores up to 0.999 on robotic and recommender systems, outperforming baseline methods in detecting anomalous systems.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces InterSAD, an interactive system-wise anomaly detection method that formulates systems as Markov decision processes and learns a policy to generate activation signals for distinguishing anomalous from normal systems. The approach combines an encoder-decoder for learning system embeddings with a policy network that generates effective activations through interaction. Experiments demonstrate superior performance on both robotic systems and recommender systems compared to baseline methods.

## Method Summary
InterSAD formulates system-wise anomaly detection as an interactive process where a policy network generates activation signals that interact with systems modeled as MDPs. The method employs an encoder-decoder to learn system embeddings from collected trajectories and rewards, while System Embedding Neutralization minimizes variance between normal system embeddings. Experience Replay Mechanism stabilizes training by storing historical interaction data in a replay buffer, allowing the model to learn from a mixture of samples across different time stages rather than relying solely on real-time interactions.

## Key Results
- Achieves ROC-AUC scores up to 0.999 on benchmark datasets
- Outperforms baseline methods in detecting anomalous systems across both robotic and recommender system domains
- Demonstrates effective separation between normal and anomalous system embeddings through learned policies

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: InterSAD learns a policy to generate activation signals that enable distinguishing anomalous systems from normal ones.
- Mechanism: The policy network generates activation signals that interact with systems to collect trajectories and rewards. By minimizing the system embedding neutralization loss, the policy encourages normal systems to respond consistently, making anomalous systems distinguishable.
- Core assumption: The activation signal generated by the policy can cause normal systems to exhibit consistent behaviors while anomalous systems remain distinguishable.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "learns a policy to generate activation signals that interact with systems to collect responses"
  - [section]: "We propose InterSAD which learns a policy and encoder-decoder for end-to-end system-wise anomaly detection"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus contains related anomaly detection papers but none specifically about interactive system-wise detection with MDP formulation.
- Break condition: If the policy fails to generate effective activation signals, normal systems may respond inconsistently, making anomalous systems indistinguishable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Experience Replay Mechanism (ERM) stabilizes training by providing more stable data distribution than real-time interactions.
- Mechanism: ERM stores tuples in a replay buffer and samples from it to update the encoder-decoder. This provides a smoothed transition of data distribution compared to non-stationary real-time interactions.
- Core assumption: The replay buffer provides a more stable data distribution than real-time interactions, leading to more stable training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we design a training method to stabilize the learning process, which includes a replay buffer to store historical interaction data and allow them to be re-sampled"
  - [section]: "To address the second challenge, we employ experience replay mechanism (ERM) to stabilize the training process"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence - corpus papers don't mention ERM in the context of system-wise anomaly detection.
- Break condition: If the replay buffer becomes too large or too small, or if the sampling strategy doesn't represent the current system state well.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: System Embedding Neutralization (SEN) improves anomaly detection by reducing variance in system embeddings.
- Mechanism: SEN minimizes the overall pairwise distance between system embeddings by updating the policy to minimize the sum of squared distances between system embeddings and the mean embedding.
- Core assumption: Reducing the variance in system embeddings will improve the ability to distinguish anomalous systems.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "a policy network to generate effective activation for separating embeddings of normal and anomaly systems"
  - [section]: "We propose System embedding neutralization (SEN) to minimize the overall pairwise distance between the embeddings of the N systems"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence - corpus papers don't mention embedding neutralization techniques.
- Break condition: If the embedding space is too high-dimensional or if the encoder-decoder fails to learn meaningful embeddings, SEN may not effectively reduce variance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Policy network (µ) → System Interaction → Trajectory/Reward Collection → Encoder-Decoder Training → Anomaly Scoring

- Critical path: Policy → System Interaction → Trajectory/Reward Collection → Encoder-Decoder Training → Anomaly Scoring

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Higher embedding dimension provides better abstraction but requires more training iterations
  - Longer interaction time steps provide more integrated system representation but increase computational cost
  - Larger replay buffer provides more stable training but requires more memory

- Failure signatures:
  - High variance in system embeddings indicates policy isn't generating effective activation
  - Slow convergence or divergence suggests replay buffer isn't providing stable data distribution
  - Low ROC-AUC indicates either policy or encoder-decoder isn't learning meaningful representations

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test policy generation: Verify the policy can generate activation signals and systems respond appropriately
  2. Validate embedding learning: Check if the encoder-decoder can reconstruct trajectories/rewards accurately
  3. Assess SEN effectiveness: Measure variance reduction in system embeddings during training

## Limitations
- The system-wise anomaly detection framework assumes systems can be effectively modeled as Markov Decision Processes, which may not hold for all real-world systems
- The effectiveness of System Embedding Neutralization relies heavily on the quality of the learned embeddings, which could fail if the encoder-decoder architecture cannot capture complex system behaviors
- Experience Replay Mechanism assumes historical data remains relevant for current system states, which may not hold in highly dynamic environments

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The fundamental approach of interactive anomaly detection through system interaction is sound
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of System Embedding Neutralization and Experience Replay Mechanism requires more empirical validation
- **Low confidence**: The generalizability of the method to systems that cannot be effectively modeled as MDPs
