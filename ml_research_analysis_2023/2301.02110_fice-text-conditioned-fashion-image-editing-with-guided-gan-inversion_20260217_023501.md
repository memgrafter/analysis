# FICE: Text-Conditioned Fashion Image Editing With Guided GAN Inversion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.02110
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02110
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes FICE, a novel text-conditioned fashion image editing approach that leverages GAN inversion and the CLIP model to edit images based on text descriptions.

## Executive Summary
FICE introduces a text-conditioned fashion image editing framework that optimizes latent codes within StyleGAN's extended latent space W+. The approach combines multiple constraints including semantic alignment via CLIP, pose preservation through DensePose, image composition using segmentation masks, and latent code regularization. The edited image is obtained by stitching relevant regions from the original and optimized images. Experimental results on VITON and Fashion-Gen datasets demonstrate superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods across multiple metrics including semantic relevance, identity preservation, pose preservation, and image fidelity.

## Method Summary
FICE employs an iterative GAN inversion approach in StyleGANv2's W+ latent space, initialized with an E4e encoder. The method optimizes a latent code using four key constraints: semantic alignment via CLIP-based image-text similarity, pose preservation through DensePose parsing of body parts, image composition guided by segmentation masks, and latent code regularization to maintain StyleGAN distribution. After optimization (typically 500 iterations with Adam learning rate 0.05), the final image is produced by stitching optimized regions with the original image using segmentation masks. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on VITON and Fashion-Gen datasets with CLIP score of 0.446, identity similarity of 0.926, IoU of 0.949, and FID of 60.96.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance with CLIP score of 0.446
- Maintains identity preservation with similarity score of 0.926
- Preserves pose with IoU of 0.949
- Produces high-fidelity images with FID of 60.96

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: CLIP-based semantic guidance aligns generated images with text descriptions.
- Mechanism: The CLIP model computes cosine similarity between image and text embeddings. During optimization, the latent code is adjusted to maximize this similarity, ensuring that generated images contain semantics matching the input text.
- Core assumption: CLIP embeddings capture the relevant semantic concepts from both images and text descriptions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We leverage the capabilities of the pretrained CLIP model to enforce the semantics, due to its impressive image-text association capabilities."
  - [section III-C] "Lclip = 1 − cos(C i(Ig), Ct(t)), where C i and C t represent the image and text encoder of the CLIP model, respectively."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus focuses on text-guided editing but doesn't specifically address CLIP's role in semantic alignment.
- Break condition: If CLIP embeddings don't capture the relevant semantic concepts or if the text descriptions are too complex for CLIP to understand.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pose preservation through DensePose constraints prevents unwanted changes to body structure.
- Mechanism: DensePose parses individual body parts from both input and generated images. The optimization process minimizes the L2 distance between these parsed representations, ensuring the pose remains consistent.
- Core assumption: DensePose can accurately parse body parts even with different clothing overlays.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we employ a sophisticated parsing model, i.e., DensePose [57], capable of parsing individual human body parts from the given input image."
  - [section III-C] "Lpose = 1/ND * sum(||Dj(I) − Dj(Ig)||^2), where ND is the number of parsed body parts, D is the pose parsing model and Dj(·) denotes the parsed mask of the j-th body part."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus mentions pose preservation but doesn't detail the DensePose implementation.
- Break condition: If DensePose fails to accurately parse body parts with complex clothing or if the clothing changes significantly alter body appearance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Latent code regularization ensures generated images remain within the StyleGAN distribution.
- Mechanism: The regularization loss minimizes the difference between the coarsest latent code and all other layers' latent codes. This encourages the optimized latent code to follow a similar distribution as original StyleGAN codes.
- Core assumption: StyleGAN's extended latent space W+ produces more realistic images than the original latent space W.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We furthermore propose a latent-code regularization technique that provides the means to better control the fidelity of the synthesized images."
  - [section III-C] "Lreg = 1/(Nw − 1) * sum(||w1 − wj||^2), where Nw denotes the number of individual codes."
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus mentions GAN inversion and latent code manipulation but doesn't specifically address regularization techniques.
- Break condition: If the regularization is too strong and prevents meaningful semantic changes, or too weak and allows unrealistic image generation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: StyleGANv2 generator (G) -> CLIP model (C) -> DensePose model (D) -> Segmentation model (S) -> E4e encoder (E)
- Critical path: Input image -> E4e encoder -> initial latent code -> optimization with CLIP, DensePose, and segmentation constraints -> final latent code -> StyleGANv2 generator -> output image
- Design tradeoffs: Semantic relevance vs identity preservation vs image fidelity; latent space choice (W vs W+); initialization method
- Failure signatures: Poor semantic alignment (low CLIP score), pose changes (low IoU), identity loss (low similarity), artifacts (high FID)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test semantic alignment by editing with text descriptions and measuring CLIP score
  2. Test pose preservation by editing with complex poses and measuring IoU
  3. Test identity preservation by editing with same person and measuring similarity score

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the choice of latent space (W vs W+) affect the semantic relevance and identity preservation in FICE?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the extended latent space W+ contributes towards higher semantic scores but requires careful initialization. The vanilla latent space W has limited expressive power, which adversely affects the semantic-relevance scores.
- Why unresolved: While the paper discusses the trade-offs between the two latent spaces, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how the choice of latent space impacts the balance between semantic relevance and identity preservation. Further investigation into this trade-off is needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study comparing the performance of FICE using W and W+ on various datasets, focusing on semantic relevance, identity preservation, and image fidelity, would provide insights into the optimal choice of latent space.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different style-mixing initialization strategies on the editing quality and consistency of FICE?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores an alternative initialization strategy using style-mixing, which improves semantic relevance but degrades other performance indicators like FID. It suggests that this approach can better infuse semantics that differ significantly from the original image.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents preliminary results but does not provide a thorough evaluation of different style-mixing initialization strategies. It is unclear how these strategies affect the overall editing quality and consistency across diverse input images and text descriptions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed comparison of FICE's performance using various style-mixing initialization strategies on a wide range of images and text descriptions, along with a user study to assess the visual quality and consistency of the results, would provide valuable insights.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can FICE be adapted to handle text descriptions with detailed objects or logos on garments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that FICE struggles with text descriptions containing detailed objects or logos, such as a skull graphic or a cathedral. It attributes this limitation to the constraints of CLIP and the GAN synthesis capabilities.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but does not propose a concrete solution to address it. Extending the GAN training dataset and fine-tuning CLIP on fashion-specific datasets are suggested but not explored in detail.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating a modified version of FICE that incorporates additional training data or a fine-tuned CLIP model specifically for fashion images with detailed objects or logos would demonstrate the feasibility of improving performance in this area.

## Limitations
- Performance may degrade with more diverse real-world fashion imagery beyond controlled datasets
- Reliance on DensePose could limit effectiveness with non-standard poses or heavily occluded body parts
- Stitching approach may introduce visible artifacts at region boundaries, particularly with complex clothing patterns
