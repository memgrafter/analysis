# Discourse over Discourse: The Need for an Expanded Pragmatic Focus in Conversational AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14543
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14543
- Reference count: 27
- Primary result: Current conversational AI systems exhibit significant pragmatic limitations, particularly in conversation summarization, due to insufficient incorporation of relevance theory and external context

## Executive Summary
This paper identifies critical pragmatic limitations in contemporary conversational AI applications, arguing that systems fail to generate contextually appropriate responses despite syntactic and semantic correctness. The authors introduce "Turing Test Triggers" (TTTs) - examples of pragmatically inappropriate content that highlight how current systems struggle with preserving meaning and incorporating external context. Through comprehensive analysis of conversation summarization and other conversational AI applications, the paper demonstrates that these failures stem from insufficient attention to relevance theory and the practical aspects of meaning in context.

## Method Summary
The paper provides a theoretical analysis of pragmatic limitations in conversational AI, drawing on relevance theory and examples from various conversational AI applications. Rather than proposing a specific machine learning method, the authors systematically identify and categorize pragmatic failures through qualitative analysis of existing systems. The approach involves examining conversation summarization models, chatbots, voice assistants, and mental health chatbots to illustrate how systems fail at preserving meaning, incorporating external context, and maintaining discourse coherence across multiple turns.

## Key Results
- Current conversational AI systems fail to preserve meaning in conversation summarization, often omitting crucial information or misrepresenting the original dialogue
- Systems lack awareness of external context and general world knowledge, leading to irrelevant or inappropriate responses
- Conversation summarization serves as a clear case study illustrating how discourse-level pragmatics are currently inadequately addressed in AI systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Relevance theory explains why current conversational AI systems fail at generating pragmatically appropriate content
- Mechanism: Systems lack awareness of what information users already know and what additional processing effort would be required to understand responses
- Core assumption: Users determine relevance based on processing effort and information gain in their specific context
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We illustrate the importance of pragmatics with so-called star sentences, syntactically acceptable propositions that are pragmatically inappropriate in conversation or its summary"
  - [section] "Relevance is necessarily determined in context. For a given stimulus to be relevant in the prevailing context, it must be worth the recipient's processing effort and be the most relevant stimulus available"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus focuses on broader pragmatics literature rather than specific relevance theory applications
- Break condition: When users have different processing capabilities or contextual knowledge than assumed by the system

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Conversational AI systems fail to maintain coherent discourse representations across multiple turns
- Mechanism: Systems lack memory for topics, speakers, and conversational context that human partners naturally maintain
- Core assumption: Human conversation relies on shared mental representations that evolve throughout dialogue
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Building on impressive progress in both semantics and syntax, pragmatics concerns meaning in the practical sense"
  - [section] "Speakers use and update these representations throughout conversation to understand their partners utterances and make relevant follow-up contributions"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus mentions discourse pragmatics but lacks specific empirical data on turn-taking failures
- Break condition: When conversations require tracking complex dependencies or correcting prior misunderstandings

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: External context awareness is critical for generating appropriate conversational responses
- Mechanism: Systems need knowledge about physical environment, user location, and general world knowledge to provide relevant answers
- Core assumption: Conversational appropriateness depends on integrating linguistic content with situational factors
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The summarization of conversation, a case of discourse over a discourse, clearly illustrates a series of pragmatic limitations in contemporary conversational AI applications"
  - [section] "When speakers engage in conversation with others, they expect that their conversation partners are aware of salient features of their shared external context"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - corpus includes work on context-aware systems but limited on external knowledge integration
- Break condition: When contextual information is ambiguous or requires reasoning about unstated assumptions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can conversational AI systems effectively incorporate external context, such as location and conceptual knowledge, to generate more relevant and appropriate responses?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper discusses the importance of external context and provides examples of how conversational AI systems often fail to incorporate this information, leading to inappropriate responses
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the problem but does not provide a comprehensive solution for integrating external context into conversational AI systems
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating the effectiveness of various approaches to incorporating external context, such as knowledge graphs or lexicons, in improving the relevance and appropriateness of conversational AI responses

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective methods for detecting and resolving inconsistencies in conversational AI systems, such as conflicting information provided by the user or system?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper discusses the issue of inconsistent details and suggests that conversational AI systems need the ability to detect and resolve these inconsistencies to generate appropriate responses
- Why unresolved: While the paper mentions the problem and suggests possible approaches, it does not provide a definitive solution for inconsistency resolution in conversational AI systems
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies evaluating the performance of different inconsistency detection and resolution methods in conversational AI systems, demonstrating their effectiveness in generating appropriate responses

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can conversational AI systems be designed to better account for user expectations and preferences regarding the sharing of personal information and privacy concerns?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper acknowledges the ethical considerations and privacy concerns associated with incorporating external context into conversational AI systems, and suggests that users should have control over the information they share
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights the importance of addressing user privacy concerns but does not provide a detailed framework for designing conversational AI systems that balance user expectations and preferences with the need for external context
- What evidence would resolve it: User studies and surveys examining user preferences and expectations regarding privacy and information sharing in conversational AI systems, along with the development and evaluation of design guidelines or frameworks that address these concerns

## Limitations

- Theoretical framework lacks systematic empirical validation across diverse conversational contexts
- Turing Test Triggers concept is subjective and lacks objective operational criteria
- Computational complexity of implementing relevance theory and common ground tracking in real-time systems is not addressed

## Confidence

- High confidence: The paper correctly identifies that current conversational AI systems struggle with pragmatic appropriateness and that these limitations are well-documented in the literature
- Medium confidence: The proposed theoretical framework (relevance theory, common ground, nonmonotonic reasoning) provides a reasonable explanation for observed failures, though empirical validation is limited
- Low confidence: The specific recommendations for improvement and the claim that addressing these theoretical concerns will resolve the identified issues in practice
