# Did the Models Understand Documents? Benchmarking Models for Language Understanding in Document-Level Relation Extraction

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.11386
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11386
- Reference count: 34
- State-of-the-art models in DocRE rely on spurious correlations rather than human-like rationales

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes the decision rules of state-of-the-art models in document-level relation extraction (DocRE) by proposing human-annotated word-level evidence (DocREDHWE). The study reveals that models rely heavily on spurious correlations—such as entity names, fixed positions, and non-causal tokens—rather than human-like rationales. To expose these weaknesses, the authors design RE-specific attacks (e.g., masked evidence, entity name manipulation) and demonstrate severe performance drops under such attacks. They introduce mean average precision (MAP) as a metric to evaluate understanding and reasoning capability, finding that models with higher MAP show better robustness and generalization. The work highlights the need to align model decision rules with human reasoning for trustworthy real-world deployment.

## Method Summary
The study uses two representative DocRE models (DocuNet and ATLOP variants) trained on the DocRED dataset. Feature attribution is performed using Integrated Gradients to identify crucial words in model predictions. The authors propose DocREDHWE, a human-annotated dataset with word-level evidence for relational facts. RE-specific attacks (masked evidence, antonym/synonym substitution, entity name manipulation) are applied to test model robustness. Evaluation metrics include F1-score and the newly introduced MAP score, which measures overlap between model-attributed important words and human-annotated evidence.

## Key Results
- Models achieve high F1-scores but low MAP scores, indicating reliance on spurious correlations rather than human-like reasoning
- Position embeddings create positional bias, with tokens in certain positions affecting predictions more significantly than others
- Under masked evidence attacks, performance drops severely, with no more than 21% of predictions changing even when evidence is removed

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Models rely on spurious correlations rather than human-like rationales.
- Mechanism: During training, models learn high-frequency function tokens and entity names as shortcuts to predict relations, bypassing actual reasoning over context.
- Core assumption: Spurious correlations are more frequent than true causal evidence in the training corpus.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the representative state-of-the-art (SOTA) models in DocRE exhibit different decision rules."
  - [section] "The visualization indicates that models learn abundant spurious correlations (e.g., entity names and irrelevant words) to minimize the training error."
  - [corpus] Found 25 related papers (using 8). Average neighbor FMR=0.514, average citations=0.0.
- Break condition: If training data is perfectly balanced between spurious and causal features, or if regularization explicitly penalizes reliance on non-causal tokens.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Position embeddings bias models toward certain document positions.
- Mechanism: Position embeddings are trained without supervision and become correlated with relation occurrence patterns, causing models to favor tokens in certain positions.
- Core assumption: The model's position embeddings are learned independently of the actual semantic content.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "tokens in certain positions will affect final predictions more significantly than the words in other positions."
  - [section] "models will discriminate words according to their positions in a document, even though the annotated rationales are almost uniformly distributed across the documents."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence for position bias in corpus.
- Break condition: If position embeddings are replaced with learned content-based positional encodings or if data augmentation breaks positional patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Models fail under RE-specific attacks due to lack of true understanding.
- Mechanism: Models cannot recover correct predictions when human-annotated evidence is masked, synonyms/antonyms are substituted, or entity names are altered, indicating reliance on shallow patterns.
- Core assumption: The model's decision process depends on surface-level cues rather than deep semantic reasoning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we next demonstrate that the significant discrepancy in decision rules between models and humans severely damages the robustness of models."
  - [section] "Under the masked word-level evidence attacks, the evidence supporting the relational facts is removed... no more than 21% of predictions is even changed."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence for attack robustness in corpus.
- Break condition: If the model is retrained with explicit reasoning supervision or if adversarial training improves robustness to these attacks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would models perform on DocREDHWE if they were explicitly trained to rely on human-annotated word-level evidence?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that models currently rely on spurious correlations rather than human-like rationales, and introduces MAP as a metric to evaluate understanding.
- Why unresolved: The paper analyzes existing models' decision rules but does not explore whether models can be trained to align their decision rules with human-annotated evidence.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training models using human-annotated word-level evidence as supervision and comparing their MAP scores and robustness to current models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would the performance of models trained on DocREDHWE generalize better to other relation extraction tasks compared to models trained on standard DocRED?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that models trained on DocRED struggle with understanding and reasoning, and suggests that guiding models to learn from human evidence could improve robustness.
- Why unresolved: The paper evaluates models on DocREDHWE and DocREDScratch but does not test their performance on entirely different datasets or tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluating models trained on DocREDHWE on other RE datasets or tasks and comparing their performance to models trained on standard DocRED.

### Open Question 3
- Question: To what extent do the spurious correlations learned by models depend on the specific pre-training corpus used, and would changing the corpus reduce these correlations?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests that pre-training introduces selection bias, leading to spurious correlations between high-frequency function tokens and current tokens.
- Why unresolved: The paper analyzes the impact of pre-training on spurious correlations but does not experiment with different pre-training corpora to isolate this effect.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training models with different pre-training corpora and measuring changes in their reliance on spurious correlations using MAP and robustness to attacks.

## Limitations
- Findings are based on DocRED dataset and may not generalize to other relation extraction tasks
- Human-annotated word-level evidence represents a relatively small subset of the full DocRED corpus
- RE-specific attacks may not capture all forms of model brittleness or reasoning failures

## Confidence
- High: The observation that models achieve high F1-scores but low MAP scores is well-supported by experimental results
- Medium: The claim that position embeddings create positional bias is supported but requires further validation across different architectures
- Low: The extent to which identified spurious correlations represent fundamental limitations versus training artifacts remains unclear

## Next Checks
1. Apply the same attribution and attack methodology to other DocRE datasets (e.g., CDR, GDA) to verify whether spurious correlation patterns are dataset-specific or represent a broader phenomenon.

2. Systematically remove or modify position embeddings in both DocuNet and ATLOP models to quantify the exact contribution of positional bias to performance drops under attacks.

3. Track the evolution of spurious correlations and MAP scores throughout model training to identify at which training stages models begin to rely on non-causal features.
