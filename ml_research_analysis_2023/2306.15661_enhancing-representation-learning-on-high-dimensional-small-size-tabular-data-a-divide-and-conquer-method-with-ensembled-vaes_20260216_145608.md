# Enhancing Representation Learning on High-Dimensional, Small-Size Tabular Data: A Divide and Conquer Method with Ensembled VAEs

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2306.15661
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15661
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Key outcome**: EnVAE achieves up to 11% improvement in downstream classification accuracy on biomedical HDLSS datasets compared to β-VAE, CAE, and SubTab baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning good latent representations from High-Dimensional, Low Sample Size (HDLSS) tabular data. The authors propose EnVAE, a divide-and-conquer method that uses an ensemble of lightweight VAEs to learn posteriors over subsets of the feature space. These posteriors are then aggregated into a joint posterior using a Mixture-of-Product-of-Experts function. The authors hypothesize that this approach induces implicit data augmentation, improving sample efficiency and robustness to overfitting. Experiments on eight real-world biomedical datasets show that EnVAE consistently outperforms standard baselines in terms of downstream classification accuracy, achieves improvements of up to 11%, and learns more disentangled representations as measured by lower Total Correlation.

## Method Summary
EnVAE is a divide-and-conquer approach for representation learning on HDLSS tabular data. The method splits high-dimensional features into m disjoint groups, trains a lightweight VAE on each group to learn a posterior distribution, and aggregates these posteriors using a Mixture-of-Product-of-Experts (MoPoE) function to form a joint posterior. The joint posterior is then used to reconstruct the full input through all decoders. The model is trained using a modified ELBO objective that averages over all possible subsets of feature groups. This architecture reduces the effective dimensionality per VAE, making learning more tractable in low-sample regimes, and the MoPoE aggregation induces implicit data augmentation by reconstructing complete samples from multiple partial views.

## Key Results
- EnVAE achieves up to 11% improvement in downstream classification accuracy compared to β-VAE, CAE, and SubTab baselines on biomedical HDLSS datasets
- EnVAE learns more disentangled representations, as evidenced by lower Total Correlation values across most datasets
- The method demonstrates robustness to missing features at inference time without requiring retraining
- Performance improvements scale with the number of experts (m), with diminishing returns observed as m increases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** The Mixture-of-Product-of-Experts (MoPoE) posterior decomposition induces implicit data augmentation by computing joint posteriors over all possible subsets of feature groups.
- **Mechanism:** The ELBO is computed as an average over all 2^m - 1 subsets of feature groups, where each subset A reconstructs the full sample xxx. This means the model is trained to reconstruct the same input from multiple different partial views of the data.
- **Core assumption:** Learning to reconstruct a complete sample from various subsets of its features improves sample efficiency and generalization, especially in low-sample regimes.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Specifically, we present an alternative factorisation of the joint posterior that induces a form of implicit data augmentation that yields greater sample efficiency."
  - [section] "By substituting Equation 4 into the ELBO expression from Equation 3 we can reinterpret the objective as: ELBO(xxx) = 1/|P(Nm)| ΣA∈P(Nm) Ezzz∼qϕ(zzz|xxxA) [Σi log pθ(xxxi|zzz)] - KL(qϕ(zzz|xxx)||pθ(zzz))"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations found for this specific MoPoE-VAE implicit augmentation claim.
- **Break condition:** If feature groups are highly correlated or if the subsets don't provide meaningfully different views of the data, the implicit augmentation effect may be minimal or even harmful.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Dividing high-dimensional features into smaller subsets reduces the effective dimensionality per VAE, making learning more tractable in HDLSS settings.
- **Mechanism:** Each lightweight VAE in the ensemble only needs to learn a posterior over a subset of features (with reduced dimensionality), rather than the full high-dimensional space. This decreases the complexity of each instance and makes the learning problem more tractable.
- **Core assumption:** Learning good global representations from subsets of features will lead to latent representations that generalize better to new data.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "We hypothesise that using a divide-and-conquer approach to perform dimensionality reduction is more tractable since (i) learning good global representations from subsets of features will lead to latent representations that generalise better to new data and ii) reducing the dimensionality / samples ratio intuitively decreases the complexity of each instance."
  - [section] "We propose a novel approach to representation learning for tabular data, Ensemble-VAE (EnVAE), which trains an ensemble of lightweight VAEs on smaller subsets of features."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations found for this specific dimensionality reduction claim.
- **Break condition:** If feature subsets are not informative or if important interactions between features are lost when splitting, the approach may underperform.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The divide-and-conquer approach improves disentanglement of learned representations, as measured by lower Total Correlation (TC).
- **Mechanism:** By learning separate posteriors for different feature subsets and aggregating them, the model may learn more independent factors of variation. Lower TC indicates that the dimensions of the latent representation are more independent from each other.
- **Core assumption:** Better disentanglement leads to higher-quality representations that improve downstream task performance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Furthermore, we verify that our approach has a positive effect on disentanglement and achieves a lower estimated Total Correlation on learnt representations."
  - [section] "Our analysis reveals that in almost every case, regardless of the model used, latent representations learnt in the low-sample setting exhibit higher TC values than in the full-sample setting. Importantly, for most datasets we observe that the TC values generally decrease as the number of experts is increased and eventually plateau."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct citations found for this specific TC improvement claim.
- **Break condition:** If the feature grouping strategy doesn't align with natural factors of variation in the data, or if the MoPoE aggregation introduces dependencies, TC may not improve.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input features D → Split into m disjoint groups {xxx1, ..., xxxm} → Each group xxxi → Dedicated encoder Enci → Computes posterior qϕ(zzz|xxxi) → All subset posteriors qϕ(zzz|xxxA) for A ∈ P(Nm) → MoPoE aggregation → Joint posterior qϕ(zzz|xxx) → Joint posterior zzz → All decoders Deci → Reconstructions {ˆxxx1, ..., ˆxxxm} → Loss: Reconstruction loss + KL divergence regularization

- **Critical path**: Input → Feature splitting → Per-group encoding → MoPoE posterior aggregation → Sampling → Per-group decoding → Loss computation → Backpropagation

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Number of experts m vs. computational cost: More experts provide better implicit augmentation but increase computation
  - Feature grouping strategy: Random vs. informed grouping affects performance
  - Encoder/decoder architecture: Shared vs. separate networks per expert

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Poor reconstruction quality: Check if feature groups are too small or if encoders/decoders are underparameterized
  - Unstable training: Check if the number of experts is too large relative to dataset size
  - No improvement over baseline: Check if feature groups are not meaningfully different or if MoPoE aggregation is not properly implemented

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Verify basic functionality: Train EnVAE with m=2 on a simple synthetic dataset and check that reconstructions are reasonable
  2. Compare to baseline: Train EnVAE vs. standard VAE on a small tabular dataset and verify improved performance
  3. Ablation study: Train EnVAE with different numbers of experts (m=2,4,6,8) and observe the effect on performance and Total Correlation

- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on comparing EnVAE to standard VAE baselines and SubTab, without exploring other aggregation methods.

## Limitations

- The implicit data augmentation mechanism through MoPoE aggregation lacks direct empirical validation and ablation studies
- The impact of different feature grouping strategies on performance is not explored, which could be critical for datasets with structured feature dependencies
- The mechanism relies on indirect evidence and lacks explicit ablation studies or theoretical justification for the MoPoE aggregation benefits

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The experimental results showing EnVAE outperforming baselines on classification accuracy and achieving lower Total Correlation are well-supported by the provided tables and figures. The reproducibility plan is sufficiently detailed.
- **Medium Confidence**: The divide-and-conquer approach reducing per-expert dimensionality and improving tractability is plausible and aligns with established dimensionality reduction principles, though direct evidence is limited.
- **Low Confidence**: The mechanism of implicit data augmentation through MoPoE aggregation is the most speculative claim, as it relies on indirect evidence and lacks explicit ablation studies or theoretical justification.

## Next Checks

3. **Direct Augmentation Test**: Create a modified EnVAE that explicitly applies data augmentation (e.g., feature dropout, noise injection) to the input subsets and compare its performance to the original MoPoE version to determine whether the observed benefits are primarily due to implicit augmentation or other factors like reduced dimensionality.
