# On the Robustness of Generative Retrieval Models: An Out-of-Distribution Perspective
## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12756
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12756
- Reference count: 39
- Key outcome: Generative retrieval models suffer significant performance degradation in out-of-distribution (OOD) settings, particularly for query variations and unforeseen query types, with pre-trained CorpusBrain showing better generalizability than BART.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the out-of-distribution robustness of generative retrieval models, which directly generate document identifiers rather than retrieving them via embedding similarity. The authors define OOD robustness across three perspectives: query variations (misspellings, paraphrasing), unforeseen query types (shifts across datasets), and unforeseen tasks (mixing datasets within tasks). Through extensive experiments on the KILT benchmark using DPR, BART, and CorpusBrain models, the study reveals that generative retrieval models exhibit significant performance degradation in OOD scenarios. CorpusBrain's pre-training with retrieval-specific tasks (ISS, LPS, HIP) provides better generalizability than BART's standard pretraining, though all models struggle with certain query variation types. The findings highlight critical limitations in current generative retrieval approaches and suggest directions for improving their robustness.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three retrieval models (DPR, BART, CorpusBrain) on KILT benchmark datasets across 5 tasks using R-precision as the primary metric. OOD robustness is measured by DR_OOD = (P_OOD - P_IID) / P_IID, comparing performance on OOD test sets versus I.I.D. test sets. Four types of query variations (misspelling, naturality, order, paraphrasing) are synthetically generated. Unforeseen query types are evaluated through cross-dataset testing within open-domain QA tasks. Unforeseen tasks are assessed by mixing datasets within each task category. CorpusBrain incorporates pre-training tasks (ISS for semantic granularity modeling, LPS, HIP) designed to encode corpus information, while BART uses standard pretraining and DPR represents dense retrieval.

## Key Results
- Generative retrieval models show significant performance degradation in OOD scenarios, with DR_OOD values indicating vulnerability to query variations and unforeseen query types
- CorpusBrain demonstrates better generalizability than BART in most OOD settings due to its pre-training with retrieval-specific tasks
- All generative models perform particularly poorly on misspelling and word order variations, likely due to their autoregressive generation process
- Dense retrieval models maintain better performance across unforeseen query types compared to generative models
- The performance gap between models varies across different OOD perspectives and task types

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we effectively mitigate the performance degradation of generative retrieval models in OOD settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper concludes that generative retrieval models require enhancement in OOD robustness and suggests this as a key area for future research.
- Why unresolved: While the paper identifies the issue, it does not propose specific solutions or mitigation strategies to address the performance drop in OOD scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and evaluating new techniques or architectures that improve OOD robustness, followed by empirical studies showing reduced performance degradation in query variations, unforeseen query types, and unforeseen tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the specific characteristics of query variations that most negatively impact generative retrieval models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper finds that generative retrieval models perform poorly on query variations, particularly with misspelling and order changes, but does not delve into why these variations are especially challenging.
- Why unresolved: The paper observes the negative impact but does not analyze the underlying reasons or identify which aspects of query variations are most detrimental.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a detailed analysis of different types of query variations to determine their specific effects on model performance, possibly leading to targeted improvements.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does pre-training influence the OOD generalizability of generative retrieval models across different tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that CorpusBrain, with its pre-training tasks, shows better generalizability than BART in most OOD scenarios, suggesting a link between pre-training and robustness.
- Why unresolved: While the paper indicates a positive effect of pre-training on OOD performance, it does not explore which pre-training tasks are most effective or how they contribute to robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimenting with various pre-training tasks and architectures to identify which ones most enhance OOD generalizability, supported by comparative performance data across tasks.

## Limitations
- The study is limited to specific model architectures (DPR, BART, CorpusBrain) and the KILT benchmark, which may not generalize to other retrieval frameworks or datasets
- The analysis relies on synthetic query variations that may not fully capture real-world query distribution shifts
- The specific mechanisms by which pre-training tasks improve OOD robustness remain underexplored and not well-validated

## Confidence
- High: Generative retrieval models exhibit significant performance degradation under OOD conditions, particularly for query variations and unforeseen query types
- High: CorpusBrain demonstrates better generalizability than BART in most OOD settings, consistent with the benefits of pre-training with retrieval-specific tasks
- Medium: The specific mechanisms by which ISS pre-training improves OOD robustness are plausible but require further validation
- Medium: The comparison between generative and dense retrieval models' OOD performance is consistent across experiments but may depend on specific dataset characteristics
- Low: The exact conditions under which CorpusBrain's pre-training advantages disappear are not well-specified
- Low: The generalization of findings to non-KILT datasets and different generative retrieval architectures is uncertain
