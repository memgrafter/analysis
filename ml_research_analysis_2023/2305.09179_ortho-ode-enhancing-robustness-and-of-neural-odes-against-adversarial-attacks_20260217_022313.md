# Ortho-ODE: Enhancing Robustness and of Neural ODEs against Adversarial Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09179
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09179
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Ortho-ODE improves adversarial robustness of Neural ODEs using orthogonal convolutions via Cayley transform

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the vulnerability of Neural ODEs (NODEs) to adversarial attacks by proposing Ortho-ODE, which uses orthogonal convolutional layers parameterized via Cayley transform. The key insight is that controlling the Lipschitz constant of ODE dynamics through orthogonality improves robustness, as justified by Gronwall-Bellman inequality. Experiments on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100 show Ortho-ODE achieves competitive robustness against FGSM and PGD attacks compared to baselines like ResNet10, Vanilla ODE, and TisODE, though computational cost is a limitation.

## Method Summary
Ortho-ODE enforces orthogonality on convolutional layers using the Cayley transform, which parameterizes orthogonal matrices efficiently. This approach bounds the Lipschitz constant of ODE dynamics, improving robustness against adversarial attacks as proven by Gronwall-Bellman inequality. The method maintains local contractivity while preserving gradient norms, preventing exploding/vanishing gradients in continuous-depth models. Training uses standard optimization with 100 epochs and learning rate 0.01, with no weight decay.

## Key Results
- Ortho-ODE achieves competitive robustness on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100
- Performs comparably to TisODE under FGSM and PGD attacks
- Improves over vanilla NODEs and ResNet10 in adversarial scenarios
- Computational cost is higher due to Cayley transform requiring FFT operations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Bounding the Lipschitz constant of ODE dynamics improves robustness against adversarial attacks.
- Mechanism: Gronwall-Bellman inequality shows that the difference between outputs of two trajectories is bounded by the initial difference times the exponential of the Lipschitz constant. By controlling this constant via orthogonal convolutions, the output representations of adversarial and clean samples stay close.
- Core assumption: The adversarial sample is in an ϵ-neighborhood of the clean sample, and the Lipschitz constant can be effectively bounded using orthogonal layers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that by controlling the Lipschitz constant of the ODE dynamics the robustness can be significantly improved."
  - [section] "From Theorem 1 we can bring two neighboring curves close to together by bounding the Lipschitz constant of the dynamics of ODE."
  - [corpus] Weak: no direct corpus evidence, but "Robust Convolution Neural ODEs via Contractivity-promoting regularization" suggests similar Lipschitz control ideas.
- Break condition: If the orthogonal constraint fails to maintain low Lipschitz constant or the adversarial perturbation exceeds the neighborhood assumption.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Orthogonal convolutional layers preserve gradient norms and ensure stable activations in infinite-depth NODEs.
- Mechanism: Cayley transform parameterizes orthogonal matrices efficiently, enforcing 1-Lipschitzness. This prevents exploding/vanishing gradients and maintains stable dynamics across continuous depth.
- Core assumption: Orthogonalization via Cayley transform remains effective throughout training and inference.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Orthogonal convolutional layers using the Cayley transform ensure stable activation, preserving gradient norms and enforcing low Lipschitz constants."
  - [section] "The Cayley transform is a bijection between skew-symmetric matrices A and orthogonal matrices Q without −1 eigenvalues."
  - [corpus] Weak: "Orthogonalizing Convolutional Layers with the Cayley Transform" cited as method basis, but no direct experimental validation in corpus.
- Break condition: If the Cayley transform implementation introduces numerical instability or the orthogonality constraint is violated during training.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Local contractivity ensures adversarial and clean samples remain close in representation space.
- Mechanism: By making the NODE locally contractive (Lipschitz < 1 in a region), trajectories of nearby initial conditions converge, limiting the effect of adversarial perturbations.
- Core assumption: The NODE can be made locally contractive without sacrificing too much expressive power.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Therefore we can say that a NODE is contractive, the Lipschitz constant between the input and the output is smaller than 1."
  - [section] "Our goal is to apply local contractivity efficiently to harness the natural robustness provided by locally contractive NODEs."
  - [corpus] Weak: "Robust Convolution Neural ODEs via Contractivity-promoting regularization" supports this, but no direct citation in paper.
- Break condition: If global expressivity is overly constrained, leading to poor accuracy on clean data.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → CNN feature extractor → Ortho-ODE (Cayley orthogonal convolutions) → Output MLP → Class prediction
- Critical path:
  1. Input image passes through pre-CNN layers
  2. Orthogonal convolution layers define ODE dynamics
  3. ODE solver integrates dynamics over [0, T]
  4. Post-ODE layers classify the final representation
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Orthogonality vs. expressivity: Imposing orthogonality may limit model capacity
  - Computational cost: Cayley transform requires FFT, slowing training/inference
  - Robustness vs. accuracy: Better adversarial robustness may come at cost of clean accuracy
- Failure signatures:
  - Training instability or NaN gradients → Cayley transform numerical issues
  - Poor clean accuracy despite good robustness → Over-constrained orthogonality
  - Slow convergence → Computational overhead of FFT in Cayley transform
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Replace one orthogonal convolution with a normal convolution and measure impact on Lipschitz constant and accuracy.
  2. Vary the ODE solver tolerance and observe effects on robustness vs. speed.
  3. Test with different adversarial attack strengths to map the robustness-accuracy tradeoff curve.
