# Co-Driven Recognition of Semantic Consistency via the Fusion of Transformer and HowNet Sememes Knowledge

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a co-driven approach for semantic consistency recognition that combines Transformer with HowNet sememes knowledge. The method addresses the challenges of synonyms, polysemy, and difficulty in understanding long text by using Transformer for data-driven multi-level encoding of sentence structures and HowNet for knowledge-driven modeling of semantic associations. The approach introduces an interactive attention mechanism with a sememes matrix and uses BiLSTM for final semantic inference. Experiments on three datasets (BQ, AFQMC, PAWSX) show the model outperforms lightweight models by 2.19-6.51% accuracy and pre-training models by up to 0.48%, while maintaining approximately 16M parameters.

## Method Summary
The method employs a multi-stage architecture where Transformer encoders capture sentence structure through multi-head attention, HowNet sememes provide explicit semantic relations through an interactive matrix, and soft-attention fuses these representations. A BiLSTM layer then performs final contextual inference, followed by max/avg pooling and fully connected layers for binary classification. The model uses Adam optimization with initial learning rate 0.01, batch size 64, and sequence length capped at 50 tokens. The HowNet sememes matrix marks shared semantic units between word pairs, addressing synonymy and polysemy challenges that pure data-driven approaches struggle with.

## Key Results
- Outperforms lightweight models (DSSM, MwAN, DRCN) by 2.19-6.51% accuracy on BQ, AFQMC, PAWSX datasets
- Achieves 0.48% improvement over pre-training model ERNIE while maintaining ~16M parameters
- Demonstrates particular effectiveness in handling long text and non-standard text scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Transformer captures sentence structure better than bag-of-words models for long texts.
- Mechanism: Multi-head attention and position encoding allow modeling of dependencies over longer distances without vanishing gradients.
- Core assumption: Longer sequences contain meaningful long-range dependencies that bag-of-words miss.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Multi-level encoding of internal sentence structures via data-driven is carried out firstly by Transformer"; [section] "The multi-head attention mechanism is formalized as in Eq.(1) to Eq.(4)" with absolute position encodings
- Break condition: If sequences exceed 50 tokens, attention patterns may degrade; model may lose context.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: HowNet sememes resolve synonymy and polysemy by aligning shared semantic units.
- Mechanism: Sememes matrix M marks shared sememes between word pairs; fused with attention via trainable weight γ.
- Core assumption: Sememes are a reliable semantic representation for Chinese/English polysemy.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "HowNet for knowledge-driven to model the semantic knowledge association among sentence pairs"; [section] "The HowNet interaction matrix is calculated as in Eq.(7) and Eq.(8)" with example of "China" vs "Huaxia"
- Break condition: If sememes are too coarse-grained, or mismatched across languages, similarity scores may be misleading.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Interactive attention + BiLSTM captures both lexical alignment and contextual inference.
- Mechanism: Soft-attention (Eq.10-11) blends Transformer output with sememes matrix; BiLSTM encodes final context.
- Core assumption: Contextual refinement after lexical matching improves consistency judgment.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "interactive attention calculation is carried out utilizing soft-attention and fusion the knowledge with sememes matrix"; [section] "Through the Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM), the forward encoding and backward encoding are concatenated"
- Break condition: If BiLSTM is too shallow or overfits to training pairs, generalization may suffer.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Tokenizer → Transformer encoder → Sememes matrix M → Attention fusion → BiLSTM → Pooling → FC → Output
- Critical path: Tokenizer → Transformer → Attention (with M) → BiLSTM → Pooling → FC → Output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - HowNet integration vs model size: Sememes matrix adds parameters but improves semantic accuracy.
  - Fixed seq length (100) vs flexibility: Simplifies batching but may truncate long inputs.
  - Transformer depth (10 layers) vs speed: Higher accuracy but slower training.
- Failure signatures:
  - Low accuracy on long texts: Possibly due to truncation or attention saturation.
  - High variance on short vs long texts: Likely imbalance in semantic feature extraction.
  - Overfitting to training vocab: May occur if HowNet coverage is incomplete.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation: Remove HowNet matrix, keep Transformer+BiLSTM, compare accuracy.
  2. Ablation: Remove BiLSTM, use only Transformer+attention, compare context handling.
  3. Parameter sweep: Vary Transformer layers (2,4,6,8,10), measure accuracy vs parameter count.

## Limitations

- HowNet Integration Coverage: The approach depends critically on HowNet's sememe coverage and quality, with unknown handling of out-of-vocabulary words.
- Attention Saturation with Long Texts: Transformer attention mechanisms face quadratic complexity and potential saturation beyond certain sequence lengths, with fixed length of 100 tokens causing possible truncation.
- BiLSTM Contextual Refinement Effectiveness: The BiLSTM layer may have limited capacity to resolve complex semantic ambiguities without deeper architecture or gating mechanisms.
