# Probabilistic Concept Bottleneck Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01574
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01574
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: ProbCBM introduces probabilistic concept embeddings to model uncertainty in concept prediction, achieving comparable or better accuracy than CBM/CEM while providing reliable uncertainty estimates.

## Executive Summary
Probabilistic Concept Bottleneck Models (ProbCBM) address the reliability issue in concept prediction for Concept Bottleneck Models (CBM) by introducing uncertainty modeling through probabilistic concept embeddings. Instead of deterministic embeddings, ProbCBM predicts normal distributions with mean and diagonal covariance for each concept, capturing ambiguity arising from diverse visual contexts. This enables explanations based on both concept existence and its corresponding uncertainty, while also allowing class uncertainty to be explained through concept uncertainty. ProbCBM achieves comparable or better classification accuracy than CBM and Concept Embedding Models (CEM) while maintaining concept prediction accuracy and effectively detecting increases in both concept and class uncertainties.

## Method Summary
ProbCBM extends CBM by replacing deterministic concept embeddings with probabilistic ones. The model consists of a shared backbone feature extractor, a Probabilistic Embedding Module (PEM) that predicts mean and covariance for each concept, a concept predictor that determines concept existence probabilities, and a class predictor that derives class predictions from concept embeddings. The PEM uses a diagonal covariance matrix to parameterize concept embedding distributions, allowing uncertainty to be quantified as the determinant of the covariance. The model is trained sequentially: first training the concept predictor using KL divergence loss, then freezing it to train the class predictor. This architecture enables both concept and class uncertainty estimation while maintaining interpretability.

## Key Results
- ProbCBM achieves comparable or better classification accuracy than CBM and CEM while maintaining concept prediction accuracy
- ProbCBM effectively detects increases in both concept and class uncertainties across a wide range of samples when image ambiguity is artificially introduced
- Uncertainty quantification through determinant of covariance matrix successfully captures ambiguity from visual context variations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ProbCBM models uncertainty in concept prediction by using probabilistic concept embeddings instead of deterministic ones.
- Mechanism: Instead of predicting a single fixed embedding for each concept, ProbCBM predicts a normal distribution with mean and diagonal covariance, capturing ambiguity arising from diverse visual contexts in images.
- Core assumption: The existence of a concept in an image is inherently uncertain due to variations in visual context, and this uncertainty can be meaningfully represented as a probabilistic distribution.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "By leveraging probabilistic concept embeddings, ProbCBM models uncertainty in concept prediction and provides explanations based on the concept and its corresponding uncertainty." [section] "ProbCBM exploits probabilistic embedding in prediction. It provides concept prediction and concept uncertainty as explanations."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Class uncertainty in ProbCBM is derived from concept uncertainty, enabling explanations of class uncertainty via concept uncertainty.
- Mechanism: The class predictor in ProbCBM receives concatenated probabilistic concept embeddings. Since class embeddings are derived from concept embeddings, the uncertainty in class prediction propagates from the uncertainty in concept embeddings.
- Core assumption: The class prediction process is sufficiently dependent on concept embeddings that uncertainty in concepts directly translates to uncertainty in classes.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Furthermore, as class uncertainty is derived from concept uncertainty in ProbCBM, we can explain class uncertainty by means of concept uncertainty." [section] "Because the distribution of the concept embedding is parameterized with a diagonal covariance matrix, the uncertainty of each concept c can be calculated..."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The use of probabilistic embeddings allows ProbCBM to effectively detect increases in uncertainty due to image transformations that introduce ambiguity.
- Mechanism: By quantifying uncertainty as the determinant of the covariance matrix, ProbCBM can measure how much uncertainty increases when images are transformed (e.g., occluded or hue-shifted), reflecting the introduction of ambiguity.
- Core assumption: Image transformations that remove or distort concept-related information will increase the uncertainty in the corresponding concept embeddings.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "We artificially introduce ambiguity in the data by occluding the upper half region of one of four digits in images..." [section] "We cut out a square patch from an image or change the hue of an image..."

## Limitations

- The effectiveness of uncertainty modeling depends on the assumption that concept ambiguity primarily stems from visual context diversity, which may not hold in all scenarios
- The sequential training approach may propagate concept label noise to class prediction, potentially affecting classification accuracy
- The method requires careful hyperparameter tuning and may not scale efficiently to datasets with a large number of concepts

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: Concept prediction accuracy improvements over CBM and CEM baselines; effective uncertainty detection in synthetic experiments with controlled ambiguity
- **Medium confidence**: Real-world dataset performance (CUB, AwA2); transferability of uncertainty modeling benefits beyond synthetic scenarios
- **Low confidence**: The fundamental assumption that concept ambiguity is primarily due to visual context diversity; completeness of uncertainty propagation from concepts to classes
