# Exploratory Analysis of Federated Learning Methods with Differential Privacy on MIMIC-III

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04208
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04208

## Executive Summary
This paper presents an exploratory analysis of federated learning methods with differential privacy on the MIMIC-III dataset for in-hospital mortality prediction. The study systematically evaluates two federation strategies (FedAvg and FedProx) and two differential privacy techniques (DP-SGD and DP-SVT) across various data distributions and parameter settings. Key findings demonstrate that FedProx effectively addresses performance degradation in heterogeneous data distributions, while both DP techniques can achieve comparable model performance to non-private models at the cost of quantifiable privacy leakage.

## Method Summary
The study uses the MIMIC-III dataset for in-hospital mortality prediction, preprocessing 17 clinical variables through the Harutyunyan et al. pipeline. The neural network architecture consists of a bidirectional RNN-LSTM with 16-neuron embedding layer, dropout (p=0.3), and sigmoid output. Training employs the Adam optimizer (learning rate 0.001) with batch size 64. Two federation strategies are implemented: FedAvg with varying communication rounds and local epochs, and FedProx with systematic µ parameter grid search. Differential privacy is applied through DP-SGD (gradient clipping and Gaussian noise) and DP-SVT (selective parameter sharing with sparse vector technique).

## Key Results
- FedProx resolves performance deterioration in extreme data distributions compared to FedAvg, with optimal results achieved through hyperparameter tuning
- Both differential privacy techniques can achieve similar model performance to non-private models but at the cost of quantifiable privacy leakage
- The choice of communication rounds and local training epochs significantly impacts model performance, requiring careful optimization

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: FedProx resolves performance deterioration in extreme data distributions by constraining local model updates through a regularization term.
- Mechanism: The regularization term (L2-norm squared) in FedProx's local loss calculation limits the divergence of local model weights from the federated model weights, smoothing convergence and improving stability.
- Core assumption: Client drift (divergence of local model weights from federated weights) is the primary cause of performance degradation in heterogeneous data distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "FedProx resolves performance deterioration in extreme data distributions compared to FedAvg, with optimal results achieved through hyperparameter tuning."
  - [section]: "This federation strategy constrains the training procedure on the sites to reduce the divergence of the trained model weights from the federated model weights (so-called client drift)."
  - [corpus]: No direct corpus evidence found for this specific mechanism claim.
- Break condition: If the regularization parameter μ is set too high (e.g., μ=1e-1), the proximity term hinders learning and prevents performance improvement.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Both DP-SGD and DP-SVT can achieve similar model performance to non-private models while reducing privacy leakage.
- Mechanism: DP-SGD perturbs gradients during back-propagation by clipping and adding Gaussian noise, while DP-SVT applies noise to model parameters using selective sharing and the sparse vector technique.
- Core assumption: Privacy leakage through model inference attacks is a significant concern that can be mitigated through noise injection without severely impacting model performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Both differential privacy techniques can achieve similar model performance to non-private models but at the cost of quantifiable privacy leakage."
  - [section]: "DP-SGD is an attractive method for applying differential privacy to deep learning models since it closely mimics the classic stochastic gradient descent-based training of neural networks."

- Break condition: Excessive noise injection (high noise multiplier or high ε1 parameter) leads to significant performance degradation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The choice of communication rounds and local training epochs significantly impacts model performance, requiring careful optimization.
- Mechanism: More communication rounds with fewer local epochs lead to better performance in heterogeneous data distributions, while the optimal configuration depends on the specific data distribution scenario.
- Core assumption: The trade-off between communication frequency and local training duration affects the ability of federated learning to handle heterogeneous data distributions.

## Limitations
- The study uses simulated data splits rather than real multi-site deployments, which may not capture practical challenges such as network latency and non-IID distributions across many sites
- The paper does not report statistical significance testing for performance differences, leaving open the possibility that some observed improvements are within the margin of error
- Differential privacy analysis focuses on quantifiable leakage metrics but does not evaluate the impact of more sophisticated inference attacks that could exploit model outputs

## Confidence
- High: FedProx improves performance in extreme data distributions
- Medium: Differential privacy findings are plausible but rely on assumptions about DP effectiveness
- Low: Broader applicability to real-world federated learning systems due to limited scope
