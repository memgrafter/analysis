# On Computable Online Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04357
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04357
- Reference count: 18
- Primary result: The Littlestone dimension no longer characterizes the optimal mistake bound of computable online learning, and computability constraints create fundamental barriers to learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper initiates a study of computable online learning, analyzing it under different notions of optimality in terms of the mistake bound. The main contributions are: (1) a necessary and sufficient condition for optimal c-online learning, showing that the Littlestone dimension no longer characterizes the optimal mistake bound; (2) introduction of anytime optimal (a-optimal) online learning, which is shown to be computationally more difficult than optimal online learning; and (3) demonstration that in the non-RER setting, finiteness of the Littlestone dimension no longer determines c-online learnability with finite mistake bound.

## Method Summary
The paper defines computable online (c-online) learning where both learners and hypotheses are required to be computable. It analyzes three notions of optimality: a-optimal (optimal after every input), optimal (optimal overall), and non-optimal (no optimality requirement). The analysis constructs specific hypothesis classes to demonstrate computability barriers, including a DR class that is not optimally c-online learnable despite having finite Littlestone dimension, and an RER class that is optimally but not a-optimally c-online learnable.

## Key Results
- The Littlestone dimension no longer characterizes the optimal mistake bound of c-online learning
- Anytime optimal online learning is computationally more difficult than optimal online learning
- Finiteness of Littlestone dimension no longer characterizes c-online learnability with finite mistake bound in the non-RER setting

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The Littlestone dimension no longer characterizes the optimal mistake bound of c-online learning.
- Mechanism: The paper constructs a class with finite Littlestone dimension where no computable optimal online learner exists, demonstrating that finiteness of the Littlestone dimension is insufficient for computability of optimal learners.
- Core assumption: The class HRER halt (or its variants) has finite Littlestone dimension but requires solving the halting problem to achieve optimality.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "main contribution is to give a necessary and sufficient condition for optimal c-online learning and show that the Littlestone dimension no longer characterizes the optimal mistake bound"
  - [section 5.3] "construct a DR class of computable hypotheses that has finite Littlestone dimension but is not optimally c-online learnable"
  - [corpus] Weak - related papers focus on Littlestone dimension but don't address computability directly
- Break condition: If a computable learner could achieve the optimal mistake bound without solving undecidable problems, the mechanism would break.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Anytime optimal (a-optimal) online learning is computationally more difficult than optimal online learning.
- Mechanism: The paper constructs a class that is optimally c-online learnable but not a-optimally c-online learnable, showing a computational separation between the two notions.
- Core assumption: A-optimal learning requires maintaining optimality after every input, which can encode undecidable problems.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we introduce anytime optimal (a-optimal) online learning, a more natural conceptualization of 'optimality' and a generalization of Littlestone's Standard Optimal Algorithm. We show the existence of a computational separation between a-optimal and optimal online learning"
  - [section 4.2] "construct an RER class that is optimally but not a-optimally c-online learnable"
  - [corpus] Moderate - related papers discuss randomized learners and mistake bounds but not the computational separation
- Break condition: If all optimal learners could be extended to maintain anytime optimality without solving undecidable problems, the separation would collapse.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Finiteness of Littlestone dimension no longer characterizes c-online learnability with finite mistake bound in the non-RER setting.
- Mechanism: The paper constructs a class with finite Littlestone dimension that is not c-online learnable at all, breaking the equivalence that holds in standard online learning.
- Core assumption: Non-RER classes can encode computability barriers that prevent any computable learner from achieving finite mistakes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we consider online learning with no requirements for optimality, and show, under a weaker notion of computability, that the finiteness of the Littlestone dimension no longer characterizes whether a class is c-online learnable with finite mistake bound"
  - [section 6.1] "construct a non-RER class of computable hypotheses that has finite Littlestone dimension but is not c-online learnable"
  - [corpus] Moderate - related papers discuss mistake bounds but don't explore the computability aspects
- Break condition: If all classes with finite Littlestone dimension could be learned by some computable learner with finite mistakes, the characterization would be restored.

- Component map: Learner (partial computable function) -> Hypothesis class (set of computable hypotheses) -> Mistake bound analysis. The critical path moves from computability constraints through specific constructions to the breakdown of standard characterizations.

- Critical path: To understand the paper's contributions, first grasp the standard results about Littlestone dimension and mistake bounds, then understand the computability definitions, then follow the constructions of classes that break the standard characterizations. The path moves from established theory to computability constraints to counterexamples.

- Design tradeoffs: The paper trades generality for computability - by requiring learners to be computable, it gains algorithmic interpretability but loses some of the clean characterizations from standard online learning. This tradeoff is fundamental to the results but also limits their applicability to settings where computability is essential.

- Failure signatures: If a class appears to have finite Littlestone dimension but no computable learner achieves finite mistakes, this suggests a computability barrier rather than a learning impossibility. Similarly, if a class is optimally learnable but not a-optimally learnable, this indicates a computational separation between these notions of optimality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does there exist an RER class of computable hypotheses with finite Littlestone dimension that is not c-online learnable?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - Lemma 33 and Lemma 34 suggest this is equivalent to finding an RER class that is not improperly CPAC learnable by a probabilistic learner.
- Why unresolved: The paper only shows this for non-RER classes and suggests this as an open direction.
- What evidence would resolve it: A concrete construction of such an RER class, or a proof that no such class exists.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is there a purely combinatorial characterization of computable learnability?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - The paper notes that characterizations of optimal c-online learning rely on computability-theoretic concepts, and finding combinatorial characterizations remains a major open problem.
- Why unresolved: Current characterizations depend on computability-theoretic concepts rather than combinatorial properties.
- What evidence would resolve it: A characterization that uses only combinatorial properties like VC-dimension or Littlestone dimension, without reference to computability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Does the finiteness of the Littlestone dimension characterize c-online learnability in the RER setting?
- Basis in paper: Explicit - Theorem 30 shows this fails for non-RER classes, and the paper suggests this as an open question for RER classes.
- Why unresolved: The paper only proves this fails for non-RER classes and suggests exploring the connection to improper CPAC learning.
- What evidence would resolve it: Either a counterexample (an RER class with finite Littlestone dimension that is not c-online learnable) or a proof that finiteness of Littlestone dimension always implies c-online learnability in the RER setting.

## Limitations
- The results are primarily negative, showing what cannot be done rather than constructive learning algorithms
- The constructions rely on complex hypothesis classes that may not be practically relevant
- The analysis focuses on mistake bounds and may not directly apply to other learning objectives

- Mechanism 2: Medium - The computational separation is demonstrated but the generality of the result needs further exploration
- Mechanism 3: Medium - The counterexample for non-RER classes is clear, but the open question for RER classes remains unresolved
