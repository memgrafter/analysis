# Meme Sentiment Analysis Enhanced with Multimodal Spatial Encoding and Facial Embedding

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2303.01781
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01781
- **Reference count:** 19
- **Primary result:** Spatial encoding and facial embeddings improve meme sentiment classification performance

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of classifying sentiment polarity in Internet memes by incorporating spatial relationships between text and visual elements. The authors propose augmenting multimodal meme representations with spatial coordinates of visual objects, faces, and text clusters before fusion. They evaluate seven multimodal architectures on two benchmark meme sentiment datasets, demonstrating that spatial encodings consistently improve performance over image-only approaches, with FaceNet embeddings outperforming CLIP-encoded visual objects.

## Method Summary
The authors design seven multimodal architectures that combine CLIP-based image and text encodings with optional spatial encodings of text clusters, visual objects, and faces. Spatial coordinates from bounding boxes are encoded using sinusoidal positional encodings. The models are trained separately on two meme datasets (Memotion 1.0 and Memotion 2.0) using AdamW optimizer with triangular cyclical learning rate scheduling. Evaluation uses macro-averaged and weighted-averaged F1 scores.

## Key Results
- Spatial encodings of objects and faces consistently improve performance over image-only approaches
- FaceNet embeddings of faces outperform CLIP-encoded visual objects
- Best models combine global image encoding with spatially encoded local features
- Fully automated approaches match or exceed baselines using human-validated OCR-extracted text

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Spatial encodings capture relative positions of visual elements within memes, providing explicit awareness of intermodal spatial relationships that convey sentiment signals not captured by CLIP's global image representation alone.

### Mechanism 2
FaceNet embeddings provide more fine-grained facial feature representations compared to CLIP's general object encodings, capturing subtle facial cues that are informative for sentiment classification.

### Mechanism 3
Combining global image encoding with spatially encoded local features leverages both high-level visual context and detailed information about specific elements and their relationships for improved classification.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Image/Text Encoding (CLIP) -> Spatial Encoding -> Local Feature Extraction -> Multimodal Fusion -> Classification
- **Critical path**: Image/text encoding → Spatial encoding → Local feature extraction → Multimodal fusion → Classification
- **Design tradeoffs**: Balancing global context vs. local detail, choosing spatial encoding granularity, selecting between specialized facial embeddings vs. general object encodings
- **Failure signatures**: Overfitting (high training accuracy, low validation/test accuracy), underfitting (low accuracy on both training and validation sets), degraded performance worse than image-only baseline
- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Baseline model evaluation on Filtered-OCR dataset, 2) Obj-Spatial vs. Obj-NoSpatial comparison to isolate spatial encoding impact for objects, 3) Face-Spatial vs. Face-NoSpatial comparison to isolate spatial encoding impact for faces

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions but leaves several areas unexplored, including performance variation across different meme structures, impact of alternative facial embedding models, and role of additional contextual information.

## Limitations
- Evaluation restricted to two meme datasets with filtered subsets, potentially limiting generalizability
- Automated OCR introduces performance variability compared to human-validated text extraction
- Face detection pipeline may miss faces or generate false positives affecting facial embedding reliability

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Spatial encodings improve performance over image-only baselines
- **Medium confidence**: FaceNet advantage over CLIP for faces, specific best architecture combining global and local features

## Next Checks
