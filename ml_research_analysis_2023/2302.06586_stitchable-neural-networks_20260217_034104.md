# Stitchable Neural Networks

## Executive Summary
Stitchable Neural Networks (SN-Net) introduces a novel framework for dynamically assembling pretrained model families to achieve flexible accuracy-efficiency trade-offs at runtime. The approach splits pretrained "anchor" models across layers and stitches them together using simple stitching layers that map activations between different model architectures. This creates an elastic model that can operate across different computational budgets while maintaining competitive performance.

Extensive experiments on ImageNet classification demonstrate that SN-Net can match or exceed the performance of individually trained networks while supporting diverse deployment scenarios. By leveraging existing pretrained model families, SN-Net reduces training costs and storage requirements compared to training individual networks from scratch. For instance, a single SN-Net stitching Swin Transformers can effectively replace hundreds of individual models in the Timm model zoo.

## Method Summary
SN-Net works by decomposing pretrained anchor models into segments across layers and connecting them through stitching layers that handle activation mapping between different architectures. The framework uses a simple yet effective approach where stitching layers are either identity mappings or linear transformations that adapt activations between models. During training, the system learns optimal ways to combine different model segments while maintaining performance. The approach supports dynamic configuration at runtime, allowing the same model to operate at different efficiency levels by selecting different combinations of stitched components. This eliminates the need to train and store multiple individual models for different deployment scenarios.

## Key Results
- SN-Net achieves on-par or better performance than individually trained networks on ImageNet classification
- A single SN-Net stitching Swin Transformers can challenge hundreds of models in the Timm model zoo
- The framework reduces training cost and local storage compared to training individual networks from scratch

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SN-Net leverages the complementary strengths of pretrained models by combining their best-performing segments. The stitching layers act as adaptors that bridge architectural differences between models, allowing the system to inherit the accuracy advantages of high-capacity models while maintaining the efficiency of smaller models in appropriate contexts. The approach works because modern architectures share enough structural similarities (layer types, normalization schemes) that simple mapping functions can effectively transfer information between them. The training process learns to optimize these combinations for different computational budgets.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Anchor models -> Stitching layers -> Combined network -> Dynamic configuration selector

Critical Path: Input -> Anchor model segments -> Stitching layers -> Output prediction

Design Tradeoffs:
- Simple stitching (identity/linear) vs. complex adapters: Simplicity reduces overhead but may limit architectural compatibility
- Number of anchors vs. model complexity: More anchors provide better trade-offs but increase stitching layer count
- Layer-wise vs. block-wise stitching: Fine-grained control vs. computational efficiency

Failure Signatures:
- Performance degradation at stitching boundaries indicates poor activation mapping
- Inconsistent results across different configurations suggest training instability
- High memory overhead suggests inefficient stitching layer implementation

## Limitations
- Stitching mechanism requires pretrained model families with compatible architectural properties
- Experimental validation limited to ImageNet classification without testing on other vision tasks or non-vision domains
- Simple stitching layers may not handle complex architectural differences between heterogeneous model families

## Confidence

- Low: Generalization claims beyond tested scenarios due to limited architectural and task diversity
