# Closing the Loop: Testing ChatGPT to Generate Model Explanations to Improve Human Labelling of Sponsored Content on Social Media

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05115
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05115
- Reference count: 37
- Primary result: ChatGPT-generated explanations improved inter-annotator agreement by 15.65% (Krippendorff's alpha) and increased identification of sponsored content by 9.46%

## Executive Summary
This study investigated using ChatGPT-generated explanations to improve human annotation of sponsored content on social media, addressing the challenge of low inter-annotator agreement in detecting undisclosed ads. The method involved displaying model-generated explanations alongside post captions during annotation, highlighting relevant sponsorship indicators. Results showed consistent improvements across agreement metrics, with a 15.65% increase in Krippendorff's alpha and a 17.20% increase in absolute agreement. User-experience surveys indicated that most annotators found the explanations helpful and confidence-boosting, though potential bias toward model predictions was noted.

## Method Summary
The study used 294.6k Instagram posts from 100 US influencers, with 1.72% disclosed as sponsored. ChatGPT-3.5-turbo generated explanations identifying relevant words and phrases for each post. Annotators labeled posts with and without explanations using the Doccano platform. The approach aimed to improve inter-annotator agreement (measured via Krippendorff's alpha and absolute agreement) while maintaining accuracy in detecting disclosed posts. The methodology involved collecting data via CrowdTangle, training GPT to predict sponsored content and generate explanations, conducting user studies, and evaluating agreement metrics and survey results.

## Key Results
- Krippendorff's alpha improved by 15.65% (from 54.98 to 63.58)
- Absolute agreement increased by 17.20% (from 67.29% to 79.02%)
- Proportion of posts labeled as sponsored increased by 9.46%
- 87.5% of annotators reported increased confidence when using explanations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Providing GPT-generated explanations alongside post captions improves inter-annotator agreement by clarifying context and identifying key sponsorship indicators.
- Mechanism: Explanations highlight relevant words and phrases (e.g., "@BRAND", "LTK") and provide reasoning that helps annotators focus on sponsorship cues, reducing ambiguity in decision-making.
- Core assumption: Annotators use the explanations as supplementary information to inform their own judgment rather than passively accepting model predictions.

- Break condition: If annotators begin to rely solely on the explanations without applying their own reasoning, or if explanations are inaccurate or misleading.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Explanations help annotators identify more sponsored content by highlighting subtle indicators that might otherwise be overlooked.
- Mechanism: The explanations surface nuanced features (e.g., discount codes, platform-specific links) that signal sponsorship relationships, leading to a higher proportion of posts being correctly labeled as sponsored.
- Core assumption: The model's identification of relevant features aligns with regulatory definitions of sponsored content.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The proportion of posts labeled as sponsored increased by 9.46%"
  - [section] "Legal experts had the most significant improvement in α (18.86%). Additionally, the proportion of posts labelled as sponsored increased significantly (16.16%)"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence: No directly comparable studies found; novel contribution.
- Break condition: If the explanations highlight features that are not indicative of sponsorship or if the model's criteria diverge from legal standards.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Explanations increase annotator confidence, leading to more decisive and consistent labeling.
- Mechanism: By providing reasoning and highlighting key indicators, explanations reduce uncertainty in ambiguous cases, allowing annotators to make more confident decisions.
- Core assumption: Increased confidence translates to better annotation quality and reliability.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Additionally, our survey of user experience in the annotation task indicates that the explanations improve the annotators' confidence and streamline the process."

- Break condition: If increased confidence does not correlate with improved accuracy or if annotators become overconfident and make more errors.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data collection (CrowdTangle) -> Model selection (GPT-3.5-turbo) -> Annotation interface (Doccano) -> Evaluation (agreement metrics + survey)
- Critical path: Collect and preprocess data → Train GPT model with prompts → Generate explanations → Annotators label posts with explanations → Evaluate agreement metrics and survey results
- Design tradeoffs: Using GPT-3.5-turbo for speed and accessibility vs. GPT-4 for potentially better explanations; providing explanations may introduce bias but increases agreement and confidence; small sample size of legal experts limits generalizability
- Failure signatures: Low agreement metrics despite explanations (indicates issues with explanation quality or annotator understanding); high agreement but low accuracy (indicates systematic bias introduced by explanations); annotators consistently disagreeing with GPT predictions (indicates model errors or misalignment with legal definitions)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare inter-annotator agreement with and without explanations on a small sample.
  2. Test different prompt engineering techniques to improve explanation quality and reduce model bias.
  3. Evaluate the impact of explanations on different subgroups of annotators (novices, experts, legal experts).

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the introduction of AI-generated explanations impact the accuracy of detecting undisclosed sponsored content over time as annotators become more familiar with the system?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that further research is needed to investigate the impact of explanations on data quality and suggests that more experiments are needed to determine the impact of annotator bias.
- Why unresolved: The study was conducted over a single annotation session, and there is no longitudinal data to assess how annotators' performance evolves with repeated exposure to the explanations.
- What evidence would resolve it: A longitudinal study tracking annotator performance across multiple sessions with and without explanations would provide insights into the long-term impact on accuracy and potential learning effects.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal balance between the amount and type of information provided in AI-generated explanations to maximize annotator performance without introducing significant bias?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The user-experience survey indicated that annotators preferred precise explanations with highlighted words and phrases, and suggested that adding the likelihood of a post being sponsored would be a useful improvement.
- Why unresolved: The study used a specific format for explanations and did not experiment with varying the amount or type of information provided to see how it affects annotator performance and potential bias.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with different explanation formats and information levels, then measuring their impact on annotation accuracy, agreement, and potential bias, would help determine the optimal balance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do explanations generated by different AI models (e.g., LLaMA vs. GPT) compare in terms of improving annotation accuracy and reducing bias?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper suggests exploring open-source models with greater transparency, such as LLaMA, instead of OpenAI's GPT, and mentions that the methodology could be reproduced with simpler models using local-explainability methods like LIME.
- Why unresolved: The study only used GPT for generating explanations and did not compare its performance with other AI models or local-explainability methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a comparative study using explanations generated by different AI models and local-explainability methods, then measuring their impact on annotation accuracy, agreement, and potential bias, would provide insights into the most effective approach.

## Limitations
- Reliance on a single model (ChatGPT-3.5) without comparison to other AI models or local-explainability methods
- Small sample size of legal experts (n=4) limits generalizability of results
- Absence of ground truth labels beyond disclosed posts constrains validation of detection accuracy
