# ChatGPT for Suicide Risk Assessment on Social Media: Quantitative Evaluation of Model Performance, Potentials and Limitations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09390
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09390
- Reference count: 12
- Primary result: ChatGPT achieved F1-score of 0.73 in Zero-Shot and 0.71 in Few-Shot learning on suicide risk assessment, with inconclusiveness rate up to 2.91% in Zero-Shot

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates ChatGPT's ability to assess suicidal ideation from social media posts using the University of Maryland Reddit Suicidality Dataset. The research compares ChatGPT's performance against fine-tuned transformer models (ALBERT, DistilBERT) in both Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Learning settings with varying temperature parameters. Results show ChatGPT achieves comparable performance to DistilBERT but falls short of ALBERT's superior F1-score of 0.869. The study highlights ChatGPT's potential for suicide risk assessment while identifying limitations in inconclusiveness rates and the need for careful evaluation in sensitive applications.

## Method Summary
The study uses the University of Maryland Reddit Suicidality Dataset to evaluate ChatGPT's performance in suicide risk assessment. Researchers employed Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Learning techniques with varying temperature parameters (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0). For Few-Shot Learning, prompt engineering was used with eight examples across risk categories. ChatGPT's performance was compared against fine-tuned ALBERT and DistilBERT models using standard classification metrics including F1-score, precision, recall, accuracy, and inconclusiveness rate.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT achieved F1-score of 0.73 in Zero-Shot and 0.71 in Few-Shot learning settings
- Inconclusiveness rate reached up to 2.91% in Zero-Shot learning at temperature 0.1
- ALBERT outperformed both ChatGPT and DistilBERT with F1-score of 0.869

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT can generate plausible suicidality assessments in zero-shot settings because its RLHF fine-tuning imbues it with conversational alignment to domain-specific instructions.
- Mechanism: The system prompt provides risk category definitions and instructs ChatGPT to classify only. RLHF fine-tuning has encoded response patterns that map structured instructions to categorical outputs.
- Core assumption: RLHF has generalized enough from conversational data to correctly interpret and apply custom risk definitions without explicit training on suicidality labels.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our study examines ChatGPT's performance in evaluating the level of suicidality in Reddit posts using Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Learning techniques."
  - [section 2.2] "ChatGPT differs from prior models as it employs Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF)."
- Break condition: If RLHF fine-tuning focused on general conversational coherence rather than task-specific classification, performance would degrade significantly on this structured prediction task.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Temperature tuning trades off decisiveness against accuracy, with low temperature reducing inconclusive responses at the cost of fewer classifications.
- Mechanism: At low temperature, the token probability distribution becomes peaked, favoring high-likelihood tokens that correspond to more confident but potentially less creative outputs. This reduces cases where the model hedges or declines to classify.
- Core assumption: The model's internal uncertainty correlates with token distribution entropy, so temperature acts as a knob to control the exploration/exploitation trade-off in its response.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4] "A low temperature value (e.g. 0.1) will result in more conservative and predictable responses..."
  - [results section] "We observed that ChatGPT's inconclusiveness rate (inability to assess the level of suicidality of instances) is 2.91% for a temperature of 0.1, which is the highest rate for all temperature values."
- Break condition: If the model's internal uncertainty is not well-calibrated to temperature, adjusting temperature may not meaningfully affect the inconclusiveness rate or may even increase it.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Few-shot learning with ChatGPT significantly reduces the inconclusiveness rate because the provided examples prime the model on the expected output format and risk definition boundaries.
- Mechanism: Prompt engineering with labeled examples provides few-shot conditioning that steers the model's response generation toward the correct classification schema, reducing the need for hedging.
- Core assumption: The few examples given in the prompt are representative enough of the task distribution to guide the model's predictions without overfitting to the specific examples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [results section] "ChatGPT achieves the highest F1-score at the temperature of 0.1. Furthermore, we observed that the inconclusiveness rate of ChatGPT in Few-Shot Learning was significantly lower compared to Zero-Shot Learning."
  - [section 4] "For Few-Shot Learning with ChatGPT, we use prompt engineering. The prompt consists of two examples for each category (eight in total) drawn from the training dataset..."
- Break condition: If the few examples are not representative or the model overfits to them, performance could degrade or become brittle to input variations.

## Limitations

- ChatGPT's F1-score (0.73) remains lower than fine-tuned ALBERT (0.869), indicating traditional fine-tuning still outperforms zero-shot approaches
- Inconclusiveness rate of up to 2.91% in zero-shot settings raises reliability concerns for real-world deployment where every classification matters
- The study only evaluated Reddit data, limiting generalizability to other social media platforms or clinical settings

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: ChatGPT's zero-shot performance (F1-score 0.73) is comparable to DistilBERT but lower than ALBERT's 0.869, establishing that traditional fine-tuning still outperforms general-purpose LLMs on this task. The inconclusiveness rate findings are robust across temperature settings.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that few-shot learning significantly reduces inconclusiveness rates is supported by the data, but the exact magnitude of improvement depends on the quality and representativeness of the few examples provided. The mechanism by which temperature affects decisiveness is theoretically sound but not empirically validated across all risk categories.

**Low Confidence**: The generalizability of these results to other social media platforms or clinical settings is uncertain, as the study only evaluated Reddit data with specific annotation schemes. The ethical implications of using LLMs for suicide risk assessment in practice require further investigation.
