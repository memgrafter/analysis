# MultiZenoTravel: a Tunable Benchmark for Multi-Objective Planning with Known Pareto Front

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14659
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14659
- Reference count: 5
- Presents a benchmark generator for multi-objective AI planning with known Pareto fronts

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces MultiZenoTravel, a novel benchmark generator for multi-objective AI planning problems with known Pareto fronts. The work extends the classic ZenoTravel planning problem by adding an additional objective function (cost or risk), creating a more complex planning scenario. The authors develop a constructive algorithm that can find all Pareto-optimal plans for this extended problem, providing a valuable tool for evaluating multi-objective planning solvers. As a demonstration, they apply their system to find optimal routes between the world's largest airports, incorporating spherical distances and risk metrics.

## Method Summary
The core contribution is a constructive algorithm based on a key proposition that characterizes optimal plans for a constrained version of the MultiZenoTravel problem. The algorithm reduces the general problem to this constrained case, enabling systematic discovery of all Pareto-optimal solutions. The authors implemented this approach in a C++ tool called ZenoSolver, which can handle realistic problem instances efficiently. The method leverages the problem structure to systematically explore the solution space while maintaining computational tractability for practical problem sizes.

## Key Results
- Constructive algorithm successfully finds all Pareto-optimal plans for MultiZenoTravel instances
- ZenoSolver implementation handles realistic instances with reasonable computational resources
- Demonstration on global airport network (50 largest airports) generates meaningful benchmark scenarios
- Benchmark provides tunable parameters for systematic evaluation of multi-objective planning solvers

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The algorithm exploits the structural properties of the MultiZenoTravel problem to systematically enumerate Pareto-optimal solutions. By characterizing optimal plans for constrained instances and showing how to reduce the general problem to this constrained form, the approach ensures completeness in finding all Pareto-optimal solutions. The constructive nature of the algorithm guarantees that no optimal solutions are missed during the search process.

## Foundational Learning
- **Multi-objective planning**: Optimization problems with multiple competing objectives requiring trade-off analysis
  - Why needed: Most real-world planning involves balancing multiple criteria simultaneously
  - Quick check: Can you identify at least two competing objectives in a given planning scenario?
- **Pareto optimality**: Solutions where no objective can be improved without worsening another
  - Why needed: Provides the theoretical foundation for defining optimal trade-off solutions
  - Quick check: Can you identify whether a given solution is Pareto-optimal in a two-objective space?
- **Constructive algorithms**: Methods that build solutions systematically rather than searching heuristically
  - Why needed: Ensures completeness in finding all optimal solutions
  - Quick check: Does the algorithm guarantee finding all solutions of a certain type?
- **Problem reduction**: Transforming complex problems into simpler, equivalent forms
  - Why needed: Enables efficient solution of the general problem through analysis of simpler cases
  - Quick check: Can you identify the reduced problem and its relationship to the original?

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: MultiZenoTravel Generator -> Pareto Front Calculator -> ZenoSolver Engine -> Benchmark Output

**Critical Path**: Problem specification → Constraint characterization → Reduced problem solving → Pareto front reconstruction → Benchmark generation

**Design Tradeoffs**: 
- Completeness vs. computational efficiency: The constructive approach guarantees finding all Pareto-optimal solutions but may face scalability limits
- Domain specificity vs. generalizability: The ZenoTravel extension provides a well-defined benchmark but may not capture all multi-objective planning complexities
- Real-world applicability vs. benchmarking utility: The airport network demonstration uses realistic distances but incorporates artificial risk metrics

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Incomplete Pareto front discovery indicates algorithmic errors in the reduction or reconstruction steps
- Excessive runtime suggests scalability limitations or inefficient constraint handling
- Non-Pareto optimal solutions in output indicate errors in the optimality characterization

**First 3 Experiments**:
1. Verify Pareto optimality of solutions on small, hand-constructed instances with known optimal solutions
2. Test scalability by gradually increasing problem size and measuring runtime growth
3. Validate benchmark quality by testing multiple multi-objective planning solvers on generated instances

## Limitations
- Scalability to very large instances remains unproven, with demonstrations limited to 50 airports
- Artificial risk metrics may not reflect real-world planning scenarios where risk is more complex
- Simplifications from the classical ZenoTravel extension may not capture all relevant multi-objective planning complexities

## Confidence
High confidence in theoretical foundation and algorithm correctness for known Pareto front generation
Medium confidence in practical utility due to limited empirical validation on large-scale instances
Low confidence in generalizability to other multi-objective planning domains beyond ZenoTravel framework
