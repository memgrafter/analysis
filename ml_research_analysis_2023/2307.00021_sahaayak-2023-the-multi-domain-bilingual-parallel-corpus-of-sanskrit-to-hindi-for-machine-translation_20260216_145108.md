# SAHAAYAK 2023 -- the Multi Domain Bilingual Parallel Corpus of Sanskrit to Hindi for Machine Translation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2307.00021
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00021
- Reference count: 3
- Primary result: SAHAAYAK 2023 is the largest publicly available Sanskrit-Hindi parallel corpus with 1.5M sentence pairs

## Executive Summary
SAHAAYAK 2023 presents a large-scale Sanskrit-Hindi parallel corpus designed to advance machine translation for the low-resourced Sanskrit language. The corpus contains 1.5 million sentence pairs spanning six domains including news, daily conversations, politics, history, sports, and ancient Indian literature. The dataset was created through a multifaceted mining approach involving machine-readable and non-machine-readable sources, OCR processing, and collation from existing corpora. The authors developed a dedicated pipeline for normalization, alignment, and cleaning tailored to the Devanagari script.

## Method Summary
The corpus was constructed through three types of mining: extraction from machine-readable sources, OCR processing of non-machine-readable scanned texts, and collation from existing corpora. Post-mining, the authors applied a dedicated pipeline for normalization, alignment, and cleaning specifically designed for Sanskrit-Hindi translation pairs using the Devanagari script. The corpus is described as balanced across multiple domains to ensure broad applicability for machine translation development.

## Key Results
- SAHAAYAK 2023 contains 1.5 million Sanskrit-Hindi sentence pairs
- Corpus covers six balanced domains: news, daily conversations, politics, history, sports, and ancient Indian literature
- This is the largest publicly available Sanskrit-Hindi parallel corpus to date
- Dataset is accessible online for research and development purposes

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism relies on the principle that large-scale parallel corpora enable neural machine translation systems to learn the mapping between source and target languages through statistical patterns. By mining from diverse sources including OCR from scanned texts, the authors were able to significantly expand the available training data for Sanskrit-Hindi translation. The multi-domain approach ensures that translation systems can handle various contexts and topics, improving generalization beyond narrow domains.

## Foundational Learning
- **OCR Processing**: Converts scanned Sanskrit texts into machine-readable format; needed because much Sanskrit literature exists only in physical form; quick check: verify OCR accuracy on test samples
- **Parallel Corpus Mining**: Extracts aligned sentence pairs from different sources; needed to build training data for translation models; quick check: measure alignment accuracy rates
- **Devanagari Script Normalization**: Standardizes text representation across sources; needed because Sanskrit and Hindi use the same script with variations; quick check: compare pre/post normalization statistics
- **Domain Balancing**: Ensures representation across multiple topics; needed for robust translation systems that work across contexts; quick check: verify domain distribution percentages

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component Map**: Source Mining -> OCR Processing -> Alignment Pipeline -> Normalization -> Quality Control -> Final Corpus

**Critical Path**: The alignment and normalization stages are critical as errors here propagate throughout the entire corpus. OCR processing quality directly impacts the non-machine-readable source mining success rate.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The authors chose breadth (6 domains) over depth (larger corpus size per domain) to ensure multi-domain applicability. They also opted for automated mining with limited human validation to achieve the 1.5M scale, potentially at the cost of translation quality.

**Failure Signatures**: 
- Domain imbalance would manifest as translation models performing well only on certain topics
- OCR errors would appear as garbled Sanskrit text or incorrect translations
- Poor alignment would show as semantically unrelated sentence pairs
- Normalization issues would result in inconsistent character representations
