# A Guide Through the Zoo of Biased SGD

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16296
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16296
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Introduces Biased ABC, a new general framework for analyzing SGD with biased gradient estimators that encompasses and is weaker than existing assumptions

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of analyzing Stochastic Gradient Descent with biased gradient estimators by introducing a new framework called Biased ABC. The authors systematically review existing assumptions in the literature and argue that many are either too restrictive or fail to capture how bias and randomness actually arise in practice. Biased ABC combines a lower bound on the inner product between the gradient and estimator with an upper bound involving a Bregman divergence term, making it more general and weaker than previous assumptions. The framework successfully recovers known convergence rates for both convex and non-convex settings while providing a unified way to analyze various practical gradient estimators.

## Method Summary
The paper introduces Biased ABC as a new assumption for biased gradient estimators, proving it is the weakest among all existing assumptions. The method involves establishing theoretical connections between Biased ABC and other assumptions, then providing convergence analysis for SGD with biased gradients across different function classes. The authors map various practical gradient estimators (Top-k, Rand-k, compression schemes, etc.) to the ABC framework by deriving appropriate constants. Numerical experiments validate the theoretical findings, showing that Biased SGD with biased estimators can achieve the same optimal rates as unbiased SGD in both convex and non-convex settings.

## Key Results
- Biased ABC is proven to be the weakest assumption among all existing assumptions on biased gradient estimators
- Convergence rates for Biased SGD under Biased ABC match optimal rates for unbiased SGD in both convex and non-convex settings
- The framework successfully encompasses a wide variety of practical gradient estimators including biased independent sampling, Top-k, Rand-k, and various compression schemes
- Experimental results validate that the theoretical findings hold in practice

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Biased ABC is the weakest assumption among all existing assumptions on biased gradient estimators
- Mechanism: The ABC structure combines a lower bound on ⟨∇f(x), E[g(x)]⟩ with an upper bound on E[∥g(x)∥²] that includes a Bregman divergence term A(f(x) − f*), providing more flexibility than assumptions that assume either pure perturbation or pure compression bias
- Core assumption: ⟨∇f(x), E[g(x)]⟩ ≥ b∥∇f(x)∥² − c and E[∥g(x)∥²] ≤ 2A(f(x) − f*) + B∥∇f(x)∥² + C
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "provably weaker than all previous assumptions"; [section] "least restrictive of all the assumptions reviewed"
- Break condition: If the Bregman divergence term cannot be bounded or if the estimator's bias and variance cannot be simultaneously characterized by the ABC structure

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Convergence rates under Biased ABC match optimal rates for unbiased SGD
- Mechanism: By controlling stepsize to satisfy 0 < γ ≤ b/(LB), the analysis recovers the same convergence behavior as unbiased SGD, with additional terms accounting for bias and variance captured by ABC parameters
- Core assumption: f is L-smooth and bounded below, and gradient estimator satisfies Biased ABC with appropriate constants
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "recover the optimal rates for general smooth nonconvex problems"; [section] "Convergence rates...compared to their counterparts"
- Break condition: If constants A, B, C, b, c are too large, making the convergence neighborhood term impractical

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Biased ABC framework encompasses wide variety of practical gradient estimators
- Mechanism: By providing specific values of A, B, C, b, c for each estimator type, the framework shows these estimators naturally satisfy Biased ABC, allowing use of same convergence analysis
- Core assumption: Each estimator type satisfies Biased ABC conditions with constants derived from estimator properties
- Evidence anchors: [section] "Table 2 we give a description of popular gradient estimators in terms of the Biased ABC framework"
- Break condition: If new estimator type cannot be characterized by ABC structure

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Bregman divergence
  - Why needed here: The ABC assumption uses a Bregman divergence term A(f(x) − f*) to capture how the second moment of the estimator relates to the function value, crucial for convergence analysis
  - Quick check question: How does the Bregman divergence term in the ABC assumption differ from a simple squared gradient norm bound?

- Concept: Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PŁ) condition
  - Why needed here: Convergence analysis under PŁ condition provides faster linear convergence rates for Biased SGD, important for strongly convex-like problems that don't satisfy strong convexity
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between the PŁ condition and strong convexity, and why is it relevant for biased gradient estimators?

- Concept: Variance decomposition for biased estimators
  - Why needed here: Understanding how bias and variance contribute separately to convergence analysis is essential for interpreting ABC parameters and their impact on rates
  - Quick check question: How does the variance decomposition for biased estimators differ from that for unbiased estimators?

## Limitations
- Convergence rates include terms that may become prohibitively large when bias and variance parameters are significant, making theoretical bounds less practical
- Experimental validation is limited to small-scale problems, with uncertain generalizability to larger, real-world datasets
- Analysis assumes bounded gradients and smooth functions, which may not hold for all optimization problems in practice

## Confidence
- Theoretical framework: High confidence in proof structure and relationships between assumptions
- Practical applicability: Medium confidence due to potentially large constants and limited experimental validation
- Generality claim: Medium confidence since novel estimator types may fall outside framework's structure
