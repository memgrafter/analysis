# On the Convergence of Stochastic Gradient Descent for Linear Inverse Problems in Banach Spaces

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05197
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05197
- Reference count: 40
- This work develops and analyzes stochastic gradient descent for linear inverse problems in Banach spaces, establishing convergence to minimum norm solutions and regularizing properties for noisy data.

## Executive Summary
This paper studies stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for linear inverse problems in Banach spaces, a setting that requires sophisticated tools like duality maps and Bregman distances due to the absence of unique gradient representations. The authors develop a Kaczmarz-type SGD algorithm that samples individual equations at each iteration, significantly reducing computational cost compared to batch methods. Under assumptions of uniform convexity and smoothness of the Banach space, along with appropriate step-size conditions, they prove almost sure convergence to the minimum norm solution for exact data and establish a regularizing property for noisy data with suitable stopping rules. Numerical experiments demonstrate the method's effectiveness in integral equations and CT reconstruction, particularly in sparsity-promoting Banach spaces.

## Method Summary
The method implements Kaczmarz-type SGD for linear inverse problems Ax = y in Banach spaces X and Y. At each iteration k, a random index ik is sampled uniformly from [N], and the stochastic subgradient is computed as gk = A*ik℧Y(Aikxk - yik) using the duality map of Y. The iterate is updated via xk+1 = JX*p(JXp(xk) - µk+1gk), where JX is the duality map of X and µk is the step-size. The algorithm converges to the minimum norm solution under summable step-sizes and when the initial point satisfies JX(x0) ∈ range(A*). For noisy data yδ = y + ξ, the method exhibits regularizing behavior with an appropriate stopping rule that depends on the noise level δ.

## Key Results
- SGD iterates converge almost surely to the minimum norm solution under summable step-sizes and uniform convexity/smoothness of the Banach space.
- For noisy data, the algorithm exhibits regularizing behavior: expected Bregman distance to the true solution vanishes as noise level δ→0 with suitable stopping rules.
- Under a conditional stability estimate, SGD achieves a convergence rate in expectation for the expected Bregman distance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SGD iterates converge almost surely to the minimum norm solution under summable step-sizes and Banach space smoothness/convexity.
- Mechanism: At each iteration, the Bregman distance to the minimum norm solution decreases in expectation due to unbiased gradient estimates and smoothness properties. Robbins-Siegmund theorem then ensures almost sure convergence.
- Core assumption: The Banach space is p-convex and smooth, and the initial point satisfies JX_p(x_0) in the range of A*.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show the almost sure convergence of the iterates to the minimum norm solution"
  - [section] "Theorem 3.8. Let (µ_k)_k∈N satisfy∑∞_k=1 µ_k =∞ and∑∞_k=1 µ^p*_k <∞, Assumption 3.1 hold, and x† be the MNS. Then the sequence (x_k)_k∈N converges a.s. to a solution of (1)"
- Break condition: If the space is not p-convex/smooth, or the step-sizes do not satisfy summability, the Bregman descent argument fails and convergence is not guaranteed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: For noisy data, the algorithm exhibits regularizing behavior: the expected Bregman distance to the true solution vanishes as noise level δ→0 with a suitable stopping rule.
- Mechanism: Stability of iterates under data perturbations (Lemma 4.2) allows controlling the bias introduced by noise. Combined with the descent property, this yields convergence in expectation for δ→0.
- Core assumption: The Banach space is uniformly smooth, the step-sizes decay appropriately, and the stopping index k(δ) grows to infinity as δ→0.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "establish the regularising property for suitable a priori stopping criteria"
  - [section] "Theorem 4.3. Let Assumption 4.1 hold... If lim_δ↘0 k(δ) =∞ and lim_δ↘0 δ^p ∑^k(δ)_ℓ=1 µ_ℓ = 0, then lim_δ↘0 E[Bp(x^δ_k(δ), x†)] = 0."
- Break condition: If the space is not uniformly smooth, or the stopping rule does not satisfy the required growth conditions, the bias does not vanish.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Under a conditional stability estimate, SGD achieves a convergence rate in expectation.
- Mechanism: The stability bound links Bregman distance to the residual norm, allowing the descent property to be translated into a rate bound for the expected Bregman distance.
- Core assumption: The forward operator satisfies a Hölder-type stability estimate (20) with exponent α≥1, and step-sizes satisfy the summability and lower bound conditions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show the almost sure convergence of the iterates to the minimum norm solution and establish the regularising property for suitable a priori stopping criteria"
  - [section] "Theorem 3.14. Let the forward operator A satisfy the conditional stability bound (20)... Then there holds lim_k→∞ E[Bp(x_k, x†)] = 0."
- Break condition: If the operator is not conditionally stable, the link between residual and solution error is lost and no rate can be derived.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Duality maps in Banach spaces (Definition 2.1)

  - Quick check question: Why is JX_p single-valued when X is smooth?

  - Why needed here: It replaces the Euclidean distance in convergence analysis, crucial for non-Hilbert spaces.
  - Quick check question: What is the three-point identity for Bregman distance and why is it useful?

  - Why needed here: It is the main tool for proving almost sure convergence of the Bregman distance sequence.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: A -> duality map -> stochastic subgradient -> Bregman distance -> update rule
- Critical path:
  1. Sample index i uniformly from [N]
  2. Compute g(x,i) = A*_i JY_p(A_i x - y_i)
  3. Update x ← JX*_p*(JX_p(x) - µ*g(x,i))
  4. Check stopping criterion (if noisy)
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Mini-batch size vs variance: larger batches reduce variance but increase cost per iteration
  - Choice of Banach spaces: impacts sparsity vs smoothness of solutions
  - Step-size decay rate: affects convergence speed vs stability
- Failure signatures:
  - Divergence: step-sizes too large or space not smooth/convex
  - High variance: too small mini-batches or poor conditioning
  - No convergence to minimum norm: initial point not in range(A*)
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify convergence on a simple integral equation with exact data in L2 vs L1.1 settings
  2. Test stability under impulse noise in different Y spaces
  3. Measure convergence rate under a known conditional stability estimate

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1**: How does the choice of p in the Banach space X influence the sparsity of the solution, and what is the optimal p for sparse recovery problems?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that L^p spaces with p closer to 1 enforce sparser solutions, and they compare different choices of X and Y spaces in numerical experiments.
  - Why unresolved: The paper provides empirical evidence but does not establish a theoretical relationship between the p parameter and sparsity recovery properties.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis proving convergence rates or stability estimates for different p values, or additional numerical experiments systematically varying p.

- **Open Question 2**: Can the regularizing property of SGD for noisy data be extended to establish convergence rates similar to those for exact data under conditional stability?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors show a regularizing property for noisy data with a priori stopping rules but note that deriving convergence rates for noisy data remains challenging.
  - Why unresolved: The analysis strategy for exact data relies on quantitative stability estimates that are difficult to extend to the noisy case.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A convergence rate theorem for noisy data under conditional stability, or a proof that such rates are impossible to achieve.

- **Open Question 3**: What are effective a posteriori stopping rules for SGD in Banach spaces that don't require knowledge of the noise level?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that the issue of a posteriori stopping rules for stochastic iterative methods is completely open, even for the Hilbert setting.
  - Why unresolved: The paper only discusses a priori stopping rules and notes the difficulty of developing practical stopping criteria.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A proposed a posteriori stopping rule with theoretical justification and numerical validation showing it selects appropriate stopping indices.

## Limitations
- The analysis requires the Banach space to be uniformly convex and smooth, which may not hold for many practical applications.
- The requirement that JX_p(x_0) lies in range(A*) is a non-trivial condition that could limit applicability.
- The numerical experiments are limited to specific integral equation and CT reconstruction examples, which may not generalize to broader problem classes.

## Confidence
- Almost sure convergence under exact data: High
- Regularizing property with noisy data: Medium
- Convergence rate claims: Low
