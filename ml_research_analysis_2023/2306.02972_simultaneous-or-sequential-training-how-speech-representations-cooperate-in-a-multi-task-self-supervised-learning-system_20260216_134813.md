# Simultaneous or Sequential Training? How Speech Representations Cooperate in a Multi-Task Self-Supervised Learning System

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.02972
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02972
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Sequential pretraining with wav2vec 2.0 followed by VGS yields superior semantic retrieval performance compared to simultaneous training.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates training strategies for a multi-task system combining self-supervised speech learning (wav2vec 2.0) and visually grounded speech (VGS) processing. The authors compare sequential vs. simultaneous training approaches and evaluate their impact on semantic retrieval and phoneme discrimination performance. The study reveals that sequential training with wav2v 2.0 first followed by VGS yields superior semantic retrieval performance, while simultaneous training mitigates catastrophic forgetting. Additionally, VGS-learned phonemic representations show better generalization across datasets compared to wav2vec 2.0.

## Method Summary
The paper implements a FaST-VGS+ model combining wav2vec 2.0-based self-supervised learning and transformer-based VGS with a shared speech encoder. The system is trained on SpokenCOCO dataset with various scenarios including VGS only, W2V2 only, and VGS+ (balanced training with α=0.5). Sequential pretraining combinations are also explored where models are first trained on one task for 20 epochs and then switched to another task for 50 epochs. Performance is evaluated using recall@k for semantic retrieval and ABX phonemic discrimination scores.

## Key Results
- Sequential training with W2V2 first followed by VGS yields higher semantic retrieval performance than simultaneous training
- Simultaneous training with both W2V2 and VGS reduces catastrophic forgetting compared to sequential training
- VGS-learned phonemic representations generalize better across datasets compared to W2V2-learned ones

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Sequential training with wav2vec 2.0 (W2V2) first and visually grounded speech (VGS) next yields higher semantic retrieval performance than simultaneous training.
- Mechanism: Pretraining on acoustic representations via W2V2 provides a robust initialization for the shared speech encoder, which facilitates subsequent semantic learning when VGS is applied.
- Core assumption: Acoustic-level features learned by W2V2 are beneficial and complementary to semantic-level visual grounding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "sequential training with wav2vec 2.0 first and VGS next provides higher performance on audio-visual retrieval"
  - [section IV.A] "when the W2V2 training precedes VGS training...there is a substantial improvement in recall scores"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence for cross-task synergy; mostly self-reported performance gains.
- Break condition: If W2V2 pretraining is done on a different dataset, the benefit diminishes or disappears.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Simultaneous training with both W2V2 and VGS reduces catastrophic forgetting compared to sequential training.
- Mechanism: Joint optimization keeps both task objectives active in the shared encoder, maintaining task-relevant representations for both modalities.
- Core assumption: Parallel updates prevent dominance of one task objective over the other, preserving shared representations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the parallel SSL-VGS training reduces the effects of catastrophic forgetting"
  - [section IV.A] "catastrophic forgetting quickly results in chance-level recall score when switching from VGS pretraining to W2V2 training"
  - [section IV.A] "performance remains fairly stable with (VGS+, W2V2)"
- Break condition: If one task loss is significantly easier to optimize, it may dominate training and still cause forgetting.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: VGS-learned phonemic representations generalize better across datasets compared to W2V2-learned ones.
- Mechanism: Multimodal training with visual context enforces more robust and abstract phoneme representations that transfer better to unseen domains.
- Core assumption: Visual grounding provides additional constraints that improve cross-domain generalization of phonemic distinctions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the results suggest that phonemic representations learned through the VGS mechanism may generalize better across datasets compared to those learned with SSL"
  - [section IV.B] "this result suggests that audiovisual training provides phonemic representations that better generalize across data domains"
  - [corpus] No external validation; claim based on single dataset experiment.
- Break condition: If both models are trained and evaluated on the same dataset, the advantage may disappear.

  - Why needed here: VGS uses contrastive loss to align speech and image embeddings in a shared space.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between the contrastive loss used in VGS and the masked prediction loss in W2V2?

  - Why needed here: Sequential training can overwrite previously learned representations when switching tasks.
  - Quick check question: How does simultaneous optimization mitigate catastrophic forgetting compared to sequential training?

- Concept: Cross-modal semantic retrieval evaluation
  - Why needed here: Recall@k metrics are used to measure how well the model maps speech to images and vice versa.
  - Quick check question: What does a recall@10 score of 70% indicate about the model's retrieval performance?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Speech encoder: 6-layer CNN + 8-layer transformer (shared between SSL and VGS)
  - Speech decoder: 4-layer transformer (W2V2 only)
  - Image encoder: 6-layer transformer
  - Shared embedding space: CLS tokens from both encoders
  - Losses: lossAV (VGS), lossAUD (W2V2), combined with coefficient α

- Critical path:
  1. Input speech waveform → CNN → transformer encoder
  2. Transformer encoder outputs → VGS branch (CLS_A) and W2V2 branch
  3. W2V2 branch → quantization → contrastive loss
  4. VGS branch → ResDAVEnet → image encoder → contrastive loss
  5. Combined loss → backpropagation → shared encoder update

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Shared vs. separate encoders: sharing reduces parameters but may cause interference
  - Loss weighting (α): balances semantic vs. acoustic learning; improper weighting can bias learning
  - Pretraining strategy: sequential vs. simultaneous affects both performance and forgetting

- Failure signatures:
  - Catastrophic forgetting: sharp drop in retrieval scores when switching tasks
  - Degraded phonemic discrimination: poor ABX scores, especially across speakers
  - Loss imbalance: one loss plateaus while the other decreases rapidly

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train W2V2 on SpokenCOCO for 20 epochs, then continue with VGS+ for 50 epochs; measure recall@k.
  2. Train VGS+ from scratch (α=0.5) for 70 epochs; compare recall@k to experiment 1.
  3. Train VGS on SpokenCOCO, then switch to W2V2; monitor ABX error and lossAV to detect forgetting.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do visual grounding mechanisms specifically improve cross-dataset generalization of phonemic representations compared to pure self-supervised learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that audiovisual training provides phonemic representations that better generalize across datasets compared to those learned with SSL alone, but the mechanisms behind this improvement are not explored.
- Why unresolved: The study demonstrates the empirical advantage but does not investigate the underlying factors (e.g., whether visual context provides more robust phonetic cues or reduces domain-specific noise).
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments isolating visual vs. acoustic components, analysis of learned embeddings for cross-dataset consistency, or ablation studies removing visual inputs to measure impact on generalization.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the optimal scheduling strategies for alternating between SSL and VGS tasks to maximize both semantic retrieval and phonemic discrimination performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper explores sequential and simultaneous training but notes that sequential training (W2V2 first, then VGS) yields better semantic performance, while simultaneous training reduces catastrophic forgetting.
- Why unresolved: The study tests specific schedules but does not systematically explore the full space of scheduling strategies (e.g., varying pretraining durations, interleaving schedules, or dynamic task weighting).
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments testing various pretraining durations, interleaving frequencies, and adaptive scheduling algorithms, measuring performance across both tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Why does visual grounding result in better cross-domain phonemic discrimination despite being trained on different data than the test set?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that VGS-trained models outperform W2V2 models on ABX tasks when tested on out-of-domain data, but the reason for this superiority is not investigated.
- Why unresolved: The study demonstrates the phenomenon but does not analyze whether visual grounding helps learn more invariant phonetic features or provides regularization against overfitting to training domain characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of learned representations comparing their invariance to domain shifts, examination of attention patterns in visual-audio alignment, or experiments testing the impact of visual input quality on cross-domain performance.

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on a single dataset (SpokenCOCO) for semantic retrieval and a single speech corpus (LibriSpeech) for phoneme discrimination, limiting generalizability.
- The catastrophic forgetting analysis depends on abrupt task switching, but gradual transitions or curriculum learning strategies are not explored.
- Visual grounding benefits are assumed to transfer across domains without cross-corpus validation.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core finding that sequential pretraining with W2V2 followed by VGS yields higher retrieval performance (Mechanism 1).
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that simultaneous training reduces catastrophic forgetting (Mechanism 2).
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that VGS-learned phonemic representations generalize better across datasets (Mechanism 3).

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-dataset validation**: Evaluate retrieval and ABX performance on a different dataset (e.g., Flickr8k Audio Captions) to verify generalization claims, particularly for phonemic representations.
2. **Alternative forgetting mitigation**: Implement gradual task transition or elastic weight consolidation to compare with simultaneous training for preventing catastrophic forgetting.
3. **Loss sensitivity analysis**: Systematically vary α in VGS+ training (α ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.7, 0.9}) to quantify the impact of loss weighting on both semantic retrieval and phonemic discrimination performance.
