# Communication-Efficient Vertical Federated Learning with Limited Overlapping Samples

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2303.16270
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16270
- **Reference count**: 38
- **Primary result**: Improves accuracy by over 46.5% and reduces communication costs by more than 330x compared to state-of-the-art methods

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses two critical challenges in vertical federated learning (VFL): high communication costs and limited overlapping samples between clients. The authors propose one-shot VFL, which dramatically reduces communication by requiring only two uploads and one download per training session while using semi-supervised learning to leverage both overlapping and unaligned samples. They further extend this to few-shot VFL, which improves accuracy by expanding supervised datasets with pseudo-labels from estimated global representations. Evaluations on CIFAR-10 and UCI credit card datasets demonstrate substantial improvements in both accuracy and communication efficiency.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a framework where clients conduct local training using both overlapping and unaligned samples through semi-supervised learning, requiring minimal communication with the server. In one-shot VFL, clients upload feature representations, receive partial gradients, conduct SSL locally, and upload new representations once more. The server then aggregates and trains the classifier. Few-shot VFL extends this by having the server estimate missing representations for unaligned data using scaled dot product attention, generate pseudo-labels with high confidence, and clients incorporate these into supervised training. This approach enables effective use of unaligned samples while dramatically reducing communication overhead.

## Key Results
- One-shot VFL improves accuracy by over 46.5% compared to existing methods
- Communication costs reduced by more than 330x compared to state-of-the-art approaches
- Few-shot VFL further enhances accuracy with minimal additional communication
- Demonstrates effectiveness on both CIFAR-10 (image) and UCI credit card (tabular) datasets

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: One-shot VFL drastically reduces communication by requiring only two uploads and one download per training session.
- Mechanism: Instead of iterative communication, clients first upload feature representations, receive partial gradients, conduct semi-supervised learning locally, and upload new representations once more. The server then aggregates and trains the classifier.
- Core assumption: The partial gradients received from the server provide sufficient guidance for clients to conduct effective local semi-supervised learning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "In our proposed framework, the clients only need to communicate with the server once or only a few times."
  - [section] "In one-shot VFL, the clients only need to conduct two upload operations and one download operation for the training session"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for this specific communication pattern
- Break condition: If partial gradients are insufficient to guide effective local SSL, model performance degrades significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Few-shot VFL improves accuracy by expanding the supervised dataset with pseudo-labels from estimated global representations.
- Mechanism: The server estimates missing representations for unaligned data using a scaled dot product attention function, generates pseudo-labels with high confidence, and clients incorporate these into their supervised training.
- Core assumption: The estimated global representations are accurate enough to generate reliable pseudo-labels for unaligned samples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Few-shot VFL expands the supervised dataset on clients to improve the performance of the local feature extractors."
  - [section] "Few-shot VFL expands the supervised dataset on clients to improve the performance of the local feature extractors."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for this specific pseudo-label expansion technique
- Break condition: If representation estimation is inaccurate, pseudo-labels will be noisy and degrade model performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Semi-supervised learning enables effective use of unaligned samples, solving the limited overlapping samples problem.
- Mechanism: Clients apply SSL algorithms (FixMatch for images, modified FixMatch-tab for tabular data) using both overlapping samples with temporary labels and unaligned samples.
- Core assumption: SSL can effectively learn from unaligned samples when guided by partial gradients from the server.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose a practical vertical federated learning (VFL) framework called one-shot VFL that can solve the communication bottleneck and the problem of limited overlapping samples simultaneously based on semi-supervised learning."
  - [section] "In one-shot VFL, the clients are guided to conduct local semi-supervised learning (SSL) using both the overlapping samples and the unaligned samples to train well-performing feature extractors."
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct corpus evidence found for applying SSL to unaligned samples in VFL
- Break condition: If SSL performance degrades with limited labeled data, the approach fails to solve the limited overlapping samples problem.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Clients (feature extractors + local SSL) -> Server (classifier + representation estimation) -> Communication channels (upload/download operations)
- **Critical path**: Client upload → Server aggregation → Client download partial gradients → Client SSL → Client upload → Server classifier training (one-shot VFL)
- **Design tradeoffs**: Reduced communication vs. potential accuracy loss from using SSL; complexity of representation estimation vs. improved accuracy in few-shot VFL
- **Failure signatures**: High communication cost if iterative communication is required; poor accuracy if SSL fails or representation estimation is inaccurate

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does one-shot VFL perform in multi-party (more than two clients) scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper states "we focus and evaluate on two-client scenarios in this paper" and "we will explore multi-party settings in future work."
- Why unresolved: The paper only evaluates two-client scenarios, which is common in VFL literature, but does not explore performance with more participants.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing accuracy, communication efficiency, and convergence behavior of one-shot VFL with three or more clients across different data modalities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How robust is one-shot VFL to non-IID data distributions across clients?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions evaluating on CIFAR-10 and UCI credit card datasets but doesn't explicitly address non-IID data scenarios or analyze performance under varying data distribution heterogeneity.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates effectiveness under limited overlapping samples, it doesn't investigate how data distribution differences between clients affect the method's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments varying the degree of data heterogeneity across clients and measuring accuracy/AUC degradation under different non-IID scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of different semi-supervised learning algorithms on one-shot VFL's performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using FixMatch for image data and proposes FixMatch-tab for tabular data, but notes "Plenty of SSL algorithms have been proposed" and focuses on these specific choices.
- Why unresolved: The paper evaluates specific SSL algorithms but doesn't systematically compare alternative SSL methods or analyze how algorithm choice affects communication efficiency and accuracy trade-offs.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments using different SSL algorithms (e.g., MixMatch, Pseudo-label, consistency regularization) measuring their impact on accuracy, communication cost, and robustness to limited overlapping samples.

## Limitations

- **Limited scalability**: Framework focuses on two-client scenarios, with unclear performance in multi-party settings
- **Dependency on SSL quality**: Effectiveness heavily relies on semi-supervised learning algorithms performing well with limited labeled data
- **Representation estimation accuracy**: Few-shot VFL's success depends on accurate estimation of missing representations for pseudo-label generation

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The communication cost reduction mechanism (one-shot VFL) is well-supported by the two-upload, one-download design.
- **Medium Confidence**: The accuracy improvements from semi-supervised learning with unaligned samples, as this depends heavily on SSL algorithm performance in federated settings.
- **Medium Confidence**: The few-shot VFL extension's effectiveness, as representation estimation quality is crucial for pseudo-label reliability.
