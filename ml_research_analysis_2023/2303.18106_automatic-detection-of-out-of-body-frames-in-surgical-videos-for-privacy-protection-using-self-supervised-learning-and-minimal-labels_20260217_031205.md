# Automatic Detection of Out-of-body Frames in Surgical Videos for Privacy Protection Using Self-supervised Learning and Minimal Labels

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.18106
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18106
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves 96-98% F1 scores for detecting out-of-body frames using only 5% of labeled data

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of protecting patient privacy in surgical videos by automatically detecting out-of-body frames that could reveal identifying information. The proposed method uses self-supervised learning with rotation-based transformations to pre-train a ResNet-50 backbone on unlabeled surgical data, then fine-tunes with minimal labeled samples for binary classification. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance with only 5% of training labels while maintaining average F1 scores above 97%, significantly outperforming fully-supervised methods that use 95% more labeled data.

## Method Summary
The framework employs a two-stage approach: first, self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled surgical videos using rotation-based transformations (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) to learn endoscope-specific visual features; second, supervised fine-tuning on limited labeled data for binary classification into relevant (inside body) and irrelevant (out-of-body) frames. The rotation transformations simulate the stochastic nature of endoscope motion, allowing the model to learn meaningful representations without manual annotations. The pre-trained ResNet-50 backbone is then fine-tuned with an MLP-2 classification head using class-weighted cross-entropy loss to handle the imbalanced dataset.

## Key Results
- Achieves average F1 scores ranging from 96.00 to 98.02 across cholecystectomy, lobectomy, hysterectomy, and hemicolectomy procedures
- Maintains F1 scores above 97% using only 5% of training labels, outperforming fully-supervised methods with 95% fewer annotations
- Rotation-based self-supervised learning outperforms Jigsaw and SimCLR methods, particularly when fine-tuning with minimal labels (2% of training data)
- Macro-averaged F1 score is the primary metric, addressing class imbalance between relevant and irrelevant frames

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Self-supervised pre-training with rotation-based transformations learns endoscope-specific visual features that transfer well to surgical frame classification. Rotation transformations (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) act as pseudo-labels that force the model to capture orientation-invariant and endoscope-specific spatial patterns without requiring manual annotations. The core assumption is that endoscope viewpoints are stochastic and rotation-like augmentations simulate real endoscope motion. Break condition: If rotation transformations do not align with real endoscope motion patterns, the learned representations may fail to generalize.

### Mechanism 2
Fine-tuning on minimal labeled data retains high performance because the pre-trained backbone already encodes meaningful visual features. Transfer learning leverages the representations learned during self-supervised pre-training, allowing the model to adapt quickly to the binary classification task with only a few labeled samples. The core assumption is that representations learned from large unlabeled data are sufficiently general to be adapted with minimal supervision. Break condition: If the downstream task differs significantly from the pre-training task, fine-tuning may require more labeled data.

### Mechanism 3
Rotation-based self-supervision outperforms Jigsaw and SimCLR on this surgical frame detection task. Rotation augmentation is more aligned with endoscope-specific variability than generic transformations like jigsaw puzzles or contrastive learning. The core assumption is that the stochastic nature of endoscope movement is better captured by simple geometric transformations than by complex pretext tasks. Break condition: If the dataset size is increased significantly, Jigsaw or SimCLR might perform better due to their more complex pretext tasks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input frames (224x224 RGB) -> ResNet-50 backbone (pre-trained via rotation SSL) -> MLP-2 head (rotation classification) -> Replace with binary classifier head -> Fine-tuning with labeled data

- Critical path: Load unlabeled dataset → Apply random rotations → Pre-train rotation classifier → Replace head with binary classifier → Fine-tune on labeled data → Evaluate

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Rotation-based SSL vs. Jigsaw vs. SimCLR: Simpler rotation transformations are more aligned with endoscope variability.
  - ResNet-50 vs. other architectures: ResNet-50 balances performance and computational cost.
  - Minimal labels vs. full supervision: Reduces annotation cost but may require careful hyperparameter tuning.

- Failure signatures:
  - Low F1 scores on irrelevant class: Model struggles to detect rare out-of-body frames.
  - High variance across folds: Inconsistent performance due to small labeled dataset.
  - Overfitting on training set: Model memorizes training samples instead of learning generalizable features.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
How do different self-supervised learning methods (e.g., rotation, Jigsaw, SimCLR) compare in terms of performance and generalizability for detecting out-of-body frames in surgical videos, and which method is most effective for this task? The paper compares rotation-based SSL with Jigsaw and SimCLR, showing that rotation-based SSL outperforms the others, especially when fine-tuning with limited labels. This remains unresolved because the paper only tests three methods and does not explore other potential transformations or contrastive learning approaches that could be more effective. Testing additional self-supervised methods and comparing their performance on the same task would provide more insights into the most effective approach.

### Open Question 2
How does the performance of self-supervised learning models for detecting out-of-body frames vary across different types of surgical procedures or specialties, and how generalizable are these models to other surgical domains? The paper notes that the experiments were performed on endoscopic videos from robot-assisted surgeries, but the generalizability to other surgical procedures or specialties was not explored. This remains unresolved because the paper does not test the models on different types of surgical procedures or specialties, so it is unclear how well the models would perform in other contexts. Testing the models on a diverse set of surgical procedures and specialties would provide insights into their generalizability and effectiveness in different surgical domains.

### Open Question 3
How does the performance of self-supervised learning models for detecting out-of-body frames change when applied to video-based feature learning, as opposed to frame-based classification, and what is the impact of incorporating temporal information? The paper mentions that the authors investigated the performance of the models at the video clip level but did not explore self-supervised video-based feature learning based on a sequential set of frames. This remains unresolved because the paper does not test the impact of incorporating temporal information or applying self-supervised learning to video-based feature learning, so it is unclear how this would affect the model's performance. Testing the models with video-based feature learning and incorporating temporal information would provide insights into the potential benefits and limitations of this approach.

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to only four surgical procedure types from two da Vinci systems, limiting generalizability
- No comparison with state-of-the-art supervised methods using full labels
- Uncertainty about real-world deployment performance with continuous video streams

## Confidence

- Overall framework effectiveness: High confidence (consistent high F1 scores across multiple procedures and label fractions)
- Self-supervised pre-training reducing annotation requirements: High confidence (97+ F1 score with 5% labeled data)
- Rotation-based SSL superiority over Jigsaw and SimCLR: Medium confidence (limited comparison conditions, only tested at 2% label fraction)

## Next Checks

1. **Generalization Test**: Evaluate the pre-trained model on surgical videos from different institutions or robotic systems to assess cross-domain performance.

2. **Label Efficiency Analysis**: Systematically compare the performance of rotation-based SSL versus fully supervised training across a wider range of label fractions (1%, 3%, 7%, 12%, 20%) to establish precise break-even points.

3. **Temporal Consistency Evaluation**: Assess the model's performance on continuous video sequences rather than individual frames to ensure practical applicability in real surgical settings.
