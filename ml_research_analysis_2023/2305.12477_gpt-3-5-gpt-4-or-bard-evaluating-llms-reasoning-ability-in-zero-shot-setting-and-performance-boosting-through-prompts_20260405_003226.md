# GPT-3.5, GPT-4, or BARD? Evaluating LLMs Reasoning Ability in Zero-Shot Setting and Performance Boosting Through Prompts

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2305.12477  
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12477  
- **Reference count:** 8  
- **Primary result:** Engineered prompts boost zero‑shot accuracy; GPT‑4 consistently outperforms GPT‑3.5 and BARD, achieving perfect deductive scores and leading in inductive and causal reasoning.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates the zero‑shot reasoning capabilities of GPT‑3.5, GPT‑4, and BARD across eleven datasets encompassing deductive, inductive, abductive, mathematical, commonsense, causal, and multi‑hop reasoning. The authors design task‑specific engineered prompts (e.g., “Let’s think step by step”) to coax models into explicit reasoning chains. Results confirm that all models benefit from prompt scaffolding, with GPT‑4 showing the strongest performance, notably perfect accuracy on some deductive tasks and sizable leads on inductive and causal benchmarks. The work underscores both the latent reasoning potential of large language models and the power of prompt engineering to unlock it.

## Method Summary
For each of eleven datasets, the authors randomly selected 30 test instances. They crafted dataset‑specific prompts that included instructions encouraging step‑by‑step reasoning. The prompts were fed to GPT‑3.5, GPT‑4, and BARD via their respective interfaces/APIs. Model outputs were manually inspected and scored against gold answers, yielding accuracy metrics per reasoning type. This zero‑shot setup avoids fine‑tuning and isolates the effect of the engineered prompts.

## Key Results
- GPT‑4 achieved perfect deductive scores (e.g., 30/30 on bAbI task 15) with prompts, surpassing GPT‑3.5 and BARD.  
- On inductive reasoning (bAbI‑16, CLUTRR), GPT‑4 more than doubled GPT‑3.5’s accuracy (28/30 vs. 14/30; 11/30 vs. 2/30).  
- Causal reasoning also favored GPT‑4 (25/30). Mathematical reasoning improved but remained limited (GPT‑4: 21/30), and multi‑hop (HotpotQA) saw only modest gains (GPT‑4: 17/30).  
- Engineered prompts increased zero‑shot accuracy for all models across most tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1  
- **Claim:** Engineered prompts that explicitly request step‑by‑step reasoning improve zero‑shot accuracy across reasoning tasks.  
- **Mechanism:** The prompt acts as a scaffold that activates the model’s internal chain‑of‑thought process, breaking a problem into intermediate deductions before the final answer. This reduces the need for the model to implicitly infer reasoning structure.  
- **Core assumption:** LLMs possess latent reasoning capabilities that can be elicited with appropriate textual cues.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “We propose engineered prompts to enhance performance. Results show … Engineered prompts boosted zero‑shot accuracy for all models.”  
  - Section 5.2 details prompt templates for each dataset (e.g., adding “Let’s think step by step” to math problems).  
  - Neighboring studies similarly report that structured prompting yields measurable gains.  
- **Break condition:** If prompts become too domain‑specific or verbose, models may ignore the scaffold or produce irrelevant intermediate text, nullifying the benefit.

### Mechanism 2  
- **Claim:** Larger‑scale models (e.g., GPT‑4) demonstrate superior reasoning across deductive, inductive, and causal tasks compared to smaller counterparts (GPT‑3.5).  
- **Mechanism:** Increased parameter count and training data improve the richness of world knowledge and the capacity to maintain logical consistency over longer inference chains.  
- **Core assumption:** Reasoning ability scales monotonically with model size.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “GPT‑4 consistently outperforms others… achieving perfect scores on deductive tasks and higher in inductive and causal reasoning.”  
  - Tables 2–4 and 6–8 show GPT‑4 exceeding GPT‑3.5 on nearly every dataset, with statistically significant gaps on several.  
  - Multiple neighbor papers find similar size‑performance gradients across diverse LLMs.  
- **Break condition:** Diminishing returns may set in beyond a certain scale, or task‑specific fine‑tuning could close the gap for smaller models.

### Mechanism 3  
- **Claim:** Decomposing complex, multi‑hop questions into explicit sub‑questions or intermediate guidance improves model performance on tasks requiring integration of several pieces of information.  
- **Mechanism:** By forcing the model to process each hop separately, the cognitive load per generation step is reduced, and error propagation is contained.  
- **Core assumption:** Explicit decomposition compensates for the model’s limited ability to internally coordinate long‑range dependencies.  
- **Evidence anchors:**  
  - Abstract: “Multi‑hop (HotpotQA) saw modest gains (GPT‑4: 17/30).”  
  - Prompt for HotpotQA asks the model to base answer on given context, a simple decomposition.  
  - Works such as “LLMs Still Can’t Avoid Instanceof” highlight that decomposing programming tasks leads to better results.  
- **Break condition:** Excessive splitting may lose contextual coherence or require manual hop identification, limiting general applicability.

## Foundational Learning
- **Zero‑shot learning** – The evaluation paradigm where models are not provided any task‑specific examples. *Why needed:* All experiments test models without fine‑tuning, making zero‑shot behavior central. *Quick check:* What distinguishes zero‑shot from few‑shot learning in LLMs?  
- **Chain‑of‑thought prompting** – Technique that encourages models to produce intermediate reasoning steps before final answers. *Why needed:* Engineered prompts rely on this to elicit reasoning. *Quick check:* How does the phrase “Let’s think step by step” affect the generation process?  
- **Reasoning typology** – Classification into deductive, inductive, abductive, causal, multi‑hop, etc. *Why needed:* The paper categorizes tasks to compare model strengths across reasoning forms. *Quick check:* What is the difference between inductive and deductive reasoning in the context of the datasets used?  

## Architecture Onboarding
**Component map:** Dataset sampler → Prompt generator → LLM inference engine → Answer parser → Scoring module  

**Critical path:**  
1. Load dataset  
2. Sample 30 items  
3. Insert sample into prompt template  
4. Query LLM  
5. Normalize response  
6. Compare with gold label  
7. Record accuracy  
8. Aggregate results  

**Design tradeoffs:**  
- Sample size (30) vs. coverage – larger samples yield more reliable estimates but increase evaluation cost.  
- Prompt generality vs. task specificity – tailored prompts boost performance but reduce portability.  
- Automatic parsing vs. manual inspection – scaling vs. nuance capture.  

**Failure signatures:**  
- Model ignores premises and defaults to common‑sense heuristics (e.g., inductive CLUTRR failures).  
- Over‑reliance on surface patterns leads to wrong answers even when reasoning chain seems plausible (abductive NLI).  
- In multi‑hop tasks, the model often loses track of intermediate facts as context length grows.  

**First 3 experiments:**  
1. **Deductive bAbI task 15:** Apply prompt from Bang et al. (2023); GPT‑4 achieves 30/30 with prompt vs. 27/30 without.  
2. **Inductive CLUTRR:** Use custom story prompt; GPT‑3.5 obtains 2/30, GPT‑4 11/30 – highlights inductive weakness.  
3. **Multi‑hop HotpotQA:** Use context‑based prompt; modest gain for GPT‑4 (17/30) over GPT‑3.5 (16/30) – shows remaining challenge.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- **Open Question 1:** What causes the large performance difference between GPT‑3.5 and GPT‑4 on inductive reasoning tasks? Is it primarily due to model scale, training data, or prompt sensitivity?  
- **Open Question 2:** Do the results from 30 random samples per dataset accurately reflect the models’ true capabilities, or could sample selection bias affect the observed rankings?  
- **Open Question 3:** How do other modern LLMs (PaLM, LLaMA, Falcon, Claude) perform on the same reasoning tasks under zero‑shot and engineered‑prompt conditions?  
- **Open Question 4:** Can prompt engineering for reasoning tasks be automated or generalized so that a single prompting strategy yields strong performance across all reasoning types?

## Limitations
- Model versions (GPT‑3.5, GPT‑4, BARD) are not precisely specified, hindering reproducibility.  
- BARD interface and API details are missing; replicating its behavior is blocked.  
- Manual evaluation with small sample sizes (30 per dataset) limits statistical reliability and prevents full‑benchmark validation.

## Confidence
- Prompt scaffolding improves zero‑shot reasoning: **High**  
- GPT‑4 superiority across reasoning types: **High**  
- Decomposition helps multi‑hop reasoning: **Medium**

## Next Checks
