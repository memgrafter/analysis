# UniFLG: Unified Facial Landmark Generator from Text or Speech

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14337
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14337
- Reference count: 0
- This paper proposes a unified facial landmark generator (UniFLG) that integrates text-driven and speech-driven talking face generation frameworks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces UniFLG, a system that generates facial landmarks from either text or speech using a shared latent representation learned through an end-to-end text-to-speech model. The approach leverages VAE-VITS to extract speaker-independent latent features common to both modalities, which are then decoded into facial landmarks. By training the landmark decoder with mixed-modality inputs, the system achieves higher naturalness in both speech synthesis and facial landmark generation compared to state-of-the-art text-driven methods, while also enabling landmark generation for unseen speakers without requiring facial video data.

## Method Summary
UniFLG integrates text-driven and speech-driven talking face generation by exploiting VAE-VITS to extract shared latent representations from both text and speech. The system trains in two stages: first, VAE-VITS is trained on unpaired speech data to learn speaker embeddings and utterance-level emotion representations; second, the landmark decoder is trained on paired text/speech/landmark data using mixed-modality training that randomly switches between text and speech inputs. This approach enables generation of facial landmarks from either modality while maintaining speaker independence through the learned latent space.

## Key Results
- UniFLG achieves higher naturalness in both speech synthesis and facial landmark generation compared to state-of-the-art text-driven method
- The system can generate facial landmarks from speech of speakers without facial video data or even speech data
- Subjective evaluations show UniFLG significantly outperforms baseline methods in speech synthesis, facial landmark generation, and lip-sync quality

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Shared latent representation allows same model to generate facial landmarks from either text or speech.
- Mechanism: VAE-VITS learns a common intermediate representation (f_spec_θ(zf)) that is aligned to both text and speech during training. This representation is speaker-independent and emotion-aware, so the landmark decoder can consume it regardless of modality.
- Core assumption: Text and speech carry sufficient overlapping information about facial articulation that a shared latent space can be learned without modality-specific conditioning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "exploits end-to-end text-to-speech not only for synthesizing speech but also for extracting a series of latent representations that are common to text and speech"
  - [section 3.1] "f_spec_θ(zf) can be regarded as a speaker-independent representation common to text and speech"
- Break condition: If the temporal alignment between text and speech is poor or if linguistic content does not correlate with articulation, the shared space may collapse or become modality-biased.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Mixed-modality training stabilizes the landmark decoder by simulating both text- and speech-based inference during training.
- Mechanism: At each training iteration, the input to the landmark decoder is randomly chosen from either text (via text encoder + duration predictor) or speech (via posterior encoder). This enforces robustness to input source and prevents overfitting to one modality.
- Core assumption: The facial landmark sequence is sufficiently deterministic from the shared representation that both input paths can produce comparable training signals.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.2.1] "the landmark decoder is trained by switching the input between text and speech at each iteration"
  - [section 4.2.1] "UniFLG-{T , S} achieved better performance than TTL and STL" suggesting benefit from mixed training.
- Break condition: If one modality consistently produces noisier or less reliable landmarks, the random mixing could introduce harmful gradients and degrade performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Speaker independence of facial landmarks allows training the landmark decoder on one speaker while VAE-VITS is trained on many.
- Mechanism: Because 2D landmarks are relatively speaker-invariant (they encode articulation, not identity), the decoder can be trained on paired text/speech/landmark data from a single speaker while VAE-VITS learns speaker embeddings from a multi-speaker corpus. This enables landmark generation for unseen speakers via the "arbitrary speaker" variant.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Another important feature of this study is that we regard the facial landmark... as common among speakers due to its little speaker dependence"
  - [section 4.2.4] Introduction of UniFLG-AS that generates landmarks for unseen speakers.
- Break condition: If the assumption of speaker invariance breaks (e.g., due to different face shapes or articulation styles), the landmark decoder may fail on unseen speakers even with VAE-VITS conditioning.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Variational Autoencoder (VAE) with normalizing flows
  - Why needed here: Provides a probabilistic latent space that can model complex speech distributions and allow sampling for synthesis. Normalizing flows improve the expressiveness of the posterior.
  - Quick check question: What is the role of the flow in VAE-VITS, and how does it differ from a vanilla VAE?

- Concept: Monotonic alignment search (MAS)
  - Why needed here: Aligns the latent speech representation to the phoneme sequence without requiring explicit alignment labels, enabling joint modeling of text and speech.
  - Quick check question: How does MAS guarantee monotonic alignment, and why is this assumption reasonable for TTS?

- Concept: Duration prediction in non-autoregressive TTS
  - Why needed here: Allows the model to expand the text encoder output into a frame-level representation without iterative refinement, enabling parallel generation.
  - Quick check question: What happens if the duration predictor is inaccurate—how does that affect both speech and landmark quality?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Text encoder → phoneme projection → duration predictor (text-driven path)
  - Posterior encoder + normalizing flow → speaker embeddings (speech-driven path)
  - Utterance encoder → emotion embedding (shared for both)
  - VAE-VITS outputs: f_spec_θ(zf) (frame-level shared rep) and zu (utterance-level emotion)
  - Landmark decoder (WaveNet) consumes f_spec_θ(zf) and zu to produce facial landmarks

- Critical path:
  - Text-driven: Text → encoder → duration predictor → f_land → landmark decoder
  - Speech-driven: Speech → posterior encoder → f_spec → resample → f_land → landmark decoder

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using landmarks instead of raw video reduces dimensionality and speaker dependency but loses fine-grained identity cues.
  - Non-autoregressive landmark decoder speeds inference but may reduce temporal coherence vs. autoregressive models.
  - Mixed-modality training increases robustness but may slow convergence if modalities are highly imbalanced.

- Failure signatures:
  - Misaligned lips despite good speech: likely duration predictor error or MAS failure.
  - Unnatural facial motion: landmark decoder overfitting or emotion embedding misconditioning.
  - Poor generalization to new speakers: insufficient diversity in VAE-VITS training or broken speaker invariance assumption.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train VAE-VITS on Paired dataset only, then landmark decoder on same speaker; measure D-LL, D-VL, D-A.
  2. Switch to mixed-modality training; compare performance and training stability.
  3. Evaluate UniFLG-AS on Unseen dataset; analyze landmark and lip-sync quality vs. baseline STL-D.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the quality of facial landmark generation scale with increasing numbers of training speakers for VAE-VITS, and is there an optimal point where additional speakers no longer improve performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "The number of speakers seen while training VAE-VITS was 27 in total, which we assume was insufficient to represent all unseen speakers with the learned latent space." This suggests that scaling the number of speakers could be beneficial.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide data on how landmark generation quality changes as the number of training speakers increases beyond 27.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing landmark generation quality on unseen speakers using VAE-VITS trained with varying numbers of speakers (e.g., 10, 50, 100, 200) would reveal whether there is a point of diminishing returns.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the UniFLG system be extended to handle multiple speakers in a single video generation, such as in a conversation, without retraining the model?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on generating facial landmarks for a single speaker. The mention of "unseen speakers" implies the system is designed for individual speaker handling, but does not address multi-speaker scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The architecture and training procedures described do not explicitly support generating facial landmarks for multiple speakers simultaneously.
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstrations of the system generating facial landmarks for multiple speakers in a conversation, or experiments showing the impact on quality when attempting to adapt the system for multi-speaker scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the introduction of an end-to-end video generation network, as suggested for future work, impact the naturalness and coherence of the final talking face video compared to using the landmark decoder alone?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states, "Future research would involve attempts to extend the proposed system to an end-to-end framework that includes the training of a video generation network."
- Why unresolved: The current system only generates facial landmarks, and the impact of adding a video generation network on the final output quality is not yet known.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies evaluating the naturalness and coherence of talking face videos generated using only the landmark decoder versus those generated with an additional end-to-end video generation network.

## Limitations

- Limited speaker diversity in evaluation, with only 10 unseen speakers tested, raising questions about generalization across different demographics
- Strong assumption that facial landmarks are speaker-invariant may not hold for speakers with significantly different face shapes or articulation styles
- Small sample size in subjective evaluations (20 participants for speech/facial landmark quality, 12 for lip-sync) may not capture full range of human perception differences

## Confidence

- High confidence: Technical implementation and performance claims for seen speakers based on quantitative metrics (D-LL, D-VL, D-A)
- Medium confidence: Speaker-independent landmark generation claims given limited unseen speaker testing and strong invariance assumption
- Low confidence: Real-time factor claims without knowing specific hardware configuration and implementation details
