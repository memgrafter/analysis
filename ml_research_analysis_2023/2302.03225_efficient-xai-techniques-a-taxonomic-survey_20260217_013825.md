# Efficient XAI Techniques: A Taxonomic Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03225
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03225
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Comprehensive survey of efficient XAI techniques categorizing non-amortized and amortized methods with experimental throughput comparisons showing CoRTX achieving 6202.85 vs SHAP's 1.0940

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive survey of efficient Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques, organizing them into non-amortized and amortized methods. Non-amortized methods accelerate individual instance explanations through data-centric approaches like feature elimination or model-centric approaches like approximation-driven techniques. Amortized methods learn unified distributions of explanations using predictive, generative, or reinforcement learning frameworks to achieve rapid multi-instance explanation generation. The survey analyzes limitations from training, deployment, and use scenarios perspectives, highlighting challenges in real-world deployment, balancing faithfulness with efficiency, and method selection.

## Method Summary
The paper surveys efficient XAI techniques by categorizing them based on their acceleration mechanisms. Non-amortized methods focus on accelerating explanation for individual instances through data-centric approaches (feature reduction, searching optimization) or model-centric approaches (optimization-driven, approximation-driven). Amortized methods learn a unified distribution of model explanations using deep neural networks to replace the local training pattern in post-hoc local explanation with a single forward pass. The survey evaluates methods across different explanation tasks including feature attribution, counterfactual examples, and example-based explanations on datasets like Adult and CIFAR-10.

## Key Results
- Amortized methods like CoRTX and FastSHAP significantly outperform traditional methods in speed, with CoRTX achieving 6202.85 throughput compared to 1.0940 for SHAP
- Non-amortized data-centric acceleration reduces computational complexity by eliminating unimportant features before explanation computation
- Approximation-driven methods accelerate feature attribution by replacing exact Shapley value computation with surrogate models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Amortized methods reduce per-instance explanation time by learning a unified distribution of explanations
- Mechanism: Instead of training a unique explainer for each instance, a single DNN model captures the global explanation distribution and generates explanations via a single forward pass
- Core assumption: The explanation distribution across instances is sufficiently regular to be approximated by a single model
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "amortized methods learn a unified distribution of model explanations, following the predictive, generative, or reinforcement frameworks, to rapidly derive multiple model explanations"
  - [section]: "Unlike non-amortized methods, amortized methodologies utilize a unified explainer to learn the distribution of model explanation. The goal of amortized ones is to replace the local training pattern in post-hoc local explanation with a deep neural network (DNN)."
- Break condition: If the explanation distribution is too heterogeneous across instances, the unified model will produce poor approximations

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Non-amortized data-centric acceleration reduces computational complexity by eliminating unimportant features before explanation
- Mechanism: Pre-filtering features based on contribution scores or importance metrics reduces the number of feature coalitions that need to be evaluated in Shapley value calculations
- Core assumption: A subset of features contributes negligibly to most explanations, allowing safe elimination
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The goal of data-centric acceleration is to dismiss the unimportant features or feature coalitions before the head, which reduces the computational complexity of explanation derivation"
  - [section]: "One of the existing work SHEAR [8] accelerates the estimation of Shapley value by contributive cooperative feature selection"
- Break condition: If the eliminated features are actually important for certain instances, the resulting explanations will be inaccurate

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Approximation-driven methods accelerate feature attribution by replacing exact Shapley value computation with surrogate models
- Mechanism: White-box models (linear, tree-based) approximate the behavior of black-box models in local neighborhoods, enabling faster computation of feature importance scores
- Core assumption: The surrogate model accurately approximates the black-box model's behavior in the relevant input region
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "White-box model does not have to work well globally, but it has to approximate the black-box model well in a small sampling neighborhood near the original input"
  - [section]: "TreeSHAP [3] decreases the time complexity from O(TL^2M) to O(TLD^2) by reducing the number of trees (T) and maximum depth (D) of trees with L leaves"
- Break condition: If the surrogate model's approximation error is too large, the resulting explanations will not be faithful to the original model

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input -> Feature selection/elimination -> Base model -> Explanation model -> Post-processing
- Critical path:
  - For non-amortized: Input → Feature selection → Local sampling → Explanation computation → Output
  - For amortized: Input → Unified explainer forward pass → Output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Speed vs. faithfulness: Amortized methods are faster but potentially less accurate
  - Generalizability vs. specificity: Non-amortized methods are instance-specific but computationally expensive
  - Training cost vs. inference cost: Amortized methods require expensive training but cheap inference
- Failure signatures:
  - Low throughput despite acceleration claims
  - Explanations that contradict known model behavior
  - Training instability in amortized models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal balance between explanation fidelity and computational efficiency for different real-world applications of XAI?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the trade-off between explanation efficacy and efficiency, particularly in Section 5.3 "Limitation on Using Scenario" and Section 6.3 "Towards Human-aware Selection"
- Why unresolved: Different applications have varying requirements for explanation quality versus speed, and there's no universal metric to determine the optimal balance point
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies across multiple real-world applications measuring both explanation quality (using established metrics like faithfulness, consistency) and computational efficiency under varying constraints

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can amortized XAI methods be effectively trained in decentralized/federated learning settings while preserving both efficiency and privacy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 6.1.2 discusses challenges with amortized methods requiring centralized training and potential privacy risks
- Why unresolved: Federated learning introduces additional complexity in maintaining model performance while ensuring data privacy and handling heterogeneous data distributions
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing federated vs centralized training of amortized XAI methods across different data distributions and privacy requirements

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the most effective approaches to mitigate fairness and security vulnerabilities in both non-amortized and amortized XAI acceleration methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Section 6.2 discusses fairness and security issues for both non-amortized and amortized methods
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies these issues but doesn't provide comprehensive solutions or comparative evaluations of different mitigation strategies
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of various fairness-aware and secure training approaches for XAI acceleration methods across multiple datasets and fairness metrics

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation of real-world deployment challenges, with most claims being theoretical assertions
- Potential benchmarking inconsistencies when comparing diverse methods with different underlying assumptions about explanation quality
- Insufficient field validation data for claims about explanation faithfulness trade-offs in production environments

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The taxonomic categorization of efficient XAI techniques into non-amortized vs amortized methods is well-founded and logically structured
- **Medium confidence**: Empirical throughput comparisons between methods, due to unspecified experimental conditions and potential benchmarking inconsistencies
- **Low confidence**: Claims about real-world deployment challenges, as these are primarily theoretical assertions without extensive field validation data
