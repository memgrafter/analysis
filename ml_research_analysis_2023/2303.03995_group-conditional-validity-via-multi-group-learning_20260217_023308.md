# Group conditional validity via multi-group learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03995
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03995
- Reference count: 40
- This paper proposes a reduction to multi-group learning for distribution-free conformal prediction with group conditional validity, improving upon existing methods in generality, noise-adaptivity, and simplicity.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of achieving group conditional validity in distribution-free conformal prediction, ensuring that prediction coverage rates are simultaneously maintained across all groups in a given collection. Existing methods either require restrictive grouping structures, distributional assumptions, or suffer from over-conservatism under heteroskedastic noise. The authors propose a novel reduction to multi-group learning, leveraging algorithms that ensure small excess conditional risk across all groups. This approach can be instantiated with existing multi-group learning methods to yield new conformal prediction algorithms with stronger theoretical guarantees.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves a reduction to multi-group learning, where the goal is to construct a set predictor that achieves group conditional validity across all groups simultaneously. The approach consists of three main steps: (1) implement the ICP procedure to construct set predictors for each group using training data, (2) run a multi-group learning algorithm on the remaining data using the collection of set predictors as the benchmark class, and (3) use the output of the multi-group learning algorithm as the final set predictor. The authors provide a new multi-group learning algorithm for hierarchically structured groups, which leads to simpler decision tree predictors and improved sample complexity guarantees compared to prior work.

## Key Results
- The reduction to multi-group learning yields new conformal prediction algorithms with stronger theoretical guarantees compared to existing methods.
- The proposed multi-group learning algorithm for hierarchical structures achieves near-optimal rates and provides improved guarantees compared to previous work.
- The resulting decision tree predictors are simpler than those from prior methods while maintaining the desired coverage properties.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by reducing the group conditional validity problem in conformal prediction to a multi-group learning problem. Multi-group learning algorithms are designed to ensure small excess conditional risk across all groups simultaneously, which directly translates to the desired coverage guarantees in conformal prediction. By leveraging these algorithms, the approach can handle arbitrary collections of groups and adapt to heteroskedastic noise, addressing the limitations of existing methods.

Design tradeoffs: The reduction to multi-group learning allows for handling arbitrary collections of groups and adapting to heteroskedastic noise, but relies on the availability of suitable multi-group learning algorithms. The hierarchical structure assumption enables simpler decision tree predictors but may not always hold in practice.

Failure signatures: If the multi-group learning algorithm fails to converge or does not provide the expected excess risk bounds, the resulting set predictor may not achieve the desired coverage rates. Overly conservative ICP procedures can lead to very large confidence regions, reducing the practical utility of the method.
