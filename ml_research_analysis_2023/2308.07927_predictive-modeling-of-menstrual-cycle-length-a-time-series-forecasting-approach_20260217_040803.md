# Predictive Modeling of Menstrual Cycle Length: A Time Series Forecasting Approach

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates multiple machine learning approaches for predicting menstrual cycle timing and duration through time series forecasting. The research focuses on comparing traditional statistical methods (ARIMA, Huber Regression, Lasso Regression, Orthogonal Matching Pursuit) against deep learning (LSTM Network) using synthetic data that mimics real menstrual cycle patterns. The synthetic datasets represent three distinct scenarios: regular cycles with small variation, regular cycles with moderate variation, and irregular cycles. The findings demonstrate that LSTM consistently outperforms traditional methods across all tested scenarios, suggesting strong potential for personalized reproductive health applications.

## Method Summary
The study generates synthetic menstrual cycle data designed to replicate statistical properties of real-world cycles across three distinct cases. Five predictive models are evaluated: ARIMA (traditional time series), Huber Regression, Lasso Regression, Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (all statistical/ML regression), and LSTM Network (deep learning). Each model is trained and tested on identical synthetic datasets representing regular cycles with small variation (Case 1), regular cycles with moderate variation (Case 2), and irregular cycles (Case 3). Performance is measured using MAE, MSE, and RMSE metrics across 30 test observations per case.

## Key Results
- LSTM achieved MAE of 0.3000 and RMSE of 0.7401 for regular cycles with small variation (Case 1)
- LSTM achieved MAE of 2.1000 and RMSE of 1.5967 for regular cycles with moderate variation (Case 2)
- LSTM achieved MAE of 3.4000 and RMSE of 2.0711 for irregular cycles (Case 3)
- LSTM consistently outperformed all traditional methods across all three cases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Assumption: LSTM's superior performance stems from its ability to capture long-term temporal dependencies and non-linear patterns in menstrual cycle data through its recurrent architecture with memory cells. The gated structure of LSTM (input, forget, and output gates) allows selective retention of relevant historical information while filtering noise, which proves advantageous for modeling the complex, multi-factor influences on menstrual cycle regularity that traditional statistical methods cannot adequately capture.
