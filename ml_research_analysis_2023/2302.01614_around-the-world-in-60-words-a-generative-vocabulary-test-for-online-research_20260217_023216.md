# Around the world in 60 words: A generative vocabulary test for online research

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01614
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01614
- Reference count: 8
- The paper presents WikiVocab, an automated pipeline to generate vocabulary tests for online research using Wikipedia text

## Executive Summary
WikiVocab is an automated pipeline that generates vocabulary tests by sampling rare nouns from Wikipedia and creating pseudowords with similar orthographic n-gram statistics. Behavioral experiments across six countries and eight languages (N=236) demonstrate that the test can reliably distinguish native speakers from non-native speakers of closely related languages, with high reliability (r=0.82) and strong correlation with existing tests like LexTALE (r=0.86). The test performance also correlates negatively with linguistic distance between tested and native languages.

## Method Summary
The WikiVocab pipeline extracts text from Wikipedia articles, processes it through lemmatization, POS tagging, and spellchecking to identify rare nouns, then generates pseudowords by sampling from n-gram transitional probabilities of letters within words. The test presents participants with real words and pseudowords and asks them to identify which are valid words in their language. The method is designed to be easily extended to additional languages and is available online in eight languages.

## Key Results
- Test can distinguish native speakers of closely related languages with high accuracy
- High reliability (r=0.82) and strong correlation with existing vocabulary tests (r=0.86 with LexTALE)
- Performance negatively correlates with linguistic distance between tested and native languages (r=-0.71)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: WikiVocab can reliably distinguish native speakers from non-native speakers of closely related languages.
- Mechanism: By sampling rare nouns from Wikipedia and creating pseudowords with similar orthographic n-gram statistics, the test leverages subtle linguistic cues that are more familiar to native speakers.
- Core assumption: Native speakers have better implicit knowledge of rare vocabulary and orthographic patterns than non-native speakers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "our test can distinguish between native speakers of closely related languages"
  - [section] "we showed that our test allows us to dissect between native speakers of closely related languages"
- Break condition: If participants can easily learn or memorize the specific pseudoword patterns, the distinguishing power would decrease.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The test performance correlates with linguistic distance between tested and native languages.
- Mechanism: As linguistic distance increases, shared vocabulary and orthographic patterns decrease, making it harder for non-native speakers to identify real words.
- Core assumption: Linguistic distance affects the degree of shared vocabulary and orthographic regularities between languages.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "test accuracy is negatively correlated with the linguistic distance between the tested and the native language"
  - [section] "we correlated the mean accuracy on the test with linguistic distance... Test score and linguistic distance are negatively correlated r =−0.71"
- Break condition: If participants have significant exposure to multiple languages, the correlation with linguistic distance might weaken.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The test has high reliability (r=0.82) and correlates strongly with existing tests (r=0.86 with LexTALE).
- Mechanism: The use of rare nouns and carefully constructed pseudowords creates a challenging but consistent measure of vocabulary knowledge that aligns with established proficiency tests.
- Core assumption: The difficulty level of WikiVocab items matches that of established tests like LexTALE.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the test is reliable (r = 0.82), and (c) performance strongly correlates with existing tests (LexTale) and self-reports"
  - [section] "We computed the correlation between the average performance in the two blocks for WikiV ocab and LexTALE for each language per participant. As shown in Figure 2A the performance in LexTALE strongly correlates (r = 0.86, p < .001) with the performance in WikiV ocab"
- Break condition: If the word frequency distribution or pseudoword generation process changes significantly, reliability might decrease.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Text extraction -> Text processing (lemmatization, POS tagging, spellchecking) -> Pseudoword generation (n-gram probability computation and sampling) -> Experimental interface
- Critical path: Text extraction → Text processing → Pseudoword generation → Experimental interface. Any delay or error in early stages propagates to later stages.
- Design tradeoffs: Using Wikipedia as a source provides a large, multilingual corpus but may introduce noise and bias. The choice of n-gram size (5-grams) balances capturing orthographic patterns with computational efficiency.
- Failure signatures: Poor test performance might indicate issues with text processing (e.g., incorrect lemmatization) or pseudoword generation (e.g., too easy or too hard pseudowords). Unexpected correlations might suggest problems with the word frequency distribution or linguistic distance metrics.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify the text processing pipeline by manually checking a sample of processed words from different languages.
  2. Test the pseudoword generation by having native speakers validate a set of generated pseudowords for each language.
  3. Conduct a pilot study with a small group of participants to assess test reliability and correlations with self-reported proficiency.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the test performance change when using n-grams larger than 5 for pseudoword generation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors used 5-grams for pseudoword generation, citing that 5-phone models capture most phonotactic regularities across languages, but did not explore other n-gram sizes.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide evidence for whether larger n-grams would improve or degrade test performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies using different n-gram sizes (e.g., 3-grams, 7-grams) to measure test reliability and validity across languages.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the WikiVocab method be effectively applied to low-resource languages without existing linguistic tools?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that linguistic resources like parsers and spell checkers are necessary, but suggest that removing some processing steps or recruiting native speakers could be alternatives.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not test the method on low-resource languages or evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed alternatives.
- What evidence would resolve it: Application of the WikiVocab method to low-resource languages with and without linguistic tools, and comparison of test performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the performance of WikiVocab compare to other vocabulary tests for languages where both are available?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors compare WikiVocab to LexTALE in eight languages but do not compare it to other existing vocabulary tests.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a comprehensive comparison with other vocabulary tests, which could validate the effectiveness of WikiVocab.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies of WikiVocab against other vocabulary tests (e.g., TOEFL, IELTS) in languages where both are available.

## Limitations
- The linguistic distance metric used (WOLD-based distance from Judaic University) may not fully capture all aspects of linguistic relatedness affecting vocabulary acquisition
- Test relies on Wikipedia text which may not be representative of all language varieties and could introduce cultural or domain-specific biases
- Performance may be affected by participants' prior exposure to multiple languages, which wasn't controlled for in the analysis

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Test reliability (r=0.82) and correlation with LexTALE (r=0.86) - based on direct empirical measurements with p<0.001 significance
- **Medium Confidence**: Distinguishing power between closely related languages - demonstrated but effect size and generalizability need further validation
- **Medium Confidence**: Correlation with linguistic distance - negative correlation (r=-0.71) is statistically significant but based on a limited set of language pairs
