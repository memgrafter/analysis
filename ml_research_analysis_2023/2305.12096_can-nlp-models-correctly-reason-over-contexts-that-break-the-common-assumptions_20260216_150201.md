# Can NLP Models Correctly Reason Over Contexts that Break the Common Assumptions?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12096
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12096
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: NLP models struggle significantly with contexts that break common assumptions, showing up to 20% absolute performance gaps compared to following-assumption contexts.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether current NLP models can correctly reason when presented with contexts that violate common assumptions. The authors systematically create a novel evaluation dataset where each instance contains both a context that follows a common assumption and one that breaks it, along with corresponding questions. Through comprehensive experiments across multiple models including GPT-3 and Flan T5, they demonstrate that while models perform reasonably well on contexts adhering to common assumptions, their performance degrades significantly (up to 20% absolute points) when reasoning over contexts that break these assumptions. The study reveals that models struggle with consistency and even show degraded performance when explicitly provided with common assumptions that are contradicted by the context.

## Method Summary
The authors systematically create an evaluation dataset where each instance contains a common assumption, a context that follows the assumption, a context that breaks the assumption, and corresponding questions. They conduct comprehensive experiments using multiple models including GPT-3 and Flan T5 to evaluate performance on both following and breaking-assumption contexts. The study employs a controlled comparison design to isolate the effect of assumption violations on model reasoning performance, with thorough analysis of model consistency and the impact of explicitly stating assumptions.

## Key Results
- Models show significant performance degradation (up to 20% absolute points) when reasoning over contexts that break common assumptions versus contexts that follow them
- Models demonstrate poor consistency in their reasoning across different contexts
- Performance degrades further when models are explicitly provided with common assumptions that are broken by the context

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper's systematic approach to creating controlled evaluation data allows for clear isolation of how assumption violations affect model reasoning. By providing both following and breaking contexts within the same evaluation instance, the authors can directly measure the impact of assumption violations on model performance. The controlled experimental design reveals that current models rely heavily on common-sense patterns and struggle when these patterns are violated, suggesting that models learn statistical regularities rather than truly understanding the underlying concepts.

## Foundational Learning

- **Evaluation dataset construction**: Creating controlled test cases with both positive and negative examples - needed to systematically measure model limitations; quick check: Does each test instance contain both following and breaking contexts for fair comparison?
- **Model consistency analysis**: Measuring whether models give coherent answers across similar but slightly different contexts - needed to reveal whether models have stable reasoning capabilities; quick check: Do models give contradictory answers when presented with closely related scenarios?

**Component Map**: Data creation pipeline -> Model evaluation framework -> Performance comparison engine -> Analysis module

**Critical Path**: The critical path follows from creating controlled test instances through evaluating model responses to analyzing performance differences between following and breaking contexts.

**Design Tradeoffs**: The study prioritizes controlled experimental conditions over real-world complexity, sacrificing ecological validity to achieve clear measurement of assumption-violation effects. This allows precise quantification of performance gaps but may not fully capture how models handle assumption violations in naturalistic settings.

**Failure Signatures**: Models show inconsistent reasoning across similar contexts, performance degradation when assumptions are explicitly stated but violated, and significant accuracy drops (up to 20%) when reasoning over broken-assumption contexts.

## Limitations
- The study's controlled experimental design, while enabling precise measurement, may not fully represent how assumption violations occur in real-world applications
- The findings are based on specific models (GPT-3, Flan T5) and may not generalize to all NLP architectures
- The paper does not explore potential solutions or training approaches to address the identified limitations

## Confidence
- **Core claim about performance gaps**: High confidence - the systematic dataset creation and controlled experiments provide strong evidence for the significant performance differences between following and breaking contexts
- **Analysis of poor consistency**: Medium confidence - while the findings are interesting and supported by experimental data, the consistency measures may be sensitive to specific experimental conditions
- **Need for more robust models**: Low confidence - this is a reasonable inference from the results but lacks concrete proposals for how current limitations could be addressed
