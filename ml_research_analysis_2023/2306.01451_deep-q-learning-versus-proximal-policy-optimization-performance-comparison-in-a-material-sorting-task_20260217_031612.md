# Deep Q-Learning versus Proximal Policy Optimization: Performance Comparison in a Material Sorting Task

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01451
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01451
- Reference count: 20
- Primary result: PPO significantly outperforms DQN in a simulated production system for material sorting tasks

## Executive Summary
This study compares Deep Q-Learning (DQN) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithms in a simulated production system using a Petri Net-based environment. The task involves correctly assembling and sorting products in a model factory with entry, assembly station, storage, and exit points. The research demonstrates that PPO significantly outperforms DQN across all evaluation metrics, including average percentage of correctly assembled/sorted products, episode length, and success rate. The study concludes that policy-based algorithms are superior for high-dimensional state and action spaces in manufacturing tasks.

## Method Summary
The research uses a Petri Net-based simulation environment to model a production system with five modules: entry point, assembly station, storage, exit point, and feedback mechanism. DQN represents value-based RL while PPO represents policy-based RL. Both algorithms were trained on the same environment with identical reward functions, including penalties for unnecessary transitions. Performance was evaluated using average success rate, episode length, and percentage of correctly assembled/sorted products. The study specifically examined how different reward structures affected learning stability and policy performance.

## Key Results
- PPO achieved significantly higher average success rates compared to DQN across all evaluation metrics
- PPO demonstrated faster learning convergence and shorter episode lengths than DQN
- Reward function design, particularly penalties for unnecessary transitions, significantly impacted PPO's performance and stability
- PPO showed superior policy performance in high-dimensional state and action spaces typical of manufacturing tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Policy-based algorithms like PPO directly optimize the policy function, making them more suitable for continuous or high-dimensional action spaces common in manufacturing environments. Unlike value-based methods like DQN that estimate state-action values, PPO learns policies that can handle complex state representations and action combinations more efficiently. The direct policy optimization allows PPO to adapt more quickly to the dynamic nature of production systems where state transitions and action requirements can be highly variable.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do PPO and DQN algorithms compare in terms of scalability to larger, more complex manufacturing environments?
- Basis in paper: The study mentions that future work will focus on exploring the impacts of factory scaling by comparing conventional RL approaches with hierarchical methods.
- Why unresolved: The current study only examines a relatively simple model factory with 5 modules. There is no evidence of how these algorithms would perform in larger, more complex manufacturing systems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing PPO and DQN in scaled-up manufacturing environments with increased complexity, more modules, and higher-dimensional state/action spaces.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of reward function design on the stability and convergence of PPO and DQN algorithms in manufacturing tasks?
- Basis in paper: The study highlights that even small changes in reward design can cause instabilities in learned policies, particularly for PPO, and mentions the need for further investigation into reward design.
- Why unresolved: While the study shows that reward design affects performance, it does not provide a comprehensive analysis of how different reward structures impact algorithm stability and convergence.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study varying reward function parameters and analyzing their effects on algorithm stability, convergence speed, and final policy performance for both PPO and DQN.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do policy-based algorithms like PPO compare to value-based algorithms like DQN in terms of sample efficiency and learning speed for manufacturing tasks?
- Basis in paper: The study demonstrates that PPO significantly outperforms DQN in learning speed and policy performance, but does not provide a detailed comparison of sample efficiency.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses on overall performance but does not quantify the sample efficiency or learning speed in terms of data requirements or training iterations.
- What evidence would resolve it: A detailed comparison of the number of samples or training iterations required for each algorithm to reach a certain performance threshold, along with analysis of learning curves and convergence rates.

## Limitations
- Single simulated environment using Petri Net modeling may not capture all real-world manufacturing complexities
- Comparison limited to only two specific algorithms (DQN and PPO), limiting generalizability to other RL approaches
- Computational efficiency and training time requirements were not addressed, which could be crucial for practical implementation
