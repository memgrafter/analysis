# Attention at SemEval-2023 Task 10: Explainable Detection of Online Sexism (EDOS)

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID:** 2304.04610
- **Source URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04610
- **Reference count:** 3
- **Primary result:** Best system achieved macro F1 scores of 0.839, 0.5835, and 0.3356 for binary, category, and fine-grained sexism classification tasks, respectively.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a transformer-based approach for explainable detection of online sexism across three classification tasks: binary detection, sexism category identification, and fine-grained classification. The authors fine-tune RoBERTa and DeBERTa models, experiment with domain adaptive pretraining on Reddit and Gab data, and employ joint learning strategies. Their system achieved strong performance in the SemEval-2023 competition, with macro F1 scores of 0.839, 0.5835, and 0.3356 for the three tasks. Domain adaptive pretraining and model ensembling were identified as key contributors to improved accuracy.

## Method Summary
The authors employed transformer-based architectures (RoBERTa and DeBERTa) for sexism detection, experimenting with fine-tuning, domain adaptive pretraining on Reddit and Gab data, and combining multiple models. They used joint learning approaches and evaluated their systems across three tasks: binary sexism detection, sexism category identification, and fine-grained classification. The methodology involved standard preprocessing, tokenization, and evaluation using macro F1 scores as the primary metric.

## Key Results
- Best system achieved macro F1 scores of 0.839, 0.5835, and 0.3356 for tasks A, B, and C respectively in the competition
- Domain adaptive pretraining on Reddit and Gab data provided accuracy gains across all tasks
- Combining RoBERTa and DeBERTa models improved performance compared to individual models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The transformer-based approach leverages contextual word representations to capture nuanced semantic patterns in sexist language. Domain adaptive pretraining on Reddit and Gab data helps the models adapt to the informal, conversational nature of online discourse where sexism typically occurs. The combination of multiple transformer architectures provides complementary strengths in pattern recognition and generalization.

- **Component map**: Input text -> Tokenizer -> Domain Pretraining (Reddit/Gab) -> Fine-tuning (RoBERTa/DeBERTa) -> Classification Head -> Output
- **Critical path**: Text preprocessing → tokenization → domain pretraining → task-specific fine-tuning → evaluation
- **Design tradeoffs**: Choice between RoBERTa and DeBERTa based on computational resources vs. performance gains
- **Failure signatures**: Poor performance on minority classes due to class imbalance; potential overfitting on domain-specific pretraining data
- **First experiments**: 1) Baseline fine-tuning without domain adaptation; 2) Individual model evaluation (RoBERTa vs DeBERTa); 3) Ablation study of domain pretraining effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies several areas for future investigation: exploring pseudo-labeling techniques for data augmentation, addressing class imbalance through oversampling or synthetic data generation, and investigating more sophisticated ensemble methods. The authors also suggest examining model interpretability to better understand decision-making processes in sexism detection.

## Limitations
- Evaluation focused primarily on competition scores without extensive analysis of model robustness or generalization
- Lack of ablation studies to isolate the contribution of individual components
- No statistical significance testing to validate performance improvements
- Limited error analysis on false positives/negatives, particularly for minority classes
