# Global Optimality in Bivariate Gradient-based DAG Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.17378
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17378
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proves global convergence of gradient-based DAG learning for bivariate case using homotopy-based optimization with decreasing penalty coefficients

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the fundamental challenge of global convergence in gradient-based directed acyclic graph (DAG) structure learning. While DAG learning is typically a non-convex problem with spurious local minima, the authors prove that a carefully designed homotopy algorithm with decreasing penalty coefficients can globally converge to the optimal DAG structure in the bivariate case. The key insight is that by starting with a large penalty coefficient and gradually decreasing it, the algorithm can track the global minimum through the changing optimization landscape, avoiding spurious local minima. This is the first work to provide global optimality guarantees for gradient-based DAG learning.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a homotopy-based optimization scheme that solves a sequence of penalized optimization problems with decreasing penalty coefficients. The algorithm starts with a large µ₀ making the objective dominated by the convex loss term, then gradually decreases µ according to a specific schedule while using the solution from the previous iteration as initialization for the next. The method employs gradient descent (approximating gradient flow) on the penalized objective gµ(W) = µf(W) + h(W), where f is the population least squares loss and h is the acyclicity penalty function. The convergence is analyzed through continuous-time gradient flow theory, then translated to discrete-time gradient descent with appropriate step sizes and tolerance parameters.

## Key Results
- Proves global convergence of gradient-based DAG learning for bivariate case using homotopy optimization
- Shows that despite non-convex landscape with spurious local minima, careful µ scheduling ensures convergence to global optimum
- Demonstrates through experiments that naive approaches get trapped in local minima while proposed method consistently finds global optimum

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: gradient descent on gµ(W) = µf(W) + h(W) -> µk update according to schedule -> Wµk₋₁ warm-start -> convergence monitoring to WG
- Critical path: 
  1. Initialize with µ₀ and W₀
  2. Solve gradient descent for gµ₀(W) to get Wµ₀
  3. Update µ₁ according to schedule
  4. Use Wµ₀ as initialization for gµ₁(W)
  5. Repeat until µk is sufficiently small
  6. Return final Wµk as global optimum estimate
- Design tradeoffs:
  - µ scheduling speed vs. convergence reliability: faster decay risks getting trapped in local minima
  - Step size selection vs. computational efficiency: larger steps converge faster but may overshoot
  - Tolerance parameter ϵ vs. solution accuracy: smaller tolerances give better solutions but require more iterations
- Failure signatures:
  - Convergence to incorrect DAG structure (non-global minimum)
  - Oscillations or divergence in parameter updates
  - Slow convergence indicating poor initialization or µ scheduling
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify gradient descent implementation on simple convex objective before adding penalty term
  2. Test µ scheduling with different initializations to confirm basin of attraction tracking
  3. Compare convergence behavior with naive approach that uses fixed µ throughout optimization

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the homotopy-based algorithm with gradient descent converge globally to the optimal DAG structure for cases with more than two nodes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Extending our analysis to higher dimensions remains a challenging future direction" and focuses on the bivariate case as the simplest nontrivial setting.
- Why unresolved: The proof techniques and analysis rely heavily on the simplicity of the bivariate case, and the authors acknowledge that extending to higher dimensions is non-trivial.
- What evidence would resolve it: A rigorous proof demonstrating global convergence of the homotopy algorithm with gradient descent for DAG learning in cases with three or more nodes, or a counterexample showing failure of global convergence.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How sensitive is the algorithm's convergence to the choice of penalty coefficient scheduling and tolerance parameters in gradient descent?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss the importance of careful scheduling of the penalty coefficient and tolerance parameter ϵk, and show through experiments that incorrect settings can lead to convergence to spurious local minima.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides some theoretical guidance on parameter choices, the sensitivity analysis is limited, and the practical implementation may require further exploration of parameter spaces.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies systematically varying the penalty coefficient scheduling and tolerance parameters across a wide range of problem instances, quantifying the impact on convergence to the global optimum.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the homotopy-based approach be extended to handle nonlinear structural equation models (SEMs) beyond the linear case?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention the potential for using other formulations of the acyclicity constraint h and discuss the transformation from population to empirical loss, suggesting broader applicability.
- Why unresolved: The current analysis is limited to linear SEMs, and the extension to nonlinear models would require new techniques to handle the increased complexity of the loss landscape and constraint.
- What evidence would resolve it: A rigorous extension of the homotopy-based algorithm to nonlinear SEMs, demonstrating global convergence to the optimal DAG structure in a class of nonlinear models, or a proof of the limitations of the approach for certain types of nonlinear models.

## Limitations

- The global convergence guarantee is currently limited to the bivariate case, with extension to higher dimensions remaining an open challenge
- The analysis relies on specific properties of the penalty function and population loss landscape that may not generalize to all formulations
- The algorithm requires careful tuning of homotopy parameters (µ scheduling, step sizes, tolerances) which are not extensively validated across diverse settings
