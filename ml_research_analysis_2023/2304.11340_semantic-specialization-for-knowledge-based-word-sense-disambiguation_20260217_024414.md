# Semantic Specialization for Knowledge-based Word Sense Disambiguation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11340
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11340
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Outperforms previous methods on knowledge-based WSD, achieving 70.7% micro F1 on SemEval-2007 (3.9pt improvement)

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces SS-WSD, a semantic specialization method for knowledge-based word sense disambiguation (WSD) that adapts contextualized embeddings using lexical knowledge. The key innovation is a joint optimization approach that combines an Attract-Repel objective for sense pairs with a self-training objective for context-sense pairs, while constraining deviations from original BERT embeddings. This method brings semantically related senses and contexts closer together while pushing unrelated and different senses farther apart, resulting in state-of-the-art performance on knowledge-based WSD tasks.

## Method Summary
SS-WSD adapts BERT embeddings through joint optimization of two objectives: (1) Attract-Repel loss that brings related senses closer and unrelated/different senses farther apart using WordNet semantic relations, and (2) self-training loss that updates context-sense similarity by bringing the nearest neighbor sense closer. A distance constraint prevents excessive deviation from original BERT embeddings to avoid inaccurate nearest neighbor selection. The method is evaluated on standard WSD datasets and shows consistent improvements over previous adaptation methods.

## Key Results
- Achieved 70.7% micro F1 on SemEval-2007, a 3.9 percentage point improvement over previous methods
- Outperformed previous adaptation methods including SREFemb and other baselines
- Correlation between similarity characteristics of specialized embeddings (∆ρ) and WSD performance was strong (R² = 0.85)
- Try-again Mechanism (TaM) heuristic further improved performance when combined with SS-WSD

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Joint optimization of Attract-Repel and self-training objectives improves distinguishability between senses while providing pseudo-supervision for context-sense associations. The Attract-Repel objective brings related senses closer and unrelated/different senses farther away, improving separability. The self-training objective updates context-sense similarity by bringing the nearest neighbor sense closer, providing pseudo-labels. Joint optimization leverages their complementary strengths. Core assumption: Attract-Repel improves sense separability while self-training provides useful pseudo-supervision that bootstraps performance beyond static heuristics. Evidence anchors: [abstract] "The key idea is, for a given sense, to bring semantically related senses and contexts closer and send different/unrelated senses farther away. We realize this idea as the joint optimization of the Attract-Repel objective for sense pairs and the self-training objective for context-sense pairs"; [section 3.5] "We jointly optimize the Attract-Repel objective for sense pairs and the self-training objective for context-sense pairs by minimizing the weighted sum of the loss functions". Break condition: If either objective dominates too much (e.g., large α overemphasizing self-training), the complementary benefits may be lost. If semantic relations are noisy or incomplete, Attract-Repel may harm rather than help.

### Mechanism 2
Distance constraint prevents excessive deviation from original BERT embeddings, avoiding inaccurate nearest neighbor selection. The transformation functions include residual mapping bounded by hyperparameter ε, ensuring L2 distance relative to original embeddings stays within limits. This prevents the embeddings from drifting too far from the reliable BERT representations. Core assumption: BERT embeddings capture coarse-grained word meanings reasonably well, so large deviations risk introducing errors rather than improvements. Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We also impose distance constraints during adaptation to control the deviation from BERT embeddings (Fig. 1-c) because excessive deviation may cause an inaccurate nearest neighbor sense selection, which would cause a performance drop"; [section 3.4] "Fw(ˆ vw) = 2σ(FFNNw(ˆ vw))− 1, (7) Fs(ˆ es) = 2σ(FFNNs(ˆ es))− 1, (8)" with sigmoid activation bounding residual to [-1, +1]; [section 6.3] "We found that performance follows an inverted U-shaped curve along ε, indicating that a sweet spot exists". Break condition: If ε is too small, embeddings won't adapt enough to improve WSD. If ε is too large, embeddings drift excessively and self-training becomes ineffective.

### Mechanism 3
Similarity between related/different/unrelated senses relative to ground-truth context-sense pairs correlates with WSD performance. The method brings related senses and contexts closer while pushing unrelated/different senses farther away. The relative similarity differences (∆ρ) between these pairs and ground-truth pairs serve as a proxy metric that correlates with actual WSD performance. Core assumption: The geometric arrangement of embeddings in semantic space directly reflects the quality of disambiguation - closer related senses and farther unrelated senses should improve nearest neighbor selection. Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We also found that the (dis)similarity of embeddings between the related/different/unrelated senses correlates well with the performance of WSD"; [section 7.1] "Fig. 5 shows that ∆ρ correlates well with WSD task performance (R² = 0.85)"; [section 7.1] "∆ρ = 1/3(∆ρSP− ∆ρSU− ∆ρSN) to estimate the WSD performance". Break condition: If the embedding space geometry doesn't align with semantic relationships (e.g., BERT embeddings already capture meaning well), this correlation may break. If the evaluation dataset has biases, correlation may not generalize.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: BERT embeddings -> Transformation functions (FFNNs with sigmoid-bounded residuals) -> Joint optimization (Attract-Repel + self-training) -> Specialized embeddings -> Nearest neighbor WSD prediction

- **Critical path**: 1) Compute BERT embeddings for all contexts and senses; 2) Apply transformation functions to get specialized embeddings; 3) Compute similarities between context and all candidate senses; 4) Select nearest neighbor sense as prediction; 5) (Optional) Apply TaM heuristic for reranking

- **Design tradeoffs**: Freezing BERT vs fine-tuning: Method freezes BERT for efficiency and to avoid overfitting, trading potential performance gains for stability; Joint vs separate optimization: Joint optimization leverages complementary benefits but adds complexity and hyperparameter tuning challenges; Distance constraint: Prevents overfitting but may limit adaptation potential

- **Failure signatures**: Performance worse than PlainBERT: Likely due to excessive deviation from original embeddings or poor hyperparameter settings; High variance across runs: May indicate instability in the joint optimization or insufficient training data; Poor correlation with ∆ρ metric: Suggests embedding geometry doesn't align with semantic relationships

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Baseline comparison: Run PlainBERT, SREFemb, and SS-WSDemb on SemEval-2007 to verify the 3.9pt improvement claim; 2) Ablation study: Remove Attract-Repel objective, self-training objective, and distance constraint individually to confirm their contributions; 3) Distance constraint sensitivity: Sweep ε from 0.01 to 0.02 to find the optimal sweet spot and verify the inverted U-shaped curve

## Limitations
- Reliance on WordNet's lexical relations, which may not capture all semantic nuances or domain-specific senses
- Distance constraint hyperparameter ε requires careful tuning and may need dataset-specific optimization
- Performance gains may be limited by the quality and completeness of the underlying lexical knowledge

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: Core mechanism of joint optimization between Attract-Repel and self-training objectives is well-supported by ablation studies and performance improvements
- **Medium Confidence**: Distance constraints prevent performance degradation is supported by inverted U-shaped curve observation but lacks extensive exploration of why degradation occurs
- **Low Confidence**: Effectiveness of Try-again Mechanism (TaM) heuristic is demonstrated but not deeply analyzed for when it succeeds vs. introduces errors

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation robustness**: Perform systematic ablation of each component (Attract-Repel, self-training, distance constraint) across multiple random seeds and datasets to verify stability of reported performance improvements
2. **Distance constraint sensitivity analysis**: Extend the ε hyperparameter sweep beyond reported range to find failure points and test whether optimal ε transfers across different WSD datasets
3. **Semantic relation quality assessment**: Evaluate performance when using different sources of semantic relations (WordNet vs. distributional similarity measures) to determine whether gains are due to optimization framework or quality of lexical knowledge
