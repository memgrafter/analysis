# Scaling Deep Networks with the Mesh Adaptive Direct Search algorithm

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06641
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06641
- Reference count: 4
- Uses MADS to find compressed neural architectures that outperform TPE-NNI on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes using the Mesh Adaptive Direct Search (MADS) algorithm for automatic neural architecture search (NAS) to compress deep neural networks for deployment on resource-constrained devices. MADS, a derivative-free optimization method, is shown to effectively explore the design space while accounting for the expensive blackbox nature of neural network validation. The method was tested on compressing ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 and ResNet-50 on ImageNet, demonstrating superior performance compared to Bayesian optimization (TPE-NNI) in finding architectures with competitive compression rates and reduced number of trials.

## Method Summary
The authors use MADS, a derivative-free optimization algorithm, to automatically search for efficient neural network architectures by optimizing over three parameters: network depth (number of blocks), width (output channels), and resolution (input channels). The method was tested in two scenarios: unconstrained optimization maximizing accuracy on CIFAR-10, and constrained optimization minimizing computational resources while maintaining accuracy on ImageNet. The search space was defined with box constraints, and the NOMAD solver implemented MADS. Results were compared against TPE-NNI using the same search space and constraints.

## Key Results
- MADS found 29 configurations with top-1 accuracy over 94% on CIFAR-10, including solutions with one-third the parameters and half the MACs of the baseline
- On ImageNet, MADS maintained better accuracy while achieving 25% less MACs compared to ResNet-50
- TPE-NNI suffered significant accuracy drops when pursuing high compression rates in the unconstrained setting

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- MADS converges faster than random search and reinforcement learning in expensive blackbox NAS settings because it balances local exploration (poll) with global search (search phase) without requiring gradient information
- Core assumption: The objective function (validation accuracy) is expensive but deterministic enough that a local search can find improvements without gradients
- Break condition: If the validation accuracy surface is highly non-smooth or stochastic, the local poll step may get stuck

### Mechanism 2
- By framing the problem as constrained optimization, MADS avoids the accuracy drop seen in unconstrained TPE-NNI runs
- Core assumption: The constraint boundary is reachable and the feasible region contains architectures with good resource-accuracy tradeoffs
- Break condition: If the feasible region is empty or extremely small, MADS may fail to find any solution meeting the constraint

### Mechanism 3
- The three-dimensional architecture parameterization allows MADS to explore meaningful tradeoffs without exploding the search space
- Core assumption: The three parameters sufficiently span the space of interesting architectures for the target tasks
- Break condition: If more complex architectural choices are needed, the three-parameter space may be too restrictive

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Derivative-free optimization (DFO)
  - Why needed here: The validation accuracy after training has no analytic gradient with respect to architecture hyperparameters, and evaluating it is expensive
  - Quick check question: Why can't we use gradient-based methods for NAS in this context?

- Concept: Constrained vs. unconstrained optimization
  - Why needed here: The paper compares two formulations—one where accuracy is maximized without constraints, and one where accuracy is constrained while resources are minimized
  - Quick check question: What is the main risk of using an unconstrained formulation when accuracy is expensive to evaluate?

- Concept: Mesh Adaptive Direct Search algorithm
  - Why needed here: MADS is the core algorithm; understanding its poll/search structure and convergence properties is essential for interpreting the results
  - Quick check question: What guarantees does MADS provide that typical random search lacks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Baseline architecture -> NOMAD (MADS) solver -> Search space (depth, width, resolution) -> Objective (accuracy or resource usage) -> Candidate architectures
- Critical path: 1. Define search space and constraints, 2. Initialize NOMAD with baseline architecture, 3. Run search-poll iterations until budget exhausted, 4. Evaluate final candidates
- Design tradeoffs: Smaller search space → fewer trials needed but may miss good architectures; Tighter accuracy constraints → fewer feasible solutions, possibly lower compression
- Failure signatures: NOMAD converges to a single architecture quickly → search space too small or constraints too tight; All candidates fail accuracy constraint → baseline too high or space misspecified
- First 3 experiments: 1. Run NOMAD on a small synthetic dataset with known ground truth to verify it finds the optimum, 2. Compare MADS vs TPE-NNI on a toy architecture search with 5 trials, 3. Vary the accuracy constraint level on ImageNet experiment to see impact on compression rate

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the MADS algorithm's performance compare to other derivative-free optimization methods like CMA-ES or DIRECT in neural architecture search tasks?
- Basis in paper: The paper only compares MADS against TPE-NNI and mentions other derivative-free optimization methods exist, but does not test them
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on demonstrating MADS effectiveness but doesn't benchmark against other derivative-free methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Direct comparison experiments running CMA-ES, DIRECT, and MADS on the same NAS tasks with identical budgets and constraints

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the computational overhead of MADS compared to the actual training time in neural architecture search, and how does this scale with network depth and dataset size?
- Basis in paper: The paper mentions MADS is tailored for expensive blackbox functions but doesn't analyze the overhead ratio between MADS operations and training time
- Why unresolved: No systematic analysis of time complexity or overhead scaling is provided
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed timing analysis showing MADS overhead vs training time across different architectures and datasets

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the search space dimensionality affect MADS's convergence rate and solution quality compared to high-dimensional problems?
- Basis in paper: The paper uses a 3-dimensional search space but doesn't explore higher-dimensional spaces
- Why unresolved: The authors don't investigate how MADS scales with increasing search space dimensions
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with progressively higher-dimensional search spaces and analysis of convergence behavior and solution quality

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the impact of constraint formulation (unconstrained vs constrained optimization) on the final network architectures discovered by MADS?
- Basis in paper: The paper presents both unconstrained and constrained formulations but doesn't deeply analyze how the constraint affects solution characteristics
- Why unresolved: While results are presented for both formulations, there's no comparative analysis of the architectural differences they produce
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed architectural analysis comparing networks found by each formulation, including parameter distributions and layer configurations

## Limitations

- The three-dimensional parameterization may not capture all relevant architectural choices, potentially limiting solution quality
- Only compares MADS with TPE-NNI, omitting other NAS methods like random search or evolutionary algorithms
- The ImageNet experiment uses a relatively small budget of 25 trials, which may not be sufficient for the complex task

## Confidence

- High: MADS's ability to find architectures with competitive accuracy and compression rates
- Medium: Claims about MADS outperforming TPE-NNI specifically
- Low: Generalization to other architecture families beyond ResNets
