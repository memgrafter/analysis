# For Pre-Trained Vision Models in Motor Control, Not All Policy Learning Methods are Created Equal

## Executive Summary
This paper benchmarks 14 pre-trained vision models for motor control tasks across three policy learning methods: reinforcement learning (RL), behavior cloning (BC), and visual reward function (VRF). The study finds that the effectiveness of pre-trained vision models is highly dependent on the choice of downstream policy learning algorithm. RL exhibits high variability and is not reliable for model comparison, while BC and VRF show more consistent results. VRF is particularly effective when vision models capture global features and task progress, but MAE underperforms due to its anisotropic representations. BC's performance correlates strongly with linear probing loss, making it a reliable evaluation method. Overall, BC and VRF are recommended for benchmarking pre-trained vision models in motor control.

## Method Summary
The paper evaluates 14 pre-trained vision models (ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16) on 21 simulated tasks across three robot manipulation environments (Meta-World, Robosuite, Franka-Kitchen). The vision models are used as frozen encoders, with a lightweight controller trained using three policy learning methods: RL (DrQ-v2 algorithm), BC (4-layer MLP with LayerNorm), and VRF (ROT algorithm with Sinkhorn distance). Success rates are measured via inter-quartile mean (IQM) with 95% confidence intervals. Linear probing loss and k-NN classification accuracy are also computed to assess the quality of visual representations.

## Key Results
- RL exhibits high variability and is unreliable for model comparison due to randomness in exploration and initialization.
- BC and VRF yield more consistent results, making them reliable evaluation methods for pre-trained vision models.
- VRF is effective when vision models capture global features and task progress, but MAE underperforms due to its anisotropic representations.
- BC performance correlates strongly with linear probing loss, indicating its reliability in assessing vision models for encoding environment-relevant information.
- VRF correlates with ImageNet k-NN accuracy, highlighting its suitability for evaluation.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Pre-trained vision models encode general world knowledge that can be transferred to motor control tasks when the downstream policy learning method is appropriate.
- Mechanism: Pre-training on large-scale natural images captures semantic and spatial information (object locations, joint positions) that is reusable across domains. The frozen encoder extracts these features, and the lightweight controller learns task-specific mappings.
- Core assumption: The visual representations retain environment-relevant information and are sufficiently general to support downstream control without extensive fine-tuning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "These visual representations are used to replace hand-engineered ground-truth features, which are hard to estimate across diverse control tasks."
  - [section 3.1] "The first two approaches (RL and BC) are widely used in the existing literature and treat pre-trained features as representations that encode environment-related information."
  - [corpus] Found 25 related papers, average neighbor FMR=0.564, indicating moderate relevance; top papers focus on pre-training for motor control, supporting the claim that general visual representations are transferable.
- Break condition: If the pre-training dataset lacks diversity or the downstream task domain is too far from natural images, the transferred features may not encode relevant information, leading to poor performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The effectiveness of a pre-trained vision model is highly dependent on the choice of downstream policy learning algorithm.
- Mechanism: Different policy learning methods place different demands on the visual representations. RL requires representations that support exploration and reward maximization, BC needs representations that encode state information for accurate imitation, and VRF requires representations that capture task progress and global features.
- Core assumption: Each policy learning method leverages visual features differently, and no single pre-trained model excels across all methods.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that the effectiveness of pre-training is highly dependent on the choice of the downstream policy learning algorithm."
  - [section 4.1] "The effectiveness of a pre-trained vision model seems environment-dependent" and "The inconsistency exists not only in different environments, but even in different training runs in the same environment."
  - [section 4.3] "An excellent vision model for VRF should learn features at a global scale" and "MAE exhibits near zero performance on all three environments."
  - [corpus] Related papers focus on specific pre-training methods for motor control, indicating that method choice matters.
- Break condition: If a policy learning method is robust to representation quality or if a universal pre-trained model is developed that satisfies all downstream needs, this dependency may diminish.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Reliable evaluation of pre-trained vision models for motor control requires methods with low variability and consistency across environments and runs.
- Mechanism: RL exhibits high variability due to randomness in exploration and initialization, making it unreliable for model comparison. BC and VRF show more consistent results, making them better evaluation methods.
- Core assumption: Variability in evaluation metrics can obscure true model performance differences, leading to incorrect conclusions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Due to high variability, RL is not a robust evaluation method. We show that the consistent results of VRF and BC make them reliable evaluation methods."
  - [section 4.1] "There is a huge difference in the success rate between the two experiments" and "the CIs strikingly overlap for most pre-trained vision models, making it difficult to compare any two models directly."
  - [section 4.2] "BC shows a high correlation with linear probing results... These all verify the reliability of using BC to evaluate different pre-trained vision models."
  - [section 4.3] "Different pre-trained vision models yield very consistent performance when using VRF."
  - [corpus] The corpus includes papers on evaluation benchmarks for visuo-motor pre-training, supporting the importance of reliable evaluation methods.
- Break condition: If RL algorithms become deterministic or if evaluation methods are developed that can reliably account for variability, RL might become a viable evaluation method.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Frozen pre-trained vision model (encoder) -> extracts image features
  - Lightweight controller (policy head) -> maps features to actions (RL) or predicts actions (BC) or uses reward function (VRF)
  - Environments (Meta-World, Robosuite, Franka-Kitchen) -> provide tasks and rewards
  - Policy learning algorithms (RL, BC, VRF) -> train the controller using features from the frozen encoder

- Critical path:
  1. Load frozen pre-trained vision model
  2. Initialize controller network
  3. For each policy learning method:
     - RL: Interact with environment, store transitions, update controller using DrQ-v2
     - BC: Use expert demonstrations, update controller to mimic actions
     - VRF: Compute imitation rewards using ROT, update controller using DrQ-v2 with rewards
  4. Evaluate policy success rate over 100 rollouts

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Frozen vs. fine-tuned encoder: Freezing ensures consistent feature extraction and reduces computational cost but may limit adaptation to control-specific features.
  - Choice of policy learning method: Each method has different data efficiency, variability, and requirements on feature quality.
  - Number of seeds and runs: More seeds reduce uncertainty but increase computational cost.

- Failure signatures:
  - High variability in RL results across seeds or environments -> suggests RL is not reliable for evaluation
  - Near-zero performance with VRF -> indicates vision model fails to capture task progress (e.g., MAE)
  - Low correlation between linear probing loss and BC success rate -> suggests features do not encode environment-relevant information

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run BC with 1 demonstration on a simple Meta-World task using MoCo v2; expect moderate success rate as MoCo v2 is a strong baseline.
  2. Run VRF on the same task with 1 demonstration; expect higher consistency and success rate compared to RL.
  3. Run RL with 6 seeds on the same task; expect high variability and overlapping confidence intervals across different vision models.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different pre-training datasets impact the effectiveness of pre-trained vision models for motor control?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that the pre-training dataset is difficult to control and that it is not a core factor affecting downstream control tasks, but does not provide a thorough investigation of the impact of different pre-training datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses pre-trained models without retraining them on a common dataset, and does not explore the impact of different pre-training datasets on the effectiveness of vision models for motor control.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with models pre-trained on various datasets, including in-domain data, and comparing their performance on downstream motor control tasks would provide evidence on the impact of pre-training datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the underlying reasons for the high variability in reinforcement learning performance across different seeds and environments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper observes that RL performance is highly variable and unreliable due to randomness arising from exploratory choices, stochasticity in tasks, and randomly initialized parameters.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies the variability but does not provide a detailed analysis of the underlying causes or propose methods to mitigate this variability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting a detailed analysis of the sources of randomness in RL, such as exploring different exploration strategies, analyzing the impact of task stochasticity, and investigating the role of initialization, would provide insights into the causes of variability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different policy learning methods interact with the architectural differences of pre-trained vision models (e.g., ResNet vs. ViT)?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper observes that ResNet-based models tend to outperform ViT-based models, but does not provide a detailed analysis of how different policy learning methods interact with these architectural differences.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides a general observation but does not explore the specific interactions between policy learning methods and the architectural characteristics of pre-trained vision models.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments that systematically vary the architecture of pre-trained models and the policy learning methods, and analyzing the performance trends, would provide insights into the interactions between these factors.

## Limitations
- RL's high variability obscures direct model comparisons, making it unreliable as an evaluation method.
- The study only tests BC and VRF with 1 or 2 expert demonstrations, which may not generalize to settings requiring more data.
- The analysis focuses on frozen encoders without exploring fine-tuning, which could potentially improve performance.

## Confidence
- High confidence: BC and VRF are more reliable evaluation methods than RL
- Medium confidence: VRF effectiveness correlates with global feature learning
- Medium confidence: BC reliability correlates with linear probing loss
- Low confidence: RL is not useful for model comparison
