# Blackout Diffusion: Generative Diffusion Models in Discrete-State Spaces
arXiv ID: 2305.11089
Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11089
Reference count: 40
Key outcome: Develops exact reverse-time processes for discrete-state diffusion models, demonstrating feasibility on CIFAR-10 (FID: 4.77), binarized MNIST (FID: 0.02), and CelebA without continuous-state approximations.

## Executive Summary
This paper establishes a theoretical foundation for discrete-state diffusion models using arbitrary Markov processes, departing from traditional continuous-state approaches. The authors derive exact reverse-time processes and score functions for discrete-state diffusion, creating a framework analogous to Anderson's continuous-state formulation. Their "Blackout Diffusion" model demonstrates that discrete diffusion modeling is viable without Gaussian approximations, showing competitive performance on standard benchmarks while enabling exploration of non-Gaussian diffusion paradigms.

## Method Summary
The authors develop a theoretical framework for generative diffusion models in discrete-state spaces by deriving exact reverse-time processes and score functions using arbitrary Markov processes. Unlike previous variational approaches, they establish a discrete analog to Anderson's continuous-state formulation. The proposed Blackout Diffusion uses a pure-death process that learns to generate samples from a blank image state. This approach eliminates the need for continuous-state approximations while maintaining theoretical rigor. The forward and reverse processes are decoupled from traditional noisification/denoising paradigms, enabling more flexible modeling of discrete data distributions.

## Key Results
- Achieved FID score of 4.77 on CIFAR-10 at 32x32 resolution
- Obtained FID score of 0.02 on binarized MNIST dataset
- Demonstrated successful generation on CelebA faces dataset
- Showed feasibility of discrete diffusion without continuous-state approximations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The theoretical framework succeeds by establishing exact reverse-time processes for discrete-state spaces through arbitrary Markov processes. By deriving precise score functions and avoiding continuous approximations, the model maintains theoretical rigor while operating directly in discrete domains. The decoupling of forward/reverse processes from traditional noisification/denoising enables more flexible modeling that can better capture discrete data structures.
