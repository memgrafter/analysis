# Self-Sampling Training and Evaluation for the Accuracy-Bias Tradeoff in Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.03419
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03419
- **Reference count**: 20
- **Primary result**: SSTE framework achieves 3.75%, 7.20%, and 12.11% gains on core financial metrics in online A/B tests on Tencent Licaitong

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of balancing accuracy and bias in industrial recommendation systems, where indiscriminate debiasing can harm performance. The proposed Self-Sampling Training and Evaluation (SSTE) framework introduces a three-module approach that generates auxiliary subsets with varying bias degrees, jointly learns from original and sampled data, and constructs robust references for model selection. The method demonstrates consistent improvements over state-of-the-art debiasing methods both offline and online, with particular success in financial recommendation contexts.

## Method Summary
The SSTE framework operates through three interconnected modules. The self-sampling module generates auxiliary subsets using truncated inverse propensity score (IPS) to create data with varying bias degrees. The self-training module jointly learns from both original and sampled data, allowing the model to infer which biases are beneficial for recommendation performance. The self-evaluation module constructs robust reference sets for model selection, addressing the challenge of evaluating debiasing effectiveness without clean ground truth data. The framework is designed to be compatible with existing recommendation architectures and requires no specialized data or training methods, making it practical for industrial deployment.

## Key Results
- SSTE consistently outperforms state-of-the-art debiasing methods on both public and real-world datasets in offline experiments
- Online A/B tests on Tencent Licaitong show 3.75%, 7.20%, and 12.11% gains on core financial metrics
- The framework demonstrates effectiveness across different recommendation scenarios while preserving beneficial biases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
SSTE addresses the fundamental tradeoff between accuracy and bias by recognizing that not all biases are harmful. The self-sampling mechanism creates controlled environments with varying bias levels, allowing the self-training module to learn which biases contribute positively to recommendation performance. The self-evaluation component provides robust model selection criteria that don't require clean ground truth data, which is typically unavailable in real-world scenarios. This three-stage approach enables the system to distinguish between harmful position bias and beneficial popularity bias, for example, while maintaining overall recommendation quality.

## Foundational Learning
- **Truncated Inverse Propensity Scoring (IPS)**: A technique for reweighting samples based on their selection probability, used here to generate auxiliary datasets with controlled bias levels. Why needed: Enables creation of diverse training subsets without requiring separate unbiased datasets. Quick check: Verify IPS weights properly adjust for position and popularity biases in sampled subsets.
- **Self-training in debiasing context**: Joint learning from original and bias-controlled data to identify beneficial biases. Why needed: Allows model to learn which biases improve recommendation quality rather than blindly removing all biases. Quick check: Monitor training loss convergence across original and sampled data streams.
- **Robust reference construction**: Methods for evaluating model performance when ground truth unbiased data is unavailable. Why needed: Essential for model selection in real-world scenarios where clean validation data doesn't exist. Quick check: Compare reference-based evaluation against known-bias scenarios to validate reference quality.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Self-Sampling -> Self-Training -> Self-Evaluation

Critical path: The self-sampling module generates auxiliary datasets, which feed into the self-training module for joint learning, while the self-evaluation module provides ongoing feedback for model selection and refinement throughout training.

Design tradeoffs: The framework trades computational overhead for better bias management, requiring generation of multiple auxiliary datasets. The truncated IPS approach balances between bias control and sample diversity. The self-evaluation component adds complexity but eliminates dependency on clean validation data.

Failure signatures: Poor self-sampling quality leads to ineffective bias isolation. Inadequate self-training causes loss of beneficial biases along with harmful ones. Weak self-evaluation results in suboptimal model selection and potential performance degradation.

Three first experiments:
1. Validate self-sampling effectiveness by measuring bias distribution differences between original and sampled datasets
2. Test self-training module with controlled bias injection to verify beneficial bias preservation
3. Evaluate self-evaluation component by comparing reference-based selection against ground truth in synthetic bias scenarios

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper acknowledges that its offline experimental validation relies heavily on synthetic bias injection rather than real-world biased data, which may not fully capture the complexity of actual recommendation environments where multiple bias sources interact. The assumption that truncated inverse propensity scoring can effectively isolate beneficial versus harmful biases remains theoretical, with limited empirical validation across diverse recommendation contexts.

## Limitations
- Offline experiments rely on synthetic bias injection rather than real-world biased data, potentially missing complex interactions between multiple bias sources
- Online A/B testing results are based on a single deployment in a financial recommendation system, raising generalizability concerns to other domains
- Limited empirical validation of the truncated IPS approach for distinguishing beneficial from harmful biases across different recommendation contexts

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The three-module architecture design is sound and the compatibility with existing recommendation models is well-established
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of self-sampling for bias identification, given the limited empirical validation across different bias types
- **Medium confidence**: The offline-to-online correlation, as the paper provides limited analysis of how offline metrics translate to the observed online improvements

## Next Checks
1. Deploy SSTE across multiple recommendation domains (e-commerce, content, social media) to assess generalizability beyond financial services
2. Conduct ablation studies specifically isolating the impact of each module (self-sampling, self-training, self-evaluation) on final performance
3. Validate the truncated IPS sampling approach against real-world biased datasets where ground truth bias sources are known, rather than relying on synthetic bias injection
