# Does Black-box Attribute Inference Attacks on Graph Neural Networks Constitute Privacy Risk?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.00578
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00578
- Reference count: 35
- Primary result: Black-box AIA on GNNs reveals no significantly more information than missing value estimation techniques

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether black-box attribute inference attacks (AIA) on Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) pose a significant privacy risk. The authors propose several attack methods that infer sensitive user attributes based on public or non-sensitive attributes, varying adversarial knowledge and assumptions. Their empirical findings reveal that when an attacker has black-box access to the target model, GNNs generally do not reveal significantly more information compared to standard missing value estimation techniques. This challenges the conventional assumption that GNNs inherently leak more private information through attribute inference attacks.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two black-box AIA methods (Fp-ma and Fp) and a shadow-based attack (Sa), compared against a random baseline (Ri). The experiments train a 2-layer GCN on three private datasets (credit defaulter graph, Facebook, LastFM) and two benchmark datasets (Cora, PubMed) with binary or continuous sensitive attributes. The attacks are evaluated on 100 candidate nodes with missing sensitive attributes, measuring percentage of correctly inferred attributes for binary values and mean squared error for continuous values. The study varies the amount of information available to the attacker (public attributes, graph structure, model outputs) and compares AIA performance against standard missing value estimation techniques.

## Key Results
- Black-box AIA methods perform similarly to baseline imputation techniques on tested datasets
- Graph structure provides more value for inferring continuous attributes than binary attributes
- Model confidence scores can help identify correct sensitive attribute values when combined with estimated attributes
- The privacy risk of black-box AIA on GNNs is comparable to traditional missing value estimation problems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Feature propagation-based attribute inference attacks (Fp and Fp-ma) can infer missing node attributes without access to the trained model.
- Mechanism: These attacks reconstruct missing sensitive attributes by propagating known attribute values from neighboring nodes using a feature propagation algorithm, iteratively updating the missing attributes until convergence.
- Core assumption: The missing attribute values are correlated with the known attribute values of neighboring nodes in the graph.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our findings reveal that when an attacker has black-box access to the target model, GNNs generally do not reveal significantly more information compared to missing value estimation techniques."
  - [section]: "The feature propagation algorithm is a method for constructing missing features by propagating the known features in the graph."
- Break condition: If the graph structure is sparse or the attribute values are not correlated among neighboring nodes, the feature propagation algorithm will fail to accurately infer the missing attributes.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Querying the target model with estimated attributes and using the model's confidence score can help identify the correct sensitive attribute values.
- Mechanism: The attacker iteratively queries the target model with the estimated attributes, computes the confidence score for each query, and selects the node with the highest confidence score that exceeds a threshold as the inferred attribute value.
- Core assumption: The target model's confidence score is a reliable indicator of the correctness of the inferred attribute values.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The underlying idea is that a model that is confident in its prediction will assign a higher probability to the corresponding class label. Thus, the attacker leverages this confidence as a measure of the confidence score."
- Break condition: If the target model's confidence score is not a reliable indicator of the correctness of the inferred attribute values, the attacker may select incorrect attribute values.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The graph structure plays a significant role in inferring sensitive continuous attribute values but not binary attribute values.
- Mechanism: The connectivity patterns of nodes in the graph structure allow for the propagation of information and the inference of missing continuous attribute values. However, for binary attribute values, the decision boundaries are more distinct, and the graph structure has less impact.
- Core assumption: The graph structure captures meaningful relationships between nodes that can be leveraged for inferring missing attribute values.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "For inferring continuous attribute, the results emphasize the importance of utilizing the graph structure, and additional information in improving the accuracy of inferring the sensitive attributes."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence, as the corpus does not provide specific information about the role of graph structure in inferring continuous attribute values.
- Break condition: If the graph structure does not capture meaningful relationships between nodes or if the attribute values are not correlated with the graph structure, the inference of continuous attribute values will be less accurate.

- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses a 2-layer GCN for experiments but does not explore other GNN architectures.
- Why unresolved: The study focuses specifically on GCNs and does not extend to other popular GNN architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments applying the same attack methods (Fp-ma, Ri-ma, Fp, Ri) across different GNN architectures (GraphSAGE, GAT, GIN) on the same datasets to measure inference success rates.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of graph structure properties (e.g., node degree distribution, clustering coefficient) on the success of attribute inference attacks?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper varies graph structure access (full graph vs KNN) but does not analyze specific graph properties.
- Why unresolved: While the paper examines whether having graph structure helps, it does not investigate how specific structural properties affect attack success.
- What evidence would resolve it: Correlation analysis between graph metrics (degree distribution, clustering coefficient, etc.) and inference success rates across multiple datasets with varying structural properties.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do attribute inference attacks perform under more realistic threat models where the attacker has limited knowledge about the training process or model hyperparameters?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper assumes the attacker knows the data distribution and model architecture.
- Why unresolved: The threat model assumes significant prior knowledge about the target model and data distribution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments where the attacker has partial or no knowledge about the target model's architecture, hyperparameters, or training process, measuring how this affects inference success rates.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can defenses specifically designed for attribute inference attacks on GNNs effectively mitigate privacy risks without significantly degrading model performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on attack methods but does not explore potential defenses.
- Why unresolved: While the paper establishes the privacy risks, it does not investigate countermeasures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of GNN-specific defenses (e.g., differential privacy, adversarial training, feature masking) on the same datasets, measuring both privacy protection (reduced inference success) and utility preservation (model accuracy).

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on synthetic attacks rather than real-world adversary behavior, limiting ecological validity
- Performance comparisons between AIA and missing value estimation may be dataset-dependent and not generalize across all graph structures
- The analysis focuses primarily on GCN models, leaving uncertainty about transferability to other GNN architectures

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The empirical finding that black-box AIA performance is comparable to baseline imputation methods on tested datasets
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that graph structure plays a more significant role in inferring continuous versus binary attributes
- **Medium Confidence**: The conclusion that black-box AIA on GNNs may not constitute a significant privacy risk beyond existing methods
