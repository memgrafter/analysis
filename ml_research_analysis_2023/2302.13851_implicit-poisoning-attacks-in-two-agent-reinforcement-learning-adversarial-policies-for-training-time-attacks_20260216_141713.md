# Implicit Poisoning Attacks in Two-Agent Reinforcement Learning: Adversarial Policies for Training-Time Attacks

## Quick Facts

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates implicit poisoning attacks in two-agent reinforcement learning scenarios, where an attacker manipulates a peer agent's policy to force a victim agent to adopt a target policy. The attack is formulated as a bi-level optimization problem that minimizes the cost of deviating from the default peer policy while ensuring the victim adopts the target policy. The authors establish that determining the feasibility of such attacks is NP-hard, highlighting the complexity of these attacks compared to standard environment poisoning. Two algorithmic approaches are proposed: a model-based conservative policy search for tabular policies and a model-free alternating policy updates method for parametric policies. Experiments on various environments demonstrate the effectiveness of these approaches in finding cost-efficient adversarial policies.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes two algorithmic approaches to find adversarial peer policies that force the victim to adopt a target policy. The first approach is model-based and uses conservative policy search for tabular policies. It alternates between computing a new peer policy that maximizes the victim's Q-value for the target policy while minimizing its cost, and updating the victim's policy using soft policy iteration. The second approach is model-free and uses alternating policy updates for parametric policies. It updates the peer policy using policy gradient ascent to maximize the victim's Q-value for the target policy while minimizing its cost, and updates the victim's policy using a modified soft actor-critic algorithm.

## Key Results
- The feasibility of implicit poisoning attacks in two-agent RL is NP-hard, contrasting with standard environment poisoning attacks which are always feasible.
- The model-based approach can find cost-efficient adversarial peer policies in tabular environments, outperforming the baseline of not modifying the peer policy.
- The model-free approach can find adversarial peer policies in environments with parametric policies, such as deep neural networks, and achieves higher target policy adoption rates compared to the baseline.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism behind implicit poisoning attacks is the manipulation of the peer agent's policy to influence the victim agent's behavior. By carefully crafting the peer policy, the attacker can create situations where the victim is incentivized to adopt the target policy to maximize its own reward. The bi-level optimization formulation ensures that the adversarial peer policy is cost-efficient, minimizing the deviation from the default peer policy while still achieving the desired effect on the victim.
