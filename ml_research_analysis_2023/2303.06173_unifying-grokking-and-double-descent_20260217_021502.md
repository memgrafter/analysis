# Unifying Grokking and Double Descent

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06173
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06173
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 13
- Primary result: Proposes grokking and double descent are instances of the same learning phenomenon involving three pattern types with different learning speeds and generalization abilities

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a unified framework for understanding both grokking and double descent phenomena in neural network training. The authors propose that these seemingly distinct behaviors arise from the same underlying learning dynamics involving three types of patterns: fast but poorly-generalizing patterns, medium-speed poorly-generalizing patterns, and slow but well-generalizing patterns. By modeling neural network training as a process of learning these patterns with different learning curves and generalization abilities, the framework can reproduce both grokking (delayed generalization after long training) and double descent (non-monotonic generalization curves with model size). The authors provide empirical evidence by demonstrating "model-wise grokking" where varying model capacity instead of training steps produces similar delayed generalization behavior.

## Method Summary
The authors formalize neural network training as pattern learning using sigmoid functions for pattern predictiveness over time, with each pattern having distinct learning speeds and generalization parameters. They introduce the concept of preferred patterns weighted more heavily in test accuracy calculations to model inductive biases. The framework is validated through transformer experiments on modular division tasks, where varying embedding dimensions (model size) produces delayed generalization curves analogous to epoch-wise grokking. The modular division task uses decoder-only transformers with causal attention masking, trained with AdamW optimizer and weight decay on division mod 97 problems.

## Key Results
- Demonstrates that grokking and double descent can be understood as two instances of the same learning phenomenon
- Provides first demonstration of "model-wise grokking" where increasing model size instead of training steps leads to delayed generalization
- Shows that combining three pattern types (fast/poor, medium/poor, slow/good generalization) can produce both grokking and double descent curves
- Empirical validation using transformer models on modular division tasks with varying embedding dimensions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Grokking and double descent are manifestations of the same underlying learning dynamics involving three pattern types with different learning speeds and generalization abilities
- Mechanism: The model proposes that neural network training can be decomposed into learning distinct "patterns" - fast but poorly-generalizing patterns, medium-speed poorly-generalizing patterns, and slow but well-generalizing patterns. The slow patterns are eventually preferred by inductive biases, causing a transition from poor to good generalization
- Core assumption: Neural networks can be modeled as learning discrete patterns with distinct learning curves and generalization properties
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We hypothesize that grokking and double descent can be understood as instances of the same learning dynamics within a framework of pattern learning speeds"
  - [section] "We propose an abstract model of neural network training as pattern learning" with equations for pattern predictiveness (pi(t)) and test accuracy weighted by generalization parameters
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus mentions related work on pattern learning speeds but doesn't directly confirm the three-pattern model
- Break condition: If empirical evidence shows that generalization improvements cannot be decomposed into distinct pattern learning dynamics with varying speeds and generalization abilities

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The same learning dynamics apply when varying model capacity instead of training time (model-wise grokking)
- Mechanism: Patterns can be viewed as functions of model size as well as training time, and the learning dynamics transfer between these two cases. Increasing model capacity mimics the effect of additional training steps
- Core assumption: Effective model complexity and training time are interchangeable in terms of their effect on pattern development
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We propose that this framework also applies when varying model capacity instead of optimization steps, and provide the first demonstration of model-wise grokking"
  - [section] "Patterns can be viewed as a function of model size as well as a function of training time, and learning dynamics are similar between these two cases"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence - the corpus mentions double descent occurring with model size variation, supporting the capacity-time duality
- Break condition: If experiments show that model-wise grokking exhibits fundamentally different dynamics than epoch-wise grokking that cannot be explained by the same pattern framework

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Preferred patterns with better generalization eventually dominate the model's behavior despite being slower to learn
- Mechanism: The model introduces a "preferred pattern" concept where certain patterns are weighted more heavily in the test accuracy calculation. This represents inductive biases favoring better-generalizing solutions
- Core assumption: Training dynamics include mechanisms that eventually favor patterns with better generalization properties
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We additionally introduce the notion of a preferred pattern, which we use to model the effect of inductive biases on pattern development"
  - [section] "This last pattern is ultimately preferred by the inductive biases of the model"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus mentions pattern learning but doesn't specifically address inductive bias mechanisms
- Break condition: If empirical results show that generalization improvements occur without any preference mechanism favoring better-generalizing patterns

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Pattern learning dynamics with different learning speeds and generalization abilities
  - Why needed here: The entire framework depends on decomposing neural network learning into distinct patterns with different characteristics
  - Quick check question: Can you explain why a slow-learning pattern with good generalization might eventually outperform fast-learning patterns with poor generalization?

- Concept: Effective model complexity and its relationship to training time
  - Why needed here: The model-wise grokking demonstration requires understanding how model capacity functions similarly to training duration
  - Quick check question: How does varying embedding dimension in the transformer experiments relate to effective model complexity?

- Concept: Double descent phenomenon and its causes
  - Why needed here: The paper builds on double descent literature to propose a unified framework
  - Quick check question: What causes the initial improvement, subsequent worsening, and eventual improvement again in double descent curves?

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation beyond simple modular division task
- Pattern framework remains abstract without direct evidence that real networks learn these specific pattern types
- Capacity-time duality may not hold precisely across all model architectures and regimes

## Confidence
- Theoretical framework coherence: High
- Empirical validation strength: Medium
- Pattern decomposition mechanism: Medium-High
- Capacity-time duality precision: Medium

## Next Checks
1. **Pattern decomposition validation**: Apply the framework to analyze actual trained networks on modular division tasks to identify whether the learned weights can be decomposed into the three pattern types predicted by the model, and whether their learning dynamics match the theoretical curves.

2. **Generalization to other tasks**: Test whether the same three-pattern framework can explain grokking and double descent on tasks beyond modular arithmetic, such as algorithmic tasks (sorting, searching) or natural language tasks where these phenomena have been observed.

3. **Capacity-time transfer precision**: Conduct controlled experiments varying both model capacity and training steps simultaneously on the same task to quantify how precisely the framework's capacity-time duality holds across different regimes and model architectures.
