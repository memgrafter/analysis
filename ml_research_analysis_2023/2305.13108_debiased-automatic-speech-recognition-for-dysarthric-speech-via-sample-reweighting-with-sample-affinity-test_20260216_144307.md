# Debiased Automatic Speech Recognition for Dysarthric Speech via Sample Reweighting with Sample Affinity Test

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13108
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13108
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Re-SAT improves ASR performance on dysarthric speech across all intelligibility levels without degrading performance on healthy speech

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses bias in automatic speech recognition (ASR) for dysarthric speech by introducing a novel sample reweighting method called Re-SAT. The approach estimates bias-blocking samples using a sample affinity test and upweights them during training, while downweighting bias-accelerating samples. Re-SAT improves ASR performance on dysarthric speech across all intelligibility levels without degrading performance on healthy speech. For example, on UASpeech corpus, Re-SAT reduces word error rate by 12.02% for very low intelligibility dysarthric speakers and 11.10% for low intelligibility speakers compared to empirical risk minimization. The method also improves performance on healthy speakers by 10.53%. The paper demonstrates that Re-SAT effectively mitigates bias in ASR for dysarthric speech while maintaining or improving performance on other groups.

## Method Summary
Re-SAT is a sample reweighting method for debiasing ASR on dysarthric speech. It estimates bias-conflicting samples based on largest losses, tests sample affinity using one-step lookahead to measure debiasing helpfulness, normalizes weights via rank-based exponential mapping, and trains with weighted average loss. The method fine-tunes Whisper-tiny (39M parameters) on UASpeech corpus using AdamW optimizer with learning rate 1e-5 and weight decay 0.1 for 30 epochs. Training uses Blocks 1 and 3 for training, Block 2 for testing, with only microphone M5 recordings included.

## Key Results
- Re-SAT reduces WER by 12.02% for very low intelligibility dysarthric speakers and 11.10% for low intelligibility speakers compared to ERM
- Re-SAT improves performance on healthy speakers by 10.53% while maintaining gains on dysarthric speech
- Re-SAT achieves consistent improvements across all intelligibility levels: very low (12.02%), low (11.10%), mid (8.50%), and high (7.34%)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Re-SAT improves ASR performance on dysarthric speech by reweighting samples based on their debiasing helpfulness.
- Mechanism: Re-SAT estimates bias-conflicting samples using loss, then measures debiasing helpfulness using sample affinity test. Samples with higher affinity to bias-conflicting samples are upweighted, while those with lower affinity are downweighted.
- Core assumption: Samples with higher affinity to bias-conflicting samples are more helpful for debiasing the model.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Re-SAT systematically measures the debiasing helpfulness of the given data sample and then mitigates the bias by debiasing helpfulness-based sample reweighting."
  - [section 3.2]: "Sample affinity test (SAT) systematically measures debiasing helpfulness. SAT is based on the novel metric dubbed sample affinity, which denotes a training effect of the given sample to the other samples' loss."
  - [corpus]: Weak - The corpus provides related papers but no direct evidence on Re-SAT's specific mechanism.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Re-SAT's normalized reweighting stabilizes learning by reducing unwanted shifts in the learning rate.
- Mechanism: Re-SAT normalizes sample affinity by extracting the rank of the sample affinity with respect to the descending order within the batch. This rank-based normalization reduces the impact of variations in sample affinity values.
- Core assumption: Rank-based normalization of sample affinity is more stable than using raw affinity values.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3]: "Sample affinity on the bias-conflicting samples approximates the bias-blocking effect of a given sample. However, sample affinity is not an absolute but relative score which depends on the bias-conflicting sample set, the current state of the model, and the maturity of the model. Therefore, Re-SAT does not directly map the sample affinity to weights. Instead, Re-SAT normalizes the sample affinity by extracting the rank of the sample affinity with respect to the descending order within the batch. This normalization stabilizes unwanted shifts of the learning rate."
  - [corpus]: Weak - No direct evidence in the corpus on the effectiveness of rank-based normalization.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Re-SAT's iterative estimation of bias-conflicting samples improves debiasing by adapting to the changing model state.
- Mechanism: Unlike previous methods that fix the bias-conflicting sample set early in training, Re-SAT continuously estimates these samples based on the current model state. This allows the method to adapt to changes in the model's bias as training progresses.
- Core assumption: The set of bias-conflicting samples changes as the model learns, and continuous re-estimation improves debiasing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.1]: "Unlike the previous research [6] which fixes the estimation results, Re-SAT keeps estimating the bias-conflicting samples during training to accurately reflect the current state of the model. That is, the estimation result for each sample can be modified throughout training."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Bias-conflicting sample estimation -> Sample affinity test -> Normalized reweighting -> Training with reweighted samples
- Critical path: The critical path is the sequence of components that directly contribute to the final debiased model: bias-conflicting sample estimation → sample affinity test → normalized reweighting → training.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Choosing the number of bias-conflicting samples (K) involves a tradeoff between capturing more underfitted samples and introducing noise.
  - The choice of normalization method (rank-based vs. raw values) involves a tradeoff between stability and sensitivity to affinity values.
- Failure signatures:
  - If the model performance on dysarthric speech does not improve, it may indicate issues with the sample affinity metric or the reweighting process.
  - If the model performance on healthy speech degrades significantly, it may indicate over-correction of bias.

## Limitations
- The exact Whisper-tiny checkpoint version, preprocessing pipeline, and hyperparameters for sample affinity calculation remain unspecified.
- The paper lacks ablation studies isolating each component of Re-SAT to quantify individual contributions to performance improvements.
- Generalizability to other ASR architectures beyond Whisper-tiny remains untested.

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Re-SAT improves ASR performance on dysarthric speech across all intelligibility levels (12.02% for very low, 11.10% for low, 8.50% for mid, 7.34% for high).
- Medium Confidence: Re-SAT improves performance on healthy speakers (10.53% WER reduction) while maintaining gains on dysarthric speech, but requires further validation.
- Low Confidence: Mechanism claims about why Re-SAT works (sample affinity captures debiasing helpfulness, rank-based normalization is optimal) lack direct supporting evidence.
