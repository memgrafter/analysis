# PA&DA: Jointly Sampling PAth and DAta for Consistent NAS

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.14772
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14772
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Achieves highest Kendall's Tau of 0.713 on NAS-Bench-201 and superior performance on DARTS and ProxylessNAS search spaces

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of large gradient variance in supernet training during one-shot neural architecture search (NAS), which leads to poor ranking consistency of sub-models. The authors propose PA&DA, a method that jointly optimizes path and data sampling distributions using normalized gradient norms to minimize variance. By employing importance sampling for both paths and data, the method significantly improves ranking consistency while maintaining negligible computational overhead. Extensive experiments on NAS-Bench-201, DARTS, and ProxylessNAS demonstrate that PA&DA achieves state-of-the-art ranking consistency and competitive search performance.

## Method Summary
The proposed method consists of two components: Path Importance Sampling (PA) and Data Importance Sampling (DA). During supernet training, PA&DA tracks the gradient norms for each operation (path) and each training sample. The sampling probabilities for paths and data are then updated based on their normalized gradient norms, with higher probability assigned to samples with larger gradient norms. To prevent degenerate distributions, smoothing parameters δ and τ linearly increase from 0 to 1 during training, mixing the importance distribution with a uniform distribution. The method is implemented as a lightweight wrapper around existing supernet training procedures, requiring only minor modifications to track gradient norms and update sampling distributions.

## Key Results
- Achieves Kendall's Tau of 0.713 on NAS-Bench-201, outperforming all existing methods
- Improves ranking consistency on DARTS and ProxylessNAS search spaces while maintaining competitive search performance
- Demonstrates significantly lower gradient variance during supernet training compared to baseline methods
- Only requires negligible additional computation cost beyond standard supernet training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Optimizing path and data sampling distributions using normalized gradient norm reduces gradient variance in supernet training.
- Mechanism: The authors derive that the optimal sampling probability is proportional to the normalized gradient norm of paths and data. By sampling paths and data with higher gradient norms more frequently, the overall gradient variance of the supernet is minimized.
- Core assumption: The gradient variance of the supernet is directly related to the sampling distributions of paths and data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We theoretically derive the relationship between the gradient variance and the sampling distributions, and reveal that the optimal sampling probability is proportional to the normalized gradient norm of path and training data."
  - [section 3.2] "Consequently, we can conclude that the optimal path sampling probability p⋆i is proportional to the normalized gradient norm of the sub-model αi, saying that sampling the sub-model with a larger gradient norm can reduce the gradient variance for the supernet training."
- Break condition: If the gradient norms do not accurately reflect the importance of paths and data, or if the relationship between gradient variance and sampling distributions is not as strong as assumed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Importance sampling of paths and data improves supernet ranking consistency.
- Mechanism: By sampling paths and data with higher gradient norms more frequently, the supernet training focuses on the most informative samples. This leads to a more accurate ranking of sub-models based on their performance.
- Core assumption: Sampling paths and data with higher gradient norms leads to more accurate performance estimation of sub-models.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our method only requires negligible computation cost for optimizing the sampling distributions of path and data, but achieves lower gradient variance during supernet training and better generalization performance for the supernet, resulting in a more consistent NAS."
  - [section 3.4] "We propose a novel and effective importance-based sampling strategy for training the supernet, including path importance sampling and data importance sampling."
- Break condition: If the ranking consistency does not improve despite the reduction in gradient variance, or if the importance sampling strategy does not lead to better generalization performance.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The smoothing parameters δ and τ help to avoid degenerate sampling distributions.
- Mechanism: The smoothing parameters add the importance sampling distribution to the uniform sampling distribution, ensuring that all paths and data have a non-zero probability of being sampled. This prevents the sampling distributions from becoming too skewed towards a few high-gradient paths and data.
- Core assumption: Adding the importance sampling distribution to the uniform sampling distribution with a smoothing parameter prevents degenerate sampling distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 3.3] "To handle those parameter-free operations and avoid the meaningless gradient information at early epochs, we employ a smoothing parameter δ to add our importance sampling distribution and the uniform sampling distribution."
  - [section 3.3] "Analogously, we use a smoothing parameter τ to add our importance sampling distribution and the uniform sampling distribution together to tackle the above problem."
- Break condition: If the smoothing parameters do not effectively prevent degenerate sampling distributions, or if they introduce too much noise into the sampling process.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Supernet -> Path Sampling -> Data Sampling -> Gradient Norm Calculation -> Sampling Distribution Update

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize the supernet and sampling distributions.
  2. For each training epoch:
     a. Sample a path and mini-batch of data based on the current distributions.
     b. Train the supernet on the sampled path and data.
     c. Calculate the gradient norms for the sampled path and data.
     d. Update the path and data sampling distributions using the calculated gradient norms.
  3. After training, evaluate the sub-models using the optimized supernet weights.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Smoothing parameters (δ and τ) vs. degenerate sampling distributions: The smoothing parameters help avoid degenerate distributions but may introduce noise into the sampling process.
  - Update frequency of sampling distributions vs. computational cost: Updating the distributions more frequently may lead to better performance but increases computational cost.

- Failure signatures:
  - High gradient variance despite optimized sampling distributions: This may indicate that the relationship between gradient variance and sampling distributions is not as strong as assumed.
  - Degenerate sampling distributions: This may occur if the smoothing parameters are not set appropriately or if the gradient norms are not calculated correctly.
  - Poor ranking consistency: This may indicate that the importance sampling strategy does not effectively capture the importance of paths and data.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the path importance sampling (PA) component and evaluate its impact on gradient variance and ranking consistency on NAS-Bench-201.
  2. Implement the data importance sampling (DA) component and evaluate its impact on gradient variance and ranking consistency on NAS-Bench-201.
  3. Combine PA and DA components and evaluate their joint impact on gradient variance, ranking consistency, and search performance on DARTS and ProxylessNAS search spaces.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but based on the content, potential open questions include:

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the optimal sampling probability scale with different gradient norm normalization schemes beyond the sum of gradient norms used in the paper?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper derives that optimal sampling probability is proportional to normalized gradient norm and uses sum of gradient norms for path sampling and per-sample gradient norm for data sampling.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests one normalization approach for each sampling type. Different normalization schemes (e.g., max, L2 norm) could yield different scaling relationships and performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparison of different normalization schemes for both path and data sampling showing which normalization yields optimal performance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does the PA&DA method maintain its performance advantage when applied to extremely large-scale NAS search spaces with millions of possible architectures?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The method shows effectiveness on DARTS and ProxylessNAS search spaces but these are relatively small compared to state-of-the-art search spaces.
- Why unresolved: The computational and memory requirements for tracking gradient norms scale with search space size, potentially diminishing returns.
- What evidence would resolve it: Benchmarking PA&DA on contemporary large-scale search spaces (e.g., OFA, ViT architectures) showing consistent performance gains.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the smoothing parameters (δ and τ) and the convergence rate of the supernet training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper linearly increases smoothing parameters from 0 to 1 but does not provide theoretical analysis of their impact on convergence.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides empirical observations of different scheduling strategies without theoretical justification for why linear increase is optimal.
- What evidence would resolve it: Mathematical proof or rigorous empirical study showing how different smoothing parameter schedules affect convergence rates and final performance.

## Limitations

- The theoretical derivation assumes smooth optimization landscape and may not fully capture real-world training dynamics with complex architectures
- The effectiveness of the method depends heavily on the search space size and complexity, with potential diminishing returns for very large search spaces
- The proposed method introduces additional hyperparameters (δ and τ) that require tuning for optimal performance
