# Characterizing Novelty in the Military Domain

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.12314

## Executive Summary
This research addresses the challenge of enabling AI agents to robustly handle novelty in military environments, where adversaries and unexpected conditions frequently arise. Using DARPA’s SAIL-ON framework, the team maps military novelty types to a domain-independent ontology and introduces statistical modeling to represent uncertainty in parameters such as missile range. They generate novelty-rich tactical scenarios (e.g., increased SAM ranges) within the SOLVE simulation environment and define evaluation metrics combining mission success and novelty detection rates (CDT, IDN, NRP, ANRP). Agent scores range from 0 to 1 based on target destruction, friendly casualties, and accurate novelty characterization. This systematic approach supports rigorous assessment of AI robustness to open-world novelty in mission-critical military contexts.

## Method Summary
The research employs DARPA’s SAIL-ON framework to map military-specific novelty types to a domain-independent ontology. Statistical distributions (Normal, Uniform, Bernoulli) are used to model uncertainty in military parameters like missile range. Tactical scenarios are generated in the SOLVE simulation environment, where controlled novelties are introduced to test AI agent robustness. Agent performance is evaluated using a combination of mission success metrics and novelty detection rates (CDT, IDN, NRP, ANRP), with scores ranging from 0 to 1 based on target destruction, friendly casualties, and novelty characterization accuracy.

## Key Results
- The research maps military novelty types to a domain-independent ontology and uses statistical distributions to model parameter uncertainty.
- Novelty-rich tactical scenarios (e.g., increased SAM ranges) are generated in the SOLVE simulation environment.
- Agent evaluation metrics combine mission success and novelty detection rates (CDT, IDN, NRP, ANRP), with scores ranging from 0 to 1.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: SAIL-ON ontology → Novelty classification → Statistical models (Normal, Uniform, Bernoulli, Poisson) → Parameter uncertainty → SOLVE simulation framework → Environment generation and control → Agent evaluation metrics (scenario-specific + OWL metrics) → Performance assessment → Configuration files → Experiment orchestration

- Critical path: Ontology classification → Statistical parameter modeling → SOLVE configuration → Agent execution → Metric calculation → Analysis

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Static vs. statistical novelty modeling: Static is simpler but less realistic; statistical adds complexity but better models uncertainty.
  - Scenario simplicity vs. richness: Simple scenarios (like Scenario Zero) are easier to analyze but may miss complex novelty interactions present in richer environments.
  - Simulation fidelity vs. development cost: Higher fidelity simulators improve realism but increase integration and computational costs.

- Failure signatures:
  - Agents score high on mission success but low on novelty detection metrics → They may be overfitting to expected scenarios.
  - High variance in agent scores across repeated runs with same novelty → Statistical models may be mis-specified or insufficient samples collected.
  - Configuration errors leading to unintended novelties → Check SOLVE config validation and logging.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement Scenario Zero with static missile range increase (no statistical variation) and validate baseline agent behavior.
  2. Introduce Normal-distributed missile range variation (easy, medium, hard levels) and measure agent detection rates using CDT.
  3. Add a second novelty (e.g., sensor failure modeled with Bernoulli distribution) and evaluate combined impact on NRP and mission success.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can statistical distributions be optimally selected and parameterized to model novelty across different military scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses using statistical distributions (Normal, Uniform, Bernoulli, Poisson) to model novelties but notes this requires further exploration for optimal selection.
- Why unresolved: Different types of novelties (objects, agents, actions, etc.) may require different distribution models, and the optimal parameters for these distributions are not yet determined.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing agent performance across various distribution models and parameters in diverse military scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the most effective metrics for evaluating AI agent performance in detecting, characterizing, and accommodating novelty in military environments?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper presents initial metrics but acknowledges the need for both scenario-specific and general metrics for open-world learning environments.
- Why unresolved: Current metrics may not fully capture the complexity of novelty handling, and new metrics may be needed as scenarios and agent capabilities evolve.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of agent performance using different metric sets across a wide range of novelty-rich scenarios.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the concept of novelty be extended to include positive or beneficial changes in military scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that while most novelties discussed make missions more difficult, novelty could also include events that make missions easier, but these are not the focus of the study.
- Why unresolved: The current framework focuses on detecting and handling negative novelties, potentially missing opportunities to leverage beneficial changes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Case studies of military scenarios where positive novelties occurred and analysis of how AI agents could be designed to recognize and exploit such opportunities.

## Limitations
- The reliance on simulation fidelity to represent real-world military environments may not capture all complexities of adversarial scenarios.
- The statistical modeling approach assumes that parameter distributions accurately reflect real-world uncertainty, but validation against empirical military data is not provided.
- The scalability of the approach to highly complex military scenarios with multiple interacting novelties is uncertain, as the current focus is on simpler scenarios like Scenario Zero.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The framework for mapping military novelty to a domain-independent ontology and the use of statistical distributions to model parameter uncertainty are well-justified and methodologically sound.
- **Medium Confidence**: The simulation-based experimental design using SOLVE is robust, but its fidelity to real-world conditions remains unverified. The evaluation metrics (CDT, IDN, NRP, ANRP) are novel and may require further validation.
- **Low Confidence**: The scalability of the approach to highly complex military scenarios with multiple interacting novelties is uncertain, as the current focus is on simpler scenarios like Scenario Zero.
