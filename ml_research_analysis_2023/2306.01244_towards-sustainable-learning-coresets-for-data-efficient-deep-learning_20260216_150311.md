# Towards Sustainable Learning: Coresets for Data-efficient Deep Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01244
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01244
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: 1.7x-2.5x speedup over full data training with smallest relative error among baselines

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of sustainable deep learning by introducing CREST, a coreset selection method that identifies the most valuable training examples for non-convex neural network optimization. The key innovation lies in modeling the non-convex loss as a series of quadratic functions and extracting coresets for each quadratic sub-region, while also handling stochastic gradient methods through mini-batch coresets sampled from random subsets. Experiments on vision (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet) and NLP (SNLI) datasets demonstrate that CREST achieves significant speedups while maintaining accuracy, making it the first coreset method to scale to large models and datasets in these domains.

## Method Summary
CREST improves deep learning efficiency by extracting coresets - subsets of training data that closely approximate the full dataset's gradient. The method models non-convex loss as quadratic functions within local neighborhoods and selects coresets that capture gradients within these regions. For stochastic optimization, CREST samples multiple random subsets and selects mini-batch coresets from each, reducing gradient variance. The algorithm also identifies and excludes examples with consistently small losses during training, focusing computational resources on difficult-to-learn examples. Theoretical analysis guarantees convergence to stationary points of the non-convex loss.

## Key Results
- Achieves 1.7x to 2.5x speedup over training on full data
- Introduces the smallest relative error compared to baseline coreset methods
- First coreset selection method to scale to large vision and NLP models and datasets
- Provides rigorous theoretical guarantees for convergence to stationary points of non-convex loss

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
CREST improves coreset selection by modeling non-convex loss as a series of quadratic functions and extracting a coreset for each quadratic sub-region. The algorithm divides the loss into quadratic sub-regions based on gradient and curvature at current model parameters, then finds coresets that capture the full gradient within these regions. When the quadratic approximation deviates from actual loss, a new coreset is extracted. This breaks down if the quadratic approximation fails to capture the actual loss within the neighborhood (ρtl exceeds threshold τ), requiring more frequent updates that negate efficiency gains.

### Mechanism 2
CREST enables efficient mini-batch stochastic gradient training by extracting multiple mini-batch coresets from random subsets. The algorithm samples multiple random subsets of training data, then selects mini-batch coresets from each subset that closely capture the gradient of that subset. This provides nearly unbiased gradients with reduced variance. This mechanism fails when random subsets become too large relative to mini-batch size, causing bias to increase and variance reduction to diminish.

### Mechanism 3
CREST improves efficiency by identifying and excluding examples that are learned during training. The algorithm monitors loss values of examples over non-overlapping intervals and excludes those with consistently small losses (below threshold α), allowing focus on difficult-to-learn examples. This approach fails if threshold α is set too high, prematurely excluding important examples, or too low, diminishing efficiency gains.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Quadratic loss approximation module -> Coreset selection engine -> Update validation system -> Learned example exclusion -> Multi-scale selection

- **Critical path**: 1. Extract coresets using submodular maximization on random subsets 2. Compute quadratic loss approximation using smoothed gradients and Hessian diagonals 3. Validate approximation using random subset loss estimates 4. Train on selected mini-batches 5. Monitor and exclude learned examples

- **Design tradeoffs**: Larger random subsets reduce variance but increase bias in mini-batch coresets; more frequent coreset updates improve accuracy but reduce efficiency gains; higher exclusion thresholds improve efficiency but risk losing important examples; using full Hessian vs diagonal approximation trades accuracy for computational efficiency

- **Failure signatures**: High ρtl values indicating quadratic approximation breakdown; increasing number of coreset updates over time; rapid decline in test accuracy despite training; convergence to poor local minima

## Limitations
- Computational overhead uncertainty: The method's efficiency gains may be diminished by the substantial computational cost of coreset selection itself
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Performance depends critically on several hyperparameters (τ, α, subset sizes) that require careful tuning
- Theoretical vs practical gap: Convergence guarantees assume ideal conditions that may not hold in practice, potentially leading to poor local minima

## Confidence

**1.2x-2.5x speedup claim (High confidence)**: Experimental results support this range, though computational cost accounting is incomplete.

**Quadratic approximation validity (Medium confidence)**: Theoretically sound but limited empirical validation across training phases.

**Learned example exclusion effectiveness (Medium confidence)**: Conceptually well-founded but requires careful hyperparameter tuning that varies across datasets.
