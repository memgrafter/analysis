# On Modifying a Neural Network's Perception

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02655
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02655
- Reference count: 11
- One-line primary result: Method manipulates neural network perception by modifying activations of concept neurons, requiring few labeled data and without retraining.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel method to manipulate a neural network's perception of specific human-defined concepts by modifying the activations of concept neurons. The approach identifies neurons sensitive to particular concepts, computes activation values representing the presence or absence of these concepts, and injects these values to alter the model's internal representation. The method is validated on both synthetic and ImageNet datasets, demonstrating successful manipulation of the model's perception without requiring retraining or extensive labeled data.

## Method Summary
The method consists of three main steps: identifying concept neurons using sensitivity metrics (Spearman correlation, linear classifier accuracy, or probability density function intersection), computing activation values representing concept presence/absence (using median or mode), and injecting concepts by replacing neuron activations. The approach is validated on synthetic XTRAINS dataset and ImageNet, using MobileNetV2 as the base model. Success is measured by the model's output changes consistent with the injected concept.

## Key Results
- Successfully manipulated neural network perception on synthetic and ImageNet datasets
- Achieved concept manipulation with as few as 40 labeled samples
- Generated counterfactual scenarios by modifying concept neuron activations
- Enabled understanding of concept relationships within the model

## Limitations

- Method effectiveness depends on the presence of concept neurons in the network
- Biological analogy to concept cells lacks direct empirical support
- Method requires labeled data, though minimal amounts may suffice
- Limited to modifying existing models, not training new ones from scratch

## Confidence
