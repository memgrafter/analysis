## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Diffusion models in NLP achieve superior generation quality by iteratively denoising latent representations through a Markov chain.
- Mechanism: The model starts from Gaussian noise and progressively denoises it using learned conditional distributions pθ(Xt-1|Xt) at each step. The iterative refinement allows the model to capture complex data distributions.
- Core assumption: The reverse diffusion process can be approximated by a Gaussian distribution with mean and variance learned by a neural network.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] states that diffusion models "have achieved great achievements and have been widely used in computer vision, speech generation, natural language processing and other aspects"
  - [section] explains that "diffusion models work by continuously adding Gaussian noise to corrupt the training data, and then learning to recover the data by reversing this noise process"
  - [corpus] shows related surveys focus on similar diffusion model applications, supporting the general mechanism
- Break condition: If the learned conditional distributions fail to approximate the true reverse process, or if the noise schedule is poorly designed, the generation quality degrades significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Classifier-free guidance enhances text-conditioned generation by interpolating between unconditional and conditional predictions.
- Mechanism: During training, the model is sometimes conditioned on text and sometimes not. At inference, the prediction is biased toward the conditional direction using a guidance scale factor, improving sample quality and alignment with text prompts.
- Core assumption: The model can learn both conditional and unconditional distributions simultaneously, and the interpolation between them improves guidance without explicit classifier training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] mentions "text-driven image generation" as a major application area, implying text conditioning
  - [section] discusses "text-conditional image synthesis problem" and compares "CLIP framing and classifier-free framing"
  - [corpus] includes surveys on text-guided image generation, supporting this mechanism's relevance
- Break condition: If the guidance scale is too high, samples may overfit to the text and lose diversity; if too low, the benefit of guidance diminishes.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Discrete diffusion models enable direct application of diffusion techniques to text by treating tokens as discrete states.
- Mechanism: Text tokens are mapped to discrete latent variables, and a diffusion process is defined over these discrete states. The model learns to denoise discrete sequences iteratively, analogous to continuous diffusion but adapted for discrete data.
- Core assumption: A suitable discretization of the diffusion process preserves the generative capabilities while allowing application to discrete text data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] lists "text generation" as a primary application of diffusion models in NLP
  - [section] describes "diffusion model for generating discrete data" and "D3PM for discrete data"
  - [corpus] lacks direct evidence for discrete diffusion mechanisms, suggesting this is an inference based on the survey content
- Break condition: If the discretization introduces significant information loss or if the discrete denoising process is inefficient, the model may not converge or generate coherent text.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Markov chains and conditional probability distributions
  - Why needed here: Diffusion models rely on Markov processes where each step depends only on the previous state, and conditional distributions are learned to reverse the noise process
  - Quick check question: In a Markov chain, what is the probability of the next state dependent on?
    - Answer: Only the current state (Markov property)

- Concept: Gaussian distributions and reparameterization trick
  - Why needed here: The diffusion and reverse diffusion processes are modeled as Gaussian distributions, and the reparameterization trick is used to sample from these distributions efficiently
  - Quick check question: What does the reparameterization trick allow you to do with Gaussian distributions?
    - Answer: Sample from a Gaussian distribution by transforming samples from a standard normal distribution

- Concept: Variational autoencoders and latent variable models
  - Why needed here: Understanding how diffusion models can be seen as a type of generative model that learns to transform noise into data, similar to how VAEs learn latent representations
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between a VAE and a diffusion model in terms of how they generate data?
    - Answer: VAEs use an encoder-decoder architecture with a single latent variable, while diffusion models iteratively denoise from noise to data

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Noise schedule -> Forward process -> Reverse process (U-Net) -> Conditioning mechanism -> Classifier-free guidance
- Critical path:
  1. Define noise schedule and forward process
  2. Design U-Net architecture for reverse process
  3. Implement classifier-free guidance during training
  4. Train on text-image pairs or text-only data
  5. Generate samples using iterative denoising
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of diffusion steps vs. generation speed
  - Model capacity vs. overfitting risk
  - Guidance scale vs. sample diversity
  - Discrete vs. continuous diffusion for text
- Failure signatures:
  - Mode collapse (low diversity in generated samples)
  - Mode dropping (missing certain modes of the data distribution)
  - Poor text alignment in text-to-image generation
  - Slow convergence or unstable training
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train a simple continuous diffusion model on a small text dataset to verify the basic denoising mechanism
  2. Implement classifier-free guidance and compare samples with and without guidance on a text-to-image task
  3. Experiment with different noise schedules (linear, cosine) and measure their impact on sample quality and training stability
