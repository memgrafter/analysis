# Achieving Diversity in Counterfactual Explanations: a Review and Discussion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05840
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05840
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Survey and discussion of diversity strategies for generating multiple counterfactual explanations in XAI.

## Executive Summary
This paper surveys and discusses the concept of diversity in counterfactual explanations within Explainable AI (XAI). The authors highlight the challenge of selecting and combining quality criteria for generating counterfactual examples, which are explanations indicating modifications to change a prediction. They argue that generating a single counterfactual explanation often fails to meet diverse user needs and may lead to suboptimal results due to the implicit aggregation of criteria. To address this, the paper reviews various approaches that generate multiple diverse counterfactual explanations, categorizing them based on their diversity strategies: diversity in criteria, feature space, and actions. The review identifies further research challenges and emphasizes the importance of observable diversity in matching user needs. The study contributes to the understanding of how to better tailor explanations to users by providing multiple, diverse counterfactual examples.

## Method Summary
The paper reviews and categorizes approaches for generating diverse counterfactual explanations in XAI. It analyzes different diversity strategies (criteria, feature space, actions) and aggregation operators (weighted sum, constrained optimization, priority order) used to combine quality criteria. The review identifies challenges in matching user needs and the importance of observable diversity. The study synthesizes existing literature and proposes future research directions, but does not implement or benchmark specific methods.

## Key Results
- Multiple diverse counterfactual explanations better match user needs than a single aggregated explanation.
- Observable diversity in explanations (e.g., different actions or features) improves user understanding and trust.
- Aggregating multiple criteria into a single objective often leads to suboptimal or misleading explanations.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multiple diverse counterfactual explanations better match user needs than a single aggregated explanation.
- Mechanism: When multiple explanations are provided, users can select the one that aligns with their personal context, preferences, or causal understanding, overcoming the limitations of implicit aggregation.
- Core assumption: Users have diverse, sometimes unarticulated needs that cannot be captured by a single optimization objective.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The study contributes to the understanding of how to better tailor explanations to users by providing multiple, diverse counterfactual examples."
  - [section] "Section 3.2.2 discusses the potential undersirable consequences of the aggregation."
- Break condition: If users cannot distinguish or evaluate the differences between diverse explanations, or if the explanations are not meaningfully diverse.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Observable diversity in explanations (e.g., different actions or features) improves user understanding and trust.
- Mechanism: By generating explanations that differ in observable ways—such as using different features or suggesting different actions—users can more easily perceive and choose explanations that make sense to them.
- Core assumption: Diversity is only useful if it is observable and interpretable by users.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Section 5.1 argues that all diversity definitions do not all unequivocally fulfill this promise... it is expected that the user directly understands the differences."
  - [section] "Section 4.2.3 Diversity in Actions... fulfills this objective of observable differences."
- Break condition: If the diversity is not observable (e.g., only in criteria or feature values not tied to user context), users may not benefit.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Aggregating multiple criteria into a single objective often leads to suboptimal or misleading explanations.
- Mechanism: Different quality criteria (e.g., closeness, sparsity, actionability) may conflict; implicit aggregation may produce explanations that satisfy none fully, especially when criteria are not commensurable.
- Core assumption: Many desirable properties for counterfactuals are mathematically incompatible when optimized jointly.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The possibly subjective and personal characteristic of the latter can be considered as advocating for making it dependent on the targeted user, his/her specific needs and prior knowledge."
  - [section] "Section 3.2.1 discusses how the aggregation operator directly impacts the nature of the solution... the result is that the counterfactual examples are quite scattered across the dataset."
- Break condition: If the optimization problem is constrained to prioritize one criterion over others, or if the user can explicitly specify trade-offs.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data ingestion: User instance and trained model
  - Explanation generation: Counterfactual optimization module(s)
  - Diversity module: Applies diversity strategies (criteria, feature space, actions)
  - Aggregation module: Combines or selects diverse explanations
  - Output: Set of diverse counterfactual explanations

- Critical path:
  1. Receive user instance and model
  2. Generate multiple counterfactual explanations using different diversity strategies
  3. Ensure diversity is observable (different actions/features)
  4. Present explanations to user for selection

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single-step vs. iterative generation: Single-step may be faster but less controllable; iterative allows exclusion of redundancy.
  - Explicit vs. implicit diversity: Explicit ensures diversity but may be computationally expensive; implicit is simpler but less reliable.
  - Number of explanations: More explanations increase diversity but may introduce redundancy or overwhelm the user.

- Failure signatures:
  - All generated explanations are nearly identical (lack of true diversity)
  - Explanations use features the user cannot control (lack of actionability)
  - Users cannot understand or choose between explanations (lack of observability)
  - Explanations are unrealistic or violate domain constraints

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Generate counterfactuals using different aggregation operators (e.g., weighted sum vs. constrained optimization) and compare their diversity and user preference.
  2. Implement and compare explicit diversity (e.g., maximizing feature space distance) vs. implicit diversity (e.g., stochastic generation) in terms of observed diversity and user satisfaction.
  3. Conduct a user study where participants choose preferred explanations from sets generated with different diversity strategies (actions vs. features vs. criteria).

## Limitations
- The review synthesizes existing approaches but relies on indirect evidence rather than full methodological details for many cited works.
- Claims about user benefit from diverse explanations are grounded in theoretical arguments rather than empirical user studies.
- The assertion that single aggregated criteria often lead to suboptimal explanations lacks quantitative benchmarks across a wide range of models and datasets.

## Confidence
- Claims about the mechanism of user benefit from diverse explanations: **Medium**
- Claims about the negative impact of aggregating criteria: **Medium**
- Claims about the importance of observable diversity: **High**
