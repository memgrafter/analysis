# LATTE: Label-efficient Incident Phenotyping from Longitudinal Electronic Health Records

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11407
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11407
- Reference count: 37
- Key outcome: LATTE achieves substantial improvements in ABCgain, AUC, and F1 scores for incident phenotyping across three diseases, outperforming benchmark methods even with limited gold-standard labels

## Executive Summary
LATTE addresses the challenge of accurately annotating the timing of clinical events from longitudinal electronic health records (EHR) using a label-efficient approach. By leveraging semantic embedding vectors and constructing informative silver-standard labels from large-scale unlabeled patients, LATTE selects predictive EHR features and captures sequential dependencies to improve incident phenotyping. Evaluated on type-2 diabetes, heart failure, and multiple sclerosis, LATTE consistently outperforms benchmark methods like SAMGEP and RETAIN, achieving substantial improvements in ABCgain, AUC, and F1 scores, even with limited gold-standard labels.

## Method Summary
LATTE introduces a novel framework for incident phenotyping that combines concept re-weighting, visit attention networks, and sequential modeling with Bi-GRUs. The method constructs longitudinal silver-standard labels from predictive surrogates to perform unsupervised pretraining, then fine-tunes using both gold-standard and silver-standard labels. LATTE also incorporates contrastive representation learning to enhance cross-site portability and provides interpretable evidence for predictions. The approach leverages pre-trained semantic embedding vectors from large-scale EHR data to select predictive features and compress their information into longitudinal visit embeddings.

## Key Results
- LATTE achieves 3.7-12.5% improvement in ABCgain compared to state-of-the-art methods across all three phenotypes
- The method consistently outperforms benchmark methods (SAMGEP, RETAIN) in both AUC and F1 scores
- LATTE demonstrates superior performance with limited gold-standard labels (as few as 50-100 examples)
- Cross-site portability is enhanced, with improved performance when applied to external cohorts

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: LATTE improves label efficiency by constructing highly informative silver-standard labels from large-scale unlabeled patients and performing unsupervised pre-training and semi-supervised joint training.
- Mechanism: The silver-standard labels are derived from predictive surrogates (like PheCodes or CUIs) and used to pretrain the model in an unsupervised manner, allowing the model to learn useful representations without requiring gold-standard labels. The pretraining is then fine-tuned using a small number of gold-standard labels.
- Core assumption: The silver-standard labels, while noisy, contain enough signal to guide the model towards useful representations that can be further refined with gold-standard labels.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: LATTE enhances cross-site portability via contrastive representation learning, making the model robust to data shifts across different clinical sites.
- Mechanism: LATTE generates synthetic data that mimics three types of data shifts (concept utilization bias, visit frequency, and visit phase) and uses a concordance measure to encourage the learned representations to be invariant to these shifts.
- Core assumption: The synthetic data generation process accurately captures the types of data shifts that occur across clinical sites, and the concordance measure effectively encourages the model to learn robust representations.
- Evidence anchors:

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: LATTE selects predictive EHR features in a concept re-weighting module by mining their relationship to the target event and compresses their information into longitudinal visit embeddings through a visit attention learning network.
- Mechanism: The concept re-weighting module uses a neural network to learn a weight for each input concept based on its semantic relationship to the target phenotype. The visit attention network then uses self-attention to highlight informative visits. The weighted concept embeddings are aggregated to form visit embeddings, which are then processed by a Bi-GRU to capture sequential dependencies.
- Core assumption: The semantic embedding vectors of EHR concepts, which are learned from large-scale unlabeled data, effectively capture the clinical semantic relationship or similarity of EHR concepts.
- Evidence anchors:

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input data -> Concept Re-weighting -> Visit Attention -> Bi-GRU -> Incident Predictor -> Silver-standard label construction -> Semi-supervised training

- Critical path: The critical path for training LATTE is: Input data -> Concept Re-weighting -> Visit Attention -> Bi-GRU -> Incident Predictor. The silver-standard label construction and semi-supervised training are auxiliary processes that guide the learning of the main components.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using semantic embeddings as prior knowledge vs. learning embeddings from scratch: Using pre-trained embeddings can provide a good starting point and reduce the amount of data needed for training, but may not capture task-specific information as well as learned embeddings.
  - Self-attention vs. other attention mechanisms: Self-attention allows the model to attend to all visits in the sequence, but may be computationally expensive for very long sequences.
  - Bi-GRU vs. other recurrent architectures: Bi-GRUs can capture both past and future dependencies, but may be more prone to overfitting than simpler architectures like LSTMs.

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on the validation set: This could indicate overfitting, underfitting, or issues with the data preprocessing or model architecture.
  - Unstable training: This could be caused by issues with the learning rate, batch size, or other hyperparameters.
  - Slow convergence: This could be due to a complex model architecture, insufficient training data, or suboptimal hyperparameters.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train LATTE on a small synthetic dataset with known ground truth to verify that the model can learn the correct patterns.
  2. Train LATTE on a real EHR dataset with a small number of gold-standard labels and evaluate its performance compared to baseline methods.
  3. Investigate the impact of the silver-standard label construction and semi-supervised training by training LATTE with and without these components.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does LATTE perform on phenotypes where no strong surrogate concept exists in the EHR data?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that for MS relapse, no predictive diagnostic code exists, and LATTE relies on complex visit dependency modeling. However, the performance comparison is only made against PheCodes and supervised methods, without exploring performance in the complete absence of any predictive surrogates.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not evaluate LATTE's ability to learn incident phenotyping without any available predictive surrogates or silver-standard labels, which would be the case for truly novel or rare phenotypes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating LATTE's performance on phenotypes with no predictive surrogate concepts, or ablation studies removing all silver-standard labels to test pure unsupervised learning capabilities.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does LATTE's performance scale with increasing label sizes beyond what was tested?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates LATTE with label sizes up to 500 for MS relapse and up to 200 for other phenotypes, but notes that the performance gap between LATTE and other methods becomes more distinct with more labels.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore performance at very large label sizes (e.g., thousands of labels) to determine if LATTE maintains its advantage or if performance plateaus.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with increasingly large gold-standard label sets (e.g., 1000+, 5000+) to compare LATTE's performance trajectory against benchmark methods.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does LATTE handle temporal misalignment between EHR visits and true phenotype onset?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses systematic biases between code timings and phenotype onset for T2D and HF, but doesn't explicitly address how LATTE handles temporal misalignment when the true onset occurs between EHR visits.
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't evaluate LATTE's ability to interpolate between visits or handle cases where the true phenotype onset occurs during periods with no EHR visits.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments simulating temporal misalignment by removing visits around known onset times, or comparing LATTE's performance on datasets with different visit frequencies.

## Limitations
- LATTE's performance depends heavily on the quality of silver-standard labels derived from predictive surrogates, which may vary by phenotype and data quality
- The cross-site portability mechanism relies on synthetic data generation that may not fully capture all real-world data shifts between healthcare systems
- Performance on rare phenotypes with few positive cases may be constrained by limited availability of meaningful silver-standard labels

## Confidence

**High confidence**: LATTE's core architecture (concept re-weighting + visit attention + Bi-GRU) is technically sound and the improvements over baseline methods are well-documented through quantitative metrics

**Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of silver-standard label construction assumes that predictive surrogates (PheCodes, CUIs) have sufficient signal, which may vary by phenotype and data quality

**Medium confidence**: Cross-site portability claims rely on synthetic data generation that may not fully capture all real-world data shifts between healthcare systems

## Next Checks

1. **Silver-label quality assessment**: Systematically evaluate the predictive power of different surrogate sources (PheCodes vs CUIs) for each phenotype to determine optimal label construction strategies and quantify the impact of surrogate quality on final model performance.

2. **Data shift characterization**: Analyze the actual data distributions across sites (concept utilization, visit frequency, visit phase) and compare them to the synthetic data used for contrastive learning to verify that the robustness mechanism addresses real data shift patterns.

3. **Rare phenotype testing**: Evaluate LATTE's performance on phenotypes with very low prevalence (<1%) to determine the practical limits of label efficiency and identify when the method may fail due to insufficient positive cases in the silver-standard label construction.
