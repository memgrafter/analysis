# Towards Explainable Conversational Recommender Systems

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18363
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18363
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: E-ReDial dataset with 756 dialogues and 2,058 high-quality explanations significantly improves explanation quality in CRS models

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of generating high-quality explanations in conversational recommender systems (CRS), which currently lack adequate evaluation methods and datasets. The authors identify ten evaluation perspectives for CRS explanations and analyze five existing datasets, finding consistently low explanation quality. To address this gap, they construct E-ReDial, a new dataset with manually rewritten explanations that meet four key quality criteria: clear recommendation reasons, representative item descriptions, encouragement or personal opinions, and contextual coherence. Through comprehensive experiments comparing training-based (T5) and prompt-based (GPT-3) approaches, the paper demonstrates that models trained on E-ReDial significantly outperform those trained on existing datasets, with knowledge-grounded methods further enhancing performance.

## Method Summary
The authors construct E-ReDial by manually rewriting 2,058 explanations from the ReDial dataset to meet defined quality criteria. They employ knowledge-grounded approaches using external sources (IMDb reviews, Wikipedia, plot data) to enhance explanation generation. The methodology involves fine-tuning T5 and BART models on E-ReDial data, using GPT-3 Davinci-002 in prompt-based in-context learning, and incorporating knowledge retrieval modules. Evaluation combines automatic metrics (F1, BLEU, METEOR, Distinct) with human assessment across ten explanation quality dimensions.

## Key Results
- E-ReDial dataset improves explanation quality significantly compared to existing CRS datasets
- GPT-3 excels at generating realistic and diverse movie descriptions, while T5 better generates clear recommendation reasons
- Knowledge-grounded approaches outperform non-knowledge models when trained on E-ReDial
- Dataset size of 50% E-ReDial provides optimal training performance, with pretraining on ReDial beneficial in low-resource settings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Manually rewritten explanations significantly outperform automatically generated ones due to higher adherence to defined quality criteria.
- Mechanism: Human annotators incorporate movie-specific background knowledge (plot, reviews, wiki) that is absent or incorrectly applied in automatic generation, resulting in more representative and contextually coherent explanations.
- Core assumption: The quality of an explanation depends on accurate and diverse information sources beyond the dialogue context.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "The automatic method often fails to meet the rewriting requirements by evaluating the explanations obtained by both methods."
  - [section 4.3]: GPT-3-based automatic rewriting misses movies and fails to generate descriptions in over 30% of cases.
  - [corpus]: No strong evidence in related papers; corpus focuses on LLM integration rather than manual vs automatic quality comparison.
- Break condition: If automatic generation models improve their ability to accurately incorporate external knowledge without factual errors, the performance gap may narrow.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge-grounded models outperform non-knowledge models when trained on E-ReDial data.
- Mechanism: E-ReDial explanations include background knowledge annotations; training models on this data enables them to generate more detailed, diverse, and persuasive movie descriptions.
- Core assumption: The inclusion of external knowledge (plots, reviews, wiki) in explanations enhances user satisfaction and persuasiveness.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "GPT-3 in the in-context learning setting can generate more realistic and diverse movie descriptions."
  - [section 5.3]: T5+KG trained on E-ReDial achieves significantly higher diversity metrics (D2, D3) than without KG.
  - [section 5.4]: Human evaluation shows KG improves effectiveness, efficiency, and persuasiveness.
- Break condition: If the external knowledge sources are noisy, outdated, or irrelevant, incorporating them may degrade explanation quality.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The size of the E-ReDial dataset is sufficient for effective training, but pretraining on ReDial provides additional gains in low-resource settings.
- Mechanism: E-ReDial's 2,058 explanations provide enough examples for the T5-Base model to learn explanation generation patterns; ReDial pretraining offers general CRS dialogue structure.
- Core assumption: A smaller, high-quality dataset can be as effective as a larger, lower-quality one if it contains the right features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.3]: Models improve with increasing E-ReDial data up to 50%, beyond which gains plateau.
  - [section 5.3]: ReDial-pretrained T5 performs better than vanilla T5 with only 10% E-ReDial data.
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence; corpus focuses on LLM-based CRS rather than dataset size effects.
- Break condition: If E-ReDial were significantly smaller (e.g., <500 explanations), the model might fail to generalize, and ReDial pretraining would become essential.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Data collection pipeline (ReDial → manual rewriting → E-ReDial)
  - Knowledge retrieval module (IMDb reviews, Wikipedia, plot data)
  - Text generation models (T5-Base, BART, GPT-2, DialoGPT, Flan-T5-XXL, Davinci-002)
  - Evaluation framework (automatic metrics + human evaluation)
  - Prompt engineering for ICL (in-context learning with exemplars)

- Critical path:
  1. Input: dialogue context + recommended movie
  2. Knowledge retrieval (if KG approach)
  3. Text generation (model inference)
  4. Output: explanation response

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Manual vs automatic rewriting: Higher quality vs scalability
  - KG vs no-KG: Better explanations vs increased input length and complexity
  - Fine-tuning vs ICL: More control vs flexibility and cost

- Failure signatures:
  - Explanations missing movie names or user preferences
  - Factual errors in movie descriptions
  - Low diversity in generated explanations (high repetition)
  - Poor correlation between explanation quality and CRS performance metrics

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Compare T5+KG on 10%, 50%, 100% E-ReDial data to find optimal size.
  2. Manual vs automatic: Human evaluation of GPT-3 vs human-rewritten explanations on same dialogue contexts.
  3. KG source impact: Test different knowledge sources (wiki-only vs wiki+reviews vs wiki+reviews+plot) on explanation quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal approach for balancing explanation quality and dialogue naturalness in conversational recommender systems?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses the trade-off between generating realistic movie descriptions (GPT-3 excels) and providing clear recommendation reasons (T5 excels), suggesting a need to balance these aspects.
- Why unresolved: The paper compares two approaches (T5 and GPT-3) but doesn't explore hybrid methods or optimal strategies for combining their strengths.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing hybrid models or different prompt strategies that leverage both T5's clarity and GPT-3's descriptiveness, with user studies measuring satisfaction and effectiveness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the quality of explanations in CRS datasets correlate with real-world user satisfaction and system adoption?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper measures explanation quality through human annotation and finds correlations with CRS performance metrics, but doesn't validate these findings with real-world usage data.
- Why unresolved: The study relies on controlled human annotation rather than actual user interactions with deployed systems.
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking user satisfaction and engagement metrics in production CRS systems with varying explanation qualities, or A/B testing different explanation strategies.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal size and composition of knowledge-grounded training data for maximizing explanation quality in CRS?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper shows that knowledge-grounded approaches improve performance but only tests a single dataset size and composition.
- Why unresolved: The experiments use a fixed dataset size and don't explore how different amounts or types of knowledge affect performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies varying the amount and diversity of knowledge sources, combined with experiments testing different knowledge retrieval strategies and their impact on explanation quality.

## Limitations

- Manual annotation process limits dataset scalability and may not generalize to other domains or languages
- Knowledge-grounded approach depends heavily on quality and relevance of external knowledge sources
- Comparison between GPT-3 (ICL) and T5 (fine-tuning) uses different operational paradigms, making direct performance comparison difficult
- Evaluation relies on human judgments that may vary across annotators and cultures

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The claim that E-ReDial explanations are higher quality than those from ReDial is well-supported by both automatic metrics and human evaluations. The methodology for dataset construction and quality verification is clearly documented.

**Medium Confidence**: The superiority of knowledge-grounded approaches over non-knowledge methods is demonstrated, but the results may be sensitive to the specific knowledge sources used. The study doesn't explore alternative knowledge retrieval strategies or their impact on explanation quality.

**Low Confidence**: The comparison between GPT-3 (ICL) and T5 (fine-tuning) for explanation generation is inconclusive regarding which approach is fundamentally better, as they operate under different paradigms with different strengths and limitations.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-domain validation**: Test the E-ReDial approach on a different recommendation domain (e.g., restaurants, books, or music) to assess generalizability of the explanation generation framework.

2. **Knowledge source ablation**: Systematically evaluate the impact of different knowledge sources (wiki-only, reviews-only, plot-only) on explanation quality to identify which types of information most improve user satisfaction.

3. **Long-term user study**: Conduct a longitudinal study measuring how explanation quality affects user behavior over multiple recommendation sessions, including metrics like return rate, trust development, and preference evolution.
