# Eyettention: An Attention-based Dual-Sequence Model for Predicting Human Scanpaths during Reading

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.10784
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10784
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Eyettention outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in predicting eye movement patterns during reading, using a dual-sequence model with local cross-attention

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Eyettention, a novel neural model for predicting human eye movement patterns during reading. It addresses the dual-sequence nature of eye-tracking data, where word order and fixation timing are separate axes, by employing a cross-attention mechanism that aligns word-level and fixation-level embeddings. The model uses a local attention window with Gaussian smoothing to simulate the human perceptual span, and outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in terms of negative log-likelihood (NLL) and similarity metrics across English and Chinese data sets, including cross-dataset generalization.

## Method Summary
Eyettention is a dual-sequence encoder-encoder architecture that processes word sequences and fixation sequences simultaneously. The Word-Sequence Encoder uses BERT embeddings passed through BiLSTM layers, while the Fixation-Sequence Encoder processes fixation embeddings with LSTM layers. A cross-attention layer with local window (D=1) and Gaussian smoothing (σ=D/2) aligns these representations. The decoder predicts the next fixation as a saccade range using a 4-layer fully-connected network. The model is trained with Adam optimizer (lr=1e-3) for 1000 epochs with early stopping on validation NLL.

## Key Results
- Eyettention achieves lower NLL than state-of-the-art baselines on both English (CELER L1, ZuCo, ZuCo 2.0) and Chinese (BSC) eye-tracking datasets
- The model shows better generalization when trained on one language and tested on another
- Ablation studies confirm the importance of local attention window and landing position features for Chinese text

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Local cross-attention with a Gaussian kernel improves model performance by focusing neural resources on words near the current fixation, mimicking human foveal/parafoveal vision.
- Mechanism: The model computes attention weights over words within a window centered at the current fixation, then smooths them with a Gaussian kernel. This constrains the model's "perceptual span" and helps it attend to relevant words for predicting the next fixation.
- Core assumption: Human reading behavior is better approximated by local, fovea-like attention than by global, sentence-wide attention.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 4.1.3]
- Break condition: If long saccades (>4 words) are crucial for the task, the local window may fail to capture relevant context, hurting performance.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The dual-sequence encoder-encoder architecture allows simultaneous processing of linguistic and temporal information, which single-sequence models cannot capture.
- Mechanism: Two separate encoders process word embeddings (linguistic axis) and fixation embeddings (temporal axis). A cross-attention layer aligns these representations, allowing the decoder to predict the next fixation conditioned on both.
- Core assumption: Eye movement patterns depend on both the properties of the text and the history of fixations, requiring joint modeling.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract], [section 4.1]
- Break condition: If one sequence axis (linguistic or temporal) dominates the prediction task, the added complexity of the dual architecture may not pay off.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Incorporating landing position and fixation duration as features significantly improves model performance, especially for Chinese text.
- Mechanism: The fixation encoder receives z-score normalized fixation duration and landing position concatenated with the word embedding. These features help the model learn saccade overshoot and corrective behavior.
- Core assumption: Readers adjust their next fixation based on how well they landed on the intended word; this adjustment varies across languages/scripts.
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.1.2], [section 5.7]
- Break condition: If the dataset lacks variability in landing positions (e.g., all fixations land near word centers), these features may not provide predictive power.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: BERT embeddings -> Word-Sequence Encoder (BiLSTM stack) -> word length concat -> z_j -> Cross-Attention (local window + Gaussian) -> c_i -> Decoder (4-layer FC) -> softmax over saccade ranges; Fixation word emb + position/duration -> Fixation-Sequence Encoder (LSTM stack) -> h_{i-1} -> Cross-Attention -> c_i -> Decoder

- Critical path: Word encoder output -> cross-attention -> decoder -> next fixation prediction

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Local vs global attention: Local improves focus but may miss long-range dependencies
  - Gaussian smoothing: Smooths attention weights but may blur sharp distinctions
  - Pre-trained BERT: Strong linguistic features but fixed context window

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor NLL on long saccades -> local window too narrow
  - Overfitting on small datasets -> too many parameters or insufficient dropout
  - Attention weights uniformly distributed -> cross-attention not learning alignment

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation: Remove local window -> observe drop in NLL, especially on long saccades
  2. Ablation: Remove Gaussian kernel -> compare attention sharpness and prediction accuracy
  3. Cross-dataset: Train on English, test on Chinese -> measure generalization and need for fine-tuning

## Limitations
- The local attention window (D=1) may be too restrictive for capturing longer saccades (>4 words), potentially limiting performance on certain reading strategies
- The model's ability to generalize to specialized vocabulary, technical texts, or non-standard writing systems is unknown due to domain-specific biases from pre-trained BERT
- The language-specific findings (landing position impact) are intriguing but not well-explained, with unclear underlying cognitive mechanisms

## Confidence
**High Confidence** - The negative log-likelihood results show Eyettention outperforms baselines across both English and Chinese datasets. The cross-attention mechanism with local window and Gaussian smoothing is technically sound and well-implemented.

**Medium Confidence** - The similarity metrics (NLD, MultiMatch) support the NLL findings, but these metrics have limitations in capturing perceptual similarity between predicted and actual scanpaths. The model's ability to generalize to new readers and text genres needs further validation.

**Low Confidence** - The language-specific findings (landing position impact) are intriguing but not well-explained. The paper doesn't investigate the underlying cognitive mechanisms or test whether these findings hold across different age groups, reading abilities, or languages.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-language Transfer Learning**: Train Eyettention on English data and evaluate on Chinese (and vice versa) without fine-tuning to assess the model's ability to generalize across writing systems and reading strategies.

2. **Saccade Range Analysis**: Perform detailed error analysis on different saccade ranges (short: 1-2 words, medium: 3-4 words, long: >4 words) to determine if the local attention window is limiting performance on longer eye movements.

3. **Attention Visualization Study**: Generate attention weight visualizations across different reading contexts (complex sentences, technical terms, familiar vs unfamiliar words) to verify that the model is attending to linguistically relevant regions and not just nearby words.
