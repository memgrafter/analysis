# Hierarchical Explanations for Video Action Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.00436
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.00436
- Reference count: 40
- The paper proposes Hierarchical ProtoPNet, a video action recognition model that provides multi-level explanations by learning hierarchical prototypes aligned to action embeddings in hyperbolic space

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Hierarchical ProtoPNet, an interpretable video action recognition model that addresses the accuracy-explainability tradeoff by leveraging hierarchical class relationships. The model learns hierarchical prototypes for action classes at multiple semantic levels (child, parent, grandparent) and matches them to hyperbolic action embeddings. This approach enables richer explanations while maintaining or improving classification accuracy compared to non-interpretable baselines. Experiments on UCF-101 and ActivityNet demonstrate that Hierarchical ProtoPNet achieves better performance than regular ProtoPNet (80.40% vs 78.30% class accuracy on UCF-101) while providing multi-level explanations that help understand misclassifications.

## Method Summary
Hierarchical ProtoPNet extracts video features using a 3D CNN, then learns spatiotemporal prototypes for child, parent, and grandparent action classes. These prototypes are aligned to hyperbolic action embeddings that capture hierarchical relationships between classes. During inference, the model matches input video features to the learned prototypes at multiple levels, providing explanations that show why a video belongs to a particular class while also revealing relevant higher-level semantic relationships. The hyperbolic space embedding allows the model to naturally represent hierarchical class relationships, where parent classes are closer to the origin than their children.

## Key Results
- On UCF-101, Hierarchical ProtoPNet achieves 80.40% class accuracy compared to 78.30% for regular ProtoPNet
- On ActivityNet, the model shows improvements in sibling and cousin accuracy metrics
- The hierarchical explanations enable better understanding of misclassifications by showing relevant prototypes at multiple semantic levels
- The approach maintains comparable or better accuracy than non-interpretable baselines while providing richer explanations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Hierarchical ProtoPNet works by explicitly modeling the semantic relationships between action classes through hierarchical prototype learning. By aligning prototypes to hyperbolic action embeddings, the model captures the natural hierarchical structure of action classes where parent classes encompass multiple child classes. This alignment allows the model to provide explanations at multiple semantic levels simultaneously, revealing not just why a video belongs to a specific class but also how it relates to broader action categories. The spatiotemporal prototype matching ensures that explanations are grounded in actual visual features rather than abstract decision boundaries.

## Foundational Learning
- **Hyperbolic space embeddings**: Represent hierarchical relationships where parent classes are closer to origin than children; needed because Euclidean space cannot efficiently capture tree-like hierarchies; quick check: verify that parent-child distances follow expected hyperbolic geometry
- **ProtoPNet architecture**: Uses prototypical parts for classification with interpretable explanations; needed to provide visual explanations for model decisions; quick check: ensure prototypes correspond to actual visual patterns in videos
- **Hierarchical class relationships**: Captures semantic relationships between action classes at multiple levels; needed to provide multi-level explanations; quick check: verify that learned prototypes align with semantic hierarchy
- **3D CNN feature extraction**: Extracts spatiotemporal features from videos; needed as input representation for prototype matching; quick check: ensure features capture both spatial and temporal information

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map:
Video -> 3D CNN -> Feature Extraction -> Prototype Matching (Child Level) -> Prototype Matching (Parent Level) -> Prototype Matching (Grandparent Level) -> Classification + Explanations

Critical Path:
Video input → 3D CNN feature extraction → Hierarchical prototype matching → Class prediction + multi-level explanations

Design Tradeoffs:
- Number of prototypes per class: More prototypes improve accuracy but increase computational cost and may reduce interpretability
- Prototype learning rate: Higher rates speed training but risk missing optimal prototype locations
- Hyperbolic embedding dimensionality: Higher dimensions capture more complex relationships but increase computational overhead

Failure Signatures:
- Poor hierarchical alignment: Explanations show inconsistent relationships between levels
- Prototype collapse: Multiple prototypes converge to similar visual patterns
- Hyperparameter sensitivity: Performance highly dependent on prototype count and learning rates

First Experiments:
1. Ablation study removing hierarchical levels to quantify contribution of multi-level explanations
2. Sensitivity analysis varying number of prototypes per class (3, 5, 10, 15)
3. Runtime comparison with regular ProtoPNet across different video resolutions

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the number of prototypes per class affect the accuracy-explainability tradeoff in Hierarchical ProtoPNet?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "we compare between prototype projection with 5 prototypes per class and 10 prototypes per class" and shows that increasing prototypes improves performance metrics.
- Why unresolved: The paper provides results for 5 and 10 prototypes but doesn't explore the full range of possible values or provide theoretical justification for the optimal number.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying the number of prototypes per class (e.g., 3, 5, 7, 10, 15) on both UCF-101 and ActivityNet, showing the relationship between prototype count and both accuracy and explanation quality.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How well does Hierarchical ProtoPNet generalize to datasets with different hierarchical structures?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only evaluates on UCF-101 and ActivityNet with their specific hierarchies, without testing on datasets with different hierarchical relationships.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates effectiveness on two datasets but doesn't establish whether the approach generalizes to other types of hierarchical relationships or completely different domains.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing Hierarchical ProtoPNet on datasets like Something-Something, Kinetics, or custom datasets with varying hierarchical structures, comparing performance to non-hierarchical baselines.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the computational overhead of providing multi-level explanations compared to single-level explanations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Our hierarchical approach thereby provides richer explanations whilst compromising less performance to gain explainability" and "Multi-level explanation for a video of rafting with Hierarchical ProtoPNet" but doesn't provide runtime comparisons.
- Why unresolved: While the paper claims no additional computational complexity, it doesn't quantify the actual computational overhead of generating multi-level explanations versus single-level ones.
- What evidence would resolve it: Runtime measurements comparing inference time and memory usage for Hierarchical ProtoPNet versus regular ProtoPNet, broken down by each level of explanation generation.

## Limitations
- Performance improvements may not generalize to datasets with different hierarchical structures or flatter class relationships
- Computational complexity of learning and matching prototypes across multiple hierarchical levels is not thoroughly analyzed
- Evaluation metrics may not fully capture practical utility of hierarchical explanations for end-users
