# Causal Recurrent Variational Autoencoder for Medical Time Series Generation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.06574
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06574
- Reference count: 5
- CR-VAE generates multivariate time series while learning Granger causal relationships using a multi-head decoder with sparsity-inducing penalties

## Executive Summary
Causal Recurrent Variational Autoencoder (CR-VAE) is a novel generative model designed to produce multivariate time series data while simultaneously learning Granger causal relationships between dimensions. The model employs a multi-head decoder architecture where each head is responsible for generating one dimension of the time series, and sparsity-inducing penalties on decoder weights are used to encode causal relations in a learned adjacency matrix. CR-VAE is trained using a two-stage strategy combining proximal gradient descent and SGD, demonstrating superior performance on both synthetic and real medical datasets (EEG, fMRI) compared to state-of-the-art time series generative models.

## Method Summary
CR-VAE introduces a novel approach to time series generation by incorporating causal discovery into the generative process. The model uses a recurrent variational autoencoder architecture with a multi-head decoder, where each head generates one dimension of the output time series. Sparsity-inducing penalties on the decoder weights encourage the learning of sparse causal structures, represented as an adjacency matrix. The training process employs a two-stage strategy: first using proximal gradient descent to encourage sparsity in the causal structure, followed by standard SGD to fine-tune the model. This approach allows CR-VAE to generate realistic time series while simultaneously discovering the underlying Granger causal relationships between dimensions.

## Key Results
- CR-VAE outperforms state-of-the-art time series generative models on synthetic and real medical datasets (EEG, fMRI) in both qualitative (t-SNE visualizations) and quantitative (MMD, TSTR-RMSE) evaluations
- The model successfully discovers accurate Granger causal graphs, with performance comparable or superior to existing causal inference methods like KGC, TE, TCDF, and NGC
- CR-VAE achieves lower distribution distance and prediction error compared to baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness in both generation and causal discovery tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
CR-VAE's effectiveness stems from its unique combination of generative modeling and causal discovery. By using a multi-head decoder architecture, each dimension of the time series is generated independently, allowing for explicit modeling of causal relationships between dimensions. The sparsity-inducing penalties on decoder weights encourage the learning of sparse causal structures, represented as an adjacency matrix, which directly encodes Granger causal relationships. The two-stage training strategy first promotes sparsity in the causal structure through proximal gradient descent, then refines the model using standard SGD, resulting in a model that can generate realistic time series while simultaneously discovering the underlying causal relationships.

## Architecture Onboarding

### Component Map
CR-VAE consists of an encoder that maps input time series to a latent space, a recurrent decoder with multiple heads (one per dimension), and sparsity-inducing penalties on decoder weights. The encoder -> latent space -> multi-head decoder forms the critical path.

### Critical Path
The critical path in CR-VAE is: Input time series -> Encoder (RNN) -> Latent representation -> Multi-head decoder (RNNs) -> Generated time series dimensions. Sparsity-inducing penalties on decoder weights encourage learning of causal relationships.

### Design Tradeoffs
CR-VAE trades increased model complexity (multi-head decoder, sparsity penalties) for the ability to simultaneously generate time series and discover causal relationships. The two-stage training strategy balances sparsity promotion with overall model performance.

### Failure Signatures
Potential failure modes include:
- Inability to learn meaningful causal structures if sparsity penalties are too strong
- Poor generation quality if the latent space does not adequately capture the data distribution
- Difficulty scaling to very high-dimensional time series due to the multi-head decoder architecture

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses primarily on datasets with known ground truth causal structures (synthetic) or where causal discovery is difficult to validate (real medical data), making it challenging to assess true causal accuracy
- The claim that CR-VAE "achieves comparable or superior performance" to existing causal inference methods lacks statistical significance testing across experiments
- The two-stage training strategy may be computationally intensive and could limit scalability to larger datasets

## Confidence
