# Large Language Models Can Be Easily Distracted by Irrelevant Context

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.00093
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00093
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Large language models show significant performance drops (macro accuracy below 30%) when arithmetic reasoning problems contain irrelevant context, with self-consistency and instruction prompts achieving up to 45% macro accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces GSM-IC, a benchmark that evaluates how language models handle irrelevant information in arithmetic reasoning tasks. The benchmark adds a single irrelevant sentence to each problem in GSM8K, then measures model distractibility using micro and macro accuracy metrics. The authors systematically evaluate prompting techniques including chain-of-thought, least-to-most prompting, and program-aided prompting, finding that all techniques are sensitive to irrelevant context. While self-consistency and explicit instructions to ignore irrelevant information improve robustness, the best macro accuracy remains below 45%, highlighting that current prompting techniques struggle with context understanding.

## Method Summary
The GSM-IC benchmark is constructed by adding irrelevant sentences to GSM8K problems using template-based generation with orthogonal factors (topic, role overlap, number range). The authors evaluate multiple prompting techniques on a 4,000-example subset, comparing greedy decoding (τ=0) against self-consistency (20 samples, τ=0.7). They measure micro accuracy (overall performance), macro accuracy (performance on individual problem variants), and normalized accuracy (to quantify distractibility). The evaluation also tests whether adding irrelevant context to exemplars and providing explicit instructions to ignore irrelevant information can mitigate distractibility.

## Key Results
- All prompting techniques show significant macro accuracy drops when irrelevant context is added
- Self-consistency and instructions to ignore irrelevant context improve robustness, with best macro accuracy reaching 45%
- Least-to-most prompting is the most robust technique overall
- Even with 96.5% recall of correct solutions via self-consistency, macro accuracy remains below 45%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Self-consistency improves robustness by marginalizing over intermediate reasoning steps that share the same final result
- Mechanism: By sampling 20 responses and taking the majority vote, self-consistency ensures that at least one of the sampled solutions retrieves the correct answer even when irrelevant context distracts the model
- Core assumption: The correct answer is among the sampled responses even when the model is distracted
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "decoding with self-consistency and adding to the prompt an instruction that tells the language model to ignore the irrelevant information"
  - [section] "Self-consistency (SC; Wang et al., 2022c) may further boost the reasoning performance by marginalizing over intermediate reasoning steps that share the same final result"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in corpus about self-consistency specifically
- Break condition: If the sampling temperature is too low (τ = 0) or too high (τ = 0.7), the model may not explore enough diverse reasoning paths to find the correct answer

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Adding irrelevant information to the exemplars in the prompt teaches the model to ignore irrelevant context
- Mechanism: By showing examples with irrelevant information, the model learns to focus on the relevant information needed to solve the problem
- Core assumption: The model can learn from examples in the prompt how to handle irrelevant information
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we also identify several approaches for mitigating this deficiency, such as decoding with self-consistency and adding to the prompt an instruction that tells the language model to ignore the irrelevant information"
  - [section] "Adding irrelevant information to the exemplars shown in the prompt consistently boosts the performance"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in corpus about adding irrelevant information to exemplars
- Break condition: If the irrelevant information in the exemplars is too similar to the irrelevant information in the test problem, the model may still be distracted

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Instructing the model to ignore irrelevant information improves robustness
- Mechanism: By adding the instruction "feel free to ignore irrelevant information in the problem description" to the prompt, the model is explicitly told to focus on the relevant information
- Core assumption: The model can follow natural language instructions to change its problem-solving behavior
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we also identify several approaches for mitigating this deficiency, such as decoding with self-consistency and adding to the prompt an instruction that tells the language model to ignore the irrelevant information"
  - [section] "we also investigate whether natural language instructions help language models ignore irrelevant context and become less distracted"
  - [corpus] Weak - no direct evidence in corpus about instructing the model to ignore irrelevant information
- Break condition: If the instruction is not clear or the model does not understand the instruction, it may still be distracted by the irrelevant information

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we improve the macro accuracy of language models on GSM-IC beyond the current 45% best result?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "Even for 0-COT, the recall of correct solutions within 20 samples is 96.5%. Despite these improvements, the best macro accuracy among all prompting techniques is only 45%, suggesting that for more than half of the base problems, SC fails to prevent the model from being distracted by different variants of irrelevant information."
- Why unresolved: The paper shows that self-consistency and adding instructions to ignore irrelevant context help, but the macro accuracy remains low. There is a gap between the high recall rate (99.7% for COT and LTM) and the low macro accuracy (45%), indicating that while correct answers can be found, the models struggle to consistently identify them across all irrelevant context variants.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a new algorithm that can better identify the correct answer from multiple sampled solutions, or finding a way to make the models more robust to all types of irrelevant context.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the underlying mechanisms that make LTM more robust to irrelevant context compared to other prompting techniques?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "LTM is the most robust technique to irrelevant context. LTM significantly outperforms all other prompting methods except for 2-step problems with greedy decoding, where COT achieves comparable micro accuracy as LTM."
- Why unresolved: The paper observes that LTM is more robust, but does not provide a detailed analysis of why this is the case. Understanding the underlying mechanisms could help in designing more robust prompting techniques.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analyzing the intermediate reasoning steps and attention patterns of LTM compared to other techniques when processing irrelevant context, and identifying the key differences that contribute to its robustness.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the complexity of irrelevant context (e.g., number of irrelevant sentences, semantic relatedness to the problem) affect the distractibility of language models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper introduces GSM-IC with a single irrelevant sentence and analyzes the effect of different factors of the irrelevant context (topic, role overlap, number range). However, it does not explore the impact of more complex irrelevant contexts.
- Why unresolved: The current study only considers a single irrelevant sentence with controlled factors. Real-world problems may contain multiple irrelevant sentences or more complex semantic relationships, which could have a more significant impact on distractibility.
- What evidence would resolve it: Extending GSM-IC to include problems with multiple irrelevant sentences and varying degrees of semantic relatedness, and evaluating the performance of language models on these more complex scenarios.

### Open Question 4
- Question: Can fine-tuning or training language models on GSM-IC improve their robustness to irrelevant context?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "In our evaluation, we show that without training or fine-tuning, adding irrelevant context into demonstrations in the prompt also mitigates the distractibility of the underlying language model and significantly improves the model performance on our GSM-IC benchmark."
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on prompting techniques without fine-tuning. It would be interesting to see if fine-tuning on GSM-IC or similar datasets could lead to more robust models that are less distracted by irrelevant context.
- What evidence would resolve it: Fine-tuning a language model on GSM-IC and evaluating its performance on the original GSM8K dataset and GSM-IC, comparing the results to the prompting techniques discussed in the paper.

## Limitations
- The benchmark construction relies on template-based irrelevant sentence generation that may not capture real-world distractor complexity
- Evaluation focuses primarily on code-davinci-002, limiting conclusions about other model architectures
- Even with self-consistency and instruction prompts, macro accuracy remains below 45%, suggesting fundamental limitations in current approaches

## Confidence
- High confidence: The core finding that language models are easily distracted by irrelevant context is well-supported across multiple prompting techniques and evaluation metrics
- Medium confidence: The effectiveness of self-consistency and instruction-based approaches for mitigating distractibility may be sensitive to implementation details
- Low confidence: The claim that least-to-most prompting should theoretically handle irrelevant context well is contradicted by experimental results
