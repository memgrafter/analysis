# WikiGoldSK: Annotated Dataset, Baselines and Few-Shot Learning Experiments for Slovak Named Entity Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04026
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04026
- Reference count: 10
- This work introduces WikiGoldSK, the first manually annotated Slovak NER dataset based on Slovak Wikipedia articles

## Executive Summary
This work addresses the lack of high-quality manually annotated Named Entity Recognition (NER) datasets for the Slovak language by introducing WikiGoldSK, a human-labeled Slovak NER dataset based on Slovak Wikipedia articles. The dataset contains 412 articles annotated with four entity classes (LOC, PER, ORG, MISC) and is publicly released under a permissive license. The authors benchmark WikiGoldSK by evaluating state-of-the-art multilingual pre-trained language models (XLM-RoBERTa, SlovakBERT, mDeBERTaV3) using three NLP toolkits (spaCy, Trankit, Transformers). They find that mDeBERTaV3 performs best on WikiGoldSK, with an F1 score of 0.9159, and that training on WikiGoldSK alone yields better results than combining it with the larger silver-standard WikiANN dataset. The authors also conduct few-shot learning experiments using the PETER method, but find that supervised models trained on silver-standard data outperform few-shot approaches, even when trained on small samples from WikiGoldSK.

## Method Summary
The authors created WikiGoldSK by sampling 412 articles from Slovak Wikipedia, tokenizing them, and manually annotating named entities with four classes (LOC, PER, ORG, MISC) using an IOB2 tagging scheme. Three expert annotators performed the annotation with high agreement (Cohen's kappa coefficient of 0.90). The dataset was split into train/dev/test sets for evaluation. For benchmarking, the authors fine-tuned three pre-trained language models (XLM-RoBERTa, SlovakBERT, mDeBERTaV3) using three NLP toolkits (spaCy, Trankit, Transformers) on the WikiGoldSK dataset. They also conducted few-shot learning experiments using the PETER method with two pattern-verbalizer pairs, sampling 1000 sentences from the WikiGoldSK training set for soft-label creation.

## Key Results
- mDeBERTaV3 achieved the best performance on WikiGoldSK with an F1 score of 0.9159
- Training on WikiGoldSK alone outperformed combining it with the larger silver-standard WikiANN dataset (F1 difference > 0.27)
- Few-shot learning approaches (PETER) underperformed supervised models even with small amounts of silver-standard data
- The dataset contains 412 manually annotated articles with high inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's kappa = 0.90)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Fine-tuning pre-trained multilingual models on manually annotated Slovak NER data outperforms training on larger silver-standard datasets because the manually annotated dataset (WikiGoldSK) contains higher quality labels than automatically generated silver-standard data, reducing noise and improving model performance. The manual annotation process produces more accurate labels than silver-standard methods, and the increased label quality compensates for the smaller dataset size.

### Mechanism 2
mDeBERTaV3 performs better than XLM-RoBERTa and SlovakBERT for Slovak NER because it uses an ELECTRA-style pre-training objective with gradient-disentangled embedding sharing, which leads to more efficient training and better performance on benchmarks compared to standard masked language modeling. The architectural improvements in mDeBERTaV3 (particularly the discriminative pre-training objective) generalize well to the Slovak language and NER task.

### Mechanism 3
Few-shot learning methods (specifically PETER) underperform supervised models even with small amounts of silver-standard data because few-shot approaches rely on pattern-verbalizer pairs and cloze-style prompts to leverage pre-trained language models, but this approach is less effective than direct supervised fine-tuning for the NER task. The few-shot learning paradigm is not yet mature enough for complex structured prediction tasks like NER, especially when reasonable amounts of silver-standard data are available.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Wikipedia article extraction -> tokenization -> manual annotation -> train/dev/test split -> model fine-tuning -> evaluation

- Critical path:
  1. Sample and tokenize Wikipedia articles
  2. Manual annotation with entity classes (LOC, PER, ORG, MISC)
  3. Split data into train/dev/test sets
  4. Fine-tune selected pre-trained models
  5. Evaluate on test set and compare to silver-standard baselines

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Manual annotation provides higher quality but is more expensive and results in smaller datasets
  - Silver-standard datasets are larger but contain more noise
  - Using Wikipedia as source provides diverse topics but may not cover all domains (e.g., newswire)

- Failure signatures:
  - Low agreement between annotators (high Cohen's kappa coefficient indicates good agreement at 0.90)
  - Significant performance gap between models trained on gold vs silver data
  - Poor transfer to out-of-domain data (e.g., newswire)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Fine-tune XLM-RoBERTa on WikiGoldSK and evaluate on the test set
  2. Fine-tune SlovakBERT on WikiGoldSK and compare performance to XLM-RoBERTa
  3. Fine-tune mDeBERTaV3 on WikiGoldSK and compare to other models

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several important questions arise from the findings: (1) Does the size of WikiGoldSK (412 articles) limit its effectiveness for training Slovak NER models compared to larger datasets like WikiANN? (2) Can few-shot learning approaches be improved for Slovak NER by using better pattern-verbalizer pairs or more sophisticated few-shot learning techniques? (3) How well do models trained on WikiGoldSK generalize to other domains beyond Wikipedia articles, such as newswire or social media text?

## Limitations
- The relatively small size of WikiGoldSK (412 articles) may limit its coverage of linguistic diversity needed for robust NER across all Slovak text domains
- The Wikipedia-based origin introduces potential bias toward encyclopedic language rather than representing domains like newswire, social media, or technical documentation
- The few-shot learning experiments only evaluate one method (PETER) with limited hyperparameter tuning, leaving open the possibility that other approaches might perform better

## Confidence
- High confidence: The finding that mDeBERTaV3 outperforms other pre-trained models on WikiGoldSK is well-supported by experimental results with substantial performance margins
- Medium confidence: The claim that training on WikiGoldSK alone outperforms combining it with WikiANN requires careful interpretation as it assumes equivalent training resources
- Medium confidence: The conclusion that few-shot approaches underperform supervised methods is supported by experiments, but is limited to the specific PETER method tested
