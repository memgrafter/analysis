# Are Defenses for Graph Neural Networks Robust?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13694
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13694
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Defenses for graph neural networks provide only marginal improvements over undefended baselines when evaluated with strong adaptive attacks

## Executive Summary
This paper critically examines the robustness of graph neural networks (GNNs) under adversarial attacks by analyzing the effectiveness of proposed defense mechanisms. The authors systematically evaluate 7 popular defenses spanning different strategies (graph purification, robust optimization, joint purification and re-training) and find that they provide only marginal improvements over undefended baselines when evaluated with strong adaptive attacks. The study reveals that most defenses forfeit a large fraction of their previously reported robustness gains when subjected to attacks that are specifically designed to exploit their weaknesses.

## Method Summary
The authors implement a methodology for designing adaptive attacks that involves understanding the defense mechanism, probing for weaknesses, launching gradient-based attacks, addressing gradient issues, adjusting the attack loss, and tuning hyperparameters. They evaluate both evasion attacks (where the attacker can only modify the graph at test time) and poisoning attacks (where the attacker can modify the graph during training) using three attack strategies: Fast Gradient Attack (FGA), Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), and Meta-PGD. Experiments are conducted on Cora ML and CiteSeer datasets with 5 different data splits, comparing the adversarial accuracy under adaptive attacks versus non-adaptive attacks.

## Key Results
- Most defenses forfeit a large fraction of their previously reported robustness gains when evaluated with adaptive attacks
- The performance of some defenses is even worse than an undefended GCN under adaptive attacks
- Only 14% of defenses (7 out of 49 examined) have been evaluated with adaptive attacks in previous literature
- The effectiveness of defenses varies significantly across different datasets, with most defenses developed only using Cora ML and Citeseer as benchmarks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adaptive attacks significantly reduce the reported robustness of graph neural network defenses compared to non-adaptive attacks.
- Mechanism: By exploiting full knowledge of the model architecture, parameters, and defense mechanisms, adaptive attacks can tailor perturbations specifically to bypass the defense strategies, whereas non-adaptive attacks use generic perturbations that may not be effective against the specific defense.
- Core assumption: The defense mechanisms are differentiable or can be approximated in a differentiable manner to allow gradient-based optimization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "virtually all of the defenses are evaluated against non-adaptive attacks leading to overly optimistic robustness estimates"
  - [section] "We design custom attacks for 7 defenses (14%), covering the spectrum of defense techniques. All examined models forfeit a large fraction of previously reported robustness gains"
- Break condition: If the defense mechanism is non-differentiable and cannot be approximated, adaptive gradient-based attacks become ineffective.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Many graph neural network defenses can be broken by exploiting their implicit filtering of edges based on certain properties.
- Mechanism: Defenses often identify and filter edges with certain characteristics (e.g., low feature similarity), but adaptive attacks can restrict their search space to edges that bypass these filters while still causing misclassification.
- Core assumption: The attacker can identify the filtering criteria used by the defense and adapt their attack strategy accordingly.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "One theme shared by some defenses is to first discover some property that can discriminate clean from adversarial edges... Often they analyze the edges from only a single attack such as Nettack"
  - [section] "The obvious pitfall of this strategy is that the attacker can easily adapt by restricting the adversarial search space to edges that will bypass the defense's (implicit) filter"
- Break condition: If the defense uses a property that cannot be easily identified or if the filtering is based on dynamic or stochastic criteria.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The effectiveness of graph neural network defenses varies significantly across different datasets.
- Mechanism: Defenses that work well on one dataset (e.g., Cora ML) may be much less effective on another (e.g., Citeseer), suggesting that the underlying graph structure and properties influence the defense's ability to mitigate attacks.
- Core assumption: The graph structure, feature distributions, and homophily levels differ substantially between datasets, affecting how attacks can be constructed and how defenses can be bypassed.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "As we can see in Fig. 4 and Fig. 5, our ability to circumvent specific defenses tends to depend on the dataset"
  - [section] "This points to another potentially problematic pitfall: most defenses are developed only using these two datasets as benchmarks"
- Break condition: If a defense is designed with dataset-agnostic principles or if the attack methodology is robust to variations in graph structure and properties.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Data preprocessing -> Model implementation (GNN + defense) -> Attack implementation (adaptive/non-adaptive) -> Evaluation (accuracy, AUC, RAUC)
- Critical path:
  1. Load and preprocess the graph data
  2. Implement the GNN model and defense mechanism
  3. Implement the adaptive attack strategy
  4. Run the attack and evaluate the model's performance
  5. Compare the results with non-adaptive attacks and baseline models
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational efficiency vs. attack strength: Stronger adaptive attacks may require more computational resources and time to converge
  - Attack generalization vs. specificity: Attacks that are highly specific to a particular defense may not generalize well to other defenses
  - Defense complexity vs. robustness: More complex defense mechanisms may provide better robustness but at the cost of increased computational overhead and potential overfitting to specific attack patterns
- Failure signatures:
  - Adaptive attacks fail to significantly reduce the model's accuracy: The defense mechanism may be too strong or the attack strategy may need to be adjusted
  - Non-adaptive attacks perform as well as or better than adaptive attacks: The defense may be relying on specific attack patterns that are not captured by non-adaptive attacks
  - The model's accuracy on clean data drops significantly after implementing the defense: The defense may be overly aggressive in filtering edges or modifying the graph structure
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a simple GNN model (e.g., GCN) and evaluate its performance on a standard dataset (e.g., Cora ML) without any defense mechanism
  2. Implement a basic defense mechanism (e.g., Jaccard-GCN) and evaluate its performance against non-adaptive attacks (e.g., Nettack)
  3. Implement an adaptive attack strategy (e.g., PGD) and evaluate its performance against the defended model, comparing the results with non-adaptive attacks

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- What specific defense mechanisms could be developed to improve the robustness of GNNs against adaptive attacks, particularly in the global and local settings?
- How can the scalability of adaptive attacks be improved to handle larger graphs with millions of nodes and edges?
- What are the potential negative societal impacts of successful adversarial attacks on GNNs, and how can these be mitigated?

## Limitations
- The study relies on standard benchmark datasets (Cora ML and Citeseer) which may not represent the full diversity of real-world graph structures
- The adaptive attack methodology, while comprehensive, may still miss certain defense strategies or edge cases
- The computational cost of running adaptive attacks against multiple defenses limits the scope of experiments that can be practically conducted

## Confidence
- Adaptive attacks reduce defense robustness: High
- Dataset-specific defense effectiveness: Medium
- Defenses provide marginal improvements: High
- Generalizability to larger graphs: Low
