# Leveraging Factored Action Spaces for Efficient Offline Reinforcement Learning in Healthcare

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.01738
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01738
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: Proposed method improves sample efficiency in offline RL with large combinatorial action spaces by leveraging factored action space structure.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of applying reinforcement learning to real-world problems with large, combinatorial action spaces, particularly in offline settings where data is limited. The authors propose leveraging the inherent factorization structure of such action spaces to improve sample efficiency and policy performance. The core method involves decomposing the Q-function into a sum of components, each depending on only one sub-action and the full state space, reducing the number of free parameters and variance of the estimator compared to standard approaches.

## Method Summary
The proposed method involves decomposing the Q-function into a linear combination of D sub-functions, each depending on one sub-action and the full state space. This is implemented as D separate output heads in a neural network, where each head computes qd(s, ad) for sub-action ad. The final Q-value is obtained by summing these sub-function outputs: Q(s,a) = Σd qd(s, ad). This factorization reduces the number of parameters from exponential in D to linear, lowering Rademacher complexity and estimator variance. The approach is validated in both sepsis simulation and real-world healthcare data, showing improved performance especially with limited sample sizes compared to baselines that do not leverage factored action spaces.

## Key Results
- Linear decomposition reduces the number of free parameters from exponential to linear in D, lowering Rademacher complexity
- Theoretical analysis provides sufficient and necessary conditions for unbiased estimates
- Experiments on sepsis simulator and real-world healthcare data show improved performance, especially with limited sample sizes
- Identified scenarios where biased Q-function still yields optimal policies despite approximation errors

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Linear decomposition reduces variance by shrinking the hypothesis space.
- **Mechanism**: The number of free parameters drops from exponential in D to linear in D, lowering Rademacher complexity and thus estimator variance.
- **Core assumption**: The factored Q-function class is realizable or close enough that bias from approximation is acceptable.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "leads to a reduction of variance, which in turn leads to an improvement in sample efficiency"
  - [section] "Proposition 5. Using the linear Q-function decomposition ... has a smaller lower bound on the empirical Rademacher complexity"
  - [corpus] weak (no direct citations on variance reduction from factored Q-space)
- **Break condition**: If the MDP's transitions/rewards violate independence enough that bias dominates variance savings, especially with large sample sizes.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Even when the linear decomposition is biased, it can still identify the optimal action.
- **Mechanism**: Sub-action independence in the reward structure allows the greedy policy under the biased Q to still pick the correct maximizing action.
- **Core assumption**: Sub-actions affect rewards independently, or their interactions do not flip the argmax ranking.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "when two sub-actions 'reinforce' their independent effects"
  - [section] "Proposition 6. There exists an MDP ... such that ˆQ ≠ Q* and yet arg maxa ˆQ(a) = arg maxaQ*(a)"
  - [corpus] weak (no literature cited on action-space independence guaranteeing policy correctness)
- **Break condition**: If sub-action interactions have opposite signs or create reward cancellation that changes the greedy action.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: Factorization is valid when the MDP can be seen as parallel sub-MDPs under abstractions.
- **Mechanism**: State abstractions that decouple sub-actions' transitions and rewards enable exact linear Q-decomposition.
- **Core assumption**: There exist abstractions φd : S → Zd satisfying the transition/reward factorization equations.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract] "sufficient and necessary conditions for this approach to yield unbiased estimates"
  - [section] "Theorem 1. Given an MDP ... if there exists D unique corresponding state abstractions φ = [φ1, ···,φD] ... then the Q-function ... can be expressed as Qπ(s,a) = ∑Dd=1 qd(s,ad)"
  - [corpus] weak (no explicit abstraction theorems cited)
- **Break condition**: If no such abstractions exist and the MDP cannot be expressed as parallel sub-MDPs.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Input state s → MLP/RNN embedding → D separate sub-action heads → Sum qd(s, ad) for final Q(s,a)
- **Critical path**:
  1. Embed state (MLP or RNN)
  2. For each sub-action head, compute qd(s, ad)
  3. For chosen a = [a1,...,aD], sum qd(s, ad) to get Q(s,a)
  4. Use in RL algorithm (e.g., FQI or BCQ)
- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - **Pros**: Fewer parameters, lower variance, better sample efficiency in small-data regimes.
  - **Cons**: Potential bias if independence assumptions violated; limited expressiveness vs. full Q-table.
- **Failure signatures**:
  - Large gap between train and test Q-values despite small dataset (overfitting to assumed structure).
  - Policy consistently choosing suboptimal actions when certain sub-action combinations are rare.
  - Model selection favors low ESS with high WIS but performance degrades in held-out data.
- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. **Baseline ablation**: Train a standard Q-network on the same dataset and compare sample efficiency.
  2. **Bias check**: Vary γ and sub-action independence in a controlled simulator; measure bias vs. variance.
  3. **Real-data sanity**: Apply to a simple healthcare task (e.g., sepsis simulator) and compare with combinatorial baseline under limited data.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the sufficient conditions for the linear Q-function decomposition to be unbiased?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that the sufficient conditions are given by Equations 2, 3, and 4 in Theorem 1.
- Why unresolved: The paper only states the conditions but does not provide a complete proof of their sufficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal proof demonstrating that satisfying these conditions guarantees zero approximation error.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the necessary conditions for the linear Q-function decomposition to be unbiased?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions deriving a necessary condition in Appendix B.5 but states it is not verifiable unless the exact MDP parameters are known.
- Why unresolved: The necessary condition is stated but not explicitly given, and the paper indicates it is difficult to verify in practice.
- What evidence would resolve it: A clear statement of the necessary conditions and a proof of their necessity.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the bias introduced by the linear decomposition affect policy learning performance?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses that bias can affect policy learning but identifies scenarios where it does not lead to suboptimal performance (Section 3.3.2).
- Why unresolved: The paper provides theoretical bounds but does not give a complete characterization of when bias does or does not lead to suboptimal policies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results showing policy performance across a range of bias levels and MDP structures, or a more comprehensive theoretical analysis.

## Limitations

- Theoretical variance reduction is proven but not directly validated experimentally
- Bias tolerance conditions are identified but not thoroughly characterized across different MDP structures
- Empirical validation is limited to sepsis simulation and one real-world healthcare dataset
- The method assumes action space factorization is available, which may not hold for all healthcare problems

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The linear Q-decomposition structure and its implementation as D separate output heads is technically sound and correctly implemented.
- **Medium confidence**: The theoretical analysis of when the factorization yields unbiased estimates (Theorem 1) is rigorous, but the practical applicability depends on finding appropriate state abstractions.
- **Low confidence**: The claim that variance reduction leads to improved sample efficiency in real-world healthcare settings lacks direct empirical support; the experiments show performance gains but don't isolate the variance reduction mechanism.

## Next Checks

2. **Bias sensitivity study**: Systematically vary the degree of sub-action independence in the simulator and measure how bias in the factored Q-function affects policy performance, identifying the threshold where bias outweighs variance benefits.

3. **Offline evaluation robustness**: Apply the method to multiple real-world healthcare datasets with different action-space structures and compare performance across varying sample sizes to assess generalizability beyond the sepsis simulator.
