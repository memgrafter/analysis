# Neighborhood-Regularized Self-Training for Learning with Few Labels

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.03726
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03726
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 15

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of training deep neural networks with limited labeled data by improving self-training methods. The authors propose Neighborhood-Regularized Self-Training (NeST), which leverages sample representations to reduce label noise and stabilize training. NeST uses a neighborhood-based sample selection approach that selects unlabeled samples based on their similarity to labeled neighbors in the embedding space. Additionally, it aggregates predictions from different iterations to mitigate training instability. Experiments on eight tasks (four text and four graph datasets) show that NeST outperforms the strongest self-training baseline with 1.83% and 2.51% performance gains on average.

## Method Summary
NeST builds upon the self-training framework by introducing neighborhood-regularized sample selection and prediction aggregation. The method extracts embeddings from a teacher model, computes k-nearest neighbors for unlabeled samples in the embedding space, and calculates divergence scores between unlabeled samples and their neighbors. These scores are aggregated across iterations to stabilize sample selection. The method is evaluated on eight datasets with varying amounts of labeled data, comparing performance against multiple self-training baselines.

## Key Results
- NeST achieves 1.83% and 2.51% performance gains over strongest self-training baselines on text and graph datasets respectively
- Reduces pseudo label noise by 36.8% compared to best baseline
- Saves 57.3% of running time compared to best baseline
- Demonstrates effectiveness across diverse tasks including classification and regression

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Neighborhood-regularized sample selection reduces label noise by leveraging representation similarity
- Mechanism: Uses k-nearest neighbors in embedding space to find similar labeled samples, then calculates divergence between unlabeled sample predictions and neighbors' labels
- Core assumption: Samples with similar labels share similar representations, making neighbor label similarity a reliable denoising signal
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Inspired by the fact that samples with similar labels tend to share similar representations, we develop a neighborhood-based sample selection approach to tackle the issue of noisy pseudo labels."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Prediction aggregation over iterations stabilizes training by reducing iteration-specific noise
- Mechanism: Maintains running weighted average of divergence scores across iterations, favoring samples with consistent low uncertainty
- Core assumption: Inconsistent predictions across iterations indicate higher uncertainty, so aggregating stabilizes sample selection
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "We further stabilize self-training via aggregating the predictions from different rounds during sample selection."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Divergence-based scoring improves over confidence/uncertainty by incorporating label consistency
- Mechanism: Combines unlabeled divergence (prediction vs neighbors) and labeled divergence (consistency among neighbors) into a single score
- Core assumption: Low divergence indicates both correct prediction and consistent neighbor labels, improving sample reliability
- Evidence anchors: [section 4.1] "We then calculate the scores for unlabeled samples... based on the weighted divergence... This score will be further used for sample selection."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Teacher model -> Embedding extraction -> KNN neighbor finding -> Divergence calculation -> Aggregation -> Sample selection -> Student model update -> Repeat
- Critical path: Embedding -> Neighborhood -> Divergence -> Aggregation -> Selection (each step must succeed for stable training)
- Design tradeoffs: k (neighborhood size) vs. noise reduction; aggregation weight m vs. responsiveness; computational cost of KNN vs. accuracy gain
- Failure signatures: High pseudo-label error rates; unstable performance across iterations; excessive runtime due to large neighborhoods

## Limitations

- Major uncertainties remain around the robustness of NeST in scenarios where the embedding space is less discriminative or where neighborhood assumptions fail (e.g., heterophily in graphs)
- The computational cost of KNN neighbor search in high-dimensional spaces is not fully addressed, which could limit scalability
- The paper assumes that samples with similar labels share similar representations, but this may not hold for all datasets or when noise is severe

## Confidence

- Mechanism 1 (neighborhood denoising): High - supported by clear theoretical motivation and experimental results
- Mechanism 2 (prediction aggregation): Medium - theoretically sound but dependent on stable embeddings
- Mechanism 3 (divergence scoring): Medium - improves over confidence/uncertainty but lacks direct ablation
