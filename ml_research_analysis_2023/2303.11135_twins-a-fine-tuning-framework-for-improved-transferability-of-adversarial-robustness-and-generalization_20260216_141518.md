# TWINS: A Fine-Tuning Framework for Improved Transferability of Adversarial Robustness and Generalization

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11135
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11135
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Proposed TWINS framework improves both clean and robust accuracy during fine-tuning of adversarially pre-trained models

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the fine-tuning of adversarially pre-trained models for downstream tasks, focusing on maintaining both generalization and adversarial robustness. The authors identify that standard model-based and data-based approaches fail to improve robustness during fine-tuning, as they overly preserve pre-training information and hinder downstream learning. To address this, they propose TWINS, a statistics-based method using two neural networks—one with frozen batch normalization (BN) statistics from pre-training and one with adaptive BN layers. TWINS retains pre-training robustness while increasing the effective learning rate by breaking the weight norm-gradient norm relationship in standard BN layers, leading to faster escape from suboptimal initialization and reduced robust overfitting. Experimental results on five image classification datasets show that TWINS improves both clean and robust accuracy compared to adversarial training (AT) and TRADES baselines. For example, TWINS-AT achieves 2.18% higher clean accuracy and 1.21% higher robust accuracy than AT on average.

## Method Summary
The TWINS framework addresses the challenge of fine-tuning adversarially pre-trained models by using two separate neural networks. One network maintains frozen batch normalization statistics from pre-training, preserving the robustness learned during adversarial training. The second network uses adaptive batch normalization layers that can learn from the downstream task data. This dual-network approach allows TWINS to maintain pre-training robustness while enabling effective learning for the new task. The key innovation lies in breaking the weight norm-gradient norm relationship in standard BN layers, which increases the effective learning rate and helps the model escape suboptimal initialization points. This mechanism reduces robust overfitting and improves both clean and robust accuracy on downstream tasks.

## Key Results
- TWINS-AT achieves 2.18% higher clean accuracy and 1.21% higher robust accuracy than standard AT on average across five datasets
- TWINS improves both clean and robust accuracy compared to adversarial training and TRADES baselines
- Experimental validation demonstrates strong potential for leveraging robust pre-trained models in downstream tasks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of TWINS stems from its dual-network architecture that balances two competing objectives: preserving pre-training robustness and enabling effective learning for downstream tasks. By maintaining frozen batch normalization statistics in one network, TWINS preserves the robustness characteristics learned during adversarial pre-training. The second network with adaptive batch normalization layers can learn task-specific features. The critical mechanism is breaking the weight norm-gradient norm relationship in standard BN layers, which increases the effective learning rate. This acceleration helps the model escape suboptimal initialization points that typically trap standard fine-tuning approaches, reducing robust overfitting while maintaining generalization.

## Foundational Learning

**Adversarial Training (AT)**: Training models to be robust against adversarial attacks by incorporating adversarial examples during training. Needed to understand the baseline robustness that TWINS aims to preserve during fine-tuning. Quick check: Verify that pre-trained models used in experiments were actually trained with AT methods.

**Batch Normalization (BN)**: Normalization technique that maintains running statistics of activations during training. Critical for understanding how TWINS uses frozen versus adaptive BN statistics. Quick check: Confirm that the frozen BN statistics are properly computed from the pre-training phase.

**Robust Overfitting**: Phenomenon where models trained for adversarial robustness initially improve but then degrade in robust accuracy during training. TWINS aims to mitigate this during fine-tuning. Quick check: Verify that robust overfitting is actually observed in the baseline methods being compared.

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**: Input Data -> Frozen BN Network + Adaptive BN Network -> Combined Output/Decision

**Critical Path**: The dual-network architecture with one frozen BN network and one adaptive BN network represents the core innovation. The frozen BN network preserves pre-training robustness while the adaptive BN network learns task-specific features.

**Design Tradeoffs**: Maintaining two separate networks increases computational overhead during fine-tuning compared to single-network approaches. The tradeoff is between computational cost and the ability to preserve robustness while learning new tasks.

**Failure Signatures**: If frozen BN statistics are poorly aligned with downstream data distributions, the method may fail to transfer robustness effectively. Similarly, if the adaptive BN network overfits to the new task, it may lose the pre-training robustness entirely.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the limitations section raises several important considerations about the method's generalizability and practical implementation.

## Limitations

- The reliance on frozen batch normalization statistics may limit applicability when BN statistics from pre-training are not well-aligned with downstream data distributions
- Experimental evaluation is restricted to image classification tasks, leaving uncertainty about effectiveness for other modalities or complex vision tasks
- The paper does not address potential computational overhead from maintaining two separate networks during fine-tuning

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core observation that standard fine-tuning approaches for adversarially pre-trained models fail to balance pre-training information preservation with downstream task learning
- **Medium confidence**: The effectiveness of the proposed two-network architecture in improving both clean and robust accuracy on the tested datasets
- **Medium confidence**: The claim that breaking the weight norm-gradient norm relationship accelerates escape from suboptimal initialization, as this is supported by empirical results but lacks extensive theoretical justification
