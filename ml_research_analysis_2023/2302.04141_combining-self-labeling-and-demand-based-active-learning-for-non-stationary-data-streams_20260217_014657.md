# Combining self-labeling and demand based active learning for non-stationary data streams

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.04141
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04141
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 21
- Primary result: Combines self-labeling with demand-driven active learning to achieve high accuracy on drifting streams while using fewer labeled samples

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of learning from non-stationary data streams when labeled data is scarce. The proposed method combines self-labeling (SL) with demand-driven active learning (AL) using an online k-NN classifier with a sliding window. High-confidence predictions are self-labeled to update the model, while prediction certainty is monitored and ground truth labels are requested through AL when certainty drops below a threshold. The approach incorporates weighting schemes to emphasize recent samples and ground truth labels, improving performance particularly in challenging drift scenarios with overlapping distributions.

## Method Summary
The method uses an online k-NN classifier operating on a sliding window of recent samples. For each incoming sample, the classifier predicts a label and calculates a certainty score based on the weighted distance to k nearest neighbors. If certainty exceeds a threshold θSL, the sample is updated with the predicted label (self-labeling). If the mean certainty over a recent window drops below θAL, the system requests ground truth labels through active learning. The model incorporates age-based and ground-truth-based weighting schemes to emphasize more recent samples and verified labels, helping it adapt to concept drift while maintaining accuracy.

## Key Results
- The combined SL+AL approach achieves accuracy comparable to fully supervised learning while using significantly fewer labeled samples
- Performance is particularly strong in challenging drift scenarios where distributions overlap, where SL alone fails
- The method demonstrates effectiveness on both artificial data streams with various drift types and a real-world spam dataset with gradual drift

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Self-labeling can approximate intrinsic manifold metrics in non-stationary streams when clusters remain separable over time.
- Mechanism: The k-NN classifier reconstructs the manifold from data points, identifying connected components that correspond to mixture components. When clusters move continuously without merging, the connected components preserve label information, enabling self-labeling to work.
- Core assumption: The underlying distribution forms a manifold where connected components correspond to labels, and drift is gradual enough that clusters don't overlap significantly.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "self-labeling (SL) with demand-driven active learning (AL)"
  - [section] "We can extend this procedure to more general data-time manifolds which do not strictly obey the assumptions"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus mentions self-labeling but not the specific manifold reconstruction mechanism
- Break condition: When clusters overlap or merge in feature space, the connected components no longer correspond to labels, making self-labeling unreliable.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Weighting schemes that emphasize recent samples and ground truth labels improve classification accuracy in drifting streams.
- Mechanism: The decision rule incorporates age-based and ground-truth-based weights, giving more influence to recent samples and those with verified labels. This helps the model adapt to concept drift while maintaining confidence in predictions.
- Core assumption: More recent samples are more relevant for current concept, and ground truth labels are more reliable than self-labeled predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "incorporates weighting schemes to emphasize more recent samples and ground truth labels"
  - [section] "weight sample xi with weight ωi_age, which is 1 if xi is the most recent sample"
  - [corpus] Moderate - corpus discusses weighting but not specifically for non-stationary streams
- Break condition: When the weighting overemphasizes recent samples at the expense of historical context needed for stable patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Demand-based active learning triggers when prediction certainty drops below a threshold, addressing challenging drift scenarios.
- Mechanism: The system monitors the mean certainty over a window of recent predictions. When this drops below a threshold, it requests ground truth labels to update the model, preventing error accumulation in difficult drift cases.
- Core assumption: Prediction certainty correlates with model accuracy, and ground truth labels can correct model errors when certainty is low.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "when prediction certainty drops below a threshold, the model requests ground truth labels through AL"
  - [section] "if 1/τstop ∑t t-τstop c(xt, yt) < θAL, i.e. the mean certainty of a window of the last τstop samples drops under a pre-defined threshold θAL"
  - [corpus] Weak - corpus mentions active learning but not the specific certainty-based triggering mechanism
- Break condition: When the certainty threshold is poorly calibrated, causing either too frequent labeling requests or insufficient adaptation to drift.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Data stream processor -> k-NN classifier -> Self-labeling module -> Certainty estimator -> Active learning trigger -> Weight manager

- Critical path:
  1. Receive sample x_t
  2. Predict label using weighted k-NN
  3. Calculate certainty score
  4. If certainty > θ_SL, update with predicted label
  5. If mean certainty over window < θ_AL, trigger active learning
  6. When labels received, update model with ground truth

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sliding window size vs. memory usage and adaptation speed
  - Certainty threshold vs. false positive rate for active learning
  - Weighting factors vs. stability vs. adaptability
  - k value in k-NN vs. noise sensitivity vs. computational cost

- Failure signatures:
  - Rapidly oscillating predictions suggest window size too small
  - Consistent low certainty indicates poor threshold calibration
  - Performance degradation despite active learning suggests need for stronger weighting

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test on Scenario 1 (no overlap) to verify self-labeling works without active learning
  2. Test on Scenario 3 (crossing at different times) to evaluate age-based weighting
  3. Test on Scenario 4 (crossing at same time) to assess active learning trigger effectiveness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we develop a theoretically rigorous framework for understanding when self-labeling is appropriate in non-stationary data streams with concept drift?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "Further work should explore the combination with unsupervised drift detection which could guide AL in the presence of more abrupt drifts" and discuss the limitations of self-labeling when distributions overlap.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies that self-labeling works well when distributions don't overlap but becomes problematic when they do. However, there's no clear theoretical framework for determining when self-labeling transitions from being reliable to unreliable.
- What evidence would resolve it: A formal characterization of the conditions under which self-labeling produces accurate predictions versus when it fails, including mathematical bounds on drift magnitude and overlap that determine self-labeling reliability.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the optimal strategies for combining self-labeling with active learning in real-world streaming applications?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose a hybrid approach but note "While AL is straightforward in batch learning, online learning adds additional requirements" and discuss challenges in budget control.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates that combining SL and AL improves performance, but the optimal triggering mechanism for when to switch from SL to AL remains unclear, especially in dynamic environments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different triggering strategies (fixed thresholds, adaptive thresholds, drift-based triggers) across diverse real-world datasets, measuring both accuracy and labeling efficiency.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we adapt more sophisticated k-NN-based online learning schemes to scarce label availability?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors state "Besides, adapting popular more sophisticated k-nn-based online learning schemes as [14] to scarce label availability would increase their applicability."
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a basic sliding window k-NN approach, but acknowledges that more sophisticated methods exist. However, these methods typically rely on ground truth information for adaptation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Modified versions of advanced k-NN methods (like self-adjusting memory) that incorporate self-labeling and can adapt to concept drift without requiring ground truth labels, with comparative performance evaluations.

## Limitations
- The method's effectiveness on complex real-world concept drift (abrupt changes, recurring concepts, hidden drift) remains untested beyond the controlled artificial scenarios
- The certainty calculation mechanism and threshold calibration details are not fully specified, potentially limiting reproducibility across different data distributions
- While effective on the tested drift patterns, the approach may struggle with more complex drift behaviors not covered in the artificial scenarios

## Confidence

- High confidence: The combination of self-labeling and active learning provides performance benefits over either approach alone in controlled drift scenarios
- Medium confidence: The weighting schemes meaningfully improve performance, though optimal parameter settings may vary significantly across domains
- Low confidence: The method's effectiveness extends beyond the specific drift patterns tested to arbitrary non-stationary environments

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-domain validation**: Apply the method to datasets with different characteristics (image streams, sensor data) to assess whether the same hyperparameters and certainty thresholds generalize effectively

3. **Ablation study on weighting components**: Systematically remove or modify the age-based and ground-truth-based weighting to quantify their individual contributions and determine if simpler approaches might suffice for certain drift types
