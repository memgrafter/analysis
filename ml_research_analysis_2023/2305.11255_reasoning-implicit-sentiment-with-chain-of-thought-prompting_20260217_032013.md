# Reasoning Implicit Sentiment with Chain-of-Thought Prompting

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.11255
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11255
- Reference count: 21
- Key outcome: THOR+Flan-T5 (11B) achieves over 6% F1 improvement over state-of-the-art on supervised fine-tuning; THOR+GPT3 (175B) yields over 50% F1 improvement on zero-shot setup for implicit sentiment analysis

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenging task of implicit sentiment analysis (ISA), where sentiment must be inferred from texts lacking explicit emotional expressions. The authors propose THOR (Three-hop Reasoning), a Chain-of-Thought framework that mimics human-like reasoning by incrementally inferring the implicit aspect, opinion, and final sentiment polarity. The framework shows substantial performance improvements over state-of-the-art methods, with effectiveness scaling with model size and key mechanisms including self-consistency and reasoning-revising.

## Method Summary
THOR employs a three-step prompting principle for large language models (LLMs): first inferring the fine-grained aspect of the target, then the underlying opinion towards that aspect, and finally the sentiment polarity. The framework uses self-consistency voting to select the most consistent answers at each step. For supervised fine-tuning, a reasoning-revising method is proposed where intermediate reasoning answers are used as inputs to predict final labels, allowing supervision from gold labels to improve the generation of correct reasoning. The approach is evaluated on SemEval14 Laptop and Restaurant datasets split into explicit and implicit sentiment subsets.

## Key Results
- THOR+Flan-T5 (11B) achieves over 6% F1 improvement over state-of-the-art on supervised fine-tuning
- THOR+GPT3 (175B) yields over 50% F1 improvement on zero-shot setup
- Effectiveness of THOR scales with LLM size
- Self-consistency and reasoning-revising mechanisms are crucial for performance
- THOR successfully solves challenging ISA cases where traditional prompting methods fail

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The three-hop reasoning framework enables LLMs to perform implicit sentiment analysis by breaking down the task into progressively harder sub-tasks.
- Mechanism: The framework guides the LLM through three reasoning steps: first identifying the implicit aspect, then inferring the underlying opinion toward that aspect, and finally determining the sentiment polarity based on the inferred opinion.
- Core assumption: LLMs can effectively perform commonsense reasoning about implicit aspects and opinions when given the right prompting structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We design a three-step prompting principle for THOR to step-by-step induce the implicit aspect, opinion, and finally the sentiment polarity."
  - [section] "We design three prompts for three steps of reasoning, each of which respectively infers 1) the fine-grained aspect of the given target, 2) the underlying opinion towards the aspect, and 3) the final polarity."
- Break condition: If the LLM fails to correctly identify the implicit aspect in the first hop, subsequent hops will likely fail as they depend on this initial inference.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Self-consistency mechanism improves the reliability of each reasoning step by aggregating multiple inferences.
- Mechanism: For each of the three reasoning steps, the LLM generates multiple candidate answers, and the most consistent answer (based on voting) is selected as the context for the next step.
- Core assumption: LLMs can generate diverse yet consistent reasoning paths when prompted multiple times for the same question.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we consider a self-consistency mechanism for CoT inspired by Wang et al. (2022b), which is to select the candidate answers (at each step) with high voting consistency of inferred aspect and opinion."
  - [section] "At each step, those answers with high voting consistency of inferred a, o or y are kept."
- Break condition: If the LLM consistently generates contradictory or low-quality candidates across multiple generations, the voting mechanism will fail to identify a reliable answer.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The reasoning-revising mechanism with supervision helps LLMs learn to generate more accurate intermediate reasoning steps.
- Mechanism: During supervised fine-tuning, the model is trained to predict sentiment labels directly from the intermediate reasoning answers rather than going through all three hops, which teaches it to generate better intermediate reasoning.
- Core assumption: Supervised learning can effectively guide LLMs to improve their intermediate reasoning generation when given explicit feedback on final sentiment labels.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For supervised fine-tuning setup, we further propose a reasoning revising method. We use the intermediate reasoning answers as model inputs to predict the final labels, where the supervision from gold labels will teach LLM to generate more correct reasoning."
  - [section] "At each step we construct a prompt by concatenating 1) initial context, 2) this step's reasoning answer text and 3) final question, and feed it into LLM to predict the sentiment label instead of going to the next step reasoning."
- Break condition: If the intermediate reasoning answers are too far from the correct reasoning path, supervision on final labels may not effectively correct the intermediate steps.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prompt templates (three-hop structure) -> LLM backbone (Flan-T5 or GPT-3) -> Self-consistency module (voting mechanism) -> (Optional) Supervised fine-tuning module (reasoning revising) -> Evaluation pipeline (F1 score calculation)

- Critical path: Prompt generation → LLM inference → Self-consistency voting → (Optional) Supervised fine-tuning → Evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Larger LLMs show better performance but increase computational cost
  - Self-consistency adds inference time but improves reliability
  - Supervised fine-tuning requires labeled data but significantly improves performance

- Failure signatures:
  - Low F1 scores on ISA despite high performance on ESA (indicates failure to capture implicit aspects/opinions)
  - Inconsistent answers across multiple LLM generations (indicates self-consistency voting failure)
  - Degradation in performance when scaling down LLM size (indicates reliance on model capacity)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare three-hop reasoning vs direct prompting on a small ISA dataset to verify the basic mechanism
  2. Test self-consistency voting with different numbers of candidate generations to find optimal balance
  3. Evaluate the impact of supervised fine-tuning with varying amounts of labeled data on reasoning quality

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the performance of THOR scale when applied to even larger language models beyond 175B parameters?
  - Basis in paper: The paper states that the effectiveness of THOR scales with LLM size, showing significant improvements with larger models like GPT3 (175B).
  - Why unresolved: The paper only tests up to GPT3 with 175B parameters. Testing with models larger than 175B would provide more insights into the scalability and potential limits of THOR.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Empirical results from testing THOR on models larger than GPT3, such as GPT4 or other future models, would provide evidence on whether the trend of improvement continues or plateaus.

- Question: Can the THOR framework be effectively adapted for sentiment analysis tasks in languages other than English?
  - Basis in paper: The paper focuses on English datasets (SemEval14 Laptop and Restaurant) and does not mention multilingual capabilities.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the adaptability of THOR to other languages, leaving the question of its effectiveness in multilingual contexts open.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with THOR on multilingual datasets or in low-resource languages would demonstrate its adaptability and effectiveness across different linguistic contexts.

- Question: What are the specific components of the reasoning-revising mechanism that contribute most to the performance gains in supervised fine-tuning?
  - Basis in paper: The paper mentions that the reasoning-revising mechanism is crucial for performance in supervised fine-tuning, but does not detail which components are most impactful.
  - Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges the importance of reasoning-revising but does not dissect its components to identify which parts are most beneficial.
  - What evidence would resolve it: A detailed ablation study isolating each component of the reasoning-revising mechanism would identify which parts contribute most significantly to performance improvements.

## Limitations

- The paper lacks publicly available prompt templates and implementation details, significantly hindering reproducibility and verification of claimed performance gains.
- The effectiveness of the self-consistency mechanism is constrained by the LLM's ability to generate diverse yet consistent reasoning paths, which may not generalize across different domains or languages.
- The reasoning-revising method requires labeled data for supervised fine-tuning, limiting its applicability in truly zero-shot scenarios.

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The core mechanism of three-hop reasoning breaking down ISA into aspect-opinion-sentiment inference is well-founded. The empirical results showing performance improvements with larger LLMs are consistent with established scaling laws in the literature.

**Medium Confidence**: The specific prompt templates and their exact formulations are not provided, making it difficult to verify the claimed performance gains. The effectiveness of the self-consistency mechanism in this specific three-hop context is plausible but not directly validated against alternative selection methods.

**Low Confidence**: The reasoning-revising mechanism's impact is demonstrated only in the supervised fine-tuning setup, with unclear generalization to other datasets or domains. The paper doesn't address potential biases introduced by the self-consistency voting mechanism when the LLM consistently generates incorrect reasoning.

## Next Checks

1. **Prompt Template Validation**: Implement and test multiple variations of the three-hop reasoning prompts to determine how sensitive the performance gains are to specific wording and formatting choices.

2. **Self-Consistency Robustness Test**: Evaluate the self-consistency mechanism across different numbers of candidate generations (3, 5, 10) and compare its performance against alternative answer selection strategies like entropy-based selection or direct majority voting without generation diversity.

3. **Cross-Domain Generalization**: Apply the THOR framework to implicit sentiment analysis datasets from different domains (e.g., social media, product reviews beyond restaurants/laptops) to assess whether the three-hop reasoning structure generalizes beyond the tested SemEval datasets.
