# Beyond the Universal Law of Robustness: Sharper Laws for Random Features and Neural Tangent Kernels

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01629
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01629
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Random features models cannot achieve robustness through over-parameterization alone, while NTK models with even activations do meet the universal lower bound when over-parameterized.

## Executive Summary
This paper challenges the universal law of robustness by showing that over-parameterization alone does not guarantee adversarial robustness in random features models, even when the universal condition is satisfied. The key insight is that the activation function's properties, captured through an "interaction matrix," fundamentally determine robustness. For NTK models with even activations, the paper establishes that the universal lower bound is achieved as soon as over-parameterization conditions are met, while for random features with non-even activations like tanh, robustness remains elusive regardless of over-parameterization degree.

## Method Summary
The paper analyzes adversarial robustness through sensitivity measures for two prototypical deep learning models: random features and neural tangent kernels. The RF model is trained via empirical risk minimization on Gaussian-distributed data with random first-layer weights, while the NTK model linearizes around initialization. Both models use the same data distribution and activation functions (tanh and x²) for comparison. The sensitivity analysis introduces an interaction matrix formalism that decouples the kernel's effect from the activation function's influence, allowing precise characterization of how activation symmetry affects robustness.

## Key Results
- Random features models are not robust for any degree of over-parameterization when E[φ'(ρ)]≠0, despite satisfying universal law conditions
- NTK models with even activations achieve the universal lower bound and become robust as soon as p>dN over-parameterization condition is met
- The interaction matrix Frobenius norm scales as k/√N·d for RF with non-even activations, directly limiting robustness
- Sensitivity bounds for NTK scale as log k·√(N·d/p), matching the universal lower bound when N=O(k)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Over-parameterization alone is insufficient for adversarial robustness in random features models, even when the universal law of robustness condition is satisfied.
- Mechanism: The interaction matrix IRF(z), which captures how the activation function's derivative interacts with the test sample, has a Frobenius norm scaling as k/√N·d when E[φ'(ρ)]≠0. This norm directly bounds the sensitivity through the lower bound SRF(z) ≥ ε·∥z∥²·∥A(z)∥F, where A(z) includes IRF(z) and the kernel inverse.
- Core assumption: The activation function's first derivative has non-zero mean under the standard Gaussian, i.e., E[φ'(ρ)]≠0, which ensures the interaction matrix contributes significantly to sensitivity.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We prove that, for random features, the model is not robust for any degree of over-parameterization, even when the necessary condition coming from the universal law of robustness is satisfied."
  - [section] Theorem 4: ∥IRF(z)∥F = Θ(k/√N·d) when E[φ'(ρ)]≠0, directly linking activation function symmetry to robustness.
  - [corpus] Weak: no direct neighbor evidence on interaction matrix role.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: For NTK with even activations, the model achieves robustness as soon as the over-parameterization condition p>dN is met, matching the universal lower bound.
- Mechanism: The sensitivity SNTK(z) is bounded by the product of three terms: ∥∇zΦNTK(z)⊤ΦNTK(X)⊤∥op, ∥K⁻¹NTK∥op, and ∥Y∥₂. The even activation ensures the kernel KNTK is centered, simplifying the analysis and enabling the bound to scale as log k · √(N·d/p), which matches the universal lower bound when N=O(k).
- Core assumption: The activation function is even, which centers the kernel and simplifies the gradient term in the sensitivity expression.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "For even activations, the NTK model meets the universal lower bound, and it is robust as soon as the necessary condition on over-parameterization is fulfilled."
  - [section] Theorem 5: SNTK(z) = O(log k · √(N·d/p)), matching the universal law's lower bound.
  - [corpus] Weak: no direct neighbor evidence on NTK kernel centering effects.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The interaction matrix IRF(z) is the key quantity capturing the impact of the activation function on robustness, decoupling the effect of the kernel from the activation's influence.
- Mechanism: By splitting A(z) = ∇zΦRF(z)⊤Φ⊤RFK⁻¹RF into interaction and kernel components, the analysis isolates how the test sample interacts with the training data through the activation's derivative. The Frobenius norm of IRF(z) scales as E²[φ'(ρ)] · k/√N·d, which directly impacts the sensitivity bound.
- Core assumption: The test sample z is independent from the training set and follows the same data distribution PX.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our analysis decouples the effect of the kernel of the model from an 'interaction matrix', which describes the interaction with the test data and captures the effect of the activation."
  - [section] Theorem 3: ∥A(z)∥F ≥ λ⁻¹max(˜KRF)∥IRF(z)∥F - C·√(N+d)/d, showing the interaction matrix's role.
  - [corpus] Weak: no direct neighbor evidence on interaction matrix concept.

- Design tradeoffs:

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can the robustness analysis be extended to activation functions that are not even, beyond the RF model?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that for even activations, NTK models achieve robustness when over-parameterized, but for non-even activations (like tanh), the sensitivity plateaus at a higher value.
- Why unresolved: The paper conjectures that symmetry in the activation function may be fundamental for robustness but does not prove this for general non-even activations.
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving lower bounds on sensitivity for non-even activation functions in NTK models that match or exceed those of even activations would demonstrate that symmetry is indeed necessary for robustness.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Is the condition N log^3 N = o(d^(3/2)) in Assumption 4 necessary for the RF model's sensitivity bounds?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] This condition is stated as necessary in the RF analysis but the authors suggest it may be possible to relax it.
- Why unresolved: The proof technique relies on this assumption to apply concentration inequalities, but a more refined analysis might remove this requirement.
- What evidence would resolve it: Demonstrating that the sensitivity bounds in Theorem 1 hold without this assumption through alternative proof techniques or tighter concentration results.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the interaction matrix formalism extend to deeper neural networks beyond two-layer models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The interaction matrix is introduced as a key technical contribution for analyzing RF and NTK models, capturing the effect of activation functions on robustness.
- Why unresolved: The current analysis is limited to two-layer networks, and extending this to deeper architectures would require understanding how interactions propagate through multiple layers.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing a generalization of the interaction matrix framework that captures layer-wise interactions and proving robustness bounds for deeper networks using this extension.

## Limitations

- The interaction matrix framework assumes independence between test sample z and training set X, which may not hold in practical settings with data dependencies
- Theoretical bounds rely on specific data distributions (Gaussian) that may not generalize to real-world datasets
- The constants in sensitivity bounds remain unspecified, limiting quantitative predictions

## Confidence

- High confidence in the core mechanism showing RF models cannot achieve robustness through over-parameterization alone (Mechanism 1)
- Medium confidence in the NTK even-activation results (Mechanism 2) due to the simplifying assumptions about kernel centering
- Medium confidence in the interaction matrix decomposition (Mechanism 3) as the theoretical framework appears sound but the empirical validation scope is limited
