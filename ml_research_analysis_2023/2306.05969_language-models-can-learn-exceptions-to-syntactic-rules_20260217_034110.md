# Language Models Can Learn Exceptions to Syntactic Rules

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05969
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05969
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: Language models can predict human judgments of passive sentence acceptability with high correlation (r=0.709)

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether language models can learn exceptions to syntactic rules, focusing on English passivization as a case study. Through a human acceptability judgment experiment, the authors identify that certain verbs (e.g., "last", "cost") are significantly less acceptable in the passive voice compared to others. The study then evaluates GPT-2 models, demonstrating that these models can predict the gradient acceptability of passive sentences, aligning closely with human judgments. The findings suggest that language models can learn exceptions to syntactic rules in a human-like manner, although other factors beyond frequency may also play a role.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a human acceptability judgment experiment where participants rated the acceptability of passive sentences using a 7-point Likert scale. The study tested 45 verbs in passive constructions, including both frequent and infrequent verbs, and verbs from different semantic classes. The authors then evaluated the predictions of GPT-2 models (small, medium, large, XL) on the same set of verbs, comparing the model rankings to human judgments using Spearman's correlation. Additionally, they analyzed the relative frequency of verbs in active vs. passive voice using corpus data from the Corpus of Contemporary American English (COCA) to test the entrenchment hypothesis.

## Key Results
- Language models can predict human acceptability judgments of passive sentences with high correlation (r=0.709 for the most human-realistic model)
- Certain verbs (e.g., "last", "cost") are significantly less acceptable in the passive voice than others, as determined by human judgments
- The entrenchment hypothesis receives weak support, as frequency alone cannot fully account for the magnitude of unacceptability for certain verbs

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The exact mechanisms underlying language models' ability to learn exceptions to syntactic rules remain unclear. The authors suggest that models may capture some combination of frequency effects, semantic factors, and other linguistic constraints that contribute to the gradient acceptability of passive constructions. However, further research is needed to identify the specific mechanisms and how they relate to human cognitive processes.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
Major uncertainties remain regarding the mechanisms underlying the observed exceptions to passive voice acceptability. While the study demonstrates that language models can predict human judgments of passive sentence acceptability with high correlation (r=0.709), the exact factors contributing to unacceptability beyond frequency effects are not identified. The entrenchment hypothesis receives only weak support, suggesting that frequency alone cannot fully explain the observed patterns. The selection of verbs for the human judgment study, while justified, may still influence the results and limit generalizability. Additionally, the computational models tested (GPT-2 variants) may not fully capture the complexity of human syntactic processing, raising questions about the extent to which these models truly "learn" exceptions in a human-like manner.
