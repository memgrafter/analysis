# Sticker820K: Empowering Interactive Retrieval with Stickers

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.06870
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06870
- Reference count: 38
- StickerCLIP achieves 66.0% absolute gain in mean recall for text-to-image retrieval on test set

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Sticker820K, a large-scale Chinese sticker dataset with 820k image-text pairs containing rich annotations including descriptions, optical characters, emotional labels, and style classifications. The authors propose StickerCLIP, a benchmark model that improves upon CLIP by better aligning sticker and text features, achieving 66.0% absolute gain in mean recall for text-to-image retrieval on the test set. They also present StickerLLM, which extends frozen large language models to perform sticker retrieval through prompt tuning by introducing special tokens, demonstrating the feasibility of expanding LLM capabilities without affecting upstream tasks.

## Method Summary
The authors develop two models: StickerCLIP fine-tunes CLIP on the Sticker820K dataset to improve sticker-text alignment, while StickerLLM extends frozen LLMs with prompt tuning by adding special tokens for retrieval functionality. The Sticker820K dataset contains 820k Chinese sticker image-text pairs with annotations including descriptions, OCR text, emotional labels (30 categories), and style classifications (5 categories). StickerCLIP uses contrastive learning to align visual and textual features, while StickerLLM aligns LLM retrieval embeddings with visual embeddings through a unified embedding space.

## Key Results
- StickerCLIP achieves 66.0% absolute gain in mean recall for text-to-image retrieval compared to baseline methods
- StickerLLM achieves 96% accuracy in tool selection on BELLE dataset, demonstrating successful integration of retrieval functionality
- StickerLLM shows significantly better performance on image-to-image retrieval (42.1% Recall@1) compared to text-to-image retrieval (27.1% Recall@1)

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Sticker820K dataset: 820k image-text pairs with rich annotations (descriptions, emotions, styles, OCR)
  - StickerCLIP: Fine-tuned CLIP model for sticker-text alignment
  - StickerLLM: Extended LLM with special tokens for sticker retrieval
  - Visual encoder: Extracted from fine-tuned StickerCLIP for feature extraction
  - Special tokens: ⟨ret⟩, ⟨/ret⟩, ⟨img⟩, ⟨/img⟩, ⟨pret⟩ for retrieval functionality

- Critical path:
  1. StickerCLIP training on Sticker820K dataset
  2. Visual encoder extraction from trained StickerCLIP
  3. StickerLLM training with special tokens and prompt tuning
  4. Inference pipeline combining LLM and visual encoder

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Fine-tuning vs. zero-shot: Fine-tuning provides better performance but requires labeled data
  - Prompt tuning vs. full fine-tuning: Prompt tuning preserves base LLM capabilities but may limit performance gains
  - Unified vs. separate embedding spaces: Unified space simplifies architecture but may introduce alignment challenges

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor retrieval performance: Indicates misalignment between visual and textual features
  - LLM task degradation: Suggests interference from prompt tuning process
  - Slow inference: May indicate inefficient embedding space or model architecture

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare StickerCLIP performance against zero-shot CLIP on Sticker820K test set
  2. Evaluate StickerLLM's ability to correctly invoke retrieval tool in mixed instruction scenarios
  3. Measure retrieval accuracy of StickerLLM on image-to-image task vs. text-to-image task

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of StickerCLIP scale with different visual encoder architectures (e.g., RN50, ViT-B, larger variants) when applied to sticker datasets?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares RN50 and ViT-B architectures, showing ViT-B achieves better performance, but doesn't explore larger models
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests two model sizes, leaving open whether further scaling would provide additional gains for sticker understanding
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing StickerCLIP with larger visual encoder variants (e.g., ViT-L, CLIP's larger models) on the Sticker820K dataset

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal ratio and method for incorporating OCR text versus manually annotated descriptions in sticker understanding models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses both OCR text and manual descriptions but doesn't explore different weighting strategies or methods for combining them
- Why unresolved: The paper concatenates all textual information but doesn't investigate whether different types of text should be treated differently or weighted differently
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies comparing models trained with different combinations of textual annotations (OCR-only, manual-only, weighted combinations)

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does StickerLLM's performance on sticker retrieval tasks compare to traditional fine-tuning approaches when computational resources are limited?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper claims StickerLLM is more parameter-efficient but doesn't directly compare retrieval performance against fully fine-tuned models under resource constraints
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows StickerLLM uses fewer parameters, it doesn't benchmark this efficiency against traditional fine-tuning methods with similar computational budgets
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparison of StickerLLM versus fully fine-tuned models with identical computational budgets, measuring both retrieval performance and training/inference efficiency

### Open Question 4
- Question: How well does the emotional classification system (30 categories) generalize to stickers from different cultural contexts or languages beyond Chinese?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses Chinese-specific emotional categories and cultural references (e.g., "Rena Belle," "Crayon Shin-chan") but doesn't test cross-cultural generalization
- Why unresolved: The dataset is specifically Chinese-focused, and the emotional taxonomy may be culturally specific, leaving open whether it transfers to other cultural contexts
- What evidence would resolve it: Evaluation of StickerCLIP/StickerLLM trained on Chinese stickers performing on sticker datasets from other cultures/languages, with cross-cultural emotion annotation consistency studies

## Limitations
- Limited evidence base: Key claims rely on standard techniques but lack direct empirical validation through corpus citations
- Cultural specificity: Dataset focuses exclusively on Chinese stickers, limiting generalizability to other languages/cultures
- Missing baseline comparisons: Absence of comparison studies with other domain adaptation methods or detailed ablation studies
