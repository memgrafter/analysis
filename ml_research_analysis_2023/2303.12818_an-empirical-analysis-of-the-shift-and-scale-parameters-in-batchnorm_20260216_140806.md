# An Empirical Analysis of the Shift and Scale Parameters in BatchNorm

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.12818
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12818
- Reference count: 30
- Key outcome: For ResNet architectures using basic residual blocks, BatchNorm-minus performs as well as standard BatchNorm, indicating normalization is the primary contributor. For bottleneck residual blocks, BatchNorm-minus performs poorly, suggesting re-parameterization (shift and scale) is critical for these architectures.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates whether BatchNorm's effectiveness comes primarily from its normalization step or from the affine re-parameterization (shift and scale parameters). Through systematic experiments on CIFAR10 using four ResNet architectures, the authors find that for basic residual blocks, normalization alone performs comparably to full BatchNorm, while for bottleneck residual blocks, the affine parameters become essential. This suggests BatchNorm's primary benefit for basic blocks is normalization, while bottleneck architectures require the full re-parameterization capability.

## Method Summary
The authors implement three custom normalization variants: AffineLayer (only affine transformation), BatchNorm-minus (only normalization), and no normalization. These are integrated into ResNet18, 34, 50, and 101 architectures and trained on CIFAR10 for 15 epochs using Adam optimizer with learning rate 0.001. Batch sizes of 20 and 50 are tested, with each configuration run 4 times. The primary metric is validation accuracy comparison across normalization schemes and ResNet architectures.

## Key Results
- BatchNorm-minus (normalization only) performs comparably to standard BatchNorm for ResNet18 and 34 with basic residual blocks
- BatchNorm-minus performs significantly worse than standard BatchNorm for ResNet50 and 101 with bottleneck residual blocks
- AffineLayer (re-parameterization only) performs poorly across all architectures, suggesting normalization is generally necessary

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: BatchNorm's primary benefit comes from its normalization step rather than the affine re-parameterization
- Mechanism: The normalization step standardizes inputs to each layer by computing batch mean and variance, then normalizing to zero mean and unit variance. This reduces internal covariate shift and creates more stable training conditions.
- Core assumption: The normalization process itself is the critical factor for performance improvement, not the additional trainable parameters.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we empirically examine the relative contribution to the success of BatchNorm of the normalization step, as compared to the re-parameterization via shifting and scaling"
  - [section]: "we provide empirical evidence that the success of BatchNorm may derive primarily from improved weight initialization"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct evidence in corpus for this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If BatchNorm-minus (normalization without re-parameterization) performs worse than AffineLayer (re-parameterization without normalization), this mechanism would be invalid.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The affine re-parameterization step is critical for architectures using bottleneck residual blocks
- Mechanism: The shift (β) and scale (γ) parameters provide two additional degrees of freedom per neuron, allowing the model to recover information lost during dimensionality reduction in bottleneck blocks.
- Core assumption: The bottleneck architecture's dimensionality reduction creates information loss that requires the additional expressive power of the affine parameters to recover.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "For architectures using bottleneck residual blocks (ResNet50, 101), BatchNorm-minus performs poorly, suggesting that the re-parameterization step (shift and scale parameters) is critical for these architectures"
  - [section]: "we conjecture that the 1 × 1 convolution of the ResNet50 bottleneck architecture is stripping the model of information, while the additional shift and scale parameters enable the model to recover some of that information"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct evidence in corpus for this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If BatchNorm-minus performs as well as BatchNorm on bottleneck architectures, this mechanism would be invalid.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Normalization step improves weight initialization consistency across epochs
- Mechanism: BatchNorm normalizes layer inputs at the start of each epoch, creating more consistent weight initialization conditions and reducing variance in training dynamics.
- Core assumption: Weight initialization variance across epochs contributes to training instability, and normalization mitigates this issue.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Another way in which BatchNorm could be improving deep neural nets is by making the weight initializations more consistent at the start of each epoch"
  - [section]: "It would also be interesting to consider classifiers where BatchNorm is commonly used that do not rely on residual blocks, and perform similar experiments as presented in this paper"
  - [corpus]: Weak - no direct evidence in corpus for this specific mechanism
- Break condition: If removing normalization doesn't affect weight initialization consistency, this mechanism would be invalid.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Why does BatchNorm-minus perform poorly on bottleneck block architectures (ResNet50, ResNet101) but comparably to BatchNorm on basic block architectures (ResNet18, ResNet34)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper's experimental results show this clear distinction between the two types of architectures.
- Why unresolved: The authors hypothesize that the 1×1 convolution in bottleneck blocks may strip information that the shift and scale parameters help recover, but this is not definitively proven.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed analysis of information flow through bottleneck blocks with and without shift/scale parameters, or ablation studies isolating the effects of dimensionality reduction and re-projection in bottleneck blocks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the primary mechanism by which BatchNorm improves training - normalization of weights at the start of each epoch, or the smoothing of the loss surface/gradient landscape?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors' analysis of weight and gradient distributions shows that AffineLayer (no normalization) has similar gradient behavior to BatchNorm, but performs worse, suggesting normalization at the start of epochs is important.
- Why unresolved: While the analysis suggests normalization is important, the exact mechanism and relative importance of different aspects of BatchNorm are not definitively established.
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments isolating the effects of initial weight normalization versus other aspects of BatchNorm on training dynamics and final performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can we develop more efficient normalization schemes that maintain the benefits of BatchNorm for basic block architectures while being computationally simpler?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper concludes that BatchNorm-minus performs comparably to BatchNorm on basic block architectures, suggesting a simpler normalization could suffice.
- Why unresolved: The authors only tested one simpler variant (BatchNorm-minus) and did not explore the full space of possible normalization schemes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of various normalization schemes (including but not limited to BatchNorm-minus) on basic block architectures to identify the most efficient approach that maintains performance.

## Limitations
- Focus on CIFAR10 with specific ResNet architectures may not generalize to other datasets or network architectures
- Analysis restricted to batch sizes of 20 and 50, potentially missing important effects at other batch sizes
- Proposed mechanisms remain speculative without direct experimental validation

## Confidence
- High confidence in the empirical finding that BatchNorm-minus performs comparably to BatchNorm for basic block architectures but significantly worse for bottleneck architectures
- Medium confidence in the conclusion that normalization is the primary contributor to BatchNorm's effectiveness for basic blocks
- Low confidence in the proposed mechanisms explaining why bottleneck architectures require affine parameters

## Next Checks
