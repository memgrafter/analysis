# Robust Linear Regression: Gradient-descent, Early-stopping, and Beyond

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.13486
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13486
- Reference count: 40
- This paper studies the robustness of gradient-descent (GD) methods for linear regression under adversarial attacks, showing early-stopped GD is nearly optimal for Euclidean-norm attacks but can be sub-optimal for Mahalanobis attacks, and proposes GD+ and a two-stage estimator to achieve near-optimal adversarial risk under general attack norms.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the robustness of gradient descent methods for linear regression under adversarial attacks. The authors show that early-stopped gradient descent achieves near-optimal adversarial risk for Euclidean-norm attacks, but can be arbitrarily sub-optimal for general Mahalanobis attacks. To address this limitation, they propose a modified gradient descent scheme (GD+) with feature-dependent learning rates that achieves near-optimal adversarial risk for various attack norms under certain conditions. Additionally, they design a simple two-stage estimator whose adversarial risk is optimal up to within a constant factor of 1.1124 for any norm, and provide statistical guarantees for its performance. The paper provides theoretical insights into the robustness of GD and offers practical solutions for achieving robustness under general adversarial attacks.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes three main methods for achieving robustness in linear regression under adversarial attacks:
1. Early-stopped gradient descent (GD) for Euclidean-norm attacks
2. Modified gradient descent (GD+) with feature-dependent learning rates for general Mahalanobis-norm attacks
3. A two-stage estimator that combines consistent estimation of model parameters and feature covariance with a convex optimization problem to minimize adversarial risk

The methods are evaluated on their ability to minimize adversarial risk under various attack norms, and their performance is compared to known optimal values in the population regime.

## Key Results
- Early-stopped gradient descent achieves near-optimal adversarial risk for Euclidean-norm attacks.
- Modified gradient descent (GD+) with feature-dependent learning rates achieves near-optimal adversarial risk for general Mahalanobis-norm attacks under certain conditions.
- A two-stage estimator achieves optimal adversarial risk up to a constant factor of 1.1124 for any norm attack.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Early-stopped gradient descent achieves near-optimal adversarial risk for Euclidean-norm attacks.
- Mechanism: Gradient descent dynamics naturally shrink the model parameters during training. Early stopping at the right time captures a model with optimal balance between bias (closeness to true parameters) and robustness (resistance to small perturbations).
- Core assumption: The feature covariance matrix Σ is isotropic or close to it, so that the dynamics of gradient descent naturally lead to the optimal trade-off between standard and adversarial risk.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "early-stopped GD is optimally robust (up to an absolute constant) against Euclidean-norm adversarial attacks."
  - [section]: "In the isotropic case, i.e. when Σ = Id, early-stopped GD even achieves the exact optimal adversarial risk."
  - [corpus]: Weak. No direct citations in corpus. Mechanism inferred from theory.
- Break condition: If the feature covariance matrix Σ is highly anisotropic, the natural shrinkage induced by gradient descent may not lead to the optimal trade-off, and early stopping may not be sufficient.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Modified gradient descent (GD+) with feature-dependent learning rates achieves near-optimal adversarial risk for general Mahalanobis-norm attacks.
- Mechanism: By applying a transformation to the data based on the attack norm, GD+ forces the dynamics to be non-uniform across different features. This allows the algorithm to adapt to the geometry of the attack and find a model with optimal robustness.
- Core assumption: The attack norm is known or can be estimated, so that the data transformation can be chosen appropriately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we propose to apply instead a GD scheme on a transformation of the data adapted to the attack. This data transformation amounts to apply feature-depending learning rates and we show that this modified GD is able to handle any Mahalanobis attack, as well as more general attacks under some conditions."
  - [section]: "This transformation amounts to apply feature-depending learning rates and we show that this modified GD is able to handle any Mahalanobis attack, as well as more general attacks under some conditions."
  - [corpus]: Weak. No direct citations in corpus. Mechanism inferred from theory.
- Break condition: If the attack norm is unknown or cannot be estimated accurately, the data transformation cannot be chosen appropriately, and GD+ may not achieve optimal robustness.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: A two-stage estimator achieves optimal adversarial risk up to a constant factor for any norm attack.
- Mechanism: The first stage estimates the true parameters and the feature covariance matrix from the data. The second stage uses these estimates to solve a convex optimization problem that directly minimizes the adversarial risk proxy. This approach combines the strengths of both estimation and optimization to achieve near-optimal robustness.
- Core assumption: The feature covariance matrix Σ and the true parameters w0 can be estimated consistently from the data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we design a simple and tractable estimator whose adversarial risk is optimal up to within a multiplicative constant of 1.1124 in the population regime, and works for any norm."
  - [section]: "Stage 1 of Algorithm 1 can be implemented using off-the-shelf estimators which adapt to the structural assumptions on Σ and w0 and Σ (sparsity, etc.)."
  - [corpus]: Weak. No direct citations in corpus. Mechanism inferred from theory.
- Break condition: If the feature covariance matrix Σ or the true parameters w0 cannot be estimated consistently, the two-stage estimator may not achieve optimal robustness.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Can gradient descent find robust models under Mahalanobis attacks in all cases, or are there inherent limitations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Proposition 4.4 shows that gradient descent can be arbitrarily sub-optimal under Mahalanobis attacks, while Proposition 5.1 shows that a modified version (GD+) can achieve near-optimality under certain conditions.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies cases where vanilla GD fails, but does not provide a complete characterization of when GD+ is optimal or when it might also fail.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive analysis of GD+ under various Mahalanobis attack scenarios, including cases where the attack matrix B is not aligned with the feature covariance matrix Σ.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of feature correlation on the optimality of gradient descent for adversarial robustness?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Theorem 5.2 shows that GD+ achieves near-optimality under ℓp-norm attacks when the features are uncorrelated (Σ is diagonal).
- Why unresolved: The paper only considers the case of uncorrelated features, but in practice, features are often correlated. It is unclear how feature correlation affects the robustness of GD+ and the conditions for optimality.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis of GD+ under ℓp-norm attacks with correlated features, and empirical studies on real-world datasets with varying levels of feature correlation.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of norm for measuring adversarial attacks impact the effectiveness of gradient descent?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper studies the robustness of GD under various attack norms, including Euclidean, Mahalanobis, and general ℓp-norms.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides insights into the behavior of GD under different attack norms, it does not provide a complete understanding of how the choice of norm affects the optimality of GD and the conditions for robustness.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comprehensive study of GD under a wide range of attack norms, including non-standard norms, and a theoretical analysis of the relationship between the attack norm and the optimality of GD.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What are the statistical properties of the proposed two-stage estimator in finite-sample regimes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Theorem 6.1 provides consistency guarantees for the two-stage estimator, but the statistical analysis is limited to the population regime.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of the statistical properties of the estimator in finite-sample regimes, such as its convergence rate and the impact of estimation errors on the adversarial risk.
- What evidence would resolve it: A rigorous statistical analysis of the two-stage estimator in finite-sample regimes, including bounds on the estimation error and the convergence rate, and empirical studies on real-world datasets.

### Open Question 5
- Question: Can the proposed two-stage estimator be extended to handle more complex regression models, such as those with non-linearities or interactions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on linear regression models, but does not explore the applicability of the proposed estimator to more complex models.
- Why unresolved: The two-stage estimator relies on the linearity of the regression model, and it is unclear how it can be extended to handle non-linearities or interactions.
- What evidence would resolve it: A theoretical analysis of the extension of the two-stage estimator to non-linear regression models, and empirical studies on real-world datasets with non-linear relationships.

## Limitations
- The theoretical guarantees assume access to infinite data, and finite-sample effects are not fully characterized.
- The applicability of results to non-isotropic feature covariances is unclear.
- The computational complexity of the two-stage estimator in high dimensions is not well-understood.
