# Tensor Decomposition for Model Reduction in Neural Networks: A Review

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.13539
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13539
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Tensor decompositions achieve up to 47375× model size reduction with minimal accuracy loss across CNNs, RNNs, and Transformers

## Executive Summary
This paper reviews tensor decomposition methods for compressing neural network parameters, focusing on six techniques: truncated SVD, tensor train, canonical polyadic, Tucker, tensor ring, block-term, and hierarchical Tucker. The methods replace over-parameterized weight tensors with low-rank approximations, achieving significant compression ratios while maintaining accuracy. The paper demonstrates applications across CNNs, RNNs, and Transformers, showing that tensor decompositions can reduce model size by thousands of times with minimal accuracy degradation, and in some cases even improve accuracy. These methods are particularly suitable for edge device deployment due to reduced computational cost and energy consumption.

## Method Summary
The paper reviews six tensor decomposition methods applied to neural networks: truncated SVD, tensor train (TT), canonical polyadic (CP), Tucker, tensor ring (TR), block-term decomposition (BTD), and hierarchical Tucker decomposition (HTD). These methods decompose high-dimensional weight tensors into combinations of smaller rank-one or low-rank tensors. The approach involves replacing standard network layers with their tensor-decomposed counterparts, either through post-hoc compression of pre-trained models or end-to-end training of decomposed architectures. Implementation requires reshaping inputs/outputs, decomposing weight tensors into cores, and adjusting backpropagation to handle the decomposed representation.

## Key Results
- Model size reductions up to 47375× for RNNs with minimal accuracy loss
- Some compressed models achieve higher accuracy than original versions
- Significant reductions in runtime and energy consumption for edge deployment
- Effective compression across CNNs, RNNs, and Transformer architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim**: Tensor decomposition replaces over-parameterized weight tensors with low-rank approximations, achieving large compression ratios with minimal accuracy loss.
- **Mechanism**: The high redundancy in neural network weight tensors allows them to be accurately approximated by combinations of smaller rank-one or low-rank tensors (e.g., via SVD, CP, Tucker, TT, TR, BTD, HTD).
- **Core assumption**: The weight tensor's singular values or eigenvalues decay rapidly, indicating low intrinsic rank.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "The accuracy of some compressed models can be higher than the original versions."
  - [section]: "Most state-of-the-art neural networks are over-parameterized and require a high computational cost. One straightforward solution is to replace the layers of the networks with their low-rank tensor approximations."
- **Break condition**: If weight tensor has slowly decaying singular values (high intrinsic rank), low-rank approximation yields poor reconstruction and significant accuracy loss.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim**: Replacing dense matrix multiplications with sequences of smaller tensor operations reduces both memory footprint and FLOPs.
- **Mechanism**: For example, TT-decomposition reshapes a weight matrix into a higher-order tensor and factorizes it into small TT-cores; the forward pass becomes a sequence of small matrix multiplications instead of one large one.
- **Core assumption**: The computational savings from small multiplications outweigh the overhead of reshaping and core management.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "Evaluations indicate that tensor decompositions can achieve significant reductions in model size, run-time and energy consumption."
  - [section]: "The decomposed layers are replaced by a sequence of new layers with significantly fewer parameters."
- **Break condition**: If reshaping or core multiplication overhead dominates, or if hardware lacks efficient small-matrix kernels, the theoretical speedup may not materialize.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim**: End-to-end training of tensor-decomposed models can yield better generalization than post-hoc compression of pre-trained models.
- **Mechanism**: The decomposition is applied during initial training; gradients flow through the decomposed representation, allowing the network to adapt to the new parameterization rather than forcing it to fit a fixed low-rank structure.
- **Core assumption**: The decomposed architecture is expressive enough to capture the task during training; fine-tuning a pre-trained model may not fully recover accuracy.
- **Evidence anchors**:
  - [abstract]: "All these models are trained end-to-end using backpropagation instead of being obtained by applying tensor decomposition methods to the pre-trained standard models. It is possible for them to achieve higher accuracy than the standard models with fewer parameters."
  - [section]: "The entire network can be trained using a standard backpropagation process."
- **Break condition**: If the decomposition rank is too low or the architecture cannot express the task, training may converge to poor solutions regardless of end-to-end or fine-tune strategy.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**:
  Input tensor → reshape → decomposed weight tensor → sequence of small tensor operations → output tensor → reshape → next layer

- **Critical path**:
  - Forward pass: reshape input → multiply by TT/CP/Tucker cores → sum/concatenate → reshape output
  - Backward pass: propagate gradient through reshaped tensors → compute gradients for each core → update core parameters

- **Design tradeoffs**:
  - Rank selection: Higher rank → better accuracy, lower compression; lower rank → higher compression, risk of accuracy drop
  - Decomposition format: TT/TR for efficient sequential operations; CP for simple rank-one sums; Tucker for more flexible core shapes
  - End-to-end training vs post-hoc fine-tuning: Trade-off between accuracy and convenience

- **Failure signatures**:
  - Accuracy collapse: Rank too low, wrong format for task
  - Training instability: Core dimensions mismatched, reshaping errors
  - No speedup: Reshaping overhead dominates, small kernels not hardware-optimized

- **First 3 experiments**:
  1. Replace one fully connected layer with TT-decomposed variant; verify forward/backward pass correctness and measure compression ratio
  2. Apply CP-decomposition to a convolutional kernel in a small CNN; check accuracy retention vs rank
  3. End-to-end train a tensor-decomposed RNN on a small sequence task; compare convergence and final accuracy to baseline

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the optimal rank selections for tensor decomposition methods to balance model compression ratio and accuracy loss across different neural network architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that "ranks play an important role in CP decomposition" and that "no polynomial time algorithm for determining the rank of a tensor exists." It also notes that "model compression ratio is dependent on the rank approximation and the performance degradation that can be tolerated."
- Why unresolved: Current approaches rely on trial and error or heuristic methods like binary search to find suitable ranks, which is computationally expensive and architecture-dependent.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic study comparing different rank selection strategies across multiple network architectures and datasets, potentially leveraging meta-learning or analytical bounds on rank vs. accuracy trade-offs.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can tensor decomposition methods be optimally integrated with other compression techniques (pruning, quantization) to achieve maximum efficiency without compromising accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper reviews tensor decomposition methods in isolation but doesn't explore their synergistic effects with other compression methods. The discussion section mentions that "Future work needs to be directed toward comparing the performance of various decomposition methods using common datasets" but doesn't explicitly address combining with other techniques.
- Why unresolved: Most existing research focuses on single compression methods rather than their combinations, and the optimal ordering and interaction between different techniques remains unclear.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating the combined effects of tensor decomposition with pruning and quantization across multiple architectures, including analysis of cumulative vs. interactive effects.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the optimal hardware-aware tensor decomposition strategies that account for specific hardware constraints and capabilities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "Hardware-aware Tucker Decomposition was proposed to efficiently generate highly accurate and compact CNN models on GPUs" and references hardware-aware designs, but doesn't provide detailed analysis of hardware-specific optimizations.
- Why unresolved: Current tensor decomposition methods are primarily algorithm-focused without deep consideration of hardware characteristics like memory hierarchy, parallelism, and energy efficiency.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of tensor decomposition methods across different hardware platforms (GPUs, TPUs, edge devices) with metrics including energy efficiency, inference latency, and memory bandwidth utilization.

## Limitations
- Lacks direct empirical comparisons between different tensor decomposition methods on identical tasks
- No hardware benchmarks or energy consumption measurements to validate efficiency claims
- Rank selection methodology relies on trial-and-error without systematic guidelines
