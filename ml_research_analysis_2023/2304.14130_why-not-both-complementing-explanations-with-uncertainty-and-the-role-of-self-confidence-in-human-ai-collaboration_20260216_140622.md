# Why not both? Complementing explanations with uncertainty, and the role of self-confidence in Human-AI collaboration

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.14130
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14130
- Reference count: 26
- Primary result: Combining model predictions with confidence improves performance, but explanations significantly enhance model understanding; the interaction between human and model confidence significantly influences accuracy, reliance, and trust.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates how uncertainty estimates and explanations affect human-AI collaboration in high-stakes tasks. An empirical study with 112 participants performing a salary prediction task found that combining model predictions with confidence improves performance, but explanations significantly enhance model understanding. The interaction between human and model confidence significantly influences accuracy, reliance, and trust. Uncertainty estimates of varying scope (local vs global) affect user behavior, with global information improving decision-making. The results suggest that while uncertainty estimates are sufficient for performance gains, explanations offer complementary benefits by enhancing understanding and trust.

## Method Summary
The study used the Adult dataset (1994 US Census data) filtered to 8 relevant features, with a salary threshold adjusted for inflation to $100,000. A gradient boosting decision tree model was trained on 80% of the data, achieving 82% accuracy. The empirical study involved 112 Amazon Mechanical Turk participants who completed a salary prediction task with varying human/model confidence combinations. Participants were randomly assigned to conditions receiving different types of model assistance (prediction, confidence, explanations) and their accuracy, reliance, understanding, and trust were measured.

## Key Results
- Combining model predictions with confidence improves human performance more than predictions alone
- Explanations significantly enhance model understanding beyond what uncertainty estimates provide
- The interaction between human and model confidence significantly influences accuracy, reliance, understanding, and trust

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Combining model predictions with confidence improves human performance more than predictions alone.
- **Mechanism:** Uncertainty estimates help humans calibrate their reliance on the model, especially when they are uncertain themselves but the model is highly confident.
- **Core assumption:** Users can interpret numerical confidence scores and use them to adjust their final decisions.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "combining model predictions with confidence improves performance"
  - [section] "participants' reliance was 9.8% higher when the model was confident, compared to when it was not"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related work focuses on model self-confidence rather than human calibration.
- **Break condition:** If users lack numeracy skills to interpret confidence scores, the mechanism fails.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** Explanations significantly enhance model understanding beyond what uncertainty estimates provide.
- **Mechanism:** Explanations reveal the decision-making process, allowing users to infer feature importance and simulate model behavior, which uncertainty estimates alone cannot provide.
- **Core assumption:** Users can extract meaningful insights from feature importance explanations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "explanations significantly enhance model understanding"
  - [section] "64.3% of the participants in the Explanations condition answered this question correctly" for local feature importance
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; related work discusses explanations but not their unique role in understanding beyond uncertainty.
- **Break condition:** If explanations are too complex or poorly communicated, users may not gain the intended understanding.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** The interaction between human and model confidence significantly influences accuracy, reliance, understanding, and trust.
- **Mechanism:** When humans are uncertain and the model is confident, users are more likely to rely on the model, improving joint accuracy. Conversely, when both are confident, over-reliance or conflict may occur.
- **Core assumption:** Users adjust their reliance based on their own confidence relative to the model's confidence.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "The interaction between human and model confidence significantly influences accuracy, reliance, and trust"
  - [section] "participants' accuracy showed a significant improvement when they were themselves uncertain, but the model showed high confidence"
  - [corpus] Moderate evidence; related work discusses confidence calibration but not the specific interaction studied here.
- **Break condition:** If users ignore model confidence or if the model's confidence is poorly calibrated, the mechanism fails.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Confidence calibration
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding how users interpret and use model confidence scores is crucial for designing effective human-AI collaboration.
  - **Quick check question:** Can users correctly interpret a 90% confidence score as "high confidence" and adjust their reliance accordingly?

- **Concept:** Feature importance explanations
  - **Why needed here:** Explanations help users understand why a model made a particular prediction, which is essential for trust and model acceptance.
  - **Quick check question:** Can users identify the most influential feature for a given prediction from a SHAP explanation plot?

- **Concept:** Simpson's paradox
  - **Why needed here:** Understanding how confounding variables (like human confidence) can distort apparent relationships is crucial for correctly interpreting experimental results.
  - **Quick check question:** Can you explain why a trend appears in different groups of data but disappears or reverses when these groups are combined?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - Data collection: Amazon Mechanical Turk participants
  - Task design: Salary prediction with varying difficulty
  - Model assistance: Prediction, local confidence, combined confidence, explanations
  - Measurements: Accuracy, reliance, understanding, trust

- **Critical path:**
  1. Recruit participants and assign to conditions
  2. Present task instances with varying human/model confidence combinations
  3. Collect initial predictions and confidence
  4. Provide model assistance based on condition
  5. Collect final predictions and subjective measures
  6. Administer objective understanding test

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - Using feature importance explanations vs. other explanation types (simplicity vs. depth)
  - Local vs. global uncertainty measures (specificity vs. generalizability)
  - Real-time interaction vs. static presentation (engagement vs. control)

- **Failure signatures:**
  - No improvement in accuracy when adding confidence information
  - Participants ignore model assistance
  - Explanations lead to confusion rather than understanding
  - Simpson's paradox obscures true relationships

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Test if confidence information improves accuracy compared to predictions alone
  2. Compare different explanation types (feature importance, counterfactuals, rules)
  3. Vary the presentation format of uncertainty (numerical vs. visual vs. verbal)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- **Question:** Does providing more granular global uncertainty information (e.g., cluster-wise recall) improve participants' ability to calibrate their reliance on model predictions compared to overall class recall?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] from Section 5.2 discussion on exploring more refined global uncertainty information
- **Why unresolved:** The paper only tested overall class recall as a global uncertainty measure, leaving the potential benefits of more granular uncertainty information unexplored
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A study comparing participants' reliance and accuracy when provided with cluster-wise recall versus overall class recall for the same prediction task

### Open Question 2
- **Question:** How does the effect of model explanations on long-term model acceptance and retention differ between users with varying levels of prior AI experience or expertise?
- **Basis in paper:** [inferred] from Section 5.2 discussion on longitudinal studies and the need to account for participants' familiarity with AI
- **Why unresolved:** The study used a cross-sectional design with participants recruited from Mechanical Turk, without accounting for their prior AI experience or conducting follow-up assessments
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A longitudinal study with repeated assessments of model acceptance and retention over time, stratified by participants' prior AI experience or expertise levels

### Open Question 3
- **Question:** Does communicating information about the robustness or stability of explanations reduce over-reliance on the model, particularly in high-confidence scenarios where both parties agree?
- **Basis in paper:** [explicit] from Section 5.3 discussion on the potential for over-reliance on explanations and the idea of communicating explanation robustness
- **Why unresolved:** The study did not test the effect of communicating explanation robustness, only observed a decline in accuracy when both parties had high confidence in the Explanations condition
- **What evidence would resolve it:** A study comparing participants' reliance and accuracy in high-confidence scenarios with and without information about explanation robustness or stability

## Limitations

- Generalizability is limited by using a single task domain (salary prediction) and specific explanation type (feature importance)
- External validity may be compromised due to Mechanical Turk participants not representing professional contexts
- The study did not test the effect of communicating explanation robustness or stability

## Confidence

- **High Confidence:** The finding that combining predictions with confidence estimates improves human performance is well-supported by the data and aligns with established literature on calibration.
- **Medium Confidence:** The claim that explanations significantly enhance model understanding beyond uncertainty estimates is supported by the study's data but could benefit from replication with different explanation types and tasks.
- **Medium Confidence:** The interaction between human and model confidence significantly influencing accuracy and reliance is supported by the experimental results but may be context-dependent.

## Next Checks

1. Replicate the study with a different high-stakes domain (e.g., medical diagnosis) to test generalizability of the findings across domains.
2. Conduct an ablation study comparing feature importance explanations to counterfactual explanations and natural language explanations to isolate the unique contribution of each explanation type.
3. Design a follow-up experiment to test if the effects persist when participants receive training on interpreting confidence scores and explanations before the task.
