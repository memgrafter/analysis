# Variational Latent Branching Model for Off-Policy Evaluation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12056
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12056
- Reference count: 40
- Outperforms existing state-of-the-art OPE methods on DOPE benchmark

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a variational latent branching model (VLBM) for off-policy evaluation in reinforcement learning. The method learns a compact latent representation of environmental dynamics from offline trajectories using a recurrent variational autoencoder with recurrent state alignment (RSA) and a branching architecture. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on the DOPE benchmark across multiple metrics including rank correlation, regret@1, and mean absolute error.

## Method Summary
The VLBM learns environmental dynamics by encoding state-action trajectories into a compact latent space, then reconstructing future states and rewards. It uses an LSTM encoder to approximate the posterior over latent variables, an LSTM decoder with multiple branches to generate predictions, and Gaussian latent space with dimensionality 16. The model is trained to maximize the evidence lower bound (ELBO) plus an RSA term that aligns encoder and decoder hidden states using pairwise squared error. The branching architecture averages predictions from 10 decoders, each weighted by learned coefficients that downweight poorly performing branches.

## Key Results
- Outperforms existing state-of-the-art OPE methods on DOPE benchmark
- Achieves higher rank correlation between estimated and true returns
- Improves regret@1 and mean absolute error metrics
- Demonstrates robustness across multiple D4RL Gym-Mujoco and Adroit environments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: RSA loss aligns LSTM hidden states between encoder and decoder to improve information flow.
- Mechanism: Pairwise squared error between h_ψ^t and h_φ̃^t regularizes structural similarity without enforcing exact equality.
- Core assumption: Structural alignment of recurrent states improves latent transitions more than mean squared error alone.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "RSA is defined as the mean pairwise squared error between h_ψ^t from the encoder and h_φ̃^t from the decoder"
  - [section] "We choose the pairwise squared loss over the classic mean squared error (MSE), because MSE could be too strong to regularize h_ψ^t and h_φ̃^t which support the inference and generative processes respectively and are not supposed to be exactly the same"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; assumption based on pairwise loss success in other domains (Gould et al., 2009; Rocco et al., 2018)
- Break condition: If structural similarity doesn't improve reconstruction accuracy or if pairwise loss becomes computationally prohibitive.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Branching architecture reduces sensitivity to random weight initialization.
- Mechanism: Multiple decoders sample from the same latent space and average weighted outputs; poorly performing branches are downweighted.
- Core assumption: Averaging predictions from multiple decoders trained on the same latent space reduces variance from initialization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "we introduce the branching architecture for decoder, with the goal of minimizing the impact brought by random weight initialization"
  - [section] "Empirically, we find that the branching weights, w_b's in (9), allows VLBM to kill branches that are not helpful"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; assumption that ensemble averaging reduces variance is standard but specific to latent space sampling not directly supported
- Break condition: If all branches perform poorly or if weighting mechanism fails to identify useful branches.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Compact latent representation captures environmental dynamics better than direct MDP modeling.
- Mechanism: Variational inference learns p(z) prior and q_ψ(z_t|z_t-1,a_t-1,s_t) encoder to map state-action space to lower-dimensional latent space.
- Core assumption: Environmental dynamics are more efficiently captured in a learned latent space than directly modeling MDP transitions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "formulating the environmental dynamics as a compact latent space, from which the next states and rewards are then sampled"
  - [section] "learn the transition function of MDPs by formulating the environmental dynamics as a compact latent space"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence; assumption based on success of latent models in RL policy optimization (Lee et al., 2020; Hafner et al., 2019)
- Break condition: If latent space dimensionality is insufficient or if posterior approximation is too poor.

## Foundational Learning

- Variational Inference: Why needed here: Enables learning intractable posterior p(z_t|s_t,a_t-1,z_t-1) for latent dynamics modeling. Quick check question: What objective function does variational inference maximize? (Answer: Evidence Lower Bound)
- LSTM Networks: Why needed here: Captures temporal dependencies in state-action sequences for both inference and generation. Quick check question: What type of recurrent architecture is used for both encoder and decoder? (Answer: LSTM)
- Reparameterization Trick: Why needed here: Allows gradient flow through stochastic sampling for training. Quick check question: How are samples drawn from diagonal Gaussian distributions during training? (Answer: z = μ + σ·ϵ where ϵ ~ N(0,I))

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prior p(z_0) → Encoder LSTM q_ψ(z_t|z_t-1,a_t-1,s_t) → Latent space Z → Decoder LSTM p_φ(z_t|z_t-1,a_t-1) → State/reward MLPs → Output
- Critical path: Encoder processes trajectory → latent space captures dynamics → branching decoders reconstruct → evaluation via simulated rollouts
- Design tradeoffs: Single decoder vs branching (robustness vs simplicity), pairwise vs MSE loss (structural vs exact alignment), latent dimension (expressiveness vs efficiency)
- Failure signatures: Poor rank correlation indicates latent space misalignment; high MAE suggests decoder reconstruction issues; uniform branching weights suggest initialization problems
- First 3 experiments: 1) Train VLM without RSA to verify improvement, 2) Test different latent dimensions (8, 16, 32), 3) Compare pairwise vs MSE loss performance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: Does the VLBM architecture with RSA and branching consistently outperform other methods on all types of MDPs, including those with complex reward structures or non-linear dynamics?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the VLBM outperforms existing methods on the DOPE benchmark, which includes environments with long horizons and high-dimensional state and action spaces. However, it doesn't explicitly test on MDPs with complex reward structures or non-linear dynamics.
- Why unresolved: The experiments focus on a specific set of environments and tasks. Further testing on a wider variety of MDPs is needed to confirm the generalizability of the VLBM's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on MDPs with complex reward structures (e.g., sparse rewards, delayed rewards) and non-linear dynamics (e.g., chaotic systems) would provide evidence for the VLBM's performance in these scenarios.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the choice of latent space dimensionality affect the performance of the VLBM?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the latent space dimension is set to 16, but it doesn't explore the impact of varying this parameter on the VLBM's performance.
- Why unresolved: The optimal latent space dimensionality might depend on the complexity of the MDP and the amount of training data available. Further investigation is needed to determine the relationship between latent space dimensionality and VLBM performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with different latent space dimensions and comparing the resulting performance of the VLBM would provide insights into the impact of this parameter.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the VLBM perform in online RL settings, where the agent can interact with the environment and collect new data?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on off-policy evaluation (OPE) using offline trajectories. It mentions that the VLBM could facilitate off-policy optimizations in the future, but doesn't explore its performance in online RL settings.
- Why unresolved: The VLBM is designed to learn from fixed offline trajectories, and its performance in online settings, where the agent can adapt to new data, is unknown.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on online RL tasks, comparing the performance of the VLBM with other online RL methods, would provide evidence for its effectiveness in these settings.

## Limitations

- Limited ablation studies on RSA vs MSE loss effectiveness
- No comparison with single-decoder variant using same latent capacity
- Performance may not generalize to MDPs with complex reward structures or non-linear dynamics

## Confidence

- Medium: The method shows consistent improvement across multiple metrics and environments, but the ablation evidence for specific architectural choices is limited. The success on D4RL benchmarks is promising but may not generalize to domains with different state-action distributions or temporal structures.

## Next Checks

1. **Mechanism isolation**: Compare VLBM with and without RSA using MSE loss instead of pairwise loss across all benchmark tasks to isolate RSA's contribution.

2. **Branching necessity**: Test single-decoder VLBM with doubled latent dimension against branching VLBM to determine if performance gains come from architecture or representational capacity.

3. **Temporal generalization**: Evaluate trained VLBM on temporally shifted trajectories (e.g., subsampled or accelerated sequences) to test whether RSA truly improves temporal coherence versus just improving reconstruction accuracy.
