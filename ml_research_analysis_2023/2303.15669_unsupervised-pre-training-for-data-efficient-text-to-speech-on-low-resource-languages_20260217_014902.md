# Unsupervised Pre-Training For Data-Efficient Text-to-Speech On Low Resource Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.15669
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15669
- Reference count: 0
- Pre-training method improves data efficiency in low-resource TTS, outperforming previous approaches by up to 1.5 MCD on Korean speech

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an unsupervised pre-training method for Tacotron 2 that leverages large untranscribed speech data to learn de-warping spectrograms. The approach significantly improves data efficiency in low-resource TTS tasks by teaching the model to reconstruct de-warped mel-spectrograms from randomly warped versions. A simple augmentation method (SegAug) further improves performance. The method learns a non-linear alignment beneficial for TTS and generalizes well across languages, achieving state-of-the-art results on Korean speech with minimal transcribed data.

## Method Summary
The method pre-trains Tacotron 2 on untranscribed speech data using random temporal warping of mel-spectrograms. During pre-training, spectrograms are randomly segmented and each segment is resized to a fixed length, creating warped spectrograms. The model learns to reconstruct the original spectrogram from these warped versions, effectively learning non-linear temporal alignment. For fine-tuning on TTS tasks, the pre-trained model is adapted to the target language with a proposed SegAug augmentation that applies random temporal warping to target spectrograms during training, further improving data efficiency.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 1.5 MCD improvement over previous unsupervised approaches on Korean speech
- Requires only 24 minutes of transcribed speech for competitive TTS performance
- SegAug augmentation provides additional improvements during fine-tuning
- Shows consistent gains across both objective (MCD) and subjective (AB preference) evaluations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Random segmentation and uniform resizing of spectrogram segments forces the model to learn non-linear temporal alignment between input and output sequences.
- Mechanism: By randomly segmenting spectrograms and resizing each segment to unit length, the model must learn to reconstruct the original spectrogram from a warped version where temporal alignment is no longer linear. This mimics the non-linear alignment required in TTS between text and speech.
- Core assumption: The temporal alignment needed for TTS (text to speech) is inherently non-linear and monotonic, similar to what the model learns through de-warping.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The main idea is to pre-train the model to reconstruct de-warped mel-spectrograms from warped ones, which may allow the model to learn proper temporal assignment relation between input and output sequences."
  - [section] "This method does not require annotation, as we synthesize the warped spectrograms by a simple random temporal warping technique. We sample random segment boundaries and resize each segment along the temporal axis to be a fixed size. Learning to de-warp as a pre-training step encourages the model to acquire both preliminary knowledge of attention alignment and autoregressive prediction."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Pre-training on untranscribed speech data enables the model to learn language-agnostic acoustic representations that transfer to low-resource languages.
- Mechanism: By pre-training on large amounts of untranscribed speech data (English in this case), the model learns general acoustic patterns and representations that are transferable to other languages during fine-tuning, even when only limited transcribed data is available.
- Core assumption: Acoustic features learned from one language contain transferable information that can be applied to other languages, especially for low-resource scenarios.
- Evidence anchors:

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: 1D Conv1D → Encoder → Attention → Decoder → Output Spectrogram
- Critical path: Input → Encoder → Attention → Decoder → Output
  - During pre-training: Spectrogram → Conv1D → Encoder → Attention → Decoder → Reconstructed Spectrogram
  - During fine-tuning: Text Embeddings → Encoder → Attention → Decoder → Mel-spectrogram

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Random segmentation vs. semantic segmentation: Random segmentation is simpler and doesn't require annotations, but semantic segmentation might provide better alignment learning
  - Segment size: Too small segments might not capture enough context; too large segments might not provide enough warping diversity
  - Number of segments: Balancing between having enough segments for warping diversity and maintaining meaningful context within each segment

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor MCD scores indicating reconstruction quality issues
  - Attention alignment plots showing incorrect or unstable alignments
  - Training instability during fine-tuning (high variance in loss)
  - Overfitting to pre-training language when working with different target languages

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the model with random segmentation but without any transformation (no resizing) to verify that the attention mechanism can still learn some form of alignment
  2. Compare different segment sizes (e.g., 4, 6, 8 segments) to find the optimal balance between warping diversity and context preservation
  3. Test the model with different amounts of pre-training data (e.g., 10h, 25h, 50h) to understand the data efficiency tradeoff and minimum effective pre-training requirements

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed unsupervised pre-training method compare to supervised methods when scaled to languages with even fewer resources?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates effectiveness on Korean as a low-resource language but doesn't explore extremely low-resource scenarios.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a moderate low-resource setting and doesn't test on languages with fewer than 24 minutes of speech data.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the method on languages with less than 24 minutes of speech data and comparing results to supervised approaches.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal segmentation method for the Speech De-warping approach across different language families?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares random segmentation to phoneme segmentation and pseudo-phoneme segmentation, but doesn't explore other segmentation approaches.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests a limited set of segmentation methods and doesn't systematically explore alternatives like syllable-based or morpheme-based segmentation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive experiments comparing multiple segmentation strategies across diverse language families.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed SegAug method compare to other state-of-the-art data augmentation techniques in TTS?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares SegAug to Gaussian noise, Mixup, and SpecAug but doesn't compare to more recent or specialized TTS augmentation methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on basic augmentation techniques and doesn't explore more sophisticated TTS-specific methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparisons with modern TTS augmentation techniques like GAN-based methods or cross-speaker augmentation.

## Limitations

- The optimal segmentation method for different language families remains unexplored

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: The effectiveness of the unsupervised pre-training approach on the KSS dataset, as evidenced by both objective (MCD) and subjective (AB preference) metrics showing consistent improvements over baseline.
- **Medium confidence**: The mechanism by which random segmentation and resizing teaches non-linear alignment, as the theoretical justification is sound but lacks direct experimental validation.
- **Medium confidence**: The cross-language transfer capability, as results show improvement but are limited to one language pair without extensive ablations or comparisons.
- **Low confidence**: The specific design choices for segmentation parameters and augmentation ranges, as these appear somewhat arbitrary without systematic exploration.

## Next Checks
