# TAPIR: Learning Adaptive Revision for Incremental Natural Language Understanding with a Two-Pass Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10845
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10845
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Achieves up to 4.5x faster inference than restart-incremental Transformers while maintaining comparable full-sequence accuracy on seven sequence labelling tasks

## Executive Summary
TAPIR introduces a novel two-pass neural model for incremental natural language understanding that learns when to revise predictions through a policy trained with Linear Transformer supervision. The model combines a monotonic incremental RNN encoder with a non-monotonic Transformer reviser, using a controller that decides between WRITE and REVISE actions based on the effect of new input on past outputs. Experiments demonstrate that TAPIR achieves better incremental performance metrics while being significantly faster than restart-incremental Transformers, effectively balancing the trade-off between processing speed and prediction accuracy in incremental settings.

## Method Summary
TAPIR is a two-pass model for incremental NLU that combines an LSTM-based incremental processor with a Transformer-based reviser and a controller that decides between WRITE and REVISE actions. The model uses a threshold-based policy (τ) to determine when revisions are necessary, with the controller learning from action sequences generated by a Linear Transformer under restart-incremental assumptions. Training occurs in two stages: first training the reviser alone, then jointly training the incremental processor and controller with combined loss. The approach includes a delay strategy where labels are expected at time step t + d, allowing the model to wait for disambiguating context before making final decisions.

## Key Results
- Achieves up to 4.5x faster inference than restart-incremental Transformers
- Maintains comparable full-sequence accuracy while improving incremental metrics (Edit Overhead, Correction Time, Relative Correctness)
- Outperforms restart-incremental Transformers on seven sequence labelling tasks across four NLU domains
- Demonstrates effectiveness of the two-pass approach with selective revision policy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: TAPIR learns when to revise incremental predictions using a policy trained with a linear Transformer-derived supervision signal.
- Mechanism: The model combines a monotonic incremental RNN encoder with a non-monotonic Transformer reviser and a controller that decides between WRITE and REVISE actions based on the effect of new input on past outputs.
- Core assumption: The Linear Transformer can capture order and hierarchical structure in sentences while allowing bidirectional influence, making it suitable for generating action sequences that indicate when revisions should occur.

  - [section 3.2]: "TAPIR predicts when to perform a recomputation. Assume that an input token xt is fed to the RNN component to obtain yt. The controller then reads xt, ht, and Γp to compute at."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss revision policies but don't specifically address linear Transformer supervision signals.
- Break condition: If the Linear Transformer fails to capture the necessary incremental quality due to its single-layer limitation, the supervision signal will be inadequate, leading to poor policy learning.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: TAPIR achieves faster inference by selectively deciding when to recompute rather than recomputing at every time step.
- Mechanism: The controller uses a threshold-based decision (τ) to choose between WRITE (adding new output to buffer) and REVISE (recomputing all existing outputs), reducing the number of expensive Transformer forward passes.
- Core assumption: The controller can effectively learn to recognize when past outputs need revision based on the incoming input and cached information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "TAPIR achieves better incremental performance and up to 4.5x faster inference than restart-incremental Transformers"
  - [section 3.1]: "Our model is equal to an RNN when τ = 1 (never recompute), and becomes a restart-incremental Transformer when τ = 0 (always recompute)."
  - [section 6.1]: "We see that, for all datasets, the vast majority of the correct prefixes indeed do not get revised."
- Break condition: If the threshold τ is set too high, the model may never revise when needed, losing the benefit of the reviser. If set too low, it may revise too frequently, losing the speed advantage.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The delay strategy improves both incremental and non-incremental performance by providing additional context for decision-making.
- Mechanism: During training and inference, the label for input xt is expected at time step t + d, where d is the delay, allowing the model to wait for disambiguating material before making final decisions.
- Core assumption: Additional right context helps the controller make better decisions about whether to revise or write, as it provides more information about the complete structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6]: "We also observe that the delay strategy helps improve the metrics. It improves the results for TAPIR, in general, but a longer delay does not always yield a better incremental performance."
  - [section 6.1]: "A longer delay means that our model needs to wait longer before producing an output. In the meantime, it still has to process incoming tokens, which might cause some difficulty in learning the relation between the input and its corresponding delayed output."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus neighbors discuss delay strategies but don't specifically address TAPIR's implementation.
- Break condition: If the delay is too long, the model may become less responsive and the benefit of incremental processing diminishes. If too short, insufficient context may lead to incorrect decisions.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Incremental Processor: LSTM network that produces output at each time step
  - Reviser: Transformer-based model that can revise outputs through recomputation
  - Memory: Caches storing hidden states, projected outputs, and input-output representations
  - Controller: Modified LSTMN that parametrizes the revision policy and decides between WRITE/REVISE actions

- Critical path:
  1. Input token xt → Incremental Processor → candidate output yt
  2. Controller reads xt, ht, and Γp → computes action at
  3. If WRITE: add yt to output buffer, update caches
  4. If REVISE: pass input buffer to reviser → recompute outputs, update caches
  5. Repeat for next token

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single-layer Linear Transformer for action sequence generation (simpler but may miss complex patterns) vs multi-layer (more expressive but violates incremental assumptions)
  - Small cache size N (faster but limited context) vs large N (more context but slower attention computation)
  - Fixed threshold τ (simpler but less adaptive) vs learned threshold (more complex but potentially better)

- Failure signatures:
  - High Edit Overhead (EO) with low Relative Correctness (RC): Controller is revising too frequently on correct outputs
  - Low EO with low RC: Controller is not revising enough when outputs are incorrect
  - High Correction Time Score (CT): Outputs are taking too long to settle, indicating unstable predictions
  - Degradation in non-incremental performance: Trade-off between incremental speed and final accuracy is unfavorable

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test with τ = 1.0 (no revision) vs τ = 0.0 (always revise) to understand the impact of the threshold on performance
  2. Compare with single-layer vs two-layer Linear Transformer for action sequence generation to validate the incremental assumption
  3. Test with different cache sizes (N=3, N=5, N=7) to find the optimal balance between context and speed

## Limitations

- The action sequence supervision relies on a single-layer Linear Transformer, which may be insufficient for capturing complex incremental qualities, potentially leading to inadequate supervision signals.
- The threshold-based revision policy (τ parameter) requires careful tuning, as setting it too high prevents necessary revisions while setting it too low eliminates speed advantages.
- The delay strategy introduces a trade-off between incremental responsiveness and final accuracy that isn't fully characterized across different delay values.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The architectural design combining RNN incremental processor with Transformer reviser is sound and the performance improvements (4.5x faster inference, better incremental metrics) are well-supported by the seven dataset experiments.
- **Medium confidence**: The Linear Transformer supervision signal effectively captures when revisions are needed, though this depends critically on the single-layer limitation being sufficient for the task.
- **Low confidence**: The optimal threshold τ and delay parameters are dataset-dependent and not fully characterized, making it unclear how well the approach generalizes without extensive hyperparameter tuning.
