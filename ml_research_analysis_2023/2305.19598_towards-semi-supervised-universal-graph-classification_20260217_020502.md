# Towards Semi-supervised Universal Graph Classification

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19598
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19598
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Introduces UGNN, a subgraph-based framework for semi-supervised universal graph classification that outperforms baselines in accuracy and OOD detection under limited labeling.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of classifying graphs when labeled data is limited and unlabeled data may contain out-of-distribution (OOD) samples from unknown classes. The proposed UGNN framework uses subgraph sampling to estimate prediction confidence and detect OOD samples, while constructing graph prototypes for both known and unknown classes. Posterior prototype assignments are computed using the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm to supervise unlabeled data across different subgraph views. Experiments on six datasets show UGNN achieves higher overall accuracy and better OOD detection compared to baseline methods, especially under low labeling ratios.

## Method Summary
UGNN combines subgraph-based OOD detection with prototype-aware semi-supervised learning. The method generates multiple perturbed subgraphs through edge and node deletion to capture prediction confidence and uncertainty. It then uses mean and variance of confidence scores to identify OOD samples. Graph prototypes are constructed for both known and unknown classes using K-means clustering, and posterior prototype assignments are computed using the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm. These assignments supervise the learning of unlabeled data across different subgraph views, enabling robust representation learning. The framework is trained using labeled data for warm-up and unlabeled data for semi-supervised learning.

## Key Results
- UGNN outperforms baseline methods in overall classification accuracy, especially under low labeling ratios
- On MNIST with low labeling, UGNN achieves 58.5% accuracy versus 48.8% for the best baseline
- The framework shows improved OOD detection performance compared to existing methods
- Mutual reinforcement between OOD detection and prototype learning enables optimal use of unlabeled data

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The subgraph sampling strategy effectively estimates prediction confidence and detects out-of-distribution (OOD) samples by exploiting individual output uncertainty.
- Mechanism: By generating multiple perturbed subgraphs through edge deletion and node deletion, the method computes mean and variance of confidence scores. High mean and low variance indicate in-distribution (ID) samples, while low mean and high variance suggest OOD samples. This dual-metric approach is more robust than using confidence alone.
- Core assumption: OOD samples exhibit lower confidence and higher variance across subgraph perturbations compared to ID samples.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "estimate the certainty of unlabeled graphs using multiple subgraphs, which facilities the discovery of unlabeled data from unknown categories"
  - [section]: "we adopt a simple yet effective selection strategy, which samples numerous subgraphs to capture both prediction confidence and individual output uncertainty based on the calibration of GNNs"
  - [corpus]: Weak. Only 5 related papers found, no direct comparison of subgraph-based OOD detection strategies.
- Break condition: If OOD samples are designed to be indistinguishable from ID samples under perturbation, the variance signal may not differentiate them effectively.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The prototype-based semi-supervised learning framework leverages posterior prototype assignments to learn from unlabeled data across different subgraph views, improving generalization.
- Mechanism: Graph prototypes are constructed for both known and unknown classes. Posterior prototype assignments are computed using the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm to ensure balanced and soft distributions. These assignments from one subgraph view supervise the learning of another view, enabling robust representation learning.
- Core assumption: The posterior prototype assignments capture meaningful semantic relationships that can guide semi-supervised learning across different subgraph views.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "construct semantic prototypes in the embedding space for both known and unknown categories and utilize posterior prototype assignments inferred from the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm to learn from abundant unlabeled graphs across different subgraph views"
  - [section]: "we construct graph prototypes in the embedding space for both known and unknown classes... utilizes the posterior prototype assignments from one subgraph view to supervise the semantics of unlabeled data from another view"
  - [corpus]: Weak. Related works focus on contrastive learning and knowledge distillation, not prototype-based semi-supervised learning with Sinkhorn-Knopp.
- Break condition: If the prototype assignments are poorly initialized or the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm fails to produce balanced distributions, the semi-supervised learning signal may be ineffective.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of subgraph-based OOD detection and prototype-aware semi-supervised learning creates a mutually reinforcing system that maximizes the utility of unlabeled data.
- Mechanism: Accurate OOD detection ensures that only ID samples contribute to prototype construction and semi-supervised learning. Conversely, effective prototype learning improves the discriminative power of the model, which in turn enhances OOD detection accuracy. This creates a positive feedback loop.
- Core assumption: The two components (OOD detection and prototype learning) are interdependent and their combined effect is greater than the sum of their individual effects.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our OOD sample selection technique and prototype-aware semi-supervised learning paradigm could mutually strengthen each other, enabling optimal use of unlabeled data"
  - [section]: "Taking both factors into consideration... A higher score indicates a small probability of being OOD samples... These prototype assignments are viewed as guidance to learn from unlabeled data"
  - [corpus]: Weak. No direct evidence of similar mutual reinforcement in related works; most approaches treat OOD detection and semi-supervised learning as separate problems.
- Break condition: If either component fails to perform adequately, the mutual reinforcement may break down, leading to suboptimal performance.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Graph Encoder -> Subgraph Generator -> OOD Detector -> Prototype Constructor -> Prototype Assigner -> Semi-supervised Learner
- Critical path:
  1. Warm up the GNN encoder using labeled data
  2. Generate subgraphs for each graph sample
  3. Detect OOD samples using subgraph-based confidence and variance
  4. Initialize graph prototypes for known and unknown classes
  5. Compute posterior prototype assignments using Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm
  6. Train the model using labeled data and prototype assignments from unlabeled data
  7. Update prototypes based on the learned representations
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Subgraph generation: More subgraphs provide better confidence estimates but increase computational cost
  - Number of prototypes: Too few prototypes may not capture class diversity, while too many may lead to overfitting
  - Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations: More iterations ensure better convergence but increase runtime
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor OOD detection: High false positive or false negative rates in identifying OOD samples
  - Unstable prototypes: Prototypes that change significantly across training iterations, indicating poor convergence
  - Low semi-supervised learning performance: Minimal improvement in accuracy when unlabeled data is added
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Verify subgraph generation: Generate subgraphs for a sample graph and visualize the perturbations to ensure they capture meaningful variations
  2. Test OOD detection: Use a dataset with known OOD samples and evaluate the subgraph-based OOD detection accuracy
  3. Validate prototype assignments: Compute prototype assignments for a small set of unlabeled data and visualize the distributions to ensure they are balanced and meaningful

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Question: How robust is UGNN's OOD detection performance when the unknown classes share significant structural or feature similarities with known classes?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that OOD detection is challenging when unlabeled data could belong to unknown classes, but does not explore scenarios where unknown classes are structurally similar to known classes.
  - Why unresolved: The experiments use datasets where unknown classes are visually or semantically distinct (e.g., digits vs. letters), but real-world scenarios may involve more subtle differences.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing UGNN's performance on datasets with closely related unknown classes (e.g., different species of animals or similar molecular structures) versus clearly distinct classes.

- Question: How does the number of subgraphs (I) used for OOD detection affect the trade-off between computational efficiency and detection accuracy?
  - Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses I=3 subgraphs but mentions that more subgraphs could capture more uncertainty, though this increases computational cost.
  - Why unresolved: The paper does not systematically study how varying I affects performance across different dataset complexities or labeling ratios.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies showing OOD detection accuracy and computational time across a range of I values (e.g., 1-10) on multiple datasets.

- Question: Can UGNN's prototype learning framework be extended to handle continuous label distributions rather than discrete class assignments?
  - Basis in paper: [inferred] The current prototype framework assumes discrete classes and clusters unknown classes into R discrete prototypes, but many real-world graph classification tasks involve continuous or hierarchical labels.
  - Why unresolved: The paper focuses on discrete classification tasks and does not explore how the prototype framework would handle more complex label structures.
  - What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying UGNN to datasets with continuous labels (e.g., molecular property prediction with continuous outputs) or hierarchical class structures.

## Limitations
- The subgraph-based OOD detection relies heavily on the assumption that OOD samples exhibit lower confidence and higher variance across perturbations, which may not hold for all types of OOD data
- The mutual reinforcement between OOD detection and prototype learning is theoretically sound but lacks empirical validation in related works
- Specific implementation details of the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm and subgraph sampling process are not fully specified, which could impact reproducibility
