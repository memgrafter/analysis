# Hallucinations in Large Multilingual Translation Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16104
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16104
- Reference count: 16
- Key outcome: Hallucinations are prevalent in low-resource and non-English-centric translation directions, often manifesting as detached or oscillatory errors

## Executive Summary
This work comprehensively investigates hallucinations in massively multilingual machine translation models across over 100 translation directions spanning different resource levels and beyond English-centric language pairs. The study examines the M2M family of NMT models and ChatGPT, revealing that hallucinations are particularly common in low-resource and non-English-centric translation directions, often manifesting as detached or oscillatory errors. The research identifies training data toxicity as a root cause for harmful hallucinations and demonstrates that using diverse external models as fallback systems substantially improves translation quality while eliminating oscillatory hallucinations.

## Method Summary
The study employs a comprehensive empirical analysis approach examining hallucination phenomena across multiple massively multilingual machine translation systems. Experiments cover over 100 language directions using the M2M family of NMT models and ChatGPT, with evaluations on diverse test sets. The methodology includes systematic analysis of hallucination types, frequency patterns across resource levels and language pairs, and investigation of training data influences. The research also tests mitigation strategies including model distillation, external model combination, and quality estimation techniques.

## Key Results
- Hallucinations are prevalent in low-resource and non-English-centric translation directions
- Smaller distilled models like SMaLL100 can reduce hallucination rates by leveraging shallow decoders and uniform sampling
- Toxic hallucinations in low-resource directions pose safety risks traceable to training data
- Using diverse external models (e.g., NLLB) as fallback systems substantially improves translation quality and eliminates oscillatory hallucinations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
Hallucinations occur when translation models generate outputs that are not faithful to the source text, often due to training data artifacts, architectural limitations, or inadequate supervision in low-resource settings. The mechanisms involve model uncertainty in under-represented language directions, memorization of spurious patterns from training data, and failure modes in the decoding process. Distillation and diverse model combination work by reducing model complexity and introducing complementary knowledge sources that can catch and correct hallucinatory outputs.

## Limitations
- Causal attributions linking specific hallucination types to training data characteristics require additional verification through controlled experiments
- Proposed mitigation strategies would benefit from larger-scale deployment testing to confirm real-world effectiveness
- Generalizability to future model generations and architectures beyond examined M2M and ChatGPT systems is uncertain
