# End-to-End Policy Gradient Method for POMDPs and Explainable Agents

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.09769
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09769
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: Proposed end-to-end policy gradient method for POMDPs using discrete internal states with improved interpretability through state-transition graph visualization

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an end-to-end policy gradient method for solving Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) by incorporating discrete internal states into the policy architecture. The method extends REINFORCE by training a joint policy over actions and internal states, enabling the use of standard policy gradient methods with minimal modifications. A key contribution is the visualization of learned internal states as state-transition graphs, which provides human-interpretable explanations of the agent's decision-making process. The approach is validated on simple POMDP problems including the Heaven/Hell and Tiger problems.

## Method Summary
The proposed method modifies standard policy gradient methods to handle POMDPs by introducing discrete internal states that act as memory mechanisms. The policy is parameterized as a joint distribution over actions and internal states, trained end-to-end using REINFORCE with a score function estimator. The internal state distribution depends on current and previous observations as well as the previous internal state, allowing the agent to retain information about hidden state variables. A visualization technique constructs state-transition graphs from trained policies by aggregating empirical transitions over observation-internal state pairs, making the learned decision process interpretable.

## Key Results
- The end-to-end method successfully solves simple POMDP problems (Heaven/Hell and Tiger) with discrete internal states
- Visualization of internal states reveals meaningful abstractions corresponding to hidden state variables (heaven/hell positions, tiger locations)
- The approach demonstrates improved interpretability compared to black-box policy gradient methods
- Performance is comparable to or better than separate training methods while providing additional interpretability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: End-to-end training of a joint policy over actions and internal states enables effective POMDP solving with minimal architecture changes.
- Mechanism: The policy gradient is modified to treat the internal state distribution and action policy as a single parameterized joint distribution, allowing standard policy gradient updates to be applied directly.
- Core assumption: Internal state can be modeled as a discrete categorical variable whose distribution depends on observations and previous internal state.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] mentions end-to-end training allows use of existing policy gradient methods with minor modifications; [section 3.1] provides explicit gradient derivation showing the modification.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Discrete internal states can encode and retain information about hidden state variables necessary for decision-making in POMDPs.
- Mechanism: The internal state distribution acts as a memory mechanism, aggregating information over time that is not directly observable, transforming the POMDP into an MDP over observation-internal state pairs.
- Core assumption: Hidden state information can be compressed into a finite discrete set without significant loss for the task.
- Evidence anchors: [section 2.4] explains the goal of estimating and retaining unobservable information; [section 4.2] shows internal states successfully distinguish heaven/hell positions and tiger locations.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Visualizing the learned internal state transition graph provides human-interpretable explanations of the agent's behavior.
- Mechanism: By collecting rollouts from the trained policy, constructing an empirical transition matrix over observation-internal state pairs, and creating a state-transition graph, the internal state dynamics become interpretable as a decision-making process.
- Core assumption: Trained internal states have learned meaningful abstractions corresponding to relevant hidden state variables.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] highlights the proposed visualization method for improving interpretability; [section 3.2] provides detailed procedure for creating transition graphs with examples.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Observation encoder -> Internal state network -> Action policy network -> Environment
  Joint policy parameterization over actions and internal states

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize internal state to zero
  2. At each step: encode observations, sample internal state, sample action, execute action, observe next observation
  3. After episode: compute returns, calculate policy gradient using score function estimator
  4. Update all parameters jointly via backpropagation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Discrete vs continuous internal states: Discrete allows visualization and simpler training but may not capture complex information
  - Number of internal states: More states can encode more information but increase training complexity and reduce interpretability
  - End-to-end vs separate training: End-to-end simplifies implementation but may have less stable training than separate updates

- Failure signatures:
  - Agent performs at chance level: Internal state network not learning useful representations
  - Training instability: High variance in policy gradient estimates due to discrete sampling
  - Redundant internal states: Multiple states with similar behavior patterns
  - Unreachable states in transition graph: Internal state dynamics not properly learned

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement REINFORCE baseline on fully observable version of Heaven/Hell problem to verify basic functionality
  2. Implement IState-GPOMDP (separate method) on Heaven/Hell problem to compare with end-to-end approach
  3. Train end-to-end method on Heaven/Hell problem with 2 internal states and visualize resulting transition graph

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the end-to-end policy gradient method perform on more complex POMDP environments with continuous or high-dimensional observation spaces?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper notes that the assumption of discrete internal states following a categorical distribution limits the application to relatively simple tasks, and future work is suggested to train agents in more complex situations.
- Why unresolved: The paper only demonstrates the method on simple POMDP problems (Heaven/Hell and Tiger problems) with discrete internal states, and does not explore its performance on more complex environments.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments applying the end-to-end policy gradient method to POMDP environments with continuous or high-dimensional observation spaces, and a comparison of its performance with other state-of-the-art methods for such environments.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the interpretability of the learned internal states and the transition graph scale with the complexity of the POMDP problem?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper demonstrates the visualization of learned internal states for simple POMDP problems, but does not explore how well the interpretability of the internal states and the transition graph scales with more complex problems.
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides examples of the visualization for simple POMDP problems, and does not discuss how well the interpretability would hold for more complex problems.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study comparing the interpretability of learned internal states and transition graphs for POMDP problems of varying complexity, and an analysis of the factors that affect interpretability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the end-to-end policy gradient method compare to other policy gradient methods for POMDPs in terms of sample efficiency and convergence speed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares the proposed method with REINFORCE and the separate method (IState-GPOMDP) in terms of cumulative rewards, but does not discuss sample efficiency or convergence speed.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on the interpretability and the ability to solve simple POMDP problems, but does not provide a comprehensive comparison with other policy gradient methods in terms of sample efficiency and convergence speed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the sample efficiency and convergence speed of the end-to-end policy gradient method with other policy gradient methods for POMDPs, using a variety of POMDP environments.

## Limitations
- The discrete internal state approach may not scale to complex real-world POMDPs where continuous memory is necessary
- Limited empirical validation on challenging domains beyond simple toy problems
- No quantitative measure of interpretability quality provided for the visualization method

## Confidence

- End-to-end policy gradient modification mechanism: **High confidence** - derivation is mathematically sound and consistent with REINFORCE framework
- Discrete internal states capturing hidden state information: **Medium confidence** - supported by simple experiments but not validated on complex environments
- State transition graph visualization for interpretability: **Low confidence** - qualitative results shown but no systematic evaluation of interpretability benefits
