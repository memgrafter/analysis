# Towards Meaningful Anomaly Detection: The Effect of Counterfactual Explanations on the Investigation of Anomalies in Multivariate Time Series

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03302
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03302
- Reference count: 40
- Participants classified anomalies with 15 percentage point higher accuracy when provided counterfactual explanations (70.24% vs 55.11%, p=0.021)

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether counterfactual explanations can improve the anomaly investigation process in multivariate time series data. While anomaly detection systems can identify unusual events, they cannot determine which anomalies are relevant to domain experts. The researchers conducted a behavioral experiment using New York City taxi trip data to test whether counterfactual explanations help users differentiate extreme weather events from other anomalies like holidays. In the experiment, 64 participants classified anomalies with or without counterfactual explanations. Results showed that counterfactual explanations significantly improved the accuracy of anomaly investigation, though they did not reduce investigation time or cognitive load. The findings suggest that explainable anomaly detection has potential for supporting human experts in anomaly investigation tasks across various domains.

## Method Summary
The researchers collected NYC taxi trip records from TLC (January 2016 - December 2018) and aggregated them hourly with 6 features: trip count, average trip duration per mile, proportion of tips in total fare, average trip distance, proportion of trips starting/ending in city center, and average passengers per trip. They trained an LSTM autoencoder with window size 8, step size 2, and hidden dimensions (8,6,4) to detect anomalies, setting thresholds at the 99th percentile of training errors. Counterfactual explanations were generated using the CoMTE framework, highlighting which features would need to change for an anomaly to be considered normal. An online experiment with 64 participants compared accuracy, efficiency, and cognitive load between control and explanation conditions, using extreme weather events as ground truth anomalies.

## Key Results
- Counterfactual explanations significantly improved anomaly investigation accuracy (70.24% vs 55.11%, p=0.021)
- Explanations did not reduce investigation time or improve efficiency compared to control condition
- Explanations trended toward increasing cognitive load rather than decreasing it

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Counterfactual explanations improve anomaly investigation effectiveness by reducing the number of features domain experts must analyze.
- Mechanism: Counterfactual explanations highlight only the features that would need to change for the anomaly to be considered normal, thereby filtering out irrelevant features.
- Core assumption: Domain experts can interpret counterfactual explanations and use them to focus on relevant features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Counterfactual explanations are as similar as possible to the sample explained while having a different classification label; i.e., "if the values of these particular time series were different in the given sample, the classification label would have been different."
  - The approach changes 3.2 features on average.
- Break condition: If domain experts lack understanding of normal value ranges or the counterfactual explanations are too complex to interpret.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Counterfactual explanations improve anomaly investigation effectiveness by providing context on how typical values look.
- Mechanism: By showing how values should have looked to avoid being flagged as anomalous, counterfactual explanations give domain experts a reference for normal behavior.
- Core assumption: Domain experts can use the counterfactual examples as a benchmark for normal values.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Second, they highlight how the time series should have looked liked to be not flagged as anomalous.
- Break condition: If the counterfactual examples are not representative of typical values or if domain experts cannot recognize the patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Counterfactual explanations do not improve efficiency because they require additional cognitive effort to interpret.
- Mechanism: While counterfactual explanations provide valuable information, the process of understanding and applying this information takes time and mental effort.
- Core assumption: The cognitive load of interpreting counterfactual explanations outweighs the time saved by focusing on fewer features.
- Evidence anchors:
  - Contrary to H2, counterfactual explanations do not increase human efficiency in anomaly investigation in our setup.
  - We find a trend that explanations do not reduce the cognitive load but instead increase it (H3).
- Break condition: If the design of counterfactual explanations is simplified or if domain experts become more proficient at interpreting them over time.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the study design and discussion imply several areas for future research, including the need to investigate the cognitive load implications of counterfactual explanations compared to other explanation methods, the generalizability of the findings to more complex anomaly scenarios, and the potential differences between lay participants and domain experts in their ability to interpret and utilize explanations.

## Limitations
- Binary classification task simplifies real-world anomaly investigation complexity
- Use of extreme weather events as ground truth may not fully capture complexity of determining anomaly relevance
- Effect size may not generalize to more complex anomaly scenarios or different domains

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The experimental methodology is sound, with proper randomization and statistical testing showing significant improvements in accuracy (p=0.021)
- Medium Confidence: The mechanism by which counterfactual explanations improve accuracy is plausible but not fully validated - the study assumes participants understood and used the explanations effectively
- Medium Confidence: The lack of improvement in efficiency and potential increase in cognitive load is observed but the study doesn't deeply investigate why this occurred

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the study with a more complex multi-class anomaly classification task to test generalizability
2. Conduct think-aloud protocols during the experiment to verify that participants actually understood and used the counterfactual explanations as intended
3. Test the approach with domain experts in operational settings (rather than students) to assess real-world applicability and efficiency impacts
