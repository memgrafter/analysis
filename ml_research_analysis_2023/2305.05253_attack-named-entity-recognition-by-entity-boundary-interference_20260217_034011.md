# Attack Named Entity Recognition by Entity Boundary Interference

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.05253
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05253
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: Proposes Virtual Boundary Attack (ViBA) achieving 70%-90% attack success rate on state-of-the-art NER models with single-word modifications

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the robustness problem in Named Entity Recognition (NER) by proposing a novel attack method called Virtual Boundary Attack (ViBA). Unlike existing text attack methods that can easily violate label consistency in NER, ViBA strategically inserts a new boundary token into the sentence to trigger Entity Boundary Interference. The attack exploits the observation that NER models heavily rely on boundary positions when making predictions. Through comprehensive experiments on English and Chinese NER datasets, ViBA demonstrates high effectiveness (70%-90% attack success rate) against state-of-the-art models like BERT, RoBERTa, and DeBERTa, while maintaining semantic similarity and single-token edit distance.

## Method Summary
The paper proposes Virtual Boundary Attack (ViBA), a one-word modification attack that inserts boundary tokens of entities into sentences to exploit Entity Boundary Interference. The method uses a safety distance parameter (w=2) to prevent label shift by ensuring inserted boundaries cannot form new entities with surrounding tokens. The attack targets the decoder component of BERT-style encoder-decoder NER models, leveraging the observation that encoders produce stable hidden states even when boundaries are perturbed. The approach is evaluated on multiple datasets including OntoNotes5.0, MSRA, and WNUT2017, measuring attack success rate, semantic similarity, and edit distance.

## Key Results
- ViBA achieves 70%-90% attack success rate on state-of-the-art NER models
- The attack maintains semantic similarity while using only single-token modifications
- Boundary Cut defense strategy improves recognition performance and reduces attack success rate
- Encoder stability analysis shows cosine similarity >0.93 between original and modified hidden states

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: NER models rely heavily on boundary tokens to identify entities, and perturbing these boundaries causes prediction errors.
- Mechanism: The model memorizes boundary patterns rather than learning contextual reasoning. When a boundary token is inserted, it disrupts the model's boundary-based recognition system, causing it to misclassify entities or fail to recognize them entirely.
- Core assumption: NER models prioritize boundary tokens over contextual information when making predictions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "NER models are always vulnerable to the boundary position of an entity to make their decision"
  - [section 2.1]: "Previous studies assume that an NER model is heavily reliant on the boundary of an entity when making decisions"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors focus on boundary discrimination but don't directly support this memorization claim
- Break condition: If NER models are retrained to prioritize contextual reasoning over boundary pattern matching, or if the model architecture fundamentally changes how boundaries are processed.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The encoder outputs stable hidden states even when boundary tokens are inserted, making the decoder the vulnerable component.
- Mechanism: When a virtual boundary is inserted, the encoder's output hidden states remain relatively unchanged (high cosine similarity >0.93), but the decoder misinterprets these stable states due to its non-robustness to boundary perturbations.
- Core assumption: The encoder is robust to small input perturbations while the decoder is not.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "the cosine similarity of the hidden-states between the H and Hm exceeds 0.93" and "the encoder is relatively stable to X and Xm"
  - [section 4.2]: "for such an encoder-decoder NER model, our ViBA mainly attacks the non-robustness of the decoder"
  - [corpus]: Missing - no corpus evidence about encoder-decoder robustness
- Break condition: If the encoder's hidden states change significantly with boundary insertion, or if the decoder is made robust to such perturbations through training techniques.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The safety distance prevents label shift by ensuring inserted boundaries cannot form new entities with surrounding tokens.
- Mechanism: By maintaining a safety buffer around the inserted boundary, the attack avoids creating new entity formations that would change the original labels, thus preserving label consistency while still exploiting boundary interference.
- Core assumption: Label shift occurs when inserted boundaries form new entities with adjacent tokens.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 2.2]: "safety areas guarantee that the original entity tags will not be affected after the insertion of a new boundary"
  - [section 2.2]: "We set safety areas to prevent two risky operations based on a safety distance w"
  - [corpus]: Weak - corpus neighbors mention boundary discrimination but don't discuss safety distance for label preservation
- Break condition: If the safety distance is set too small and boundary insertions create new entities, or if the model architecture changes how entities are formed from token sequences.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Input sentence -> Encoder (BERT/RoBERTa/DeBERTa) -> Hidden states -> Decoder (MLP/CRF) -> Entity predictions
  ViBA: Inserts boundary token -> Re-encodes -> Re-decodes -> Checks for prediction changes

- Critical path: Sentence -> Entity extraction -> Boundary insertion positions -> Adversarial example generation -> Model prediction -> Success criteria checking

- Design tradeoffs:
  Single-word modification vs. multi-word modifications: ViBA chooses single-word to avoid label shift but may have lower ASR
  Insertion vs. replacement: Insertion maintains sentence length but is more detectable; replacement preserves length but loses semantic information
  Safety distance setting: Larger distances prevent label shift but reduce attack opportunities

- Failure signatures:
  Low ASR: Safety distance too large, boundary tokens don't trigger interference, or model is robust to boundary perturbations
  High semantic similarity but low attack success: Model architecture changes or defensive training has been applied
  Runtime issues: Inefficient candidate generation or model inference bottlenecks

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test ViBA on a simple NER model (not BERT-based) to verify if boundary interference is architecture-specific
  2. Vary safety distance parameter to find optimal balance between label preservation and attack success
  3. Compare ViBA against a baseline that inserts random tokens (not boundary tokens) to confirm boundary-specific vulnerability

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed Virtual Boundary Attack (ViBA) perform against different types of Named Entity Recognition (NER) models beyond the ones tested in the paper?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates ViBA on BERT-base, RoBERTa-large, DeBERTa-large, and MacBERT-large models.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests ViBA on a limited set of models. It is unclear how well ViBA would perform against other NER models, such as those using different architectures or pre-training methods.
- What evidence would resolve it: Additional experiments testing ViBA on a wider range of NER models would provide insights into its generalizability and effectiveness across different model types.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the Entity Boundary Interference (EBI) phenomenon manifest in other languages beyond English and Chinese?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on English and Chinese datasets, and the effectiveness of ViBA is demonstrated on these languages.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the occurrence of EBI in other languages, leaving uncertainty about its prevalence and impact across different linguistic contexts.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments on NER datasets in various languages would reveal whether EBI is a universal phenomenon or specific to certain languages.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the long-term effects of the Boundary Cut strategy on the overall performance and robustness of NER models?

- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate the long-term impact of the Boundary Cut strategy on the model's ability to handle other types of attacks or its performance on different NER tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Long-term studies evaluating the effects of the Boundary Cut strategy on model robustness, adaptability, and performance across various NER tasks and attack scenarios would provide a comprehensive understanding of its implications.

## Limitations
- Safety distance parameter w=2 appears arbitrary without sensitivity analysis
- Limited evaluation to BERT-style architectures, leaving generalizability uncertain
- Does not explore edge cases where safety distance might be insufficient or overly conservative

## Confidence
- Mechanism 1 (Boundary Memorization): Medium confidence - attack succeeds but lacks ablation studies proving this is the true vulnerability
- Mechanism 2 (Encoder-Decoder Stability): Medium confidence - hidden state analysis supports claim but limited to BERT-style models
- Mechanism 3 (Safety Distance Effectiveness): Medium confidence - prevents label shift empirically but lacks theoretical justification for w=2 choice
