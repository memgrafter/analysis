# Foundation Models for Decision Making: Problems, Methods, and Opportunities

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04129
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04129
- Reference count: 20
- One-line primary result: Comprehensive survey of how foundation models can be adapted for sequential decision making tasks through generative modeling, representation learning, and interactive agent approaches.

## Executive Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive survey of the intersection between foundation models and decision making, outlining how large-scale pretrained models can be adapted to perform sequential decision making tasks involving interaction with external entities and long-term reasoning. The authors categorize approaches into using foundation models as generative models of behavior or environment dynamics, as representation learners, and as interactive agents or environments themselves. They review recent works in each category and identify open challenges such as bridging the dataset gap between broad foundation model pretraining data and task-specific decision making data, structuring environments and tasks for effective knowledge transfer, and improving foundation models for decision making. The paper provides a useful overview of the current state and future directions in this emerging research area.

## Method Summary
This paper is a conceptual survey that reviews and analyzes recent approaches at the intersection of foundation models and decision making. The authors examine how pretrained models can be adapted for sequential decision making through generative modeling of behaviors and world dynamics, representation learning from interactive data, and treating language models as interactive agents or environments. The survey categorizes existing work, identifies open challenges including dataset gaps and task structuring issues, and proposes potential directions for future research. Rather than presenting new empirical results, the paper synthesizes findings from 20 referenced works to provide a framework for understanding this emerging research area.

## Key Results
- Foundation models can be adapted for decision making through three main approaches: generative modeling of behavior or environment dynamics, representation learning from interactive data, and serving as interactive agents or environments
- Key challenges include bridging the dataset gap between broad pretraining data and task-specific decision making data, structuring environments for effective knowledge transfer, and improving foundation models' decision making capabilities
- The survey provides a useful conceptual framework for understanding how foundation models can be applied to sequential decision making tasks across domains like robotics, dialogue, and games

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Foundation models can serve as conditional generative models of behavior by modeling the distribution of actions given trajectory-level information.
- Mechanism: Pretrained models (VAEs, autoregressive transformers, diffusion models) can be adapted to maximize likelihood of actions conditioned on state trajectories, effectively learning behavioral priors from offline datasets.
- Core assumption: The trajectory dataset DRL contains sufficient diversity and quality of behaviors to learn meaningful priors.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "review recent approaches that ground foundation models in practical decision making applications through a variety of methods such as prompting, conditional generative modeling..."
  - [section] "foundation models can serve as conditional generative models of behaviors (i.e. actions) or the underlying world models (i.e., environment dynamics)"
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus lacks specific studies on behavioral generative modeling with foundation models.
- Break Condition: If DRL lacks diversity or contains only limited behavior types, the learned priors will be insufficient for novel tasks.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Foundation models can serve as representation learners that compress task-specific interactive data into compact state-action representations.
- Mechanism: Foundation models can optimize objectives like temporal contrastive learning, masked autoencoding, and model-based representations on DRL to extract useful features for downstream RL.
- Core assumption: The representation learning objectives are sufficiently aligned with the downstream decision making task.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "provide conceptual tools and technical background for understanding the problem space and exploring new research directions"
  - [section] "foundation models can also be used to support task-specific representation learning via task-specific objectives and interactive data, DRL"
  - [corpus] Missing - the corpus lacks specific evidence on representation learning from interactive data.
- Break Condition: If the representation learning objective is misaligned with the downstream task, the learned features will not transfer effectively.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Large language models can serve as interactive agents or environments, enabling new problems and applications to be considered under a sequential decision making framework.
- Mechanism: Language models can be treated as agents that interact with humans, tools, or other entities, or as environments that process prompts and generate responses.
- Core assumption: Language models have sufficient knowledge and reasoning ability to effectively interact with external entities.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Research at the intersection of foundation models and decision making holds tremendous promise for creating powerful new systems that can interact effectively across a diverse range of applications"
  - [section] "language foundation models can serve as interactive agents and environments, enabling new problems and applications to be considered under a sequential decision making framework"
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus lacks specific studies on language models as interactive environments.
- Break Condition: If the language model lacks sufficient knowledge or reasoning ability, the interactions will be ineffective or incorrect.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Foundation model (transformer) -> Adaptation for decision making role (generative model, representation learner, or interactive agent/environment)
- Critical path: 1) Pretrain foundation model on broad data 2) Adapt model to decision making task using appropriate objective 3) Evaluate performance on downstream task
- Design tradeoffs: Using foundation models as representation learners vs. generative models involves tradeoffs between flexibility and task-specific optimization. Representation learning is more general but requires additional finetuning, while generative modeling can directly produce actions but may be less flexible.
- Failure signatures: If the adapted model performs poorly, potential causes include: insufficient diversity in the dataset, misalignment between the learning objective and the downstream task, or limitations in the foundation model's capacity or architecture.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Adapt a pretrained language model to generate actions conditioned on state trajectories using behavioral cloning loss.
  2. Use a pretrained vision model as a representation learner with temporal contrastive learning on a decision making dataset.
  3. Treat a language model as an interactive agent that responds to human feedback using reinforcement learning.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we effectively bridge the gap between broad vision/language datasets (D) and task-specific interactive datasets (DRL) for decision making?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses this as a key challenge in Section 6.1, noting the modality and structure differences between D and DRL.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies this as a major obstacle but does not provide a definitive solution. It suggests approaches like hindsight relabeling and incorporating task information, but these are not proven to be effective.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating improved decision making performance when using D to augment DRL, or vice versa, through the proposed bridging techniques.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How can we structure environments and tasks to enable foundation models to generalize across different state-action spaces?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses this challenge in Section 6.2, noting the environment diversity in decision making compared to vision/language.
- Why unresolved: The paper proposes potential solutions like universal encoding and text/video as interfaces, but does not provide a conclusive answer on the best approach.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparisons of different structuring approaches on their ability to enable foundation model generalization across diverse decision making tasks.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we improve the decision making capabilities of foundation models beyond conditional generative modeling and representation learning?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses this in Section 6.4, noting the need for better methods to extract desirable behavior and transition from offline to online learning.
- Why unresolved: The paper suggests iterative approaches and tool use as potential directions, but does not provide a definitive solution.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies demonstrating significant improvements in decision making performance using novel approaches beyond the current methods discussed in the paper.

## Limitations
- The survey is conceptual rather than empirical, lacking specific implementation details and quantitative validation of proposed approaches
- Evidence anchors for several key claims are weak or missing, particularly regarding behavioral generative modeling and language models as interactive environments
- The paper does not provide a concrete experimental setup or benchmark for evaluating foundation models for decision making

## Confidence

**High**: The categorization of foundation model applications (generative models, representation learners, interactive agents) is well-supported by the literature and provides a useful organizing framework

**Medium**: The identification of open challenges (dataset gaps, task structuring, model limitations) is reasonable but lacks quantitative evidence of their relative importance

**Low**: Specific claims about mechanism effectiveness (e.g., representation learning objectives, behavioral priors quality) lack empirical validation in the current corpus

## Next Checks

2. **Objective Alignment Study**: Design controlled experiments testing how different representation learning objectives (temporal contrastive vs. model-based) perform on downstream decision making tasks, measuring both transfer efficiency and task performance.

3. **Interactive Agent Benchmark**: Develop a standardized evaluation framework for language models as interactive agents/environments, testing their ability to maintain coherent interactions across different domains and task types.
