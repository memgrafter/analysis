# Spherical Inducing Features for Orthogonally-Decoupled Gaussian Processes

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2304.14034
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14034
- **Reference count**: 16
- **Primary result**: A method for building data-dependent inducing features for Gaussian Processes that corrects variance overestimation while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses limitations in deep neural network (NN) induced Gaussian processes (GPs), where incorporating NN activation features leads to inaccurate variance estimates and computational inefficiencies. The authors propose a novel extension that leverages orthogonally-decoupled GPs to construct more flexible data-dependent basis functions for both principal and orthogonal components of the GP approximation. Their key innovation is using spherical NN activation features as inducing functions, which not only alleviates the aforementioned shortcomings but is more scalable than alternative strategies. Experiments on multiple benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach, showing improved predictive performance over comparable alternatives while maintaining similar computational costs.

## Method Summary
The authors extend orthogonally-decoupled sparse variational Gaussian processes (SVGP) by introducing inter-domain inducing variables based on spherical harmonic decompositions of neural network activations. This creates data-dependent basis functions that better align with the spectral properties of common kernels like Matérn. The approach uses both standard inducing variables for the primary approximation and orthogonal inducing variables to capture residual errors. The spherical harmonics provide a compact representation that maintains computational efficiency while offering more flexibility than simply increasing the number of standard inducing points. The method is implemented using the GPFlow library and optimized with the L-BFGS algorithm.

## Key Results
- The spherical NN activation features achieve comparable or better results than standard inducing points across most UCI datasets while offering more flexibility
- The orthogonal decomposition effectively corrects variance overestimation issues present in standard ACTIVATED SVGP
- The computational cost scales favorably compared to alternatives, with roughly O(M³ + K³) complexity rather than O((M+K)³)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Spherical NN activation features provide basis functions that better match the spectral properties of Matérn kernels
- Mechanism: The spherical harmonics decomposition ensures the inducing features' Fourier coefficients decay at a rate compatible with stationary kernels, avoiding the spectral mismatch that causes variance overestimation in standard ACTIVATED SVGP
- Core assumption: The kernel's Fourier coefficients and feature coefficients must align for good Nyström approximation
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "incorporating NN activation features under this framework not only alleviates these shortcomings but is more scalable than alternative strategies"
  - [section] "For the Matérn kernel (left column of panes in Figures 2 and 3), we see that there are multiple levels ℓ at which the feature coefficients are zero while the corresponding kernel coefficients are nonzero"
  - [corpus] Weak - related work focuses on sparse GPs but doesn't address spectral alignment specifically
- Break condition: If the spherical harmonics decomposition fails to capture the kernel's spectral properties, or if the NN activations produce features with fundamentally incompatible frequency content

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Orthogonal decomposition allows separate modeling of mean and variance components, correcting errors from the primary basis
- Mechanism: The orthogonally-decoupled framework introduces additional inducing variables that span a basis orthogonal to the primary inducing features, allowing the model to compensate for approximation errors in both predictive mean and variance
- Core assumption: Errors in the primary basis approximation can be captured by an orthogonal basis with sufficient flexibility
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "by introducing inter-domain variables to construct more flexible data-dependent basis functions for both the standard and orthogonal components"
  - [section] "This term constitutes the orthogonal basis, and provides added flexibility that is effective at nullifying errors introduced by the original basis"
  - [corpus] Weak - related work mentions sparse GPs but doesn't discuss orthogonal decomposition for error correction
- Break condition: If the orthogonal basis doesn't have sufficient capacity to capture the residual errors, or if the cross-covariance structure prevents effective error correction

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Computational efficiency is maintained because the orthogonal decomposition reduces the effective cost of adding flexibility
- Mechanism: Instead of increasing the number of primary inducing variables (which scales as O(M³)), adding orthogonal variables scales as O(M³ + K³), providing more flexibility per computational unit
- Core assumption: The orthogonal basis provides more modeling capacity per parameter than simply increasing the primary basis size
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "is more scalable than alternative strategies"
  - [section] "This approach leads to a (B³+1)-fold increase in the constant rather than a (B+1)³-fold increase"
  - [corpus] Weak - related work discusses scalability but not in the context of orthogonal decomposition
- Break condition: If K becomes large relative to M, the O(K³) term dominates and negates the computational advantage, or if the orthogonal variables don't provide sufficient additional modeling capacity to justify their cost

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) and the reproducing property
  - Why needed here: The entire framework relies on representing functions as inner products in RKHS, and the inducing variables are defined as projections onto basis functions using the reproducing property
  - Quick check question: Why does the reproducing property allow us to express [ku(x)]m = φm(x) when φm is an inter-domain feature?

- Concept: Spectral properties of kernels and basis functions
  - Why needed here: The paper's central innovation addresses the spectral mismatch between NN activation features and common kernels, which requires understanding Fourier/Mercer decompositions
  - Quick check question: What happens to the quality of the Nyström approximation when the feature and kernel spectra are misaligned?

- Concept: Orthogonal decomposition of GPs and the statistical meaning of orthogonality
  - Why needed here: The orthogonally-decoupled framework explicitly decomposes the GP into components that are orthogonal in the statistical sense, which is crucial for understanding how the additional basis functions correct errors
  - Quick check question: What does it mean for two GP components to be "orthogonal" in the statistical sense versus the functional sense?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Spherical NN activations → Spherical harmonics decomposition → Primary inducing features → Orthogonal inducing variables (standard points) → Variational parameters for both bases → Predictive mean and variance computation
- Critical path: Forward pass through NN → Spherical harmonics decomposition → Compute cross-covariances Kvu and Kvv → Compute posterior predictive using both bases → Backpropagation through all components
- Design tradeoffs: More orthogonal variables improves accuracy but increases computation (O(K³)); deeper NN layers improve representation but may exacerbate spectral mismatch; spherical harmonics provide good spectral properties but limit to zonal kernels
- Failure signatures: Overestimated variance indicates spectral mismatch; poor fit to data suggests insufficient capacity in either basis; numerical instability in K⁻¹ suggests ill-conditioning from poorly chosen inducing locations

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do different combinations of inter-domain inducing features and orthogonal bases perform across various kernel types and activation functions beyond those tested?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention they will explore alternative designs of inter-domain inducing features to construct new standard and orthogonal bases in future work.
- Why unresolved: The paper only tested a limited set of combinations (RELU and SOFTPLUS activations with Arccos and Matérn kernels). There are many other possible kernel types and activation functions that could be explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing various combinations of kernels (e.g., rational quadratic, periodic) and activation functions (e.g., tanh, ELU) with different orthogonal bases.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal trade-off between the number of standard inducing variables (M) and orthogonal inducing variables (K) for different problem types and dataset sizes?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that incorporating orthogonal bases has roughly the same cost as doubling truncation levels but leads to better performance improvements, yet they don't systematically explore the optimal M:K ratio.
- Why unresolved: The experiments used fixed ratios (128 base, 128 orthogonal or 256 base, 0 orthogonal) without exploring how this ratio should vary with dataset characteristics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies examining performance across different M:K ratios on datasets with varying sizes, dimensionalities, and noise levels.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the proposed method scale to very large datasets and high-dimensional problems compared to other scalable GP approximations?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention computational costs but only tested on moderate-sized UCI datasets, noting the approach has advantages over simply increasing inducing point counts.
- Why unresolved: The experiments were limited to datasets with up to ~8,600 points and 8 dimensions, leaving questions about scalability to millions of points or hundreds of dimensions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Performance benchmarks on large-scale datasets (millions of points), high-dimensional problems, and comparisons with other scalable GP methods like deep kernel learning or sparse variational approaches with different inducing point strategies.

## Limitations
- Limited experimental validation on diverse real-world datasets, with most improvements demonstrated on synthetic 1D data
- Computational complexity claims regarding orthogonal decomposition lack rigorous benchmarking against standard methods with increased inducing point counts
- Limited exploration of hyperparameter sensitivity, particularly regarding the number of spherical harmonics levels and orthogonal inducing variables

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The theoretical framework for orthogonal decomposition and inter-domain features is well-established in GP literature. The mechanism by which spherical harmonics can provide better spectral properties than standard NN activations follows from known properties of these functions.

**Medium confidence**: The experimental results showing improved RMSE/NLPD metrics across benchmark datasets are promising, but the sample size is limited (5 datasets) and the improvements are modest. The claim about computational efficiency requires more thorough benchmarking.

**Low confidence**: The assertion that this approach is "more scalable than alternative strategies" lacks quantitative comparison with standard approaches that simply increase inducing point counts. The practical benefits of orthogonal decomposition for error correction need more extensive validation across diverse data distributions.

## Next Checks
