# Boosting Reinforcement Learning and Planning with Demonstrations: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.13489
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13489
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Demonstrations can significantly improve RL efficiency by providing expert knowledge that bypasses exploration challenges

## Executive Summary
This survey comprehensively examines how demonstrations from human teleoperation or artificial agents can enhance reinforcement learning and planning algorithms. Demonstrations address fundamental challenges in RL including sample inefficiency and exploration difficulties by providing expert trajectories that bootstrap the learning process. The paper categorizes demonstration utilization into offline methods (learning policies, skills, world models, and representations) and online methods (exploration, reward shaping, and curriculum learning). A case study on the ManiSkill manipulation benchmark demonstrates practical implementation of a demonstration collection and utilization pipeline.

## Method Summary
The survey synthesizes approaches for leveraging demonstrations across the RL pipeline, distinguishing between offline utilization (before environment interaction) and online utilization (during interaction). Methods include imitation learning for direct policy cloning, inverse reinforcement learning for reward inference, offline reinforcement learning for value function learning, and various techniques for using demonstrations to shape rewards, guide exploration, and structure learning curricula. The ManiSkill case study implements a complete pipeline: collecting demonstrations via human teleoperation, storing them in a demonstration replay buffer, learning an initial policy through offline RL, and fine-tuning through online interaction.

## Key Results
- Demonstrations significantly reduce sample complexity by providing expert trajectories that bypass random exploration
- Demonstration-based reward shaping can transform sparse reward problems into dense learning signals
- Pre-training policies on demonstrations followed by online fine-tuning accelerates learning compared to training from scratch
- The ManiSkill benchmark case study demonstrates practical implementation and measurable performance improvements

- Mechanism: Demonstrations act as pre-collected expert trajectories that bypass the need for random exploration, reducing the sample complexity of RL algorithms.
- Core assumption: The demonstrations represent near-optimal behavior relevant to the target task, enabling the agent to bootstrap learning from a strong prior.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Demonstrations can be used to shape the reward function when the true reward is sparse or unavailable.
- Mechanism: By defining rewards based on proximity to demonstration trajectories or matching trajectory distributions, demonstrations provide dense feedback signals that guide learning.
- Core assumption: The demonstration trajectories contain implicit information about the desired behavior that can be extracted and used as reward signals.

- Claim: Demonstrations can bootstrap policy learning by providing a strong initialization for subsequent fine-tuning.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Demonstration collection (human teleoperation, autonomous agents, planners) -> Demonstration storage -> Policy learning modules (IL, IRL, offline RL) -> World model learning (transition/reward functions) -> Online learning integration (policy refinement, reward shaping)
- Critical path: 1) Collect high-quality demonstrations 2) Store and preprocess demonstration data 3) Learn initial policy or world model from demonstrations 4) Fine-tune policy through online interaction 5) Evaluate and iterate
- Design tradeoffs: Tradeoff between demonstration quality (expert vs. suboptimal) and quantity, between offline learning speed and online adaptation capability, between using demonstrations for policy initialization vs. reward shaping
- Failure signatures: Poor performance due to distribution shift between demonstrations and actual task states, overfitting to demonstration trajectories, inability to generalize beyond demonstrated scenarios, slow convergence when demonstrations are sparse or irrelevant
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test policy learning from demonstrations on a simple benchmark (e.g., CartPole) with both expert and random demonstrations to measure the impact of demonstration quality.
  2. Implement reward shaping based on demonstration proximity on a sparse-reward task to evaluate the effectiveness of demonstration-based reward signals.
  3. Compare online fine-tuning of a policy pre-trained on demonstrations versus training from scratch on a complex manipulation task to quantify the benefits of demonstration-based initialization.

## Limitations
- Demonstration Quality Dependence: Effectiveness critically depends on demonstration quality and relevance; low-quality or mismatched demonstrations can lead to poor learning outcomes.
- Distribution Mismatch: Fundamental challenge when demonstration data distribution differs from actual task distribution, leading to potential deployment failures.
- Generalization Boundaries: While demonstrations accelerate learning, they may limit the agent's ability to discover novel solutions outside the demonstrated space.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The fundamental benefit of demonstrations in reducing sample complexity and exploration burden is well-established through multiple successful applications.
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of different demonstration utilization strategies (policy learning, reward shaping, curriculum learning) varies significantly based on task characteristics and implementation details.
- **Low Confidence**: The optimal balance between demonstration quantity and quality, and the best strategies for handling distribution shift, remain open research questions.

## Next Checks
1. **Cross-domain transfer evaluation**: Test whether demonstrations from one task/domain can effectively transfer to similar but distinct tasks, measuring both success rates and failure modes.
2. **Quality-quantity tradeoff analysis**: Systematically vary demonstration quality (expert vs. suboptimal) and quantity to identify the optimal balance for different learning scenarios.
3. **Distribution shift robustness test**: Evaluate system performance when the agent encounters states far from the demonstration distribution, measuring both recovery ability and failure patterns.
