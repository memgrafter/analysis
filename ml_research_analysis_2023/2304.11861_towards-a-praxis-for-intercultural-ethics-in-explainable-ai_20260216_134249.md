# Towards a Praxis for Intercultural Ethics in Explainable AI

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.11861
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11861
- Reference count: 40
- This paper introduces an intercultural ethics approach to AI explainability that addresses the gap in making XAI accessible to users in the Global South.

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes an intercultural ethics framework for developing explainable AI (XAI) systems that are accessible and meaningful to users in the Global South. The authors identify a critical gap where current XAI approaches, primarily developed in Western contexts, fail to address the diverse cultural needs and contexts of non-Western users. By integrating participatory design methods, cultural studies, and human-centered evaluations, the framework aims to create XAI systems that are more effective, trusted, and inclusive across different cultural contexts.

## Method Summary
The paper synthesizes a methodological approach for developing intercultural XAI systems that involves engaging diverse stakeholders through participatory design workshops, studying cultural values and perspectives on AI concepts like fairness and privacy, addressing power dynamics between researchers and communities, and conducting human-centered evaluations with local partners. The approach emphasizes co-creation, local knowledge incorporation, and iterative refinement based on community feedback to ensure XAI systems meet the specific needs of diverse user groups.

## Key Results
- Identifies the need for culturally-informed XAI approaches to bridge the accessibility gap for Global South users
- Proposes participatory design as a key mechanism for uncovering cultural nuances in XAI interpretation
- Emphasizes the importance of addressing power dynamics and conducting human-centered evaluations in diverse contexts

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Participatory Design Engine -> Cultural Studies Module -> Power Dynamics Handler -> Human-Centered Evaluation Suite -> XAI System Builder

- Critical path:
  1. Engage diverse stakeholders through participatory design
  2. Study cultural values and perspectives on AI concepts
  3. Address power dynamics and implement feedback mechanisms
  4. Conduct human-centered evaluations with local partners
  5. Build and refine XAI systems based on insights

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Depth vs. breadth of cultural engagement: More in-depth studies may provide richer insights but limit the number of cultures studied
  - Standardization vs. customization: Balancing the need for consistent XAI methods with the desire to tailor explanations to specific cultures
  - Speed vs. inclusivity: Faster development may exclude important voices from historically marginalized groups

- Failure signatures:
  - Explanations are not trusted or understood by target users
  - XAI systems perform well in lab settings but fail in real-world contexts
  - Feedback from local communities is not incorporated into system design
  - Power imbalances between researchers and communities lead to biased results

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Conduct a co-creation workshop with users from different cultural backgrounds to identify key values and concerns around AI explanations
  2. Implement a feedback mechanism in an XAI system and test its effectiveness in addressing power dynamics
  3. Partner with a local organization to evaluate an XAI system in a real-world setting, focusing on cultural appropriateness and user understanding

- What evidence would resolve it: Development and validation of evaluation metrics that capture cultural factors, user trust, and system adoption rates across different regions

## Limitations
- The paper is primarily conceptual and lacks empirical validation of the proposed approach
- No specific metrics or evaluation criteria are provided for measuring the success of intercultural XAI systems
- The paper does not address potential conflicts between different cultural values or how to resolve them

## Confidence
- High confidence in the identification of the problem space: There is clear evidence that XAI systems developed primarily in Western contexts may not effectively serve users in the Global South.
- Medium confidence in the proposed mechanisms: While the general approach of participatory design and cultural studies is sound, the paper lacks specific implementation details and empirical validation.
- Low confidence in the integration of power dynamics: The paper acknowledges this as important but provides minimal guidance on how to address these dynamics in practice.
