# A Bayesian Optimization approach for calibrating large-scale activity-based transport models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03480
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03480
- Reference count: 40
- A Bayesian Optimization approach using Random Forest surrogates successfully calibrates 477 parameters in the SimMobility MT activity-based transport model for Tallinn, Estonia.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of calibrating large-scale activity-based transport models by proposing a Bayesian Optimization (BO) approach. The method uses an improved Random Forest as a surrogate model to handle the high dimensionality of the calibration problem, which involves hundreds of behavioral parameters. Tested on a case study for Tallinn using the SimMobility MT software, the approach shows satisfactory performance in major calibration indicators, with a 4% error in overall trips and a 15.92 vehicles per day average error in the OD matrix. The BO framework automates the calibration process, enabling the tuning of a wider set of behavioral parameters than traditional manual methods and potentially fostering wider adoption of activity-based modeling in transportation research and practice.

## Method Summary
The proposed method combines Bayesian Optimization with a Random Forest surrogate model to calibrate large-scale activity-based transport models. The process begins with generating an initial population of parameter vectors using Latin Hypercube Sampling, which are then evaluated in the SimMobility MT simulator. A Random Forest is trained on these parameter-output pairs to approximate the simulator's behavior. The Expected Improvement acquisition function, optimized using L-BFGS-B, guides the selection of new parameter vectors to evaluate. After the BO iterations, a multi-criteria Pareto analysis filters robust solutions based on six additional criteria. The approach is tested on a case study for Tallinn, Estonia, calibrating 477 behavioral parameters in the SimMobility MT software.

## Key Results
- Satisfactory performance in major calibration indicators: 4% error in overall trips
- 15.92 vehicles per day average error in the OD matrix
- Successfully calibrated 477 behavioral parameters in SimMobility MT for Tallinn, Estonia

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Random Forest surrogate with adapted uncertainty estimates can efficiently approximate high-dimensional likelihood surfaces for BO.
- Mechanism: RF ensemble predictions are averaged to estimate the mean (μθ), and standard deviation (σθ) is computed from individual tree predictions, providing approximate predictive uncertainty. This uncertainty feeds into Expected Improvement (EI) to guide sampling in BO.
- Core assumption: RF's variance among trees correlates with true prediction uncertainty and is sufficient for EI-based exploration-exploitation trade-off.

- Break condition: If RF variance underestimates true uncertainty, EI may over-exploit regions and miss global optima.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: L-BFGS-B acquisition optimization balances gradient efficiency with bounded parameter space constraints.
- Mechanism: L-BFGS-B iteratively updates parameter values to maximize EI using gradient information while enforcing box constraints, enabling efficient search in high-dimensional spaces.
- Core assumption: EI landscape is sufficiently smooth and differentiable for quasi-Newton methods to perform well.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III.D] L-BFGS-B chosen for EI maximization

- Break condition: If EI is noisy or discontinuous, gradient-based methods may converge to local optima.

- Claim: Multi-criteria Pareto analysis filters robust solutions beyond the single BO objective.

- Core assumption: BO objective correlates with but does not fully capture all important calibration benchmarks.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  SimMobility MT (expensive simulator) -> Evaluation of candidate β vectors
  Random Forest surrogate -> Fast approximation of simulator outputs
  Expected Improvement acquisition -> Scoring function for candidate βs
  L-BFGS-B optimizer -> Acquisition function maximization within bounds
  Pareto analysis -> Post-filtering of BO candidates

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize β population via Latin Hypercube Sampling
  2. Evaluate βs in SimMobility (expensive step)
  3. Train RF surrogate on β-output pairs
  4. Use RF to predict outputs and uncertainties for new βs
  5. Maximize EI via L-BFGS-B to select next βs
  6. Iterate until budget exhausted
  7. Apply Pareto analysis to select final βs

- Design tradeoffs:
  - RF depth vs. overfitting: deeper trees may overfit; shallower trees may underfit
  - Number of trees (C) vs. computational cost: more trees increase stability but slow training
  - Acquisition optimization method: L-BFGS-B fast but assumes smoothness; random search more robust but slower

- Failure signatures:
  - BO stagnates early -> EI landscape flat or surrogate poorly trained
  - Pareto front empty or trivial -> BO objective poorly aligned with practical benchmarks
  - RF predictions unstable -> Too few trees or too deep trees overfitting

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run BO with only 2 parameters (e.g., two βs in mode choice) to verify surrogate and EI behavior
  2. Increase to 10 parameters, compare RF vs. GP surrogate performance
  3. Run full 477-parameter calibration, inspect RF variance vs. actual prediction error to validate uncertainty estimates

## Limitations
- The validation is limited to a single case study (Tallinn), raising questions about generalizability to other cities with different travel patterns.
- The RF surrogate uncertainty estimates are assumed sufficient for EI but not empirically validated against ground truth uncertainty.
- Claims about enabling "wider adoption" of activity-based models are speculative and not directly supported by the empirical results.

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The basic BO framework (surrogate + EI + L-BFGS-B) is well-established and the implementation details are clearly documented.
- **Medium Confidence**: The RF adaptation for uncertainty estimation is reasonable but not validated against alternatives; results are promising but case-study-specific.
- **Low Confidence**: Claims about the method enabling "wider adoption" of activity-based models are speculative and not directly supported by the empirical results.

## Next Checks
1. Cross-validate RF uncertainty estimates by comparing ensemble standard deviation against actual prediction errors on held-out data.
2. Apply the same BO pipeline to at least two additional cities with different travel patterns to test generalizability.
3. Implement traditional manual calibration on the same Tallinn dataset and quantify improvements in both computational efficiency and calibration accuracy.
