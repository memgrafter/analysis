# Dual-interest Factorization-heads Attention for Sequential Recommendation

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03965
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03965
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: DFAR achieves 2.19% and 0.54% GAUC improvement on micro-video and Amazon datasets respectively

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of modeling both positive and negative feedback in sequential recommendation, particularly for video feed scenarios where users exhibit skip/no-skip behaviors. The authors propose DFAR (Dual-interest Factorization-heads Attention for Sequential Recommendation), which introduces feedback-aware encoding with factorization-heads attention to capture transition patterns between different feedback types. The model incorporates dual-interest disentangling to separate positive and negative interests and employs pair-wise contrastive learning for prediction. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements over state-of-the-art baselines on two real-world datasets.

## Method Summary
The proposed DFAR model addresses sequential recommendation by explicitly modeling both positive and negative user feedback through a novel architecture. It introduces a feedback-aware encoding layer with factorization-heads attention that captures transition patterns between different feedback types, a dual-interest disentangling layer that separates positive and negative user interests, and a prediction layer enhanced with pair-wise contrastive learning. The factorization-heads attention mechanism allows the model to learn distinct transition patterns for positive and negative interactions, while the dual-interest disentangling layer ensures these interests remain separated throughout the modeling process. The pair-wise contrastive loss helps the model learn more discriminative representations by pulling together similar items and pushing apart dissimilar ones.

## Key Results
- DFAR achieves 2.19% improvement in GAUC on the Micro-video dataset compared to state-of-the-art baselines
- DFAR achieves 0.54% improvement in GAUC on the Amazon dataset compared to state-of-the-art baselines
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of factorization-heads attention and pair-wise contrastive loss as particularly crucial components

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The mechanism works by explicitly modeling the dual nature of user feedback in sequential recommendation scenarios. Traditional sequential models often treat interactions uniformly, missing important distinctions between positive (clicked, watched) and negative (skipped, ignored) feedback. DFAR's factorization-heads attention mechanism allows the model to learn distinct transition patterns for positive and negative interactions, capturing how users' preferences evolve differently based on their feedback type. The dual-interest disentangling layer ensures these interests remain separated, preventing information contamination between positive and negative preferences. The pair-wise contrastive learning further enhances the model's ability to distinguish between items the user is likely to engage with versus those they would skip, creating more discriminative representations that better reflect actual user preferences.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Input sequence -> Factorization-heads Attention -> Dual-interest Disentangling -> Pair-wise Contrastive Prediction

Critical path: The critical path flows from the factorization-heads attention layer through the dual-interest disentangling layer to the final prediction layer with contrastive loss. The factorization-heads attention captures transition patterns, the disentangling layer separates interests, and the contrastive loss optimizes the final representations.

Design tradeoffs: The main tradeoff is between model complexity and performance gain. While DFAR introduces multiple novel components, each adds computational overhead. The factorization-heads attention increases parameter count but captures more nuanced patterns. The dual-interest disentangling adds separation constraints but may limit information flow between positive and negative interests.

Failure signatures: Potential failure modes include: 1) Over-separation of interests leading to loss of complementary information, 2) Contrastive loss dominating other learning signals, 3) Factorization-heads attention failing to capture meaningful transition patterns in sparse feedback scenarios, 4) Poor performance on datasets with weak feedback signals.

## Limitations
- The study focuses primarily on video feed and e-commerce datasets, limiting generalizability to other sequential recommendation domains
- Ablation studies show component effectiveness but lack detailed analysis of relative importance across different dataset characteristics
- Visualization analysis provides qualitative insights but lacks rigorous quantitative validation of the dual-interest disentangling quality

## Confidence

High confidence: The experimental methodology is sound, and reported improvements over baselines are statistically significant and reproducible.

Medium confidence: The effectiveness of individual components is demonstrated through ablation studies, but the relative contribution of each component to overall performance could be better quantified.

Medium confidence: The visualization analysis provides qualitative insights but lacks rigorous quantitative validation of the dual-interest disentangling.
