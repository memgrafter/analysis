# Exploring Vision-Language Models for Imbalanced Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.01457
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01457
- Reference count: 25
- Primary result: VLMs with lightweight decoder and imbalanced learning algorithms achieve 6.58%-69.82% accuracy gains on imbalanced datasets

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of applying Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP to imbalanced classification tasks. The authors propose a method that adds a lightweight decoder after the frozen vision transformer and incorporates imbalanced learning algorithms such as Focal Loss, Balanced Softmax, and Distribution Alignment. Through experiments on ImageNet-LT, iNaturalist18, and Places-LT datasets, the approach demonstrates significant improvements over zero-shot classification, achieving average accuracy gains of 6.58%, 69.82%, and 6.17% respectively. The study also analyzes the impact of pre-training data size, backbone architectures, and training costs.

## Method Summary
The method involves freezing the pre-trained CLIP vision encoder and adding a lightweight decoder to transform CLIP embeddings into task-specific representations. This is combined with imbalanced learning algorithms (Focal Loss, Balanced Softmax, Distribution Alignment) to adjust training dynamics and mitigate class imbalance. The approach avoids the computational overhead of full fine-tuning while capturing nuanced features for tail classes through the decoder. The text encoder is not used in this setup, focusing solely on visual representations and classifier adjustments.

## Key Results
- VLMs with decoder and imbalanced algorithms achieve 6.58% average accuracy gain on ImageNet-LT
- Method achieves 69.82% average accuracy gain on iNaturalist18 dataset
- Approach shows 6.17% average accuracy gain on Places-LT dataset
- Imbalanced learning techniques increase VRAM usage by only 13-17% compared to linear probing

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Lightweight decoder captures nuanced features for tail classes
- Mechanism: Transforms frozen CLIP embeddings into discriminative representations for minority classes
- Core assumption: Linear projection layer insufficient; decoder extracts richer features without full encoder fine-tuning
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions decoder avoids OOM and captures nuanced features; section 4.2 states decoder needed for tail class representation
- Break condition: If decoder fails to learn meaningful transformations, performance gains minimal

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Imbalanced learning algorithms adjust gradients/boundaries to mitigate imbalance
- Mechanism: Reweights logits or recalibrates classifier to give more importance to minority classes
- Core assumption: Standard cross-entropy leads to majority class bias; adjusted training dynamics correct this
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions incorporating Focal Loss, Balanced Softmax, and Distribution Alignment; section 4.3 notes text encoder difficulty combining with imbalanced methods
- Break condition: If adjusted gradients don't align with true distribution, performance may not improve

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Freezing encoder reduces computational cost while maintaining performance
- Mechanism: Avoids high memory/computational demands of full fine-tuning by keeping pre-trained encoder fixed
- Core assumption: Frozen encoder provides sufficient feature extraction; only downstream components need adaptation
- Evidence anchors: Section 4.3 explains avoiding text encoder complexity; section 5.5 shows 13-17% VRAM increase vs linear probing
- Break condition: If frozen encoder features aren't transferable, limiting performance gains

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input image -> CLIP Vision Encoder (frozen) -> Decoder -> Classifier -> Output probabilities
- Critical path: 1) Image to CLIP encoder 2) Decoder transforms embeddings 3) Classifier maps to class probabilities 4) Imbalanced algorithm adjusts training
- Design tradeoffs: Freezing encoder reduces cost but may limit adaptability; decoder increases complexity but captures tail class features; imbalanced algorithms improve minority performance but add hyperparameters
- Failure signatures: No improvement over zero-shot (decoder not learning), overfitting to majority classes (imbalanced algorithm ineffective), high computational cost (decoder too complex)
- First 3 experiments: 1) Baseline zero-shot CLIP on ImageNet-LT 2) CLIP with linear probing (no decoder, no imbalanced algorithm) 3) CLIP with decoder and Balanced Softmax on ImageNet-LT

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does proposed decoder architecture compare to other modifications (attention layers, transformer blocks) for VLM performance on imbalanced datasets?
- Basis: Paper proposes lightweight decoder with three transformer blocks but doesn't explore alternatives
- Why unresolved: Focuses on demonstrating effectiveness rather than comparing to other options
- What evidence: Systematic experiments comparing decoder to other architectural modifications on same datasets

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does performance scale with increasing dataset size and class diversity beyond three benchmark datasets?
- Basis: Evaluates approach on three imbalanced datasets without exploring larger/more diverse datasets
- Why unresolved: Study limited to three benchmarks, leaving generalizability unexplored
- What evidence: Experiments on wider range of imbalanced datasets with varying sizes and characteristics

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do proposed methods perform on semi-supervised imbalanced learning tasks with partial labeling?
- Basis: Paper acknowledges limitation of not exploring semi-supervised imbalanced learning
- Why unresolved: Focuses on fully supervised learning, not addressing semi-supervised challenges
- What evidence: Experiments comparing proposed methods to other semi-supervised approaches on benchmark datasets with varying labeled data

## Limitations

- Exact lightweight decoder architecture not fully specified, making it difficult to assess contribution relative to imbalanced algorithms
- No detailed ablation study isolating decoder versus imbalanced algorithm contributions to performance gains
- Computational cost analysis limited to VRAM usage during training, excluding inference time and memory requirements

## Confidence

- **High**: Imbalanced learning algorithms improve VLMs for imbalanced classification (supported by established literature and experimental results)
- **Medium**: Lightweight decoder captures nuanced features for tail classes (some evidence but lacks detailed specifications and ablation studies)
- **Low**: Proposed method is computationally efficient compared to full fine-tuning (analysis limited to VRAM, doesn't consider other factors)
