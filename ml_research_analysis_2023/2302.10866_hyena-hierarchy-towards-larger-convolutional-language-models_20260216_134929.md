# Hyena Hierarchy: Towards Larger Convolutional Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.10866
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10866
- Authors: Poli, G. P., Wang, K., Massaroli, S., Ryzhakov, S., Hillier, C., Darrell, T., & Hernandez, N.
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Hyena operators achieve subquadratic complexity by interleaving implicit long convolutions and data-controlled gating, matching Transformer perplexity with 20% fewer FLOPs and 2-100x speedup at long sequence lengths

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces Hyena, a subquadratic drop-in replacement for attention that decouples sequence length from parameter count through a recurrence of implicit long convolutions and element-wise gating. Hyena operators achieve Transformer-level language modeling quality with significantly reduced computational cost, particularly at long sequence lengths where attention becomes prohibitively expensive. The approach demonstrates that attention's pattern of data-controlled linear transformations can be effectively approximated through FFT-based convolution operations, enabling efficient processing of sequences up to 64K tokens.

## Method Summary
Hyena operators are constructed as a recurrence of two subquadratic primitives: implicit long convolutions parametrized by feed-forward networks with exponential decay modulation, and data-controlled multiplicative gating. The convolution filters are implemented using FFT-based algorithms with O(L log² L) complexity, and window functions control the frequency response of the filters. The architecture maintains expressivity through N recurrence steps while sharing FFT computations across layers to improve hardware utilization. Training follows standard GPT-style procedures with AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate decay, and standard hyperparameters.

## Key Results
- On The Pile at 355M parameters, Hyena matches Transformer perplexity with 20% reduction in total floating point operations
- Hyena operators are twice as fast as highly optimized attention at sequence length 8K, and 100x faster at sequence length 64K
- The quality gap with attention significantly shrinks at scale, with Hyena achieving similar perplexity and downstream performance with smaller computational budget

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hyena matches attention quality by implementing a data-controlled linear operator without quadratic cost
- Mechanism: Hyena interleaves implicit long convolutions and element-wise gating to form a recurrence that constructs a data-controlled matrix, effectively encoding a family of linear functions conditioned on the input
- Core assumption: The ability to decouple sequence length from parameter count while maintaining long-range dependencies is sufficient to match attention's performance
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Recent advances in deep learning have relied heavily on the use of large Transformers due to their ability to learn at scale. However, the core building block of Transformers, the attention operator, exhibits quadratic cost in sequence length, limiting the amount of context accessible."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Hyena operators are twice as fast as highly optimized attention at sequence length 8K, and 100x faster at sequence length 64K
- Mechanism: Hyena uses FFT-based convolution algorithms with O(L log² L) complexity instead of O(L²) for attention, and shares FFT computations across layers to improve hardware utilization
- Core assumption: FFTConv implementations can be efficiently fused and optimized to overcome lower hardware utilization compared to attention
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "Hyena operators are twice as fast as highly optimized attention at sequence length 8K, and 100x faster at sequence length 64K."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hyena matches Transformer perplexity with a 20% reduction in training compute required at sequence length 2K
- Mechanism: By decoupling sequence length from parameter count, Hyena can allocate more parameters to FFNs between layers while maintaining expressivity through long-range convolutions
- Core assumption: The quality gap between attention and Hyena is primarily due to parameter allocation inefficiencies rather than fundamental expressivity differences
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "On the The Pile at the 355M parameter scale, Hyena matches Transformer perplexity with a 20% reduction in total floating point operations."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input projections -> Recurrence (N steps of gating + convolution) -> Output
- Critical path: Input → Projections → Recurrence (N steps of gating + convolution) → Output
- Design tradeoffs:
  - More recurrence steps (larger N) increases expressivity but also computational cost
  - Window function choice affects filter specialization (exponential decay vs other patterns)
  - Positional encoding dimensionality impacts filter initialization quality
- Failure signatures:
  - Vanishing/exploding gradients in deep Hyena stacks
  - Poor performance on recall tasks suggests insufficient long-range modeling
  - High perplexity on language tasks indicates expressivity gaps

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do Hyena operators scale beyond the sub-billion parameter regime, and what is the upper bound on sequence length they can effectively process?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions preliminary results for 1.3B parameter models and training up to 130B tokens, but scaling laws are not fully established
- Why unresolved: The paper only provides limited scaling experiments and does not explore the limits of sequence length or model size
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments scaling Hyena models to trillion parameters and sequence lengths of millions of tokens, measuring perplexity and computational efficiency

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the precise relationship between Hyena's performance on synthetic reasoning tasks and its real-world language modeling capabilities?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes a correlation between synthetic task performance and The Pile perplexity, but the nature of this relationship is not fully characterized
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a quantitative analysis of how synthetic task performance predicts real-world language modeling quality
- What evidence would resolve it: A large-scale study mapping synthetic task scores to language model benchmarks like GLUE or SuperGLUE, establishing a predictive model

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the choice of implicit convolution parametrization (e.g., FFN vs SSM) affect Hyena's ability to learn long-range dependencies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares different parametrizations (Conv1d, FNO, H3, TransferFunc, CKConv, Hyena) and finds FFN-based approaches outperform others on synthetic tasks
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of why certain parametrizations are more effective or how they influence the learned representations
- What evidence would resolve it: An ablation study varying the implicit parametrization in Hyena operators, measuring performance on synthetic and real-world tasks, and analyzing the learned convolution filters

## Limitations

- Scalability to extremely long sequences (>100K tokens) remains unproven and may encounter expressivity limitations
- Hardware-dependent performance gains may not translate across all architectures, particularly non-GPU platforms
- Limited evaluation on structured tasks requiring precise positional relationships where attention's exact interaction patterns may be crucial

## Confidence

**High Confidence**:
- Hyena operators achieve subquadratic complexity through FFT-based convolution algorithms
- The 20% reduction in training compute on The Pile dataset at 355M parameters is empirically validated
- Speedup claims (2x at 8K, 100x at 64K) are supported by runtime measurements

**Medium Confidence**:
- The claim that Hyena "matches" Transformer quality is supported but with a quality gap that persists even at scale
- The assertion that Hyena is an "attention-free" alternative is technically accurate but may understate the need for hybrid approaches in some scenarios

**Low Confidence**:
- Claims about Hyena's ability to replace attention in all scenarios without quality degradation
- The assertion that decoupling sequence length from parameter count is sufficient for matching attention's expressivity
