# A Hybrid Tensor-Expert-Data Parallelism Approach to Optimize Mixture-of-Experts Training

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.06318
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06318
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Framework achieves 26% speedup over baseline when training 40B parameter MoE model (6.7B base model with 16 experts) on 128 V100 GPUs

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of scaling Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models with large base models, which current frameworks struggle to support efficiently. The authors propose a novel three-dimensional hybrid parallel framework that combines tensor, expert, and ZeRO-powered data parallelism to enable training of MoE models with 4-8x larger base models than the state-of-the-art. They identify and resolve a significant memory spike in the optimizer step using a tiled optimizer approach, and eliminate redundant communication in all-to-all and all-reduce operations through duplicate token dropping and communication-aware activation checkpointing optimizations.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a three-dimensional hybrid parallel framework combining tensor, expert, and ZeRO-powered data parallelism. They address memory spikes during optimizer steps through a tiled optimizer that partitions expert parameters into tiles, keeping temporary memory usage constant. They also implement communication optimizations including Duplicate Token Dropping (DTD) to eliminate redundant tokens in all-to-all communication, and Communication-Aware Checkpointing (CAC) to eliminate redundant communication in the second forward pass induced by activation checkpointing.

## Key Results
- Framework enables training of MoE models with 4-8x larger base models compared to DeepSpeed-MoE
- Tiled optimizer eliminates memory spike during optimizer step, enabling training with large base models and/or many experts
- Communication optimizations reduce all-to-all and all-reduce communication time by 42% and 33% respectively
- Achieves 26% speedup over baseline when training 40B parameter MoE model on 128 V100 GPUs
- Improves percentage of peak half-precision throughput from 20% to 27%

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The hybrid tensor-expert-data parallelism framework enables training of MoE models with 4-8x larger base models compared to state-of-the-art.
- Mechanism: By combining tensor parallelism (for sharding non-expert parameters), expert parallelism (for sharding expert parameters), and ZeRO-powered data parallelism (for sharding optimizer states), the framework distributes the computational and memory load across GPUs more efficiently. This allows larger base models to fit within the memory constraints of the available GPUs.
- Core assumption: The base model parameters can be effectively partitioned along the three dimensions without causing significant performance degradation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our new framework can efficiently support the training of MoE models with 4 − 8× larger base models compared to DeepSpeed-MoE"
  - [section 3] "We propose a novel, three-dimensional, hybrid parallel framework that combines tensor, expert, and data parallelism to enable the training of Mixture-of-Experts with large base models"
- Break condition: If the partitioning of parameters along any of the three dimensions becomes highly imbalanced, or if the communication overhead between GPUs becomes prohibitive, the claimed scaling factor may not be achieved.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The tiled optimizer eliminates a significant memory spike during the optimizer step, enabling training of MoEs with large base models and/or a large number of experts.
- Mechanism: In mixed precision training, the optimizer needs to upcast 16-bit gradients to 32-bit gradients, which requires a temporary buffer. When expert and tensor parallelism are combined, the size of this temporary buffer becomes proportional to the number of experts and the base model size, causing a memory spike. The tiled optimizer partitions the expert parameters into tiles and processes them iteratively, keeping the temporary memory usage constant.
- Core assumption: The tile size is chosen such that the overhead of multiple kernel launches is negligible compared to the memory savings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "We propose a novel tiled formulation of the optimizer that strives to alleviate the aforementioned issue"
  - [section 4.1] "For a tile size ts, we now only need 4 × ts bytes of memory to materialize the 32-bit gradients"
- Break condition: If the tile size is chosen too small, the overhead of multiple kernel launches may outweigh the memory savings. If the tile size is too large, the memory spike may not be sufficiently reduced.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Communication optimizations (DTD and CAC) significantly reduce the time spent in collective communication, improving overall training performance.
- Mechanism: The naive combination of tensor and expert parallelism leads to redundant communication. DTD eliminates redundant tokens in the all-to-all communication by dropping duplicates before the communication and reassembling the full input using all-gather afterwards. CAC eliminates redundant communication in the second forward pass induced by activation checkpointing by stashing and reusing the outputs of communication calls.
- Core assumption: The overhead of the additional operations (drop and all-gather for DTD, stashing and reusing for CAC) is less than the savings from reduced communication.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.1] "To resolve this bottleneck, we propose duplicate token dropping (DTD), a communication optimization geared towards eliminating redundant messages in the all-to-all communication"
  - [section 5.2] "To this end, we propose communication-aware checkpointing (CAC), a novel communication optimization that eliminates the additional communication in the second forward pass induced by activation checkpointing"
- Break condition: If the overhead of the additional operations becomes significant compared to the savings from reduced communication, the claimed performance improvements may not be realized.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Tensor parallelism (shards non-expert parameters) -> Expert parallelism (shards expert parameters) -> Data parallelism (shards input batch) -> ZeRO-powered data parallelism (shards optimizer states) -> Tiled optimizer (partitions expert parameters) -> DTD (eliminates redundant tokens) -> CAC (eliminates redundant communication)

- Critical path: The forward pass of an MoE layer involves tensor parallel computation, all-reduce, routing, all-to-all (with DTD), expert parallel computation, all-reduce, and all-to-all (with DTD). The backward pass is similar but in reverse order. The optimizer step involves the tiled computation of the expert parameters.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Increasing the degree of tensor parallelism allows for larger base models but increases the communication overhead.
  - Increasing the degree of expert parallelism allows for more experts but also increases the communication overhead.
  - The tile size in the tiled optimizer needs to be chosen carefully to balance memory savings and kernel launch overhead.

- Failure signatures:
  - If the framework runs out of memory, it may be due to insufficient degrees of parallelism or an inappropriate tile size.
  - If the training performance is poor, it may be due to high communication overhead or an imbalance in the partitioning of parameters.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but the research raises several important considerations about the scalability and limitations of hybrid parallelism approaches for MoE training.

## Limitations

- Memory optimization scope: The tiled optimizer's effectiveness depends heavily on the chosen tile size (1.8M parameters in experiments), which may not be optimal for all hardware configurations.
- Communication overhead assumptions: The DTD and CAC optimizations assume specific patterns of token distribution and communication patterns that may not generalize to all network topologies.
- Performance generalization: The 26% speedup and throughput improvement from 20% to 27% of peak half-precision performance are demonstrated on a specific 40B parameter MoE model configuration and may not generalize to other model sizes.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The core architectural framework combining tensor-expert-data parallelism is well-grounded in established distributed training techniques. The memory spike problem in mixed-precision training with MoE is a real phenomenon, and the tiled optimizer represents a reasonable solution approach.

**Medium confidence**: The specific implementation details of DTD and CAC optimizations, while theoretically sound, may have implementation-specific effects that aren't fully captured in the paper. The claimed 4-8x scaling factor for base model sizes is supported by experimental results but may be sensitive to configuration choices.

**Low confidence**: The absolute performance numbers (26% speedup, 27% peak throughput) are highly specific to the experimental setup and may not generalize to other environments without careful tuning.

## Next Checks
