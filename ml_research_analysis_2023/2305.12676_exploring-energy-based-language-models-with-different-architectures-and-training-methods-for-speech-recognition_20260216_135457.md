# Exploring Energy-based Language Models with Different Architectures and Training Methods for Speech Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.12676
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12676
- Reference count: 0
- Key outcome: Energy-based language models (ELMs) with Dynamic NCE training and Hidden2Scalar architecture achieve competitive results with fine-tuned GPT2 and significantly outperform fine-tuned BERT for speech recognition rescoring.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates energy-based language models (ELMs) for speech recognition rescoring, addressing limitations of autoregressive models like exposure bias. The study explores three ELM architectures—SumTargetLogit, Hidden2Scalar, and SumTokenLogit—trained with three methods: Noise Contrastive Estimate (NCE), Dynamic NCE (DNCE), and Maximum Likelihood Estimate (MLE). Using large pretrained models (BERT, GPT2) as backbones, experiments on AISHELL-1 and WenetSpeech datasets show that ELMs with DNCE training and Hidden2Scalar architecture achieve competitive performance with fine-tuned GPT2 while significantly outperforming fine-tuned BERT. The research also demonstrates that ELMs provide better confidence calibration than fine-tuned GPT2 models.

## Method Summary
The paper implements energy-based language models using pretrained transformers (BERT for bidirectional, GPT2 for unidirectional) as backbones, with three distinct architectures for computing sentence energies. Training employs NCE and its dynamic variant DNCE, which adapts the noise distribution during training, as well as MLE with importance sampling. The models are evaluated on speech recognition rescoring tasks using n-best lists from Conformer-RNNT ASR systems on Mandarin datasets. The study compares ELM performance against fine-tuned BERT and GPT2 baselines, measuring character error rate (CER) and confidence calibration metrics.

## Key Results
- ELMs with Hidden2Scalar architecture and DNCE training achieve CER comparable to fine-tuned GPT2 and significantly better than fine-tuned BERT
- Bidirectional ELM architectures (Hidden2Scalar, SumTokenLogit) outperform unidirectional SumTargetLogit architecture
- ELMs demonstrate superior confidence calibration performance compared to fine-tuned GPT2 models
- Dynamic NCE training provides consistent improvements over standard NCE across all architectures

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Dynamic NCE (DNCE) outperforms standard NCE by adaptively updating the noise distribution during training through maximum likelihood optimization. This dynamic adaptation forces the ELM to learn more discriminative energy functions as the noise distribution becomes increasingly difficult to distinguish from the data distribution.

Core assumption: The noise distribution needs to closely match the data distribution for effective contrastive learning, and a static noise distribution cannot adapt to the evolving ELM during training.

### Mechanism 2
Bidirectional architectures (Hidden2Scalar and SumTokenLogit) outperform unidirectional architectures (SumTargetLogit) by capturing richer contextual information from both directions using BERT-based encoders. This bidirectional context provides more discriminative features for scoring sentences than unidirectional context.

Core assumption: Bidirectional context provides more discriminative features for scoring sentences than unidirectional context, and this advantage outweighs the computational overhead of bidirectional processing.

### Mechanism 3
ELMs provide better confidence calibration than fine-tuned GPT2 by directly modeling unnormalized probability distributions rather than relying on local normalization. This global normalization approach avoids exposure bias and label bias problems inherent in autoregressive models with local normalization.

Core assumption: The global normalization in ELMs provides more accurate probability estimates than the local normalization in autoregressive models, especially for long sequences where local normalization can accumulate errors.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: ELM architecture (SumTargetLogit/Hidden2Scalar/SumTokenLogit) -> Training method (NCE/DNCE/MLE) -> Backbone PLM (BERT/GPT2) -> Noise/proposal distribution (fine-tuned GPT2) -> n-best lists from ASR system -> CER and confidence calibration metrics

- Critical path: 1) Choose architecture based on computational constraints and bidirectional context needs, 2) Select training method based on dataset size and sampling capabilities, 3) Initialize backbone PLMs and noise/proposal distribution, 4) Train with chosen method while monitoring convergence and confidence calibration.

- Design tradeoffs: SumTargetLogit is computationally efficient but limited to unidirectional context; Hidden2Scalar captures bidirectional context but requires BERT and is more computationally intensive; SumTokenLogit is a compromise using BERT's MLM head but may not fully leverage bidirectional context. NCE is simpler but DNCE adapts better to training dynamics. MLE provides maximum likelihood estimates but is computationally expensive and unstable.

- Failure signatures: Training loss not decreasing or becoming negative infinity suggests issues with sampling or learning rate; poor rescoring performance despite training convergence may indicate architecture mismatch with task; overfitting to training data suggests need for stronger regularization or data augmentation.

- First 3 experiments: 1) Implement and train SumTargetLogit with NCE on AISHELL-1 to establish baseline performance and verify implementation correctness, 2) Compare SumTargetLogit with Hidden2Scalar using NCE to evaluate bidirectional context benefits, 3) Train best-performing architecture with DNCE to assess adaptive noise distribution advantages.

## Limitations

- Comparison to fine-tuned BERT and GPT2 baselines lacks detailed training information, making it difficult to assess whether the comparison is truly apples-to-apples
- Computational complexity of different ELM architectures and training methods is not thoroughly characterized
- Implementation specifics of DNCE training procedure (noise distribution update scheduling, hyperparameter settings) are not fully specified
- Experiments limited to Mandarin datasets, restricting generalizability to other languages or domains

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: ELM architectures are correctly defined and implementable; mathematical formulations for energy functions and training objectives are sound; claim that ELMs can be trained using pretrained models as backbones is well-supported.

**Medium Confidence**: Claim that DNCE outperforms NCE due to adaptive noise distribution is plausible but exact mechanism not fully elucidated; observation that ELMs provide better confidence calibration is supported but practical significance not quantified; claim that bidirectional architectures outperform unidirectional ones is supported but computational tradeoffs not thoroughly analyzed.

**Low Confidence**: Claim that ELMs achieve "competitive results" with fine-tuned GPT2 may be overstated given higher CER; assertion that MLE with importance sampling is "unstable" lacks detailed diagnostic information; claim about superiority of Hidden2Scalar over SumTokenLogit is based on limited comparisons.
