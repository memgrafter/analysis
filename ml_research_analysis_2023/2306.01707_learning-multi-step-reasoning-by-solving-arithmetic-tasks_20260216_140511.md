# Learning Multi-Step Reasoning by Solving Arithmetic Tasks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01707
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01707
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 6
- Key outcome: Pre-training on Multi-step Arithmetic Task (MSAT) dataset improves language models' math reasoning abilities, with adapter-tuning outperforming full fine-tuning on downstream tasks.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how to enhance moderate-sized language models with multi-step reasoning capabilities for solving mathematical problems. The authors propose a method that pre-trains language models on a synthetic dataset, MSAT, to learn multi-step reasoning skills. Through experiments on four math word problem datasets, the method demonstrates effectiveness in improving language models' math reasoning abilities, particularly when using digit tokenization and code-style multi-step reasoning formats.

## Method Summary
The method involves two main stages: pre-training on the synthetic MSAT dataset using adapter-tuning, followed by fine-tuning on downstream math word problem (MWP) tasks. The MSAT dataset is generated using predefined equation templates and consists of 80,000 training examples and 5,000 evaluation examples. During pre-training, models use digit tokenization for numbers and generate output in a code-style multi-step reasoning format. The adapter-tuning approach is used to mitigate catastrophic forgetting while preserving the original language model's capabilities.

## Key Results
- Models pre-trained with MSAT outperform symbolic mask baselines on all four math word problem datasets.
- Adapter-tuning achieves better performance on downstream MWP tasks compared to full fine-tuning.
- The method shows improved robustness in handling out-of-distribution numbers.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Digit tokenization improves numerical comprehension for moderate-sized LMs.
- Mechanism: By splitting numbers digit-by-digit, the model receives consistent token representations for numbers, eliminating the ambiguity caused by subword tokenization like BPE that splits numbers based on frequency in the corpus.
- Core assumption: The numerical reasoning ability of LMs is hindered by inconsistent tokenization of numbers.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "Such inconsistent tokenization strategy for numbers undermines LM's numerical understanding ability. Hence, we tokenize numbers digit-by-digit for both pre-training and fine-tuning."
  - [abstract]: "To make sure LMs capture the information carried by the numbers, we keep the numbers in the questions instead of masking them during both pre-training and fine-tuning."
- Break condition: If the model still shows poor numerical comprehension even with digit tokenization, it suggests that the issue lies deeper in the model architecture or pre-training methodology rather than tokenization alone.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Multi-step reasoning skills are acquired through structured code-style output expressions.
- Mechanism: By generating intermediate reasoning steps in a code-like format, the model learns to break down complex arithmetic problems into simpler steps, improving its ability to handle multi-step reasoning tasks.
- Core assumption: Producing intermediate reasoning steps during pre-training is essential for learning multi-step reasoning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "The output sequence of MSAT is constructed in a code-style multi-step reasoning format. Each step consists of two sub-steps: variable assignment and calculation."
  - [section]: "We can observe that models with math expressions perform consistently worse than models with code-style multi-step reasoning format on both pre-training task MSAT and MWP solving task SV AMP."
- Break condition: If the model fails to generalize to new arithmetic problems or if the performance on multi-step reasoning tasks does not improve with code-style outputs, it indicates that the structured format is not effectively teaching the model the intended reasoning skills.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adapter-tuning mitigates catastrophic forgetting while preserving the original LM's language capabilities.
- Mechanism: By attaching adapter modules to each layer of the LM encoder and fixing the LM's parameters during pre-training, the model can learn new reasoning skills without losing its pre-existing language understanding.
- Core assumption: Fine-tuning all parameters of the LM on synthetic data that are different from the pre-training corpus can lead to catastrophic forgetting.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "We adopt a two-stage tuning strategy (Wang and Lu, 2022) to inject reasoning skills into LMs. Specifically, we perform adapter-tuning (Houlsby et al., 2019) on MSAT and then jointly fine-tune adapter and LM backbone on downstream tasks."
  - [section]: "Both full fine-tuning and adapter-tuning can achieve good performance on MSAT, but adapter-tuning outperforms fine-tuning on all downstream MWP datasets, which demonstrates the benefits of performing adapter-tuning on MSAT."
- Break condition: If the model's performance on language understanding tasks degrades significantly after adapter-tuning, it suggests that the adapters are not effectively preserving the original LM's capabilities.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Numerical comprehension in language models.
  - Why needed here: The model must understand numbers to perform arithmetic tasks accurately.
  - Quick check question: Can the model correctly parse and process numbers when they are presented in different formats (e.g., "520" vs. "5 20")?
- Concept: Multi-step reasoning.
  - Why needed here: The model needs to decompose complex problems into simpler steps to solve them effectively.
  - Quick check question: Can the model generate a step-by-step solution for a given arithmetic problem, breaking it down into manageable parts?
- Concept: Adapter-tuning.
  - Why needed here: The model needs to learn new skills without losing its pre-existing language capabilities.
  - Quick check question: Does the model maintain its performance on language understanding tasks after adapter-tuning?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → Encoder → Adapter modules → Decoder → Output
- Critical path: Input questions are encoded, processed through adapter modules, and decoded into step-by-step reasoning solutions.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using digit tokenization vs. masking numbers with symbolic tokens.
  - Choosing between Seq2Seq and DAG structured models.
  - Deciding on the difficulty level of the MSAT dataset.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor numerical comprehension despite digit tokenization.
  - Inability to generalize to new arithmetic problems.
  - Degradation in language understanding tasks after adapter-tuning.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare the performance of models with digit tokenization vs. symbolic masks on a simple arithmetic task.
  2. Evaluate the effectiveness of code-style vs. math expression outputs in multi-step reasoning tasks.
  3. Assess the impact of adapter-tuning vs. full fine-tuning on the model's performance on downstream MWP tasks.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How would the performance of the MSAT pre-training method change if different types of operators (e.g., sum(), max()) were included in the arithmetic tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the current method only considers binary operators (+, -, ×, ÷) and suggests that introducing other non-binary operators supported by Python could be an interesting research direction.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experimental results or analysis on the impact of including additional types of operators in the MSAT tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of models pre-trained with and without additional non-binary operators in the MSAT tasks, using the same evaluation metrics and datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Would using beam search decoding instead of greedy decoding significantly improve the performance of the models on the MWP tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that all results are reported using greedy decoding and mentions that using beam search for symbolic neural reasoners can be challenging due to the exponential increase in search space.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide experimental results comparing the performance of models using greedy decoding versus beam search decoding.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments comparing the performance of models using greedy decoding versus beam search decoding on the MWP tasks, with the same pre-training and fine-tuning procedures.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the difficulty level of the MSAT tasks affect the transfer learning performance on downstream MWP tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the difficulty level of a reasoning task is defined as the average number of reasoning steps required to solve the problems and shows that pre-training on harder MSAT tasks leads to better results on downstream tasks.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed analysis of how different difficulty levels of MSAT tasks impact the transfer learning performance on downstream MWP tasks.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments training models on MSAT tasks with varying difficulty levels and evaluating their performance on downstream MWP tasks, with a focus on the relationship between task difficulty and transfer learning performance.

## Limitations

- The MSAT dataset generation process relies on predefined equation templates, which may not fully capture the diversity of real-world math problems.
- The study focuses on moderate-sized language models, leaving open questions about scalability to larger models.
- While adapter-tuning shows promise in preventing catastrophic forgetting, the long-term stability of the learned reasoning skills across diverse tasks remains to be thoroughly evaluated.

## Confidence

**High Confidence:**
- The effectiveness of digit tokenization in improving numerical comprehension for moderate-sized LMs is supported by consistent experimental results across multiple datasets.
- The superiority of code-style multi-step reasoning format over math expressions in teaching multi-step reasoning skills is well-demonstrated.

**Medium Confidence:**
- The claim that adapter-tuning effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting while preserving original LM capabilities is supported by experimental results but requires further validation across a broader range of tasks.

**Low Confidence:**
- The assertion that the proposed method enhances robustness in handling out-of-distribution numbers is based on limited experimental evidence and requires more comprehensive testing.

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Domain Generalization Test**: Evaluate the pre-trained models on math word problems from domains not represented in the MSAT dataset to assess their ability to generalize reasoning skills to unseen problem types.

2. **Long-Term Stability Analysis**: Conduct a longitudinal study to track the model's performance on both math reasoning tasks and language understanding tasks over extended periods and across multiple fine-tuning cycles to confirm the effectiveness of adapter-tuning in preventing catastrophic forgetting.

3. **Ablation Study on MSAT Dataset Complexity**: Systematically vary the complexity and diversity of the MSAT dataset by adjusting the equation templates and analyze the impact on the model's performance on downstream MWP tasks to determine the optimal dataset characteristics for effective pre-training.
