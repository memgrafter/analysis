# Level Generation Through Large Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.05817
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05817
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 31
- Key outcome: GPT-2 trained on Boxoban dataset achieves score of 0.53, GPT-3 trained on Microban dataset achieves score of 0.51

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the use of large language models (LLMs) for procedural content generation of Sokoban game levels. The authors find that LLMs are capable of generating novel, playable, and diverse Sokoban levels, with performance scaling dramatically with dataset size. Specifically, GPT-2 trained on the full Boxoban dataset achieves a score of 0.53, while GPT-3 trained on a small Microban dataset achieves a score of 0.51. The authors also find that LLMs can be controlled to generate levels with specified characteristics, such as the proportion of empty space, using simple data augmentation and prompting techniques. However, the models struggle with more complex characteristics like solution length.

## Method Summary
The authors train GPT-2 and GPT-3 models on annotated Sokoban levels, represented as ASCII grids. They use beam search with sampling to generate levels and evaluate them for novelty, playability, and diversity. For controllability experiments, they annotate the dataset with desired characteristics and train new models to generate levels that match specified prompts. Hyperparameter sweeps over temperature, top-p, and beam size are conducted to optimize performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Large language models can generate functional game levels by learning to predict spatial sequences encoded as text.
- Mechanism: LLMs treat game levels as linear token sequences, leveraging their ability to capture long-range dependencies through attention mechanisms. By training on large datasets of valid levels, the model learns to generate new levels that are novel, playable, and diverse.
- Core assumption: Spatial relationships and functional constraints of game levels can be preserved when levels are represented as sequences of tokens.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Game levels, with their complex functional constraints and spatial relationships in more than one dimension, are very different from the kinds of data an LLM typically sees during training."
  - [section 3] "A level can easily be represented as a grid of ASCII characters, where each character is mapped to either: a wall, an empty space, the player, a box, a goal, a box on top of a goal, or a player on top of a goal."
- Break condition: If spatial dependencies are too complex or local, token-based representation may fail to capture necessary constraints, leading to invalid or unplayable levels.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Model performance scales with dataset size, improving novelty and playability.
- Mechanism: Larger training datasets provide more examples of valid level structures, enabling the model to learn a broader distribution of playable levels. This leads to higher novelty and playability in generated levels.
- Core assumption: The diversity and quality of generated levels directly correlate with the amount and variety of training data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.2] "We see effects of sampling levels conditioned on simple prompts... in the conditions where GPT-2 failed to produce accurate levels, it nonetheless continued to generally produce novel and playable ones."
  - [section 7.2] "The results in Table 2 and Table 3 indicate that dataset size is indeed an important factor for an LLM's ability to generate game levels."
- Break condition: Beyond a certain dataset size, additional data may not improve performance due to diminishing returns or overfitting to specific level patterns.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Controllability through natural language prompts is achievable for observable level characteristics.
- Mechanism: By annotating training data with desired characteristics (e.g., proportion of empty space), the model learns to generate levels that match specified prompts. This leverages the model's ability to generalize from annotated examples.
- Core assumption: The model can effectively learn and apply constraints specified in natural language prompts during generation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 6.3] "For this experiment, we focus on two annotated characteristics: the proportion of empty space... and the solution length."
  - [section 7.3] "In the first row, we see that the GPT-2 model is able to produce levels that are novel, playable, and within a single tile of the specified proportion of empty space."
- Break condition: If the characteristic is too complex or not directly observable (e.g., solution length), the model may fail to generate levels that match the prompt accurately.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Why unresolved: The paper only tests LLMs on Sokoban levels and does not explore their ability to generate levels for other games or adapt to new level types.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the impact of different data augmentation techniques on LLM performance for game level generation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that simple data augmentation approaches like flipping and rotating levels improved LLM performance, but suggest there is room for more sophisticated techniques.
- Why unresolved: The paper only explores basic augmentation methods and does not investigate the full potential of data augmentation for improving LLM level generation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative experiments testing various data augmentation techniques, such as combining levels from different games, introducing noise, or using more complex transformations, and evaluating their impact on LLM performance.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we improve the controllability of LLMs for game level generation, particularly for complex characteristics like solution length?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors find that LLMs can be controlled to generate levels with specified characteristics like empty space proportion using simple prompting, but struggle with more complex characteristics like solution length.
- Why unresolved: The paper demonstrates limited success in controlling LLM outputs for complex level characteristics and suggests that more sophisticated techniques are needed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments testing advanced control methods, such as reinforcement learning-based decoding or prompt engineering, to improve LLM controllability for complex level characteristics.

## Limitations
- The exact tokenizer configuration for the untrained GPT-2 model is not specified, which could affect how spatial relationships are captured in the generated levels.
- The specific hyperparameter values for the limited GPT-3 evaluation sweep and training configuration are not provided, introducing potential variability in model performance.
- The evaluation metrics, while reasonable, may not fully capture the nuances of level quality and playability.

## Confidence
- **High**: LLMs can generate novel, playable, and diverse Sokoban levels, with performance scaling with dataset size. This is supported by extensive experiments and evaluations across different models and datasets.
- **Medium**: Controllability through natural language prompts is achievable for observable level characteristics. While the study demonstrates success for simple characteristics like proportion of empty space, the results for more complex characteristics like solution length are less conclusive.
- **Low**: The study's findings can be directly generalized to other game genres or level types. The specific focus on Sokoban levels and the unique characteristics of this domain may limit the applicability of the results to other contexts.
