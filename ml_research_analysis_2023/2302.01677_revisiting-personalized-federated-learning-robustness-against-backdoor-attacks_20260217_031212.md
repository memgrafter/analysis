# Revisiting Personalized Federated Learning: Robustness Against Backdoor Attacks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.01677
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.01677
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Partial model-sharing pFL methods (FedBN, FedRep) achieve <10% backdoor success rate, while full model-sharing pFL methods lack robustness

## Executive Summary
This paper conducts a comprehensive empirical evaluation of personalized federated learning (pFL) methods against backdoor attacks. The study systematically examines six pFL algorithms across four backdoor attack types on FEMNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets, totaling 600 experiments. The key finding reveals that pFL methods employing partial model-sharing architectures (FedBN, FedRep) demonstrate significantly improved robustness against backdoor attacks compared to full model-sharing approaches. Additionally, the authors propose Simple-Tuning, a lightweight post-training defense mechanism that empirically enhances backdoor defense performance across evaluated pFL methods.

## Method Summary
The research evaluates six personalized federated learning methods: FedAvg, FedPer, LG-FedAvg, FedRep, Per-FedAvg, and FedBN. The experimental framework tests these algorithms against four backdoor attack variants (BadNets, Blended, SIG, WaNet) across two datasets (FEMNIST, CIFAR-10). Each pFL method undergoes training under attack conditions, followed by defense evaluation using Simple-Tuning. The study employs a white-box attack assumption where adversaries possess full knowledge of the pFL algorithms and can manipulate client data during training.

## Key Results
- Partial model-sharing pFL methods (FedBN, FedRep) achieve backdoor success rates below 10% for some attacks
- Full model-sharing pFL methods show no significant robustness against backdoor attacks
- Simple-Tuning defense method provides empirical improvements in backdoor defense performance
- Comprehensive evaluation across 600 experiments demonstrates consistent patterns in attack vulnerability

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of partial model-sharing approaches stems from their architectural design that isolates client-specific parameters while sharing only task-relevant components. This separation limits the propagation of backdoor triggers through the shared model components, as the malicious patterns cannot effectively influence the personalized layers that directly impact classification decisions. The Simple-Tuning defense leverages post-training fine-tuning on clean data to overwrite the backdoor patterns that may have been embedded during federated training.

## Architecture Onboarding
Component map: Clients -> FL Server -> Global Model -> Personalized Models
Critical path: Client local training → Model aggregation → Personalization → Defense evaluation
Design tradeoffs: Full sharing maximizes knowledge transfer but increases attack surface; partial sharing reduces attack surface but may limit global knowledge
Failure signatures: High backdoor success rate indicates ineffective sharing strategy; low personalization accuracy suggests over-constrained sharing
First experiments: 1) Baseline FedAvg attack evaluation, 2) FedBN robustness testing, 3) Simple-Tuning ablation study

## Limitations
- Evaluation focuses primarily on computer vision tasks, limiting generalizability to other domains
- White-box attack assumption may overestimate vulnerability compared to black-box scenarios
- Defense analysis emphasizes post-training mitigation without exploring preventive training-phase strategies

## Confidence
High confidence in experimental results due to extensive validation across 600 experiments and systematic comparison methodology.
