# The African Stopwords project: curating stopwords for African languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.12155
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.12155
- Reference count: 8
- Authors: Not specified in input
- One-line primary result: Project to curate standardized stopwords for African languages using statistical methods and human evaluation

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the African Stopwords project, which aims to address the underrepresentation of African languages in NLP toolkits by curating standardized stopwords. The project has collected stopwords for ten African languages with varying numbers per language and plans to expand this work by leveraging monolingual data from multiple domains. Using statistical methods like TF-IDF and entropy to automatically identify candidate stopwords, followed by human evaluation, the project seeks to create more inclusive language processing tools that improve model performance and efficiency for African languages.

## Method Summary
The project employs a multi-step approach: first gathering monolingual data from multiple domains, then using statistical methods (TF-IDF, entropy, information gain, and Kullback-Leibler divergence) to automatically identify candidate stopwords, followed by human evaluators reviewing the results, and finally publishing the validated stopwords. The methodology combines automated statistical analysis with human validation to ensure the stopwords are appropriate for each language's specific characteristics and domain contexts.

## Key Results
- Collected stopwords for ten African languages (Afrikaans, Hausa, Nigerian Pidgin, isiZulu, kiSwahili, Kirundi, Kinyarwanda, Somali, and Sesotho)
- Current stopwords lists range from 16 to 571 stopwords per language
- Plan to use TF-IDF, entropy, and other statistical methods for automatic identification
- Implementation of human evaluation process before open-sourcing final lists

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Using statistical methods like TF-IDF and entropy enables automated identification of stopwords from monolingual data.
- Mechanism: The statistical methods assign low scores to high-frequency, low-information words, which are likely stopwords.
- Core assumption: High-frequency words with low discriminative power in a language corpus are stopwords.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "using statistical methods (such as TF-IDF and entropy) to automatically identify candidate stopwords"
  - [section] "Research on stopwords identiﬁcation have employed various statistical metrics, such as term-frequency-inverse-document-frequency (TF-IDF)... entropy... We plan to use these statistical methods too to automatically identify candidate stopwords"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence, but TF-IDF and entropy are well-established methods in NLP
- Break condition: If domain-specific words appear frequently enough to be misclassified as stopwords by statistical methods

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Human evaluation ensures the automatically curated stopwords align with language-specific characteristics.
- Mechanism: Human evaluators review the statistically identified candidate stopwords to validate their status as true stopwords for the target language.
- Core assumption: Human language experts can reliably distinguish between stopwords and domain-specific high-frequency words.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "having human evaluators review the results before open-sourcing the final lists"
  - [section] "As a ﬁnal step in ensuring the automatically curated stopwor ds are actually in line with the language, we will employ a number of human evaluators to review our stopwords. Only the stopwords that pass the evaluation w ill be published"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence, but human evaluation is standard practice in language resource development
- Break condition: If human evaluators cannot reach consensus on borderline cases or if there's bias in evaluation

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Creating language-specific stopwords improves NLP model performance and efficiency for African languages.
- Mechanism: Removing language-appropriate stopwords reduces token count and noise in training data, improving model training time and performance.
- Core assumption: Stopwords that are specific to a language and domain have more impact than generic stopwords lists.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "addresses the gap in NLP resources for African languages and contributes to more inclusive language processing tools"
  - [section] "However, stopword removal is highly dependent on the language, domain and task... Therefore, the use of one 'standard' stopword list is proble matic because it ignores the domain-knowledge speciﬁcity of stopwords"
  - [corpus] No direct corpus evidence, but supported by research on domain-specific stopwords cited in the paper
- Break condition: If the language has minimal stopwords or if stopwords removal actually harms downstream task performance

  - Balancing breadth (many languages) vs depth (quality per language)

  - High disagreement among human evaluators

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test TF-IDF method on English corpus and compare results with established English stopwords list
  2. Apply statistical methods to a small African language dataset and manually verify results
  3. Conduct human evaluation with 5-10 evaluators on a sample of candidate stopwords to establish inter-rater reliability

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the optimal statistical methods for automatically identifying candidate stopwords in African languages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using TF-IDF, entropy, information gain, and Kullback-Leibler divergence as statistical methods to identify candidate stopwords
- Why unresolved: The paper states they "plan to use these statistical methods" but does not provide results or comparison of their effectiveness for African languages
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis showing which statistical methods perform best for African languages, including precision/recall metrics and domain-specific effectiveness

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do domain-specific variations affect the effectiveness of stopwords in African languages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that "stopword removal is highly dependent on the language, domain and task" and references research showing domain-specific stopwords can make significant impact
- Why unresolved: While the paper acknowledges this dependency, it does not investigate how stopwords vary across different domains (news, social media, academic texts, etc.) for African languages
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing stopwords effectiveness across multiple domains for African languages, showing how the optimal stopword list varies by domain

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the relationship between stopwords in African languages and their cross-linguistic influences?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The abstract states that "Stopwords in the context of African languages are understudied and can reveal information about the crossover between languages"
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions this as a research opportunity but does not investigate the linguistic relationships or shared stopwords between African languages
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative linguistic analysis identifying shared stopwords across African languages and documenting the historical or cultural reasons for these overlaps

## Limitations

- The project is currently in proposal stage with no demonstrated results for African languages
- Limited to ten African languages with varying quality and completeness of stopwords lists
- Effectiveness of proposed statistical methods specifically for African languages is unproven
- Human evaluation process lacks specification of evaluator qualifications and evaluation criteria

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The general approach of using statistical methods combined with human evaluation is a standard and reasonable methodology for stopword identification. The motivation for creating language-specific stopwords is well-supported by NLP literature.

**Medium Confidence**: The specific application of TF-IDF and entropy to African languages is theoretically sound but unproven. The claim that this will improve NLP model performance for African languages is plausible but not yet demonstrated.

**Low Confidence**: The effectiveness of the entire pipeline (data collection → statistical analysis → human evaluation → integration) cannot be assessed without implementation details and evaluation results. The paper does not provide evidence that the proposed methods will work well for the target languages.

## Next Checks

3. **Domain impact study**: Test the effect of language-specific stopwords on a simple NLP task (like text classification) using one African language, comparing performance with generic stopwords versus language-specific ones.
