# Identification of Novel Classes for Improving Few-Shot Object Detection

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.10422
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10422
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- This work addresses few-shot object detection when unlabeled novel objects appear in training data, achieving state-of-the-art performance on PASCAL VOC and COCO datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of few-shot object detection (FSOD) when unlabeled objects from unseen novel classes appear in training data, which degrades model performance. The authors propose a hierarchical ternary classification region proposal network (HTRPN) that identifies and utilizes these unlabeled novel objects as positive samples during training. Their method includes an improved hierarchical sampling strategy for the RPN that balances object perception across different sizes, and integrates contrastive learning to better distinguish between different objectness categories. Experimental results show their approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on PASCAL VOC and COCO datasets, particularly excelling in lower-shot scenarios.

## Method Summary
The method implements a two-stage training procedure using Faster R-CNN with ResNet-101 and FPN backbone. First, the model pre-trains on abundant base class annotations, then fine-tunes on both base and novel classes. The key innovation is the HTRPN that identifies unlabeled novel objects through hierarchical ternary classification with a 0.75 threshold, combined with balanced anchor sampling across feature pyramid layers (p2-p6) and contrastive learning on objectness scores. The approach modifies the RPN to detect potential novel objects and assign them a separate objectness label, reducing confusion between labeled and unlabeled objects.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art performance on PASCAL VOC and COCO datasets
- Shows significant improvement in lower-shot scenarios (1-3 shots)
- Outperforms existing methods particularly in novel class detection accuracy

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Hierarchical ternary classification RPN identifies unlabeled novel objects by detecting proposals with high classification scores for known classes despite being labeled as background
- Mechanism: The RPN's feature map shows strong attention to unlabeled novel objects, causing them to be predicted as similar known classes. By applying instance-level sub-classification with a threshold (Thre cls), these objects are detected and assigned a new objectness label (tobjgt = 2) separate from true objects (1) and background (0)
- Core assumption: Novel unseen objects have feature similarity to base/seen classes, causing the model to predict them as those classes despite missing labels
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "unlabeled novel objects... appear in the background... These objects behave similarly to label noise, leading to FSOD performance degradation"
  - [section 4.1] "Our experiments demonstrate that this phenomenon exists in PASCAL VOC [4] and COCO [21] datasets... We have observed the fact that the network often has clear attention to the potential objects in the RPN layer"
  - [section 4.2] "We hypothesize that this ability is because of the feature similarity between the features of some novel unseen classes and the base classes"
- Break condition: If novel unseen objects have no feature similarity to base/seen classes, the model cannot detect them as known classes, making this mechanism ineffective

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Hierarchical sampling strategy balances anchor selection across feature pyramid levels to ensure large object detection
- Mechanism: Instead of random anchor selection from all feature maps, HSamp equally distributes negative anchors across all five feature layers (p2-p6), ensuring large objects in higher layers get sampled
- Core assumption: Original random sampling under-samples large anchors in higher feature layers due to fewer total anchors at those levels
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "to improve the training speed, not all of the anchors are used for determining proposal boxes... this random selection process dramatically reduces the chance to get desired negative anchors for large objects in higher feature maps"
  - [section 4.1] "We equally assign them to each feature layer so that each layer will have around m/5 anchors... the anchors in each feature layer would share the same possibility for being selected"
  - [Figure 4 caption] "when HSamp is used, the anchors are more balanced for each layer and the large objects are more likely to be contained"
- Break condition: If the number of required negative anchors is too small relative to feature map size, even balanced sampling may not capture all object sizes

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Contrastive learning on objectness improves inter-class separation between object categories in RPN proposals
- Mechanism: Encodes cropped proposal features into 128-dimensional vectors, then applies contrastive loss to increase cosine similarity between proposals with same objectness label and decrease similarity between different labels
- Core assumption: Better feature separation between objectness categories leads to more accurate objectness classification
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.3] "To further increase the inter-class distances between Aa, At n, and Ap subsets in HTRPN, we also include an objectness contrastive learning head"
  - [section 4.3] "the cosine similarity scores are measured between every two proposals... would give a higher objectness score"
  - [section 4.4] "The global total loss is composed of... our ternary objectness loss LT obj in Equation. 2 is a sum of the cross entropy objectness loss (LObj) and ternary RPN feature contrastive learning loss (LT con)"
- Break condition: If feature representations are already well-separated, additional contrastive loss may provide diminishing returns or could even harm performance

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Feature Pyramid Network (p2-p6 layers) → Hierarchical Ternary RPN with HSamp and Ternary Objectness Classification → RoI Pooling → Instance Classification and Bounding Box Regression → Contrastive Learning Head
- Critical path: Input image → FPN feature extraction → Hierarchical Ternary RPN (with HSamp and ternary classification) → Top proposals selection (combining tobjpre_1 and tobjpre_2) → RoI Pooling → Final detection
- Design tradeoffs: Ternary classification adds complexity but enables detection of unlabeled novel objects; HSamp improves coverage but may slow training slightly; contrastive learning adds parameter overhead but improves objectness accuracy
- Failure signatures: Poor nAP despite good bAP indicates failure to identify novel objects; low bAP during fine-tuning indicates catastrophic forgetting; high false positives indicates poor objectness classification
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement HSamp alone on baseline model to verify large object detection improvement
  2. Add ternary classification with fixed threshold to verify novel object identification
  3. Add contrastive learning to verify objectness classification improvement

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal threshold value (Thre cls) for identifying potential novel objects across different datasets and few-shot settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions using Thre cls = 0.75 and shows results for values 0.05, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.95, but notes this was not extensively tuned
- Why unresolved: The paper states these hyperparameters were not fine-tuned and only provides results for a single dataset/scenario
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic ablation studies across multiple datasets (VOC, COCO), shot settings (1-shot to 10-shot), and object categories would identify optimal thresholds

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the performance of HTRPN scale with larger numbers of novel classes beyond the 5-class setup used in experiments?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper only evaluates on splits with 5 novel classes, leaving scalability to larger class sets untested
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate scenarios with significantly more novel classes, which would be important for real-world applications
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating HTRPN with 10, 20, or more novel classes would demonstrate scalability limits and performance degradation patterns

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the hierarchical ternary classification approach be extended to zero-shot learning scenarios where novel objects are completely unlabeled and unseen during training?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions "Future works include extensions to identify novel unseen classes in a zero-shot learning setting" in the conclusions
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on semi-supervised detection of potential novel objects that appear during training, not completely unseen objects
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments adapting HTRPN to detect and classify objects from classes that never appear in training data would validate zero-shot extensions

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the computational overhead introduced by HTRPN compared to standard Faster R-CNN, and how does it affect real-time detection performance?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions using 4 GPUs but does not provide inference speed comparisons or computational complexity analysis
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows accuracy improvements, it does not quantify the trade-off between performance gains and increased computational requirements
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed timing comparisons showing FPS/FLOPs differences between standard Faster R-CNN and HTRPN across different hardware configurations would clarify the computational trade-offs

## Limitations
- Performance heavily depends on feature similarity between base and novel classes, limiting effectiveness for highly dissimilar object categories
- The 0.75 threshold for instance-level classification appears arbitrary and may require dataset-specific tuning
- Computational overhead from contrastive learning and hierarchical sampling could limit practical deployment in resource-constrained settings

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The existence of unlabeled novel objects in training data and their negative impact on FSOD performance (supported by observational evidence)
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of hierarchical ternary classification in identifying novel objects (limited ablation studies)
- **Low Confidence**: The generalizability of the 0.75 threshold and contrastive learning benefits across diverse datasets
