# Delving into the Adversarial Robustness of Federated Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09479
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09479
- Reference count: 11
- Primary result: DBFAT achieves significant improvements in both clean accuracy and robust accuracy against various attacks compared to state-of-the-art baselines in federated learning

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the adversarial robustness of federated learning systems, revealing that directly adopting adversarial training significantly hurts test accuracy, especially under non-IID data settings. The authors propose Decision Boundary based Federated Adversarial Training (DBFAT), a novel algorithm that improves both accuracy and robustness through local re-weighting and global regularization. Extensive experiments on multiple datasets demonstrate that DBFAT consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines under both IID and non-IID settings.

## Method Summary
The paper addresses the challenge of applying adversarial training in federated learning, which typically degrades clean accuracy particularly under non-IID data distributions. DBFAT introduces two key mechanisms: (1) local re-weighting of adversarial examples based on their estimated distance to the decision boundary, and (2) global regularization using logits from the aggregated global model to align local decision boundaries. The method is evaluated across multiple benchmark datasets including MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-12 under both IID and non-IID settings.

## Key Results
- DBFAT consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines under both IID and non-IID settings
- Achieves significant improvements in both clean accuracy and robust accuracy against various attacks including FGSM, MIM, PGD, CW, and AutoAttack
- Effectively addresses the accuracy drop issue associated with standard adversarial training in federated learning
- Demonstrates robust performance across multiple datasets from MNIST to ImageNet-12

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adversarial training directly in FL degrades clean accuracy, especially under non-IID data.
- Mechanism: Non-IID data causes imbalanced adversarial examples across clients, leading to biased decision boundaries and poor model aggregation.
- Core assumption: The number of adversarial training epochs needed to achieve near-zero error is higher than for clean data, amplifying the impact of data imbalance.
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "directly adopting adversarial training in FL significantly hurts test accuracy, especially under non-IID data settings." [section] "we examine the performance of both clean accuracy and robustness on a randomly selected client... Acln drops heavily for both the plainly-trained models and AT-trained models under non-IID distribution."
- Break condition: If local data distributions are perfectly balanced or if adversarial training is applied only to IID data.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DBFAT improves accuracy and robustness by re-weighting adversarial examples based on their distance to the decision boundary.
- Mechanism: Samples closer to the decision boundary are assigned larger weights during local training, focusing learning on the most informative examples.
- Core assumption: The minimum number of PGD steps to misclassify an example approximates its distance to the decision boundary.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "we attempt to re-weight the adversarial examples to guide adversarial training. For clean examples that are close to the decision boundary, we assign larger weights; while those examples that are far from the boundary are assigned with smaller weights." [section] "We use PGD- S to approximately measure the geometric distance to the decision boundary... We generate adversarial examples as follows..."
- Break condition: If the approximation of distance to boundary via PGD steps is invalid for the dataset or model architecture.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: DBFAT uses global model logits to regularize local training, reducing boundary error.
- Mechanism: A KL divergence loss between local and global model logits is added to the local objective, aligning local decision boundaries with the global model.
- Core assumption: The global model has a more desirable decision boundary for natural data due to aggregation of diverse local models.
- Evidence anchors: [section] "we take advantage of logits from the global model fglo, which is trained after aggregation... we modify the local objective... as below: minℓce(ρ ·floc(xadv),y ) + β ·ℓkl(floc(xadv),f glo(x))." [section] "the model owns the information obtained from the averaged parameters on distributed clients."
- Break condition: If the global model's logits are not representative of a desirable decision boundary (e.g., due to highly heterogeneous data).

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Local client -> Local training (re-weighting + KL loss) -> Parameter aggregation -> Global server -> Global logits computation -> Next round local training

- Critical path: Local training (re-weighting + KL loss) → Parameter aggregation → Global logits computation → Next round local training

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Re-weighting: Focuses learning on boundary samples but may increase variance if weights are poorly estimated
  - Global regularization: Aligns local boundaries but may slow convergence if β is too high

- Failure signatures:
  - Clean accuracy drops significantly: Likely issue with re-weighting or β parameter
  - Robust accuracy remains low: Likely issue with adversarial example generation or local training

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement DBFAT on a simple CNN with IID MNIST/FMNIST to verify baseline performance
  2. Test DBFAT on non-IID MNIST/FMNIST (sharding) to observe impact of re-weighting
  3. Vary β parameter on CIFAR10 to find optimal trade-off between accuracy and robustness

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

The paper does not explicitly call out open questions, but several remain:

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the effectiveness of DBFAT scale with increasingly non-IID data distributions beyond the tested Dirichlet and Sharding scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper tests on Dirichlet and Sharding distributions but doesn't explore more extreme non-IID scenarios
- Why unresolved: The paper only tests moderate non-IID settings and doesn't push the limits of data heterogeneity
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing DBFAT on synthetic extreme non-IID distributions (e.g., clients with single-class data) and comparing performance to other methods

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the theoretical relationship between the re-weighting parameter ρ and the trade-off between accuracy and robustness in DBFAT?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses ρ for re-weighting but doesn't provide theoretical analysis of how it affects the accuracy-robustness trade-off
- Why unresolved: The paper presents empirical results but lacks theoretical grounding for the re-weighting mechanism
- What evidence would resolve it: Formal analysis of how ρ influences the decision boundary and theoretical bounds on accuracy-robustness trade-off

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does DBFAT perform against adaptive attacks that specifically target its decision boundary-based defense mechanism?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper evaluates against standard attacks but doesn't test adaptive attacks designed to circumvent DBFAT's specific defense
- Why unresolved: Standard attack evaluation doesn't capture potential vulnerabilities in DBFAT's unique approach
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing DBFAT against attacks that specifically exploit its decision boundary-based re-weighting and regularization mechanisms

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the optimal communication strategy between local and global models in DBFAT for different federated learning scenarios?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper uses standard FedAvg communication but doesn't explore how different communication strategies affect DBFAT's performance
- Why unresolved: The paper doesn't experiment with different communication frequencies or aggregation methods
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic comparison of DBFAT performance under different communication strategies (e.g., varying aggregation frequency, partial model updates)

## Limitations
- Effectiveness of the decision boundary approximation via PGD steps has not been validated across diverse model architectures
- The choice of β parameter appears critical but is not thoroughly explored across different datasets
- Results are primarily demonstrated on image classification tasks; generalization to other domains remains unclear

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The observation that direct adversarial training in FL degrades accuracy under non-IID settings is well-supported by experimental evidence
- **Medium Confidence**: The effectiveness of DBFAT's two proposed mechanisms (re-weighting and global regularization) is demonstrated, but the underlying assumptions lack external validation
- **Low Confidence**: The specific re-weighting strategy based on PGD steps as distance approximation is novel but not compared against alternative boundary estimation methods

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study**: Test DBFAT with only re-weighting, only global regularization, and neither to quantify individual contributions
2. **Alternative Boundary Measures**: Compare PGD-based distance approximation against alternative methods like input gradient magnitude
3. **Architecture Robustness**: Validate DBFAT performance across different model architectures (ResNet, EfficientNet) to test generalizability beyond the "simple CNN" and "larger CNN" used in experiments
