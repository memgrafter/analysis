# Guided Deep Kernel Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09574
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09574
- Reference count: 22
- One-line primary result: GDKL outperforms standard DKL and competing methods in predictive performance and uncertainty estimation on small to mid-sized datasets.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses overfitting and poor uncertainty estimation in Deep Kernel Learning (DKL) by introducing Guided Deep Kernel Learning (GDKL). GDKL uses a Neural Network Gaussian Process (NNGP) as a guide to train the DKL model, matching its posterior distribution with that of the NNGP. This approach combines the Bayesian benefits of NNGPs (robustness to overfitting, accurate uncertainty estimation) with the flexibility and scalability of deep kernels. Experiments on small to mid-sized datasets with varying dimensionality show that GDKL outperforms standard DKL and competing methods in terms of both predictive performance and reliable uncertainty estimation, demonstrating superior calibration and reduced overfitting.

## Method Summary
GDKL improves DKL by incorporating an NNGP guide to enhance uncertainty estimation and reduce overfitting. The method trains the DKL model to match the posterior distribution of the NNGP, conditioned on the target value, using a combined objective of expected log-likelihood and KL divergence. This hybrid objective balances predictive accuracy with Bayesian behavior. For scalability, GDKL can incorporate inducing points, defining a variational distribution over function values at these locations to avoid the computational burden of evaluating the NNGP on all data points.

## Key Results
- GDKL achieves superior predictive performance and uncertainty estimation compared to standard DKL and competing methods.
- The method demonstrates improved calibration metrics (ECE, MCE) and reduced overfitting across various datasets.
- GDKL effectively scales to larger datasets using inducing points while maintaining performance.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GDKL improves uncertainty estimation by matching the latent function distribution of DKL to that of the NNGP, conditioned on the target value.
- Mechanism: The KL divergence between qθ(f*|x*,D1) and p(f*|x*,y*,D1) guides the DKL model to adopt uncertainty estimates from the NNGP, which are more reliable due to the Bayesian nature of infinite-width networks.
- Core assumption: The NNGP provides a better calibrated uncertainty estimate than standard DKL training, especially in regions with limited data.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our approach harnesses the reliable uncertainty estimation of the NNGPs to adapt the DKL target confidence when it encounters novel data points."
  - [section] "Consider a regression task, the DKL will try to match a Gaussian centered near the target with an adaptive level of certainty that depends on the NNGP."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. No direct mention of this specific mechanism in related papers.
- Break condition: If the NNGP's uncertainty estimates are not reliable or if the conditioning on y* introduces bias.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: GDKL combines the predictive performance of DKL with the Bayesian benefits of NNGPs by optimizing a hybrid objective.
- Mechanism: The GDKL objective combines the expected log-likelihood term (promoting predictive accuracy) with the KL divergence term (promoting Bayesian behavior), balancing the two with a hyperparameter β.
- Core assumption: The combination of these two terms leads to a model that generalizes well and estimates uncertainty reliably.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "As a result, we get the best of both worlds, we leverage the Bayesian behavior of the NNGP, namely its robustness to overfitting, and accurate uncertainty estimation, while maintaining the generalization abilities, scalability, and flexibility of deep kernels."
  - [section] "It is not hard to see that our objective is equivalent to maximizing the ELBO... We can think of it as having a data term, which is comparable to the DKL marginal likelihood objective, plus a regularizer that prevents the model from over-fitting the training points."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. No direct mention of this specific hybrid objective in related papers.
- Break condition: If the balance between the two terms is not optimal, leading to either overfitting or underfitting.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GDKL can handle large datasets by incorporating inducing points, avoiding the computational burden of evaluating the NNGP on all data points.
- Mechanism: By defining a set of inducing locations and optimizing a variational distribution over the function values at these locations, GDKL can scale to large datasets while still leveraging the NNGP's uncertainty estimates.
- Core assumption: The inducing points mechanism is effective in approximating the full GP and the NNGP's uncertainty estimates are still informative at the inducing points.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we also proposed an extension of our model to incorporate inducing points."
  - [section] "We follow the common practice... and define the posterior now as qθ(f) = ∫ pθ(f|u)q(u)du, where pθ(f|u) is a Gaussian density according to the GP prior of the DKL, and q(u) is a variational Gaussian distribution with learned parameters."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence. No direct mention of this specific inducing points extension in related papers.
- Break condition: If the inducing points are not well-placed or if the variational approximation is too crude.

- Component map:
  - DKL model -> NNGP model -> Inducing points (optional)
- Critical path:
  1. Initialize DKL and NNGP models.
  2. Pre-train NNGP hyper-parameters.
  3. Train DKL model using the GDKL objective.
  4. (Optional) Incorporate inducing points for large datasets.
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Exact GP vs. inducing points: Exact GP is more accurate but computationally expensive; inducing points scale better but introduce approximation error.
  - Choice of β: Balancing predictive accuracy and Bayesian behavior.
- Failure signatures:
  - Overfitting: High training accuracy but poor test accuracy and unreliable uncertainty estimates.
  - Underfitting: Poor performance on both training and test sets.
  - Computational issues: Memory or runtime errors due to large datasets or complex models.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Small UCI dataset (e.g., Boston Housing) with exact GP: Verify that GDKL outperforms standard DKL in terms of both accuracy and uncertainty estimation.

  3. Ablation study on β: Investigate the effect of different β values on the balance between predictive accuracy and Bayesian behavior.

## Limitations
- Computational complexity of NNGP guide remains a concern, particularly for high-dimensional datasets.
- Effectiveness of inducing points mechanism for scalability is not thoroughly validated.
- Hyperparameter β requires careful tuning and may not generalize well across different datasets without extensive cross-validation.

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The core mechanism of using NNGP as a guide for DKL uncertainty estimation is well-supported by theoretical derivations and experimental results.
- **Medium confidence**: The scalability improvements through inducing points are promising but less thoroughly validated.
- **Medium confidence**: The superiority of GDKL over other uncertainty-aware methods is demonstrated, but the comparison is limited to specific datasets and model architectures.
