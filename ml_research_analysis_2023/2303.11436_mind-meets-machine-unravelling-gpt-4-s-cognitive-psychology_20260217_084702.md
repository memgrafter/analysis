# Mind meets machine: Unravelling GPT-4's cognitive psychology

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.11436

## Executive Summary
This paper evaluates GPT-4's performance on cognitive psychology tasks using established datasets like CommonsenseQA, SuperGLUE, MATH, and HANS. The study demonstrates that GPT-4 achieves 83% accuracy on CommonsenseQA, outperforming GPT-3.5 (66%) and the original language model tested in the dataset (56.5%), while approaching human-level performance (89%). The findings highlight GPT-4's strong reasoning and commonsense understanding capabilities, suggesting its potential to bridge the gap between human and machine reasoning. The research strengthens confidence in GPT-4's capabilities for cognitive psychology tasks and its potential to revolutionize AI applications in psychology and related fields.

## Method Summary
The study uses ChatGPT-Plus to test GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 on the CommonsenseQA dataset containing 12,247 examples. Performance is evaluated using accuracy metrics, comparing results against human-level performance (89%). While the paper mentions using multiple datasets (SuperGLUE, MATH, HANS) for comprehensive evaluation, only CommonsenseQA results are presented. The methodology relies on direct model responses to benchmark questions without detailed specification of prompt engineering techniques.

## Key Results
- GPT-4 achieves 83% accuracy on CommonsenseQA, significantly outperforming GPT-3.5 (66%) and previous models (56.5%)
- Performance approaches human-level accuracy (89%) on commonsense reasoning tasks
- Results demonstrate strong integration of contextual information and commonsense knowledge through self-attention architecture

- Evidence anchors: GPT-4's performance on CommonsenseQA (83%) compared to GPT-3.5 (66%) and the original language model (56.5%) shows a significant improvement in commonsense reasoning.

- Core assumption: The model's performance reflects genuine understanding rather than pattern matching or memorization.
- Evidence anchors: GPT-4 exhibits a high level of accuracy in cognitive psychology tasks relative to prior state-of-the-art models, approaching human-level performance.
