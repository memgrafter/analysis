# Understanding Why ViT Trains Badly on Small Datasets: An Intuitive Perspective

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.03751
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03751
- Reference count: 9
- Primary result: ViT underperforms ResNet-18 on small image datasets like CIFAR-10/100 due to lack of inductive bias for locality

## Executive Summary
This study investigates why Vision Transformers (ViT) consistently underperform ResNet-18 on small image datasets. Through comparative analysis of model accuracy, attention map visualization for ViT, feature map visualization for ResNet, and representation similarity using CKA, the authors identify that ViT's learned representations on small datasets differ significantly from those learned on large datasets. The key finding is that ViT lacks spatial inductive bias for locality, making it struggle to capture local relationships in complex small datasets, while ResNet's convolutional architecture naturally preserves this inductive bias. This fundamental architectural difference explains why CNNs outperform ViTs on limited data.

## Method Summary
The authors train ViT and ResNet-18 models on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN datasets with identical hyperparameters (Adam optimizer, learning rate 1e-4, batch size 100, 500 epochs). They compare top-1 accuracy performance and analyze the learned representations through two visualization approaches: attention maps for ViT layers and feature maps for ResNet layers. Additionally, they compute Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) similarity between corresponding layers of both models to quantify representation differences. The analysis focuses on understanding why ViT performs worse on small datasets compared to ResNet-18.

## Key Results
- ViT achieves significantly lower accuracy than ResNet-18 on complex small datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100) but performs comparably on simpler datasets (SVHN)
- CKA analysis reveals that ViT's representation when trained on small datasets is fundamentally different from its representation when trained on large datasets
- Attention visualization shows ViT's lower layers focus on high-contrast local areas, but this strategy is less effective for complex small datasets compared to ResNet's feature maps

- Mechanism: ViT relies on self-attention without built-in spatial priors, so on small datasets it struggles to learn local feature representations that CNNs naturally capture through convolution operations. This leads to a representation that is fundamentally different from ViT trained on large datasets, causing poor performance.

  - Input: 32x32 RGB images from CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, or SVHN
  - ViT: Patch embedding, 6-layer transformer encoder (8 heads, 64 dim each), MLP head
  - ResNet-18: 17-layer CNN with residual connections, average pooling, fully connected layer
  - Training: Adam optimizer, learning rate 1e-4, batch size 100, 500 epochs
  - Evaluation: Top-1 accuracy on test set
  - Analysis: Attention map visualization (ViT), feature map visualization (ResNet), CKA similarity analysis

- Critical path:
  1. Data preprocessing and augmentation
  2. Model training and accuracy tracking
  3. Layer visualization extraction (attention maps for ViT, feature maps for ResNet)
  4. CKA similarity computation between model layers
  5. Comparative analysis and interpretation of results

- Design tradeoffs:
  - ViT vs ResNet: No spatial inductive bias vs built-in locality bias; potentially more flexible representations vs more efficient learning from limited data
  - Visualization methods: Attention maps provide insight into ViT's processing but are not directly comparable to feature maps; CKA analysis offers quantitative comparison but may not capture all relevant differences
  - Dataset choice: Using multiple small datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN) provides broader insights but introduces complexity in analysis and interpretation

- Failure signatures:
  - ViT consistently underperforming ResNet-18 on small datasets but matching on larger datasets (like SVHN)
  - CKA similarity patterns between ViT and ResNet diverging significantly when trained on small vs large datasets
  - Attention maps showing focus on high-contrast areas that may not be most discriminative for classification

## Confidence

- Medium confidence: The hypothesis that lack of spatial inductive bias causes ViT's performance degradation is supported by visualization and CKA analysis but not definitively proven
- Medium confidence: The claim that CKA similarity patterns indicate fundamental representation differences between ViT trained on small vs large datasets is reasonable but relies on the assumption that CKA is a reliable similarity metric
