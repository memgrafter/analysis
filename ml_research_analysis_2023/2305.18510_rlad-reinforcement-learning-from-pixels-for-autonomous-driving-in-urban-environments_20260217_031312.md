# RLAD: Reinforcement Learning from Pixels for Autonomous Driving in Urban Environments

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18510
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18510
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RLAD achieves 62% success rate on CARLA NoCrash benchmark, outperforming all RLfP methods

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces RLAD, the first Reinforcement Learning from Pixels (RLfP) method for urban autonomous driving that learns simultaneously the image encoder and driving policy network. Unlike prior approaches that decouple perception from control, RLAD proposes three key innovations: an image encoder with A-LIX layers and data augmentations to prevent overfitting, a WayConv1D waypoint encoder leveraging 2D geometric information, and an auxiliary traffic light classification loss. Experimental results show RLAD significantly outperforms all state-of-the-art RLfP methods on the CARLA NoCrash benchmark, achieving 62% success rate in regular traffic conditions compared to 30-42% for competing methods.

## Method Summary
RLAD is a vision-based autonomous driving system that uses Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) with a custom architecture including an image encoder with 10 convolutional layers plus Adaptive Local Signal Mixing (A-LIX) layers, a WayConv1D waypoint encoder using 1D convolutions with 2×2 kernels, and an auxiliary traffic light classification loss. The system processes 256×256 RGB images, 2D waypoints, and vehicle measurements, training end-to-end for 10⁶ environment steps on CARLA simulator with various weather conditions and traffic densities.

## Key Results
- RLAD achieves 62% success rate on CARLA NoCrash benchmark (empty traffic), compared to 42% for second-best RLfP method
- Ablation study shows A-LIX layers and auxiliary loss are critical, with their removal reducing performance by 33%
- RLAD significantly outperforms prior RLfP methods but does not yet match state-of-the-art methods that decouple encoder training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Simultaneous training of encoder and policy network aligns feature representations with driving task rewards.
- Mechanism: By backpropagating RL rewards through both the image encoder and policy network, the encoder learns to prioritize task-relevant visual features (e.g., traffic lights, lane boundaries) over irrelevant background details.
- Core assumption: The reward signal is strong enough to guide the encoder toward useful representations despite the complexity of urban scenes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "it is beneficial to use them to improve simultaneously the feature representation of the sensor data and the driving policy network"
  - [section II]: "RLfP aims to fix this limitation by updating the image encoder alongside the driving policy network"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - no direct citations of RLAD's specific mechanism in corpus neighbors

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: A-LIX layers prevent catastrophic self-overfitting in the image encoder during RL training.
- Mechanism: A-LIX randomly mixes each feature map component with its neighbors, smoothing gradient discontinuities during backpropagation while minimally disrupting spatial information. This prevents the encoder from producing spatially inconsistent feature maps.
- Core assumption: Spatially inconsistent gradients are a primary cause of encoder overfitting in RLfP settings.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III-B1]: "A-LIX is applied on the features produced by the convolution layers... by randomly mixing each component... with its neighbors belonging to the same feature map"
  - [section III-B1]: "This process can be succinctly summarized as... A-LIX... smoothing discontinuous component of the gradients signal during back-propagation"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus neighbors

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: WayConv1D encoder leverages 2D geometric information of waypoints to improve trajectory following stability.
- Mechanism: Instead of flattening waypoint coordinates into a vector, WayConv1D applies 1D convolutions with 2×2 kernels over the 2D coordinates, preserving spatial relationships between waypoints while maintaining computational efficiency.
- Core assumption: The 2D geometric structure of waypoints contains information that linear flattening loses, which is important for stable trajectory following.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section III-B1]: "we propose WayConv1D, a waypoint encoder that leverages the 2D geometrical structure of the input by applying 1D convolutions with a 2 ×2 kernel over the 2D coordinates"
  - [section III-B1]: "We found that with WayConv1D, the agent learns more efficiently to follow the trajectory without oscillating near the center of the lane"
  - [corpus]: No direct evidence in corpus neighbors

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Reinforcement Learning from Pixels (RLfP)
  - Why needed here: Traditional RL methods for autonomous driving decouple perception from control, leading to suboptimal feature representations. RLfP trains both simultaneously using raw pixel inputs.
  - Quick check question: Why is training a convolutional encoder alongside a policy network more challenging than using pre-trained features?

- Concept: Temporal Difference (TD) Learning instability
  - Why needed here: The paper mentions that TD learning with convolutional encoders leads to unstable training and degenerated feature representations, which RLAD addresses through various techniques.
  - Quick check question: What are the main sources of instability when combining convolutional encoders with TD learning algorithms?

- Concept: Catastrophic self-overfitting
  - Why needed here: A-LIX layers are specifically designed to prevent this phenomenon, which occurs when the encoder produces spatially inconsistent feature maps that lead to discontinuous gradients.
  - Quick check question: How does spatially inconsistent feature representation affect the quality of gradients during backpropagation?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input (images, waypoints, vehicle measurements) → Image Encoder (CNN + A-LIX) → WayConv1D (waypoint encoder) → MLP (vehicle encoder) → Concatenation → SAC Actor-Critic → Output (throttle, brake, steering) + Auxiliary traffic light classifier
- Critical path: Image → CNN → A-LIX → Concatenate → SAC Q-networks → Policy update. The auxiliary loss path (traffic light classification) runs in parallel and updates the image encoder weights.
- Design tradeoffs: Larger image encoders provide better feature extraction but are harder to train with RL; smaller encoders train more easily but may miss important features. RLAD balances this with ~1M parameters plus regularization.
- Failure signatures: Agent oscillates near lane center (suggests waypoint encoder issues), fails to stop at red lights (suggests traffic light perception failure), gets stuck frequently (suggests exploration/representation problems).
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run RLAD on empty traffic scenario to verify basic functionality before adding complexity
  2. Disable A-LIX layers and compare performance to measure their impact on stability
  3. Replace WayConv1D with simple MLP waypoint encoder to quantify the geometric information benefit

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of RLAD compare to state-of-the-art methods that decouple perception from control when scaled to larger image encoders?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper states that state-of-the-art methods use image encoders with around 25 times more parameters than RLAD, and that RLAD is not yet competitive with these methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore scaling up RLAD's image encoder to match the parameter count of state-of-the-art methods, which would require significant computational resources.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training RLAD with an image encoder of comparable size to state-of-the-art methods and evaluating its performance on the NoCrash benchmark.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the traffic light classification auxiliary loss be further optimized to improve red light infraction rates?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that RLAD with the auxiliary loss achieves 17% better red light infraction rates than the second-best method, but still performs poorly compared to IL-based methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses a simple three-class traffic light classification, which may not capture all relevant information for decision-making.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with alternative auxiliary losses, such as distance to traffic light or time to red light, and their impact on red light infraction rates.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does RLAD generalize to real-world driving scenarios beyond the CARLA simulator?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper evaluates RLAD exclusively in the CARLA simulator, which may not capture all real-world complexities.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide any real-world validation of RLAD's performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing RLAD in real-world autonomous driving systems or more complex simulation environments with additional real-world factors.

## Limitations

- RLAD does not yet match state-of-the-art methods that decouple encoder training, suggesting inherent limitations in simultaneous training approach
- Lack of direct corpus evidence for proposed mechanisms (A-LIX layers, WayConv1D, auxiliary loss benefits) prevents validation against existing research
- Performance on red light infractions remains poor compared to imitation learning methods, indicating limitations in traffic light perception

## Confidence

- High confidence: RLAD architecture and implementation details, comparative performance on NoCrash benchmark
- Medium confidence: Benefits of A-LIX layers and WayConv1D design choices (supported by ablation but not external validation)
- Low confidence: Mechanism claims for why simultaneous training works better (lacks corpus evidence)
