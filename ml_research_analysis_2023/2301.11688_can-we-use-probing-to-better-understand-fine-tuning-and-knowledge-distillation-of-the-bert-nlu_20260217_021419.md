# Can We Use Probing to Better Understand Fine-tuning and Knowledge Distillation of the BERT NLU?

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2301.11688
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11688
- **Reference count**: 15
- **Primary result**: Current probing methods are inadequate for analyzing fine-tuning and distillation in BERT-based NLU models due to their inability to distinguish between information presence and information decodability.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates whether probing methods can effectively analyze fine-tuning and knowledge distillation processes in BERT-based Natural Language Understanding (NLU) models. Through systematic experiments, the authors demonstrate that traditional probing approaches show decreased scores during fine-tuning and distillation, but these decreases reflect changes in decodability rather than actual information loss. The research reveals a fundamental limitation in current probing methodologies: they conflate the presence of information with the ease of extracting that information. This finding has significant implications for how we interpret probing results in model analysis and suggests that current practices may misrepresent what distilled models have actually learned.

## Method Summary
The study employs a comprehensive experimental framework using BERT-based models for NLU tasks, specifically focusing on slot-filling. The authors conduct controlled experiments comparing frozen BERT, fine-tuned BERT, and distilled models across multiple probing tasks including structural probing and conditional probing. They systematically vary the NLU architecture while maintaining consistent task accuracy to isolate the effects of architectural differences on probing results. The experiments measure both NLU accuracy and various probing metrics, with particular attention to how different decoders affect the extraction of syntactic and semantic information from model representations.

## Key Results
- Fine-tuned BERT achieved 99% NLU accuracy but showed lower structural probing scores compared to frozen BERT, indicating probing score decreases during fine-tuning do not reflect information loss
- Different NLU architectures with similar accuracy produced varying probing results, demonstrating the decoder's significant role in structuring information
- Conditional probing revealed near-zero syntactic information in distilled models, suggesting current probing methods fundamentally misrepresent distilled model learning

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The core mechanism underlying these findings is that probing methods conflate two distinct concepts: the presence of information in model representations and the decodability of that information. When models undergo fine-tuning or distillation, their representations may become more specialized or compressed, making information harder to extract with standard decoders but not necessarily removing that information. This creates a situation where lower probing scores indicate changes in information structure rather than information reduction. The decoder architecture itself plays a crucial role in determining what information can be successfully extracted, meaning that probing results are heavily influenced by the specific decoder used rather than reflecting the true information content of the representations.

## Foundational Learning

**Information Decodability vs Presence**: Understanding the distinction between whether information exists in representations versus whether it can be easily extracted is fundamental to interpreting probing results. This concept is needed because traditional probing assumes that information presence and decodability are equivalent, which this study demonstrates is false. Quick check: Compare probing scores using multiple different decoder architectures on the same frozen representations.

**Representation Invariance**: The idea that useful information should be extractable regardless of the specific decoder architecture is crucial for valid probing. This concept matters because the study shows different decoders yield different probing scores, suggesting current methods lack invariance. Quick check: Train multiple decoders with varying architectures on identical frozen BERT representations and compare results.

**Conditional Information**: Understanding how information changes when conditioned on specific task contexts is essential for interpreting probing results. This matters because conditional probing revealed near-zero syntactic information in distilled models, challenging assumptions about what distilled models learn. Quick check: Apply conditional probing across different layers and fine-tuning stages to track information flow.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Input Text -> BERT Encoder -> [Frozen/Fine-tuned/Distilled] Representations -> Decoder(s) for Probing Tasks -> Probing Metrics

Critical Path: The critical path for understanding these results follows: Model Training (fine-tuning/distillation) → Representation Generation → Decoder Application → Probing Score Calculation. The key insight is that changes at the representation level propagate through the decoder to affect final probing scores, making it essential to understand the interaction between representations and decoders.

Design Tradeoffs: The primary tradeoff is between model specialization (which improves task performance) and representation accessibility (which affects probing scores). Fine-tuning and distillation optimize for task performance at the potential cost of making representations less accessible to standard probing decoders. This tradeoff suggests that probing methods optimized for one type of representation may fail to capture information in more specialized representations.

Failure Signatures: When probing fails to accurately reflect model capabilities, warning signs include: (1) declining probing scores despite improving task accuracy, (2) dramatically different probing results across architectures with similar task performance, and (3) near-zero conditional probing scores in models that demonstrably perform well on syntactic tasks. These signatures indicate that the probing methodology, rather than the model, may be the source of the problem.

## Limitations
- Probing methods conflate information presence with information decodability, making them unreliable for analyzing model training processes
- Different architectures with identical NLU accuracy yield different probing scores, questioning whether probing captures task-relevant information
- Conditional probing showing near-zero syntactic information in distilled models suggests current methods may fundamentally misrepresent distilled model learning

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The finding that frozen BERT achieves higher structural probing scores than fine-tuned BERT while maintaining lower NLU accuracy is robust and clearly demonstrated, directly challenging assumptions about probing score interpretation.

**Medium confidence**: The claim that current probing methods are inadequate for analyzing fine-tuning and distillation is well-supported by experimental evidence, though generalizability across all probing methodologies and tasks requires further validation.

**Low confidence**: The assertion that probing results decreased due to changes in decodability rather than information reduction is based on indirect evidence, and establishing causal relationships requires more direct measurement techniques.

## Next Checks

1. Implement direct decodability measurement experiments by training multiple decoders with varying architectures on the same frozen representations to quantify how much probing scores depend on decoder choice versus actual information content.

2. Conduct ablation studies across diverse NLU tasks (beyond slot-filling) and model architectures to determine whether the observed decoupling between probing scores and NLU performance is task-specific or represents a fundamental limitation of probing methods.

3. Develop and validate alternative probing metrics that explicitly account for information decodability, potentially by incorporating measures of representation invariance or transferability across different decoding mechanisms.
