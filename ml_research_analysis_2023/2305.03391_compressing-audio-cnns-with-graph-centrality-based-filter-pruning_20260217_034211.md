# Compressing audio CNNs with graph centrality based filter pruning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03391
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03391
- Reference count: 0
- This paper proposes a filter pruning method for CNNs using graph centrality to compress audio classification models, achieving significant reductions in computations and parameters with minimal accuracy loss.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a novel filter pruning method for compressing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) used in audio classification tasks. The approach represents CNN filters as nodes in a graph and measures redundancy using centrality scores, allowing for the removal of filters that contribute less to network performance. The method is evaluated on the DCASE 2021 Task 1A and PANNs for audio tagging, demonstrating substantial reductions in computational requirements while maintaining high accuracy. The proposed passive pruning method outperforms existing techniques without requiring feature maps, making it scalable and efficient.

## Method Summary
The method involves representing CNN filters as nodes in a graph, where edges are weighted by the similarity between filters calculated using rank-1 approximations via SVD. Centrality measures, specifically weighted degree centrality (WDC) and betweenness centrality (BC), are used to identify and prune redundant filters. After pruning, the network is fine-tuned to regain performance. The approach is applied layer-wise with pruning ratios of 25%, 50%, and 75%, and is evaluated on acoustic scene classification and audio tagging tasks.

## Key Results
- For DCASE 2021 Task 1A, the method reduces computations per inference by 71% with 50% fewer parameters and less than a 2 percentage point drop in accuracy.
- For PANNs, it reduces 24% computations per inference with 41% fewer parameters at a slight performance improvement.
- The proposed passive pruning method performs better than existing methods without requiring feature maps.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: High centrality filters represent commonality and can be replaced by other filters without affecting network performance much.
- Mechanism: The method uses graph centrality to measure how interconnected a filter is with others. High centrality filters are seen as redundant because their functionality is duplicated by other filters in the network.
- Core assumption: Filters with high centrality scores are less important because their contribution is already covered by other filters in the network.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We hypothesise that a filter with a high centrality should be eliminated as it represents commonality and can be replaced by other filters without affecting the performance of a network much."
  - [section] "We measure this commonality using the graph-theoretic concept of centrality."
- Break condition: If the network relies heavily on specific filters for unique features, removing high centrality filters could degrade performance significantly.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Using SVD to find rank-1 approximations of filters captures the most significant singular value and corresponding vectors, providing a good representative for similarity calculations.
- Mechanism: Each filter is flattened and decomposed using SVD. The most significant singular value and vectors are used to create a rank-1 approximation, which is then used as the filter's representative for computing cosine similarity.
- Core assumption: The rank-1 approximation adequately represents the filter's essential characteristics for similarity measurement.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We can now approximate the original filter with ˆF = (σ1l1rT1 )i,j√∑whk=1((σ1l1rT1 )k,j )2 ."
- Break condition: If the filters have complex structures that cannot be captured by rank-1 approximations, the similarity measurements may be inaccurate.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Weighted degree centrality (WDC) and betweenness centrality (BC) are effective measures for identifying redundant filters.
- Mechanism: WDC sums the weights of edges connected to a node, while BC measures how often a node is on the shortest path between other nodes. Filters with high scores in these metrics are considered redundant.
- Core assumption: Filters that are highly connected (WDC) or lie on many shortest paths (BC) are more likely to be redundant.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "We utilise this graphical setup and two centrality algorithms to compute the importance of each filter."
- Break condition: If the graph structure does not accurately reflect the functional relationships between filters, centrality measures may not identify the correct filters to prune.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Input: Pre-trained CNN model
  - Filter Preprocessing: Flattening and SVD for rank-1 approximation
  - Graph Construction: Filters as nodes, similarity as edge weights
  - Centrality Calculation: WDC and BC to score filters
  - Pruning: Remove top p% of filters with highest centrality scores
  - Fine-tuning: Retrain pruned network to regain performance
- Critical path: Filter preprocessing → Graph construction → Centrality calculation → Pruning → Fine-tuning
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Passive vs. Active Pruning: Passive methods are data-free and more scalable but may be less precise.
  - Centrality Measures: WDC is simpler but may miss nuanced redundancies that BC could capture.
  - Rank-1 Approximation: Reduces computational complexity but may oversimplify filter representations.
- Failure signatures:
  - Significant drop in accuracy after pruning indicates too many important filters were removed.
  - Minimal reduction in parameters or MACs suggests the pruning ratio or centrality measures are not effective.
  - Increased training time during fine-tuning may indicate the pruned network has lost essential features.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Apply the method to a small CNN (e.g., DCASE21 Net) with a pruning ratio of 25% and evaluate accuracy and computational savings.
  2. Compare WDC and BC centrality measures on the same network to determine which yields better results.
  3. Test different rank approximations (e.g., rank-2) to see if they improve similarity measurements and pruning effectiveness.

## Limitations

- The method assumes high centrality filters are redundant, which may not hold for all network architectures or tasks.
- Reliance on SVD rank-1 approximations may oversimplify filter representations, affecting the accuracy of similarity measurements.
- The pruning ratios and layer-wise approach may not generalize well to all CNN architectures or tasks beyond audio classification.
