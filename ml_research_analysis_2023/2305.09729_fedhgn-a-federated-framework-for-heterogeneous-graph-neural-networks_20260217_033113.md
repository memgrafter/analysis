# FedHGN: A Federated Framework for Heterogeneous Graph Neural Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.09729
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09729
- Reference count: 15
- Key outcome: FedHGN achieves up to 7% accuracy improvements in federated heterogeneous graph learning while preserving schema privacy

## Executive Summary
FedHGN is a federated learning framework designed specifically for heterogeneous graph neural networks (HGNNs) that addresses the challenge of schema privacy in collaborative training. The framework introduces schema-weight decoupling to separate graph schemas from model weights, enabling schema-agnostic knowledge sharing without revealing sensitive schema information. By employing coefficients alignment, FedHGN stabilizes the training process and improves performance while maintaining privacy. Experimental results on three heterogeneous graph datasets demonstrate that FedHGN consistently outperforms both local training and conventional federated learning methods like FedAvg and FedProx, achieving accuracy improvements of up to 7% in node classification tasks.

## Method Summary
FedHGN decouples graph schemas from model weights by decomposing HGNN weights into schema-agnostic basis weights and client-specific schema coefficients. This enables clients to collaboratively train without sharing their actual graph schemas. The framework uses coefficients alignment with a regularization term that penalizes local node/edge types' coefficients deviating from the most similar counterpart from other clients, encouraging alignment without requiring schema matching. During training, the server coordinates the federated learning process, iteratively sampling clients, sending latest model parameters, collecting updated parameters, and aggregating schema-agnostic weights while distributing schema coefficients. The approach is implemented with an RGCN-like architecture using 2 layers, embedding dimension 64, and 20 bases, trained with SGD optimizer and early stopping.

## Key Results
- FedHGN consistently outperforms local training and conventional FL methods (FedAvg, FedProx) on three heterogeneous graph datasets
- Achieves accuracy improvements of up to 7% in node classification tasks while preserving schema privacy
- Demonstrates stable training and convergence across different client configurations (K=3,5,10)
- Shows that schema-weight decoupling and coefficients alignment are essential components for effective federated HGNN learning

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Server -> Clients (each with heterogeneous graph data and local HGNN model) -> Schema-weight decoupling (SWD) and Coefficients alignment (CA) components -> Federated averaging

- Critical path:
  Initialize HGNN models and SWD parameters -> Iteratively: Server samples clients and sends latest model parameters -> Clients perform local training with SWD and CA -> Clients send updated parameters to server -> Server aggregates schema-agnostic weights and distributes schema coefficients -> Convergence criteria met or max iterations reached

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of bases (B) in SWD: More bases may capture more patterns but increase communication cost and risk overfitting
  - Alignment regularization factor (λ): Higher values enforce more alignment but may restrict model flexibility
  - Local epochs (E): More epochs may improve local model quality but increase communication rounds needed

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance compared to local training: Likely issues with SWD or CA implementation, or hyperparameters not well-tuned
  - Unstable training or slow convergence: CA regularization may be too weak or too strong, or client data heterogeneity may be too high
  - Privacy breaches: SWD or CA may be leaking schema information; need to audit communications and model updates

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run FedHGN on a small, simple heterogeneous graph dataset (e.g., AIFB) with 3 clients using the RE splitting strategy, compare to local training baseline
  2. Vary the number of bases (B) in SWD and measure impact on performance and communication cost
  3. Disable the CA component and observe the impact on training stability and final performance, to verify its importance

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does FedHGN's performance scale with the number of clients in federated heterogeneous graph learning settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows results with varying client numbers (K=3,5,10) but doesn't extensively explore scaling beyond this or discuss theoretical limits
- Why unresolved: The experimental results only cover a limited range of client numbers, and the paper doesn't provide theoretical analysis of how performance scales with an increasing number of clients or discuss practical limitations
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with larger client numbers (e.g., K=50,100) and theoretical analysis of convergence properties and communication overhead as the number of clients grows

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can FedHGN be effectively extended to handle dynamic heterogeneous graphs where node/edge types and relationships change over time?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on static heterogeneous graphs and doesn't address the challenges of dynamic graph structures in federated settings
- Why unresolved: The proposed framework assumes fixed graph schemas and doesn't discuss how to handle schema evolution or temporal changes in the graph structure while maintaining privacy and performance
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementation and evaluation of FedHGN on dynamic heterogeneous graph datasets, showing how the framework adapts to changing schemas and maintains performance over time

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal strategy for determining the number of bases (B) in the schema-weight decoupling component?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that FedHGN is sensitive to the choice of B and shows empirical results for different values, but doesn't provide a systematic method for determining the optimal B
- Why unresolved: The authors only provide empirical observations on B's impact on performance without offering theoretical guidance or automatic selection methods for different heterogeneous graph scenarios
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of a principled approach or algorithm for selecting B based on graph characteristics, along with empirical validation showing improved performance compared to manual selection

### Open Question 4
- Question: How does FedHGN perform when clients have highly imbalanced or skewed data distributions across different node/edge types?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper doesn't address the scenario where clients have significantly different distributions of node/edge types, which could affect the effectiveness of the coefficients alignment mechanism
- Why unresolved: The experiments use relatively balanced splits of the datasets, and the paper doesn't discuss how the coefficients alignment component would handle situations where some node/edge types are overrepresented in certain clients
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments with intentionally imbalanced data distributions across clients, showing how FedHGN's performance degrades or adapts to such scenarios and whether the coefficients alignment mechanism remains effective

## Limitations
- Limited evaluation scope: Experiments only cover three heterogeneous graph datasets with relatively simple schema structures
- Fixed hyperparameter choices: The number of bases (B=20) and other hyperparameters are set empirically without systematic optimization
- Scalability concerns: The framework's performance and communication efficiency with large numbers of clients remains unexplored

## Confidence

- Overall claims about performance improvements: Medium confidence - Results show consistent improvements but are based on a limited set of datasets and hyperparameters
