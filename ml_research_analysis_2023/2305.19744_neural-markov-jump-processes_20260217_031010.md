# Neural Markov Jump Processes

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.19744
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19744
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: NeuralMJP successfully infers hidden Markov jump processes and predicts future events using neural ODE-based variational inference

## Executive Summary
Neural Markov Jump Processes (NeuralMJP) introduces a variational inference algorithm for continuous-time Markov jump processes that leverages neural ordinary differential equations. The method learns continuous-time representations of observed data to approximate posterior process dynamics while training prior rates using a generative adversarial network approach. Experiments demonstrate NeuralMJP's ability to handle irregularly sampled data and capture both long-term and short-term dynamics across synthetic, ion channel, and molecular dynamics datasets.

## Method Summary
NeuralMJP employs a variational inference framework where neural ODEs approximate the time-dependent transition rates of the posterior MJP. The algorithm uses a parametric approximation for both initial state distribution and transition rates, trained through backpropagation. A key innovation is the use of a generative adversarial network-like approach to train the time-independent rates of the prior process. The method can handle irregularly sampled data and operates in continuous time without requiring discretization.

## Key Results
- Successfully infers hidden Markov jump processes on synthetic datasets
- Demonstrates superior performance on irregularly sampled data compared to BD-FKER benchmark
- Captures both long-term and short-term dynamics in molecular dynamics applications
- Shows bias toward fitting faster time scales in multi-scale systems

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
NeuralMJP works by combining continuous-time process modeling with neural network function approximation. The neural ODE framework enables exact likelihood computation for continuous trajectories while maintaining differentiability for end-to-end training. The variational approach allows tractable inference in high-dimensional state spaces by learning compact representations of the posterior distribution.

## Foundational Learning
- Markov jump processes: Stochastic processes with discrete states and exponentially distributed waiting times between jumps. Essential for modeling systems with discrete states and continuous time evolution.
- Neural ODEs: Neural networks that parameterize the derivative of hidden states, enabling continuous-depth models. Required for learning smooth, continuous-time representations of MJP dynamics.
- Variational inference: Approximate Bayesian inference using parametric families to approximate posterior distributions. Necessary for tractable inference in complex MJP models.
- Generative adversarial networks: Framework for training generative models through adversarial optimization. Used here to train prior process rates without explicit likelihood computation.
- Backpropagation through ODE solvers: Technique for computing gradients of ODE solutions with respect to parameters. Enables end-to-end training of the neural MJP model.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Observed data -> Neural ODE encoder -> Posterior initial distribution and rates -> Prior generator (GAN) -> MJP simulator -> Likelihood computation

Critical path: Data input → Neural ODE parameterization → Posterior approximation → Prior rate training → Likelihood evaluation → Parameter update

Design tradeoffs: The method trades exact inference for computational tractability by using neural approximations. This enables handling of large state spaces but may introduce approximation errors, particularly for complex multi-scale dynamics.

Failure signatures: Poor performance on multi-scale systems where fast and slow dynamics coexist. Difficulty capturing rare transitions or events. Potential mode collapse in the GAN component training prior rates.

Three first experiments:
1. Validate basic inference on simple 2-state synthetic MJP with known ground truth
2. Test handling of irregular sampling by comparing against ground truth on subsampled data
3. Evaluate sensitivity to neural network architecture choices (depth, width, activation functions)

## Limitations
- Only tested on small-scale problems (4-10 states) limiting scalability assessment
- Performance validation relies entirely on synthetic datasets with known ground truth
- Exhibits bias toward faster time scales, potentially missing important slow dynamics
- Limited comparison against diverse inference methods beyond single BD-FKER benchmark
