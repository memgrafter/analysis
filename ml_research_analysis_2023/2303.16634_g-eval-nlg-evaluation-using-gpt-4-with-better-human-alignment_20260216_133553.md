# G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.16634
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces G-Eval, a framework for using large language models (LLMs) with chain-of-thoughts and a form-filling paradigm to evaluate the quality of natural language generation (NLG) outputs. The method involves generating a chain-of-thought of detailed evaluation steps and using the prompt along with the generated CoT to evaluate NLG outputs in a form-filling paradigm. The probabilities of the output rating tokens are used to refine the final metric. Experiments on text summarization and dialogue generation tasks show that G-Eval with GPT-4 as the backbone model achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.514 with human judgments on the summarization task, outperforming all previous methods by a large margin. However, the paper also highlights the potential issue of LLM-based evaluators having a bias towards LLM-generated texts, which may lead to the self-reinforcement of LLMs if LLM-based metrics are used as the reward signal for improving themselves.

## Method Summary
G-Eval is a framework for evaluating NLG outputs using LLMs with chain-of-thoughts (CoT) and a form-filling paradigm. The method involves generating a CoT that outlines specific evaluation steps for the given task, using the prompt along with the generated CoT to evaluate NLG outputs, and computing a probability-weighted summation of discrete scores to obtain a more fine-grained evaluation score. GPT-4 is used as the backbone model, which achieves higher correlation with human judgments compared to GPT-3.5 and other baseline evaluators. The evaluation is conducted on three meta-evaluation benchmarks: SummEval, Topical-Chat, and QAGS for text summarization and dialogue generation tasks.

## Key Results
- G-Eval with GPT-4 achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.514 with human judgments on the summarization task, outperforming all previous methods by a large margin.
- GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5 and other baseline evaluators on QAGS, with a large margin on QAGS-Xsum.
- G-Eval shows the potential issue of LLM-based evaluators having a bias towards LLM-generated texts, which may lead to the self-reinforcement of LLMs if used as reward signals.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Chain-of-thoughts (CoT) generation by the LLM improves evaluation performance by providing detailed intermediate reasoning steps.
- Mechanism: The LLM first generates a CoT that outlines specific evaluation steps for the given task. This CoT is then used as additional context when evaluating the NLG output, guiding the LLM's judgment process and making it more systematic and thorough.
- Core assumption: LLMs can generate useful and relevant CoT for evaluation tasks when prompted appropriately, and these CoTs improve the quality of the final evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Auto Chain-of-Thoughts for NLG Evaluation... The chain-of-thoughts (CoT) is a sequence of intermediate representations that are generated by the LLM during the text generation process... We find that LLM can generate such evaluation steps by itself."
  - [section] "Table 1 shows that GPTEVAL-4 with CoT has higher correlation than GPTEVAL-4 without CoT on all dimensions, especially for fluency."
- Break condition: If the LLM generates irrelevant, incorrect, or unhelpful CoT, or if the CoT generation process fails or is too resource-intensive.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Probability-weighted summation of discrete scores provides more fine-grained and continuous evaluation scores compared to integer-only outputs.
- Mechanism: Instead of outputting a single integer score, the LLM is prompted to output a probability distribution over the possible scores. The final score is computed as a weighted sum of the discrete scores using their respective probabilities.
- Core assumption: The probabilities assigned by the LLM to different scores reflect the confidence in each score and can be meaningfully combined to produce a more nuanced final score.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "To address these issues, we propose using the probabilities of output tokens from LLMs to normalize the scores and take their weighted summation as the final results."
  - [section] "This method obtains more fine-grained, continuous scores that better reflect the quality and diversity of the generated texts."
- Break condition: If the probability distribution is uniform or if the LLM consistently outputs very low entropy distributions, the weighted sum will not provide meaningful differentiation.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: GPT-4 as the backbone model achieves higher correlation with human judgments compared to GPT-3.5 and other baseline evaluators.
- Mechanism: GPT-4, being a larger and more capable model, is better at understanding the evaluation criteria and generating accurate judgments that align with human preferences.
- Core assumption: Model size and capability directly translate to better evaluation performance, especially for complex tasks like NLG evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "GPTEVAL-4 achieved much higher human correspondence compared with GPTEVAL-3.5 on both Spearman and Kendall-Tau correlation."
  - [section] "GPTEVAL-4 outperforms all state-of-the-art evaluators on QAGS, with a large margin on QAGS-Xsum."
- Break condition: If the evaluation task is simple enough that the difference between GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 is negligible, or if the task requires domain-specific knowledge that neither model possesses.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Chain-of-thought prompting
  - Why needed here: CoT allows the LLM to break down the evaluation task into systematic steps, improving the quality and consistency of the evaluation.
  - Quick check question: How would you prompt an LLM to generate a CoT for evaluating the coherence of a summary?

- Concept: Probability-weighted scoring
  - Why needed here: Standard LLM outputs are discrete, but evaluation often requires continuous scores to capture subtle differences. Probability weighting allows us to extract more information from the LLM's output.
  - Quick check question: How do you compute a weighted sum from a probability distribution over discrete scores?

- Concept: Form-filling paradigm for evaluation
  - Why needed here: Form-filling structures the LLM's output in a consistent and parseable format, making it easier to extract scores and other information.
  - Quick check question: What are the advantages of using a form-filling paradigm compared to free-form generation for evaluation tasks?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Prompt Generator -> CoT Generator -> Evaluator -> Probability Processor -> Final Score
- Critical path: Prompt → CoT Generation → Evaluation → Probability Processing → Final Score
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using GPT-4 vs. GPT-3.5: Higher accuracy vs. higher cost and slower inference.
  - Using CoT vs. direct evaluation: More context and guidance vs. increased complexity and potential for irrelevant CoT.
  - Probability weighting vs. direct scoring: More fine-grained scores vs. potential for unreliable probability estimates.
- Failure signatures:
  - Low variance in scores: Could indicate that the evaluation criteria are too vague or that the LLM is not sensitive enough to differences in quality.
  - High correlation with LLM-generated outputs: Could indicate a bias in the evaluator towards LLM-generated text.
  - Slow evaluation times: Could indicate that the CoT generation or evaluation process is too complex.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate a simple summarization task with and without CoT to measure the impact of CoT on correlation with human judgments.
  2. Compare the performance of GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 on a standard NLG evaluation benchmark.
  3. Test the probability weighting mechanism by comparing the correlation of probability-weighted scores vs. direct integer scores on a dataset with subtle quality differences.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
The paper identifies a critical limitation: LLMs may exhibit bias toward LLM-generated texts, potentially leading to self-reinforcement when used as reward signals. This raises questions about the long-term reliability of LLM-based evaluators. Additionally, the exact prompt formats and probability normalization methods are not fully specified, making precise reproduction challenging. The evaluation focuses primarily on summarization and dialogue tasks, leaving open questions about generalizability to other NLG domains.

## Limitations
- LLM-based evaluators may have a bias towards LLM-generated texts, potentially leading to self-reinforcement when used as reward signals.
- The exact prompt formats and probability normalization methods are not fully specified, making precise reproduction challenging.
- The evaluation focuses primarily on summarization and dialogue tasks, leaving open questions about generalizability to other NLG domains.

## Confidence
- High Confidence: The experimental results showing GPT-4 outperforming GPT-3.5 and achieving higher correlation with human judgments are well-supported by the data presented.
- Medium Confidence: The claim that probability-weighted scoring provides more fine-grained evaluation is supported by the experiments, but the robustness of this approach across different tasks and quality distributions could be further validated.
- Low Confidence: The assertion that G-Eval can serve as a reliable reward signal for improving LLMs is questionable given the identified bias toward LLM-generated outputs.

## Next Checks
1. **Bias Validation**: Conduct a controlled experiment comparing G-Eval scores for human-written versus LLM-generated summaries to quantify and characterize any systematic bias toward LLM outputs.
2. **Cross-Domain Generalization**: Test G-Eval on NLG tasks beyond summarization and dialogue (e.g., story generation, code generation) to assess whether the chain-of-thought and form-filling approach generalizes across different domains.
3. **Robustness to Quality Distribution**: Evaluate G-Eval's performance on datasets with varying quality distributions (e.g., predominantly low-quality vs. mixed-quality outputs) to determine if the probability-weighted scoring remains reliable when quality differences are subtle or pronounced.
