# UIT-OpenViIC: A Novel Benchmark for Evaluating Image Captioning in Vietnamese

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.04166
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04166
- **Reference count**: 35
- **Key outcome**: Introduces UIT-OpenViIC, first large-scale Vietnamese image captioning dataset with 13,100 images and 61,241 manually annotated captions, demonstrating significant performance gaps for Transformer models compared to English benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces UIT-OpenViIC, the first large-scale Vietnamese image captioning dataset containing 13,100 images and 61,241 manually annotated captions. The dataset was created under strict supervision to ensure high-quality, diverse descriptions reflecting Vietnamese daily life and culture. Experiments with recent Transformer-based models (e.g., MDSANet, AoANet, RSTNet) showed significant performance gaps compared to English benchmarks, highlighting the dataset's challenging nature. The authors also propose CAMO (Cross-Attention on Multi-level Encoder Outputs), a module that enhances visual feature fusion across encoder layers, improving caption quality especially for simpler models. Results demonstrate UIT-OpenViIC as a valuable benchmark for advancing Vietnamese image captioning research.

## Method Summary
The UIT-OpenViIC dataset was created with 13,100 images and 61,241 Vietnamese captions, using pretrained ResNeXt-152++ for visual features and Vietnamese NLP tools for processing. The paper evaluates Transformer-based captioning models (ORT, M2, AoANet, RSTNet, DLCT, DIFNet, MDSANet) trained with cross-entropy and SCST losses. The CAMO module was implemented to fuse multi-level encoder outputs through cross-attention, with hyperparameters α and β controlling information preservation and fusion. Training used a single RTX 2080Ti with Adam optimizer and dataset-specific learning rate schedulers.

## Key Results
- UIT-OpenViIC dataset contains 13,100 images with 61,241 Vietnamese captions, the largest Vietnamese image captioning benchmark to date
- Transformer models trained on MS-COCO show significant performance degradation on UIT-OpenViIC, proving the dataset's challenging nature
- CAMO module improves captioning quality, particularly for simpler models, by effectively fusing multi-level encoder outputs
- Performance metrics (BLEU@1/2/3/4, METEOR, ROUGE, CIDEr) on UIT-OpenViIC validation/test sets reveal substantial room for improvement compared to English benchmarks

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The UIT-OpenViIC dataset challenges existing Transformer-based models due to its domain-specific Vietnamese cultural context.
- Mechanism: Images in UIT-OpenViIC capture daily life and culture in Vietnam, which differs significantly from the Western-centric MS-COCO dataset. This domain shift makes visual and linguistic patterns harder for models trained on English datasets to generalize to.
- Core assumption: Vietnamese image captioning requires culturally relevant visual features and linguistic patterns not present in standard English benchmarks.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Recent captioning models trained on the MS-COCO Caption dataset only have good performance in language patterns of English; they do not have such good performance in contexts captured in Vietnam or fluently caption images using Vietnamese."
  - [section]: "In spite of having a larger number of images, VieCap4H [14] only focused on healthcare contexts, and the ratio of captions per image was meager (1.15)."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - only 25 related papers found, with 0 citations on average, suggesting limited prior work in this specific Vietnamese domain.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: The CAMO module improves captioning quality by effectively fusing multi-level encoder outputs through cross-attention.
- Mechanism: CAMO learns joint representations from all encoder layers (not just the final one) and uses cross-self-attention to let each layer attend to the previous layer's output. This augments representation ability while preserving original information through skip connections.
- Core assumption: Low-level encoder outputs contain valuable information that gets lost when only using the final encoder output, and cross-attention can effectively combine this information.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we present a CAMO approach that effectively enhances the image representation ability by a multi-level encoder output fusion mechanism"
  - [section]: "We propose the CAMO module, which effectively fuses all low to high-level encoder outputs, to enhance the ability of encoded outputs during the decoding scheme."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - limited citations suggest this specific fusion approach is novel and not yet widely validated.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The dataset's Vietnamese language complexity, including non-visual words and cultural context, creates a challenging benchmark that reveals limitations of existing models.
- Mechanism: Vietnamese has unique linguistic features and cultural references that are not captured in English datasets. Models struggle with these non-visual words and cultural context, leading to lower performance metrics.
- Core assumption: Language-specific features and cultural context significantly impact image captioning performance beyond just visual feature extraction.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "the modest results prove that UIT-OpenViIC has room to grow, which can be one of the standard benchmarks in Vietnamese"
  - [section]: "Vietnamese vocabulary contains various non-visual syllables, and the appearance frequency is much less than the MS-COCO dataset, which could confuse the Language Adaptive Attention of RSTNet"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - limited related work suggests this is an underexplored area.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Image feature extractor (ResNeXt-152++ for region and grid features) -> Transformer encoder with N layers -> CAMO module (fuses multi-level encoder outputs) -> Transformer decoder (generates captions) -> Vietnamese language model (for tokenization and post-processing)

- Critical path:
  1. Extract visual features from input image
  2. Pass features through transformer encoder layers
  3. Apply CAMO module to fuse multi-level outputs
  4. Feed fused representation to transformer decoder
  5. Generate Vietnamese caption using decoder

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using region-based vs. grid-based features: Region features capture object-specific information but require more computational resources
  - CAMO hyperparameters (α, β): Balance between preserving original information and incorporating fused representations
  - Training strategy: Two-stage approach (cross-entropy + SCST) vs. single-stage training

- Failure signatures:
  - Low CIDEr scores on Vietnamese test set compared to English benchmarks
  - Attention maps focusing on irrelevant regions or missing key objects
  - Generated captions missing cultural context or using incorrect Vietnamese vocabulary

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Compare baseline transformer performance on UIT-OpenViIC vs. MS-COCO to establish domain difficulty
  2. Evaluate different α, β combinations in CAMO to find optimal fusion parameters
  3. Test region-based vs. grid-based features with CAMO to determine which visual representation works best for Vietnamese context

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of image captioning models vary when trained on UIT-OpenViIC versus MS-COCO Caption, and what specific challenges in Vietnamese captioning are revealed?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors show that models perform significantly worse on UIT-OpenViIC compared to MS-COCO, highlighting the dataset's challenging nature.
- Why unresolved: While the performance gap is noted, a detailed comparative analysis of model behavior, error types, and linguistic/cultural factors contributing to the gap is not provided.
- What evidence would resolve it: A systematic error analysis comparing model outputs on both datasets, including qualitative examples and metrics breakdown by linguistic features (e.g., syntax, semantics).

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the CAMO module specifically improve visual feature fusion, and what are its limitations compared to other attention-based fusion methods?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors propose CAMO and show it improves captioning quality, especially for simpler models, but do not extensively compare it to other fusion methods.
- Why unresolved: The paper presents CAMO's effectiveness but lacks a comprehensive comparison with alternative attention mechanisms or fusion strategies.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical comparisons of CAMO against other fusion methods (e.g., self-attention, cross-attention) on multiple datasets, with ablation studies on different encoder layers.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the long-term impacts of using English-centric pre-trained models (e.g., object detectors, segmentation models) on the performance of Vietnamese image captioning models?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that using pre-trained models trained on English datasets may fail on UIT-OpenViIC due to complex scenes in Vietnam, suggesting a limitation.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the specific ways in which English-centric models affect Vietnamese captioning performance or potential solutions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments training Vietnamese-specific object detectors and segmentation models, and retraining captioning models using these, to measure performance improvements.

## Limitations

- Limited related work in Vietnamese image captioning domain makes it difficult to establish comprehensive baselines and contextualize results
- Performance gap attribution to cultural and linguistic factors lacks quantitative analysis of individual contribution factors
- CAMO module effectiveness demonstrated but lacks ablation studies and comparison with alternative fusion methods

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: Dataset creation and basic statistics are well-documented and explicitly described.
**Medium Confidence**: Performance gap between models on UIT-OpenViIC versus English benchmarks is demonstrated but attribution to cultural/linguistic factors is less certain.
**Low Confidence**: CAMO module effectiveness is supported by results but lacks ablation studies and comprehensive comparison with alternatives.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study for CAMO Module**: Conduct experiments removing the CAMO module from the best-performing model while keeping all other components constant, then compare performance metrics to isolate the specific contribution of multi-level encoder fusion.

2. **Cultural Context Analysis**: Design a study where human evaluators assess the cultural relevance of generated captions versus ground truth, and correlate these assessments with model performance metrics to provide direct evidence for cultural context challenges.

3. **Domain Transfer Experiment**: Train the same models on UIT-OpenViIC and evaluate them on MS-COCO (with Vietnamese translation of captions), and vice versa, to quantify the actual domain shift between Vietnamese and English image captioning tasks through bidirectional transfer learning.
