# Contrastive Graph Clustering in Curvature Spaces

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.03555
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03555
- Reference count: 22
- Introduces first heterogeneous curvature space for graph clustering with augmentation-free reweighted geometric loss

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces CONGREGATE, a novel approach to graph clustering that operates in heterogeneous curvature spaces rather than Euclidean space. The method leverages Ricci curvature computation on graph edges and employs an end-to-end contrastive learning framework with a reweighted geometric loss function. By modeling region-specific geometric properties through a product space of multiple learnable factor manifolds, the approach achieves state-of-the-art clustering performance on four real-world attributed graphs.

## Method Summary
CONGREGATE operates by first computing Ricci curvatures on graph edges as preprocessing, then learning node representations through multiple fully Riemannian GCNs (fRGCNs) that generate views in different factor manifolds. These views are combined into a heterogeneous curvature space - a product of learnable factor manifolds with different curvatures and a free coordinate space. The model is trained using an augmentation-free reweighted contrastive loss that emphasizes both hard positives and hard negatives, jointly learning discriminative node encodings and cluster centroids.

## Key Results
- Achieves state-of-the-art clustering performance on Cora, Citeseer, MAG-CS, and Amazon-Photo datasets
- Outperforms 19 strong baseline methods across multiple metrics
- 5-factor variant (with d0=32, d1=32, d2=16, d3=16, d4=16) consistently outperforms 4-factor variant
- Demonstrates the effectiveness of heterogeneous curvature modeling in graph clustering

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1: Heterogeneous Curvature Space
The product space PH = ⊗M m=0Mcm,dm m combines multiple learnable factor manifolds with different curvatures and a free coordinate space, enabling fine-grained clustering by modeling region-specific geometric properties. Each factor manifold can have its own curvature characteristics, allowing different regions of the graph to have distinct geometric properties.

### Mechanism 2: Reweighted Contrastive Loss
The dual reweighting function W(zm i ,z0 j ) = |πi⊤πj − Sim(ˆzm i ,z0 j )|β increases the loss contribution from node pairs that are similar in representation but belong to different clusters (hard negatives) or dissimilar in representation but belong to the same cluster (hard positives). This hardness-aware weighting improves clustering by focusing on challenging sample pairs.

### Mechanism 3: Ricci Curvature Clustering
Positive Ricci curvature indicates dense connections within clusters while negative Ricci curvature indicates sparse connections between clusters. This provides geometric supervision for clustering without requiring data augmentation, as Ricci curvatures can be computed efficiently as preprocessing.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input Graph → Ricci Curvature Computation → Multiple fRGCNs → Product Space → Reweighted Contrastive Loss → Cluster Assignments
- Critical path: Input → fRGCN → Product Space → Reweighted Contrast → Cluster Assignments
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Number of factor manifolds (M+1) vs. computational cost
  - Curvature heterogeneity vs. model complexity
  - Hard sample weighting (β) vs. stability of training
  - Manifold preserving operations vs. representational power
- Failure signatures:
  - Training instability: Check β parameter and reweighting function
  - Poor clustering quality: Verify Ricci curvature computation and product space construction
  - Slow convergence: Examine the number of factor manifolds and learning rates
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Ablation study: Remove fRGCN and replace with GATc to test fully Riemannian operations
  2. Dimensionality sensitivity: Vary the dimensionality of factor manifolds (32, 32, 16, 16 vs. other combinations)
  3. Reweighting ablation: Remove hard positive weighting (keep only hard negative) to isolate effects of dual reweighting

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the number of factor manifolds (M) in the heterogeneous curvature space affect the performance and scalability of CONGREGATE?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that "more factors give further flexibility for the fine-grained curvature modeling" and that the 5-factor variant outperformed the 4-factor variant on Cora and Citeseer datasets.
- Why unresolved: The paper only compares 4 and 5 factors. The optimal number of factors likely depends on the dataset size and graph structure, but this relationship is not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying M across a range of values on diverse datasets, measuring both clustering performance and computational efficiency.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can the heterogeneous curvature space and geometric clustering approach be extended to dynamic graphs where node relationships change over time?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper focuses on static attributed graphs. The formulation of Ricci curvature and the construction of the heterogeneous curvature space are for a fixed graph structure.
- Why unresolved: Dynamic graphs introduce challenges like temporal consistency of embeddings and adapting to changing curvatures, which are not addressed in the static setting.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development of a temporal extension of CONGREGATE, with theoretical analysis of how Ricci curvatures evolve over time and experimental validation on dynamic graph datasets.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is CONGREGATE to the choice of the curvature estimation MLP (Equation 9) and its architecture?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a "multilayer perceptron (MLP) to approximate the map" for estimating node curvatures, but does not discuss the impact of different architectures.
- Why unresolved: The MLP is a key component for bridging the gap between the heterogeneous curvature space and the graph's Ricci curvatures. Its design choices could significantly impact performance.
- What evidence would resolve it: Ablation studies varying the MLP architecture (number of layers, hidden units, activation functions) and analyzing the impact on clustering performance and curvature estimation accuracy.

## Limitations
- Limited empirical validation of heterogeneous curvature advantage over homogeneous alternatives
- No systematic sensitivity analysis for the reweighting parameter β
- Computational scalability concerns for large graphs due to Ricci curvature computation and Riemannian operations

## Confidence
- High Confidence: Mathematical framework of product spaces and Riemannian manifolds is sound
- Medium Confidence: Reweighted contrastive loss mechanism is theoretically justified but lacks extensive empirical validation
- Low Confidence: Specific advantage of heterogeneous curvature modeling over simpler approaches is not directly demonstrated

## Next Checks
1. **Ablation Study**: Remove the heterogeneous curvature space and replace with a single manifold of equivalent total dimension. Compare clustering performance to isolate the contribution of curvature heterogeneity versus simply having more representational capacity.

2. **Hyperparameter Sensitivity**: Conduct a systematic grid search over β values (0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0) and analyze how clustering performance varies. Include plots showing the trade-off between hard sample emphasis and training stability.

3. **Scalability Benchmark**: Test the model on progressively larger graphs (e.g., 10K, 100K, 1M nodes) and measure both clustering quality and training time. Compare against simpler baselines to quantify the computational overhead of the geometric operations.
