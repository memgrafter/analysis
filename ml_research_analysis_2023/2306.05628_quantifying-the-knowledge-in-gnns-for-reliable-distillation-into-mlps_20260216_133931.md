# Quantifying the Knowledge in GNNs for Reliable Distillation into MLPs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05628
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05628
- Reference count: 19
- Key outcome: This paper addresses the under-confidence problem in GNN-to-MLP knowledge distillation, where distilled MLPs tend to make less confident predictions than teacher GNNs. The authors identify that this is due to a lack of reliable supervision from teacher GNNs, and propose a method to quantify the reliability of knowledge points in GNNs based on their invariance to noise perturbations. They then use this reliability measure to sample additional reliable knowledge points as supervision for training student MLPs. Experiments on 7 datasets show that the proposed method improves over vanilla MLPs by 12.62% and outperforms teacher GNNs by 2.16% on average.

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the under-confidence problem in GNN-to-MLP knowledge distillation, where student MLPs fail to match the prediction confidence of their teacher GNNs. The authors identify that this stems from missing neighborhood regularization and unreliable supervision from teacher GNNs. They propose a method to quantify knowledge reliability in GNNs by measuring entropy invariance to noise perturbations, then use this to sample reliable knowledge points for additional supervision during MLP training. The approach significantly improves MLP performance across seven real-world graph datasets.

## Method Summary
The authors propose a knowledge distillation framework that first quantifies the reliability of each node in a pre-trained teacher GNN by measuring the invariance of its information entropy to Gaussian noise perturbations. This reliability measure is used to initialize a sampling probability model based on a learnable power distribution. During training, the student MLP receives supervision from labeled nodes (classification loss) and additional reliable knowledge points sampled from neighborhoods (distillation loss). The sampling probabilities are dynamically updated using momentum-based learning to prioritize reliable knowledge points. This creates a self-improving loop where more reliable knowledge points are sampled as training progresses.

## Key Results
- The proposed method improves over vanilla MLPs by 12.62% on average across 7 datasets
- Student MLPs outperform their teacher GNNs by 2.16% on average
- The approach is effective on both small citation networks and large-scale academic graphs like ogbn-arxiv

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GNN-to-MLP distillation suffers from under-confidence because student MLPs lack the neighborhood regularization that GNNs receive through message passing.
- Mechanism: Message passing in GNNs implicitly performs Dirichlet energy minimization on label distributions between neighboring nodes, creating additional self-supervision that MLPs miss. Without this regularization, MLPs produce less confident predictions even when correct.
- Core assumption: The under-confidence problem is fundamentally caused by missing neighborhood-based regularization, not just architectural differences.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we identify a potential under-confidence problem for GNN-to-MLP distillation, i.e., the distilled MLPs may not be able to make predictions as confidently as teacher GNNs"
  - [section 4.1] "we described in detail what it represents, how it arises, what impact it has, and how to deal with it"
  - [corpus] Weak - the corpus mentions similar distillation approaches but doesn't specifically discuss the under-confidence mechanism

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Knowledge points in GNNs have varying reliability that can be quantified by measuring entropy invariance to noise perturbations.
- Mechanism: Reliable knowledge points maintain stable information entropy when subject to Gaussian noise perturbations, while unreliable points show high entropy variance. This allows filtering out noisy supervision during distillation.
- Core assumption: Entropy invariance to perturbations correlates with knowledge reliability for distillation purposes.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we first quantify the knowledge reliability in GNN by measuring the invariance of their information entropy to noise perturbations"
  - [section 4.2] "we propose to quantify the reliability of a knowledge point (node) vi ∈ V in GNNs by measuring the invariance of its information entropy to noise perturbations"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Student MLPs learn from highly reliable knowledge points first during training, then gradually from less reliable ones.
- Mechanism: The distillation process has temporal dynamics where MLPs initially fit to stable, reliable knowledge before adapting to noisier supervision. This suggests sampling additional reliable points can accelerate learning.
- Core assumption: The order in which MLPs learn from different knowledge points follows a reliability-based pattern.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Teacher GNN (pre-trained) -> Knowledge quantification module -> Sampling probability model -> Student MLP -> Momentum-based power updating

- Critical path:
  1. Pre-train teacher GNN on labeled data
  2. Quantify knowledge reliability using noise perturbation experiments
  3. Initialize sampling probability model with fixed parameters
  4. For each training epoch:
     - Compute node representations from teacher and student
     - Update sampling probabilities using momentum-based learning
     - Sample reliable neighbors and compute additional distillation loss
     - Update student parameters with combined loss

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Sampling reliability vs. computational cost: More reliable sampling improves performance but increases computation
  - Fixed vs. learnable power parameters: Learnable parameters adapt better to dataset characteristics but require additional tuning
  - Number of sampled neighbors: More neighbors provide better supervision but increase training time

- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance despite reliable sampling: Could indicate teacher GNN isn't well-trained or noise level is inappropriate
  - Slow convergence: May suggest momentum rate is too low or initial sampling probabilities are poorly calibrated
  - Overfitting on small datasets: Could indicate the additional supervision is too strong relative to the available data

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline comparison: Run vanilla MLP and GLNN on a small dataset to establish performance floor
  2. Ablation study: Test KRD with fixed vs. learnable power parameters to verify momentum updating helps
  3. Noise sensitivity: Vary the Gaussian noise variance parameter to find optimal reliability quantification

## Limitations
- The reliability quantification method relies on entropy invariance to noise perturbations, which may not generalize well to graphs with different structural properties or noise characteristics
- The method requires pre-training teacher GNNs, adding computational overhead to the overall pipeline
- The sampling strategy's effectiveness depends heavily on the learnable power distribution parameters, which aren't fully explored across datasets
