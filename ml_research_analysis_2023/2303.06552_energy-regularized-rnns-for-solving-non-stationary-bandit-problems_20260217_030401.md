# Energy Regularized RNNs for Solving Non-Stationary Bandit Problems

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a recurrent neural network (RNN) approach for non-stationary multi-armed bandit problems where rewards depend on past actions and contexts. The method combines an RNN to model sequences of actions and contexts with an energy regularization term that limits the gap between maximal and minimal action probabilities, balancing exploration and exploitation. The approach is tested across multiple benchmark problems including Bernoulli, time-dependent, Wheel, and Rotting Bandits, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing methods.

## Method Summary
The proposed method uses a recurrent neural network to model sequences of actions and contexts in non-stationary bandit environments. The key innovation is an energy regularization term added to the loss function, which constrains the difference between the highest and lowest action probabilities output by the network. This regularization encourages exploration by preventing the model from becoming too confident in any single action while still allowing exploitation of learned patterns. The RNN processes sequences of context-action-reward tuples, maintaining an internal state that captures temporal dependencies in the reward structure.

## Key Results
- Outperforms existing approaches like SWA, Bernoulli Bandit, and NeuralLinear in terms of convergence speed and cumulative regret
- Shows superior performance across both stationary and non-stationary scenarios
- Energy regularization is more effective than alternatives like epsilon-greedy or softmax temperature scaling

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by using an RNN to capture temporal dependencies in the reward structure while the energy regularization term prevents premature convergence to suboptimal actions. The RNN maintains a hidden state that evolves as new context-action-reward sequences are observed, allowing it to model complex dependencies between past actions and current rewards. The energy regularization ensures that the model maintains sufficient uncertainty across all actions, preventing it from becoming overconfident in any single choice before sufficient exploration has occurred.

- Regularization in neural networks: Techniques to prevent overfitting by constraining model complexity. Why needed: Energy regularization is a novel form of regularization specifically designed for bandit exploration.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component map: Context/Action Sequence -> RNN Encoder -> Energy Regularized Output -> Action Selection

Critical path: Input sequence → RNN hidden state update → Energy regularization constraint → Action probability distribution → Action selection

Design tradeoffs: The energy regularization strength must be carefully tuned to balance exploration and exploitation. Too much regularization leads to excessive exploration, while too little results in premature convergence. The RNN architecture (depth, width, cell type) affects the model's ability to capture temporal dependencies without overfitting.

Failure signatures: If energy regularization is too strong, the model will explore excessively and never converge. If too weak, the model will converge prematurely to suboptimal actions. Poor RNN architecture choices can lead to either insufficient memory of past actions or overfitting to noise in the reward signal.
