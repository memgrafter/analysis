# Word Embeddings for Banking Industry

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01807
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01807
- Reference count: 0
- Domain-specific embeddings outperform general embeddings in capturing banking-specific semantics

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates the effectiveness of domain-specific word embeddings for the banking industry by constructing embeddings from a corpus of 1.2 million customer complaints. The study compares these domain-specific embeddings against general-purpose embeddings using human-annotated word relatedness scores. The central finding demonstrates that bank-specific embeddings, particularly those generated through LSA with autoencoder dimensionality reduction, achieve significantly higher Spearman correlation (0.51 vs 0.44 baseline) in capturing banking-specific semantic relationships. The research suggests that domain-specific embeddings can enhance various NLP tasks in banking and recommends further exploration of hyperparameter tuning and integration with pretrained models.

## Method Summary
The method involves constructing a bank-specific corpus from 5% of 1.2 million customer complaints, generating a 38K x 38K co-occurrence matrix with a context window of 10, and applying PPMI weighting. Dimensionality reduction techniques including LSA, autoencoders, and LSTM are used to create embeddings, which are then compared against general embeddings from GloVe, Word2Vec, BERT, and ADA-002. Evaluation uses 1.8K human-annotated word pairs scored for relatedness, with Spearman correlation measuring the alignment between human judgment and embedding-derived similarity. The study also employs clustering analysis to verify that highly related word pairs cluster together in the embedding space.

## Key Results
- Bank-specific embeddings achieved Spearman correlation of 0.51 versus 0.44 for general embeddings
- LSA with autoencoder dimensionality reduction performed best among the techniques tested
- Domain-specific embeddings better captured banking-specific semantics like "credit" and "line" or "overdraft" and "fees"

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Domain-specific corpora capture semantic relationships unique to the domain better than general corpora.
- Mechanism: Bank-specific word embeddings are constructed using a co-occurrence matrix from banking complaints, capturing domain-specific word associations like "credit" and "line" or "overdraft" and "fees".
- Core assumption: Words that frequently appear together in domain-specific contexts carry domain-specific semantic meaning.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "word embeddings built from bank-specific corpora does a better job of capturing the bank-specific semantics and word relatedness"
  - [section]: "The central finding is that the word embeddings built from bank-specific corpora better captures the word relatedness of the word-word pairs in the test dataset."
  - [corpus]: Corpus signals show high FMR (0.538) and citations (0.2) for neighbor papers, suggesting domain relevance and scholarly interest.
- Break condition: If the co-occurrence matrix does not reflect true semantic relationships (e.g., due to data sparsity or noise), the embeddings will fail to capture domain semantics accurately.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Dimensionality reduction techniques like LSA, autoencoders, and LSTM architectures can effectively compress co-occurrence information into meaningful embeddings.
- Mechanism: The 38K x 38K co-occurrence matrix is reduced using LSA (100/300/500 dimensions), autoencoders (500→300 dimensions), and LSTM to produce embeddings that retain semantic relationships.
- Core assumption: Dimensionality reduction preserves the most salient semantic information while reducing noise.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "A bank-specific corpus of 1.2 million customer complaints is used to generate co-occurrence matrices and embeddings via various dimensionality reduction techniques"
  - [section]: "Several techniques need to be explored to identify a set of embeddings that best capture the word relatedness."
  - [corpus]: The corpus size (1.2M complaints) and vocabulary (38K) provide sufficient data for dimensionality reduction to be effective.
- Break condition: If the dimensionality reduction technique is not suitable for the data structure, important semantic relationships may be lost.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Human-annotated word relatedness scores provide a reliable ground truth for evaluating embedding quality.
- Mechanism: Three bankers scored 1.8K word pairs on a scale of 0 (no relatedness) to 1 (completely related), which is used to evaluate Spearman correlation between human judgment and embedding-derived similarity.
- Core assumption: Human experts can accurately judge semantic relatedness in the banking domain.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "These are compared against general embeddings from GloVe, Word2Vec, BERT, and ADA-002 using human-annotated word relatedness scores"
  - [section]: "Scores range between 0 and 1, where 0 is no relatedness and 1 is completely related. This scored dataset is used to evaluate models"
  - [corpus]: The corpus provides the vocabulary and context for generating word pairs that are then annotated by humans.
- Break condition: If the human annotations are inconsistent or biased, the evaluation will not accurately reflect embedding quality.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do domain-specific word embeddings impact downstream NLP tasks beyond word relatedness evaluation?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that bank-specific embeddings could improve NLP tasks like sentiment analysis and information retrieval, and suggests incorporating them with pretrained models for such tasks.
- Why unresolved: The study focused on evaluating word relatedness scores rather than directly testing performance on specific NLP tasks.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal dimensionality for domain-specific embeddings that balances performance and computational efficiency?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper found that larger embedding dimensions (500 vs 100 for LSA, 300 vs 100 for GloVe) performed better, but didn't explore the full hyperparameter space or efficiency trade-offs.
- Why unresolved: The study conducted minimal hyperparameter tuning and only compared a few dimension sizes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of embedding performance across a range of dimensions (e.g., 50, 100, 200, 300, 500) while measuring both accuracy and computational costs.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do domain-specific embeddings handle out-of-vocabulary words compared to pretrained contextual models?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper suggests leveraging pretrained contextual models to provide embeddings for out-of-vocabulary words as future work.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated embeddings for the vocabulary present in the bank-specific corpus, not addressing OOV word handling.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of OOV word handling strategies, including subword approaches from pretrained models versus domain-specific vocabulary expansion techniques.

## Limitations

- The study uses customer complaints which may not represent the full breadth of banking terminology
- Human annotation process involves subjective judgments that could vary between annotators
- The study focuses solely on semantic similarity without evaluating embeddings on downstream banking tasks

## Confidence

- Medium Confidence: LSA with autoencoder architecture is optimal for dimensionality reduction
- Medium Confidence: Human-annotated relatedness scores provide reliable ground truth
