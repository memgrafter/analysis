# Leveraging Unlabelled Data in Multiple-Instance Learning Problems for Improved Detection of Parkinsonian Tremor in Free-Living Conditions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.00249
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00249
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: MI-VAT improves Parkinsonian tremor detection F1-score by up to 9% by leveraging unlabelled data in semi-supervised multiple-instance learning.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of detecting Parkinsonian tremor from hand acceleration signals collected in free-living conditions using a semi-supervised multiple-instance learning approach. The proposed method, MI-VAT, adapts Virtual Adversarial Training to the multiple-instance setting by introducing a bag perturbation scheme that encourages consistency in the model's predictions. Two variants are explored: sparse perturbations based on uniform probability and attention weights. Experiments on synthetic datasets (MNIST and CIFAR-10) demonstrate significant improvements in performance when incorporating unlabelled data, with the sparse-Attention variant showing the best results. Applying the method to real-world tremor detection data from 45 labelled subjects and 454 unlabelled subjects yields an F1-score improvement of up to 9% compared to the fully supervised baseline.

## Method Summary
The method builds on Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT) adapted for multiple-instance learning (MIL). It introduces a bag perturbation scheme where either all instances (dense) or a single instance (sparse, selected via uniform probability or attention weights) are perturbed to maximize prediction change. The model consists of a 1D CNN feature extractor, instance embeddings, attention mechanism, bag embedding via weighted sum, and classifier. MI-VAT encourages consistency across perturbed bags using a regularizer that complements the classification loss. The approach is evaluated using Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) validation on hand acceleration data collected via smartphone during phone calls.

## Key Results
- MI-VAT with sparse-Attention perturbations achieves best performance on synthetic datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10)
- Real-world tremor detection shows up to 9% F1-score improvement over fully supervised baseline
- Performance improves with increasing unlabelled set size, saturating around 300 subjects
- Dense perturbations perform worse than sparse variants in both synthetic and real experiments

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Leveraging unlabelled bags in MIL via MI-VAT improves classification by encouraging smoother decision boundaries in high-density regions.
- Mechanism: MI-VAT adapts Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT) to MIL by computing bag perturbations that maximally disturb the model's predictions. This encourages the model to be invariant to label-preserving transformations of bags, effectively regularizing the classifier.
- Core assumption: The smoothness assumption holds: if two bags are close in a high-density region, their labels should be similar.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our approach builds on the Virtual Adversarial Training principle... which we adapt and modify appropriately for the multiple-instance setting."
  - [section]: "Building ML models using few labelled and much unlabelled data corresponds to a learning approach called semi-supervised learning."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Sparse perturbations based on attention weights improve performance by targeting the most informative instances within bags.
- Mechanism: Instead of perturbing all instances in a bag, MI-VAT variants sample a single instance to perturb based on uniform probability or attention weights. This focuses the regularization on the most important instances for bag-level classification.
- Core assumption: Perturbing a single, potentially positive, instance is more informative than perturbing all instances indiscriminately.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section]: "This situation is regularly encountered in practice. In our case, for example, PD tremor may manifest only for a fraction of time during everyday life..."
  - [section]: "An interesting variation of this approach would be to make Rvadv sparse... we opt for a stochastic approach, in which we select the index j of the instance to be perturbed at random."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The combination of semi-supervised learning with MIL is effective because it addresses the label scarcity problem inherent in both paradigms.
- Mechanism: MIL naturally handles the case where detailed instance-level labels are unavailable, while SSL leverages unlabelled data to improve model generalization. Their combination is particularly powerful when both types of label scarcity are present.
- Core assumption: The labelled and unlabelled bags are drawn from the same underlying data distribution.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Yet for large scale studies, obtaining even the necessary coarse ground-truth is not trivial, as a complete neurological evaluation is required."
  - [section]: "In this paper we follow a different direction and propose an approach based on consistency-regularization that is able to leverage unlabelled bags in order to improve a deep multiple-instance learning classifier."

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Multiple-Instance Learning (MIL)
  - Why needed here: PD tremor manifests intermittently and is only observable in a fraction of time during everyday life. Detailed ground-truth of on-off periods is difficult to obtain, so tremor detection is naturally framed as a MIL problem.
  - Quick check question: In MIL, what is the relationship between the labels of individual instances and the label of the bag they belong to?

- Concept: Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL)
  - Why needed here: Acquiring labelled data for large-scale PD studies requires expensive and time-consuming neurological evaluations. Unlabelled data, on the other hand, can be collected easily and cheaply.
  - Quick check question: What are the key assumptions that allow unlabelled data to improve a classifier in SSL?

- Concept: Consistency Regularization
  - Why needed here: MI-VAT is based on the idea of encouraging the model to be invariant to label-preserving transformations of the input. This is particularly important in MIL, where the model must learn to be robust to the presence or absence of positive instances within bags.
  - Quick check question: How does encouraging consistency across label-preserving transformations of the input regularize a model?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input acceleration segments -> 1D CNN feature extractor -> Instance embedding layer -> Attention mechanism -> Weighted sum bag embedding -> Classifier -> MI-VAT regularization
- Critical path: Input acceleration segments → Feature extraction → Instance embedding → Attention → Bag embedding → Classification → MI-VAT regularization
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Dense vs. sparse perturbations: Dense perturbations regularize all instances but may be less targeted; sparse perturbations focus on the most important instances but may miss important information in other instances.
  - Attention dimension: Higher dimensions allow for more complex attention patterns but increase computational cost and risk of overfitting.
  - Bag size: Larger bags provide more information but increase computational cost and may make the problem harder.
- Failure signatures:
  - Poor performance on labelled data but good performance on unlabelled data: The model may be overfitting to the unlabelled data or the labelled and unlabelled data may come from different distributions.
  - No improvement over baseline: The MI-VAT regularization may not be effective, possibly due to poor perturbation strategy or insufficient unlabelled data.
  - High variance across runs: The model may be sensitive to initialization or the perturbation strategy may introduce too much noise.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train the baseline MIL model on the labelled data only and evaluate its performance.
  2. Train the MI-VAT model with dense perturbations on both labelled and unlabelled data and compare its performance to the baseline.
  3. Train the MI-VAT model with sparse perturbations based on attention weights and compare its performance to the dense perturbation model.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the performance of MI-VAT scale with different unlabelled set sizes beyond 300 subjects, and is there a point of diminishing returns?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows F1-score improvement with increasing unlabelled set size up to 300 subjects, but does not explore larger sizes.
- Why unresolved: The study only tested unlabelled set sizes up to 300 subjects, leaving uncertainty about scalability and optimal dataset size.
- What evidence would resolve it: Conducting experiments with progressively larger unlabelled datasets (e.g., 500, 1000, 2000 subjects) and measuring F1-score improvements would clarify scalability and identify diminishing returns.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Can MI-VAT be adapted to handle data types where additive perturbations are inappropriate, such as histograms or time-series with temporal dependencies?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors acknowledge that MI-VAT's reliance on additive perturbations limits its applicability to certain data types like histograms or typing patterns.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not propose or test alternative perturbation strategies for such data types.
- What evidence would resolve it: Developing and testing modified perturbation methods (e.g., multiplicative noise, temporal smoothing) on datasets like typing patterns or ECG signals would demonstrate adaptability.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust is MI-VAT to distribution shifts between labelled and unlabelled datasets, such as those caused by differences in geography, demographics, or data collection conditions?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention that semi-supervised learning assumes similar distributions for labelled and unlabelled data, but do not test this assumption.
- Why unresolved: No experiments were conducted to assess MI-VAT's performance under distribution shift scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training MI-VAT on labelled data from one population (e.g., urban) and unlabelled data from another (e.g., rural), then measuring performance degradation, would quantify robustness to distribution shifts.

### Open Question 4
- Question: What is the theoretical mechanism by which MI-LDS encourages better separation of bag embeddings in the label space?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors hypothesize that MI-LDS acts as a regularizer for inter-class separation but do not provide a formal theoretical explanation.
- Why unresolved: The paper lacks a rigorous mathematical analysis of how the MI-LDS objective affects the embedding space structure.
- What evidence would resolve it: Deriving bounds or conditions under which MI-LDS improves inter-class separation, or analyzing the Hessian structure of the loss, would provide theoretical grounding.

## Limitations
- Self-reported PD status of unlabelled subjects is not validated against clinical diagnosis, introducing potential noise
- Assumption of similar distribution between labelled and unlabelled data may not hold in practice
- Key parameters for power iteration in Algorithm 1 (iterations, ξ constant, perturbation magnitude) are not fully specified

## Confidence
- Medium confidence: Performance improvements are demonstrated but rely on specific dataset characteristics (45 labelled, 454 unlabelled subjects)
- Low confidence: Synthetic experiments may not translate directly to real-world tremor detection due to domain differences
- Low confidence: Attention-based perturbation assumes effective instance identification which may not generalize across sensor modalities

## Next Checks
1. Validate the distribution alignment between labelled and unlabelled cohorts by comparing statistical features and stratifying performance by self-reported PD status.
2. Conduct ablation studies to quantify the contribution of each MI-VAT variant (dense, sparse-uniform, sparse-attention) and determine optimal hyperparameters for the power iteration.
3. Test the method on additional datasets with different sensor modalities (e.g., wrist-worn accelerometers) to assess generalizability across data collection protocols.
