# The Contribution of Knowledge in Visiolinguistic Learning: A Survey on Tasks and Challenges

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.02411
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02411
- Reference count: 40
- One-line primary result: Knowledge graphs offer transparent reasoning paths while large language models provide scalability for enhancing visiolinguistic learning

## Executive Summary
This survey examines how external knowledge sources, specifically knowledge graphs (KGs) and large language models (LLMs), enhance visiolinguistic learning frameworks. While pre-trained VL models perform well on standard benchmarks, they often lack the commonsense and factual knowledge needed for complex visual questions. The paper categorizes knowledge types and reviews hybrid approaches that integrate KGs or LLMs with VL models for tasks like visual question answering, visual commonsense reasoning, and image captioning. It highlights the trade-off between KG transparency and LLM scalability, discusses emerging trends, and identifies key challenges including the need for more knowledge-demanding datasets and improved interpretability.

## Method Summary
The paper conducts a comprehensive survey of recent approaches that integrate external knowledge sources into visiolinguistic learning frameworks. It categorizes knowledge types, analyzes how KGs and LLMs contribute different capabilities to VL models, and reviews hybrid architectures combining these approaches. The survey examines knowledge retrieval and injection mechanisms, discusses task-specific implementations, and evaluates the trade-offs between different knowledge integration strategies. The methodology involves systematic literature review across multiple visiolinguistic tasks, analysis of architectural patterns, and identification of open challenges in the field.

## Key Results
- KGs provide transparent reasoning paths through structured triplet representations, enabling deterministic path tracing for multi-step reasoning
- LLMs serve as implicit knowledge bases that scale to vast information sources without manual supervision
- Hybrid architectures combining KGs and LLMs leverage complementary strengths, though integration complexity remains a challenge

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Knowledge graphs (KGs) provide transparent reasoning paths that improve interpretability of VL model decisions.
- Mechanism: KGs store knowledge as structured triplets (h, r, t) where relationships between entities are explicitly defined, enabling deterministic path tracing for multi-step reasoning.
- Core assumption: Visual questions requiring commonsense or factual knowledge can be decomposed into sequences of KG triplets that can be retrieved via SPARQL queries.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Knowledge Graphs (KGs) offer transparent reasoning paths"
  - [section] "Extracting an answer from a KB is a fully transparent process, and the path followed can be deterministically recovered"
- Break condition: When visual-linguistic concepts cannot be mapped to KG entities due to vocabulary mismatch or missing coverage in the KG.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Large language models (LLMs) serve as implicit knowledge bases that scale to vast information sources without manual supervision.
- Mechanism: LLMs learn relational knowledge during pre-training on massive text corpora, storing this information in neural weights that can be accessed through prompting or fine-tuning.
- Core assumption: The knowledge required for VL tasks is implicitly captured in the LLM's parameters and can be retrieved through appropriate prompts.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Large Language Models (LLMs) are able to cover such generalization gaps by filling in missing knowledge"
  - [section] "Language Models (LMs) such as BERT [27] have been proven capable of storing relational knowledge learned from linguistic data during pre-training"
- Break condition: When the LLM lacks specific visual context needed to ground its linguistic knowledge, or when prompt engineering fails to retrieve relevant information.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hybrid architectures combining KGs and LLMs leverage complementary strengths for enhanced VL reasoning.
- Mechanism: KGs provide factual knowledge with transparent reasoning paths while LLMs offer scalability and implicit knowledge retrieval, with the two modalities integrated through multimodal transformers.
- Core assumption: The factual precision of KGs can compensate for LLM hallucinations, while LLM flexibility can address KG coverage limitations.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "resulting in the emergence of hybrid architectures"
  - [section] "There are a few implementations combining explicit and implicit knowledge sources to enjoy advantages of both worlds"
- Break condition: When integration complexity outweighs performance benefits, or when conflicting knowledge sources confuse the reasoning process.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Visual encoder (e.g., CLIP, ResNet) -> Text encoder (e.g., BERT, GPT) -> Knowledge retriever (KG SPARQL queries or LLM prompts) -> Multimodal transformer -> Answer generator/classifier

- Critical path:
  1. Extract visual features from input image
  2. Encode question/context text
  3. Retrieve relevant knowledge from KG or LLM
  4. Fuse multimodal representations in transformer
  5. Generate or classify final answer

- Design tradeoffs:
  - KG vs LLM: transparency and precision vs. scalability and implicit knowledge
  - Fine-tuning vs. prompt engineering: parameter updates vs. input optimization
  - Multimodal fusion strategies: early vs. late integration of knowledge

- Failure signatures:
  - KG failures: concept mapping errors, incomplete coverage
  - LLM failures: hallucinations, sensitivity to prompt variations
  - Integration failures: modality misalignment, conflicting knowledge sources

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement basic VQA with KG knowledge injection using SPARQL queries
  2. Add LLM knowledge retrieval through prompting to compare performance
  3. Build hybrid architecture combining both knowledge sources and evaluate reasoning paths

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How do multimodal prompt design strategies compare to unimodal prompts in improving knowledge retrieval for vision-language tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper highlights the challenge of designing multimodal prompts for VL models and suggests that multimodal prompt design is still unexplored.
- Why unresolved: Current research focuses on unimodal prompts, and the effectiveness of multimodal prompts in enhancing knowledge retrieval for VL tasks has not been thoroughly investigated.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative studies between multimodal and unimodal prompts in VL tasks, evaluating retrieval accuracy, reasoning quality, and task-specific performance metrics.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent can the knowledge stored in Large Language Models (LLMs) be considered reliable and trustworthy for complex vision-language reasoning tasks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper discusses concerns about the reliability and trustworthiness of knowledge in LLMs due to potential biases, errors, and sensitivity to input variations.
- Why unresolved: While LLMs demonstrate impressive capabilities, their knowledge base's quality and consistency remain uncertain, especially in complex reasoning scenarios.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic evaluation of LLM outputs for factual accuracy, bias detection, and consistency across diverse VL tasks and input variations.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can knowledge-demanding datasets be designed to effectively evaluate the contribution of external knowledge sources in vision-language tasks beyond VQA and VCR?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper emphasizes the lack of knowledge-demanding datasets for tasks like image captioning and sequential generation, which limits the evaluation of knowledge-enhanced models.
- Why unresolved: Existing datasets do not adequately test the impact of external knowledge in tasks requiring complex reasoning or creativity, hindering progress in this area.
- What evidence would resolve it: Development and benchmarking of new datasets tailored to specific VL tasks, incorporating explicit knowledge requirements and evaluating model performance.

## Limitations
- The paper serves as a survey rather than presenting original experimental results, limiting empirical validation of claims
- Comparison between KG and LLM approaches lacks quantitative benchmarks for systematic evaluation
- The assessment of hybrid integration strategies is qualitative rather than providing comprehensive performance comparisons

## Confidence
- High confidence: The categorization of knowledge types and task requirements is well-supported by the cited literature
- Medium confidence: The mechanism descriptions for how KGs and LLMs integrate with VL models are accurate but implementation details vary
- Low confidence: The comparative assessment of KG vs LLM superiority is primarily based on qualitative arguments
