# Using Positive Matching Contrastive Loss with Facial Action Units to mitigate bias in Facial Expression Recognition

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.04896
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04896
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Achieves Fairness Score of 97.6% on RAF-DB and 97.1% on IASLab while maintaining classification accuracy

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses bias in facial expression recognition (FER) systems by guiding models to focus on task-relevant features (facial action units) rather than protected attributes like gender or race. The proposed Feature-based Positive Matching Contrastive Loss computes similarities between positive samples based on their AU embeddings, thereby weighing the distances in the contrastive loss. This approach improves fairness (equality of opportunity) at minimal cost to classification performance. The method achieves state-of-the-art fairness scores on both RAF-DB and IASLab datasets while maintaining or slightly improving classification accuracy compared to cross-entropy baseline methods.

## Method Summary
The method introduces a Positive Matching Contrastive Loss that uses extracted facial action units (AUs) to compute similarities between faces with the same emotion label. During training, the model learns to bring positive samples closer in the embedding space based on both their emotion labels and AU similarity, while pushing apart samples with different emotions. The approach combines this contrastive loss with standard cross-entropy classification loss, allowing the model to optimize for both fairness and accuracy simultaneously. The method operates without requiring bias labels or knowledge of protected attributes during training, relying instead on AU detection as a proxy for task-relevant features.

## Key Results
- Achieved Fairness Score of 97.6% on RAF-DB dataset, outperforming baseline methods
- Achieved Fairness Score of 97.1% on IASLab dataset, the highest among tested methods
- Maintained or slightly improved classification accuracy compared to cross-entropy baseline
- Demonstrated effectiveness without requiring bias labels during training

- Mechanism: The Positive Matching Contrastive Loss weights positive sample distances by their AU similarity, making samples with similar AU patterns closer in the embedding space regardless of protected attributes.
- Core assumption: Task-relevant features (AUs) are more predictive of emotion than protected attributes like gender or race.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input image -> Face detection -> AU extraction -> ResNet50 -> Projection -> Image embedding; AU embedding -> Projection -> AU embedding; Compute cosine similarity -> Positive Matching Contrastive Loss -> Combined with cross-entropy loss

- Critical path: 1. Input image → face detection → AU extraction 2. Image → ResNet50 → projection → image embedding 3. AU embedding → projection → AU embedding 4. Compute cosine similarity between positive samples using both image and AU embeddings 5. Calculate Positive Matching Contrastive Loss 6. Add to cross-entropy loss for final training objective

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using pre-trained ResNet50 vs training from scratch (trade-off between speed and potential performance)
  - Number of AU projection dimensions (32) vs model capacity
  - Weighting between contrastive loss and cross-entropy loss
  - AU detection accuracy vs computational cost

- Failure signatures:
  - Low fairness scores despite high classification accuracy (model still relying on protected attributes)
  - High variance in fairness scores across emotion classes (method not working uniformly)
  - Significant drop in classification accuracy (trade-off too steep)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the Positive Matching Contrastive Loss module and verify it produces reasonable similarity scores between positive samples
  2. Train with only the contrastive loss (no classification) to observe if AU-based similarity learning occurs
  3. Gradually introduce the classification head and adjust the loss weighting to find the optimal balance between fairness and accuracy

## Limitations
- Dataset generalizability: Performance on more diverse or differently distributed datasets remains uncertain
- AU detection reliability: Method depends on accurate AU extraction, which may vary across demographics
- Trade-off characterization: The fairness-accuracy trade-off curve is incompletely characterized
