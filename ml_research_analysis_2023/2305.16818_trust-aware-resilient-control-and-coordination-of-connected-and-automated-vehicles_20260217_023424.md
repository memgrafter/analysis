# Trust-Aware Resilient Control and Coordination of Connected and Automated Vehicles

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.16818
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16818
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 39
- Primary result: Trust-aware framework detects Sybil attacks, prevents collisions, and improves traffic flow efficiency in CAV coordination

## Executive Summary
This paper proposes a trust-aware control and coordination framework for Connected and Automated Vehicles (CAVs) to address adversarial attacks, particularly Sybil attacks. The framework uses a trust-based monitoring system that assigns trust scores to CAVs based on behavioral specifications including co-observation consistency, initial conditions, dynamic model checks, and rule compliance. The system incorporates trust information into the control problem through trust-based search to guarantee safe coordination and prevent collisions. Additionally, it includes a lane priority-based rescheduling scheme to mitigate traffic congestion caused by uncooperative CAVs.

## Method Summary
The method employs a decentralized control architecture where a roadside coordinator unit (RSU) monitors CAV behavior and computes trust scores based on four behavioral specifications. The framework uses control barrier functions to ensure safety constraints are met. Trust-based search is used to identify potential conflict vehicles and incorporate trust scores into the vehicle passing sequence. For traffic jam mitigation, the system monitors abnormally slow-moving CAVs and reschedules traffic using an integer linear programming formulation. Attack detection is performed by monitoring trust scores over observation windows, and soft mitigation is implemented through local sensing to un-constrain CAVs affected by fake CAVs during overtaking maneuvers.

## Key Results
- Trust-based search prevents collisions between adversarial and normal CAVs by incorporating trust scores into the coordination algorithm
- Lane priority-based rescheduling effectively mitigates traffic jams caused by uncooperative CAVs through dynamic priority adjustment
- Soft mitigation using local sensing maintains safety while allowing normal traffic flow during attack scenarios

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Trust-based search guarantees safe coordination by preventing collisions between adversarial and normal CAVs
- Mechanism: The coordinator incorporates trust scores into the vehicle passing sequence to identify potential conflict vehicles. For each CAV, it performs a trust-based search to find preceding vehicles at merging points, continuing the search until finding a vehicle with trust ≥ 1-δ
- Core assumption: Trust scores accurately distinguish between normal and adversarial vehicles based on behavioral specifications
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The framework incorporates trust into the control problem via trust-based search to guarantee safe coordination and prevent collisions"
  - [section IV] "We propose a resilient coordination and control framework...to mitigate the adversarial effects of the attack namely i. collision and ii. traffic congestion"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers directly discuss trust-based search for collision prevention
- Break condition: Trust framework fails to accurately distinguish between normal and adversarial vehicles, causing false negatives or false positives

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Lane priority-based rescheduling mitigates traffic jams caused by uncooperative CAVs
- Mechanism: The system monitors for abnormally slow-moving CAVs and assigns lane priorities based on the number of slow vehicles. When the fraction of slow vehicles exceeds a threshold, it reschedules CAVs to move those in high-priority lanes ahead of those in low-priority lanes
- Core assumption: Uncooperative CAVs will move slower than normal traffic flow, and lane priority correlates with traffic congestion severity
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "It also includes a lane priority-based rescheduling scheme to mitigate traffic jams caused by uncooperative CAVs"
  - [section V-B] "We propose a central, event-driven, robust scheduling scheme...reschedule the CAVs in the presence of adversarial CAVs to prevent any traffic jam"
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers directly discuss lane priority-based rescheduling for traffic jam mitigation
- Break condition: The system fails to detect uncooperative CAVs or incorrectly prioritizes lanes, causing inefficient traffic flow

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Soft mitigation through local sensing restores normal cooperation while guaranteeing safety
- Mechanism: When fake CAVs are detected, the system relaxes constraints for vehicles that should be able to overtake them based on local sensing. Real CAVs that are falsely identified as fake won't be overtaken because they'll be detected through local vision
- Core assumption: Local sensing is sufficiently accurate to distinguish between real and fake CAVs during overtaking maneuvers
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The attack detection and mitigation scheme identifies fake CAVs using trust scores and implements a soft mitigation approach using local sensing and overtaking"

  - [corpus] Weak evidence - no corpus papers directly discuss soft mitigation using local sensing for Sybil attack scenarios
- Break condition: Local sensing fails to accurately detect CAVs, leading to collisions during overtaking maneuvers

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - CAV vehicles with onboard sensing and control capabilities
  - Roadside coordinator unit (RSU) that monitors traffic and computes trust scores
  - Communication network for V2X and V2I communication
  - Trust framework for behavioral monitoring and scoring
  - Control and coordination algorithms incorporating trust information

- Critical path:
  1. CAV enters control zone and communicates state to RSU
  2. RSU updates trust scores based on behavioral specifications
  3. RSU computes passing sequence using trust-based search
  4. RSU communicates conflict information to CAVs
  5. CAVs execute control to satisfy safety constraints
  6. If traffic jam detected, RSU performs lane priority-based rescheduling

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Centralized vs. decentralized trust computation
  - Granularity of trust scoring (per-vehicle vs. per-behavior)
  - Frequency of trust score updates vs. computational overhead
  - Strictness of safety constraints vs. traffic efficiency

- Failure signatures:
  - Trust framework produces false positives/negatives
  - Control algorithm fails to find feasible solutions
  - Communication delays cause outdated information
  - Local sensing inaccuracies during overtaking maneuvers

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Simulate Sybil attack with 2-3 fake CAVs and verify trust-based search prevents collisions
  2. Introduce uncooperative CAVs and test lane priority-based rescheduling effectiveness
  3. Test soft mitigation by creating scenario with real CAV falsely identified as fake and verify safety during overtaking

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the proposed framework perform under simultaneous Sybil attacks and other cyber-attacks like false data injection (FDI) attacks?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper focuses on Sybil attacks but mentions that attacks like FDI attacks can also introduce adversarial effects. It would be interesting to see how the framework handles multiple concurrent attacks.
- Why unresolved: The paper primarily validates the framework against Sybil attacks. Evaluating its robustness under multiple attack vectors is crucial for real-world applicability.
- What evidence would resolve it: Simulation results showing the framework's performance under simultaneous Sybil and FDI attacks, including metrics like detection accuracy, collision avoidance, and traffic flow maintenance.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the trust framework's performance scale with increasing network size and traffic density?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The trust framework is central to the proposed solution, but the paper does not discuss its computational complexity or performance under high traffic loads. Understanding scalability is essential for practical deployment.
- Why unresolved: The paper focuses on a single intersection scenario. Scaling to larger networks with multiple intersections and higher vehicle densities could introduce challenges in trust computation and coordination.
- What evidence would resolve it: Analysis of the trust framework's computational complexity, simulation results under varying network sizes and traffic densities, and comparisons with alternative trust mechanisms.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How sensitive is the proposed framework to the choice of trust thresholds (δ) and observation window length (η)?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions specific values for δ and η but does not discuss their impact on the framework's performance. Understanding the sensitivity to these parameters is crucial for tuning the system.
- Why unresolved: The paper uses fixed values for δ and η without exploring their effects on false positives, detection accuracy, or mitigation effectiveness. Different scenarios might require different parameter settings.
- What evidence would resolve it: Sensitivity analysis showing how varying δ and η affects the framework's performance metrics, such as false positive rate, detection accuracy, and traffic flow.

## Limitations

- The framework's effectiveness depends heavily on the accuracy of the trust computation, which lacks detailed implementation specifications in the paper
- The soft mitigation approach using local sensing during overtaking maneuvers is not thoroughly validated and may not guarantee safety in all scenarios
- The paper does not address how the framework performs under multiple concurrent cyber-attacks beyond Sybil attacks
