# Multilingual Neural Machine Translation System for Indic to Indic Languages

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.12693
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12693
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Multilingual NMT baseline models for 11 Indic languages using Samanantar corpus, with best model achieving 24.29 BLEU score (PA-HI pair)

## Executive Summary
This paper presents multilingual neural machine translation (MNMT) baseline models for 11 Indic languages, focusing on direct and English-pivot translation approaches. The study groups languages into East Indo-Aryan (EI), Dravidian (DR), and West Indo-Aryan (WI) families to examine the impact of language relatedness on translation quality. The research utilizes the Samanantar corpus and evaluates performance on the Flores-200 corpus using BLEU scores. A key innovation is the use of transliteration into a modified ITRANS script, which shows significant improvements, particularly for low-resource and lexically diverse languages.

## Method Summary
The research develops MNMT models using both direct translation and English as a pivot language. Languages are grouped by family (EI, DR, WI) to study the effects of relatedness on translation quality. The models are trained on the Samanantar corpus and evaluated using the Flores-200 corpus. Transliteration into a modified ITRANS script is employed to standardize the input representation. The study compares translation quality across different language pairs and model configurations, with a focus on identifying the benefits and limitations of language grouping and transliteration strategies.

## Key Results
- Related language grouping benefits WI and English-pivot models but hinders EI models, with inconclusive effects on DR
- Transliteration improves performance across all models, especially for low-resource and lexically diverse languages
- Best model achieves 24.29 BLEU score for PA-HI pair using transliterated corpus
- ML, TA, and BN show highest improvements with transliteration, particularly due to high lexical diversity

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The effectiveness of MNMT models for Indic languages is influenced by language family relatedness and lexical diversity. Related languages sharing similar linguistic features can enhance translation quality when grouped together, but this benefit is not uniform across all language families. The use of English as a pivot language provides additional context and improves translation quality, particularly for low-resource languages. Transliteration into a modified ITRANS script standardizes input representation, reducing the impact of script diversity and improving model performance, especially for languages with high lexical diversity due to agglutination.

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Data -> Preprocessing (Transliteration) -> Model Training (Direct/Pivot) -> Evaluation (BLEU Score)

Critical Path: Data collection and preprocessing (including transliteration) is critical for model performance, followed by training and evaluation.

Design Tradeoffs: Language grouping can improve performance for related languages but may hinder unrelated ones. Transliteration improves input standardization but may lose script-specific information.

Failure Signatures: Poor performance in EI language groups when using related languages, inconsistent results across language families.

First Experiments:
1. Train a baseline MNMT model without language grouping or transliteration
2. Implement language grouping for WI languages and evaluate performance
3. Apply transliteration to low-resource language pairs and measure BLEU score improvements

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What are the reasons for the detrimental effects of using related languages on East Indo-Aryan (EI) language groups in MNMT models?
- Basis in paper: The paper states that using related languages is beneficial for the WI group only, while it is detrimental for the EI group and shows an inconclusive effect on the DR group.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not provide a detailed explanation for the negative impact on EI languages, only stating the observed effect.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further analysis of the linguistic features and corpus characteristics of EI languages that may hinder the effectiveness of related language grouping in MNMT models.

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does the use of pivot languages, specifically English, impact the translation quality of MNMT models for low-resource Indic languages?
- Basis in paper: The paper explores the use of English as a pivot to enhance translation quality and observes that pivot models greatly improve MNMT baselines.
- Why unresolved: While the paper shows that pivot models improve performance, it does not delve into the specific reasons or mechanisms behind this improvement for low-resource languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed comparative studies of MNMT models with and without pivot languages, focusing on low-resource language pairs, to identify the factors contributing to improved translation quality.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What are the specific linguistic features of Dravidian languages that contribute to their high lexical diversity and how does this affect their performance in MNMT models?
- Basis in paper: The paper notes that Dravidian languages like Malayalam and Tamil possess high lexical diversity due to agglutination, which affects their performance in MNMT models.
- Why unresolved: The paper mentions the high lexical diversity but does not explore the specific linguistic features or their impact on model performance in detail.
- What evidence would resolve it: In-depth linguistic analysis of Dravidian languages focusing on agglutination and other features, along with experimental studies to quantify their impact on MNMT model performance.

## Limitations

- BLEU scores as the sole evaluation metric may not fully capture translation quality nuances
- Inconsistent impact of language grouping across language families requires further investigation
- Limited analysis of transliteration's impact on different linguistic features and quantitative evidence for its benefits

## Confidence

1. BLEU score improvements with transliteration: High confidence
2. Language family grouping effectiveness: Medium confidence
3. Transliteration benefits for low-resource languages: Low confidence

## Next Checks

1. Conduct human evaluation studies across multiple Indic language pairs to validate BLEU score improvements and assess translation quality in context, particularly for languages showing significant BLEU improvements with transliteration.

2. Perform ablation studies to isolate the impact of different model components (language grouping, transliteration, pivot language) on translation quality for each language family, with particular focus on the East Indo-Aryan group where related language grouping showed negative effects.

3. Implement cross-validation with additional evaluation metrics (chrF, COMET) and test the models on out-of-domain data to assess the robustness and generalizability of the improvements, especially for languages with high lexical diversity.
