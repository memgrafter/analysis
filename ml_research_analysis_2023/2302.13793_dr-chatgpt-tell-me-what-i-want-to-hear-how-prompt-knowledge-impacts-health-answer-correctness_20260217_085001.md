# Dr ChatGPT, tell me what I want to hear: How prompt knowledge impacts health answer correctness

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.13793
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13793
- Reference count: 13
- Key outcome: ChatGPT's accuracy drops from 80% to 63% when given external evidence in prompts, with half of errors occurring when correct answers are changed to incorrect ones based on prompt content

## Executive Summary
This study examines how ChatGPT's answers to health questions change when provided with additional evidence in prompts. Researchers compared answer correctness when only the question was given versus when the question was paired with supporting or contrary evidence. They found that ChatGPT correctly answered 80% of questions without evidence but accuracy dropped to 63% when evidence was included. Notably, half of incorrect answers with evidence occurred because the model changed a correct answer to an incorrect one based on the prompt content, rather than fixing initial errors. This suggests that misleading or incorrect information in prompts can significantly undermine ChatGPT's reliability in health contexts, even when the model initially had correct knowledge. The findings highlight the importance of careful prompt design and the risks of integrating external information in AI-assisted health advice systems.

## Method Summary
The study used 100 TREC 2021/2022 Health Misinformation topics, with 35 used for evidence-biased experiments. Researchers tested two conditions: question-only (no evidence) and evidence-biased (supporting/contrary evidence documents from TREC qrels). ChatGPT was prompted with questions and, in the evidence-biased condition, additional quoted passages. Answers were manually evaluated as correct, incorrect, or unsure against ground truth, with accuracy calculated for each condition and analysis of answer changes between conditions.

## Key Results
- ChatGPT's accuracy dropped from 80% to 63% when external evidence was included in prompts
- Half of incorrect answers with evidence occurred when the model changed a correct answer to an incorrect one
- The evidence presented in prompts heavily influenced ChatGPT's responses, regardless of the model's pre-trained knowledge

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: ChatGPT's pre-trained knowledge can be overridden by external prompt evidence, leading to decreased accuracy.
- Mechanism: The model uses prompt knowledge as additional context during inference, potentially overriding its own pre-trained knowledge when the evidence strongly supports a different conclusion.
- Core assumption: ChatGPT's attention mechanism treats prompt content as equally valid as its internal knowledge.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "knowledge passed in the prompt can overturn the knowledge encoded in the model"
  - [section] "when evidence contrary to the ground truth is provided and ChatGPT overturns an answer generated when no evidence is provided, this is often for the worse"
  - [corpus] Corpus signals show weak related citations, suggesting limited prior work on this specific mechanism.
- Break condition: If the prompt evidence is contradictory or ambiguous, the model may show uncertainty or refuse to answer.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: External evidence in prompts can introduce misinformation that the model accepts and uses to generate incorrect answers.
- Mechanism: ChatGPT lacks the ability to independently verify external information, so it incorporates prompt evidence directly into its response generation process.
- Core assumption: The model treats all prompt information as factual without critical evaluation.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "incorrect evidence is able to deceive the model into providing an incorrect answer"
  - [section] "the evidence presented in the prompt can heavily influence the answer"
  - [corpus] Related paper "Polarization of Autonomous Generative AI Agents Under Echo Chambers" suggests similar echo chamber effects.
- Break condition: If the prompt explicitly asks for verification or includes contradictory evidence from multiple sources.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The format and presentation of evidence in prompts affects how the model processes and weights that information.
- Mechanism: Prompt structure (e.g., quoted passages, explicit instructions) influences the model's attention distribution and response generation.
- Core assumption: ChatGPT's attention mechanism is sensitive to prompt formatting and explicit instructions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "it is not just the 'form' of the prompt that matters"
  - [section] "the prompt can overturn the model answer about a treatment"
  - [corpus] Weak corpus signals suggest limited prior work on prompt formatting effects.
- Break condition: If the prompt format is too complex or ambiguous for the model to parse effectively.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input (question + evidence) -> Processing (ChatGPT transformer) -> Output (Yes/No answer) -> Evaluation (ground truth comparison)
- Critical path: 1. Receive question, 2. Retrieve relevant documents (if evidence-biased), 3. Construct prompt with question and evidence, 4. Generate response using ChatGPT, 5. Evaluate response correctness against ground truth
- Design tradeoffs: Tradeoff between model knowledge and external evidence, balance between prompt length and information content, accuracy vs. explainability in model responses
- Failure signatures: High variance in responses to the same question with different evidence, model consistently agreeing with prompt evidence regardless of ground truth, reduced accuracy when evidence is introduced, even if model initially had correct knowledge

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does ChatGPT's performance vary across multiple runs of the same question, given its stochastic nature?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that answer generation is stochastic in ChatGPT and large pre-trained generative language models, but they did not study the variability of answer generation across multiple runs of the same question.
- Why unresolved: The paper did not conduct experiments to measure the consistency of ChatGPT's answers when the same question is posed multiple times.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results showing the variation in ChatGPT's answers to the same question across multiple trials, along with corresponding accuracy metrics for each trial.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the specific characteristics of evidence documents that lead to divergent answers from ChatGPT, despite having identical stances?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors mention that they did not analyze what characteristics of the evidence documents trigger divergent answers, even when the stance is the same.
- Why unresolved: The paper did not perform a detailed analysis of the content and structure of the evidence documents to identify factors that influence ChatGPT's responses.
- What evidence would resolve it: A comparative analysis of evidence documents that led to different answers, identifying common features or differences that correlate with the variation in responses.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the ability to hold multi-turn conversations affect ChatGPT's performance in providing accurate health advice?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors note that ChatGPT can hold multi-turn conversations, but their experiments only considered single-turn answers.
- Why unresolved: The paper did not explore the impact of multi-turn interactions on the accuracy and reliability of ChatGPT's health advice.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experimental results comparing the accuracy of ChatGPT's answers in single-turn versus multi-turn conversation settings, with metrics on the quality and consistency of responses.

## Limitations
- Small sample size of 35 questions may limit generalizability
- Manual evaluation introduces potential subjectivity in determining answer correctness
- Focus on health misinformation topics may not represent all domains where ChatGPT is used

## Confidence
- **High Confidence**: The core finding that external evidence in prompts can override ChatGPT's internal knowledge and lead to decreased accuracy
- **Medium Confidence**: The mechanism by which prompt knowledge affects answer correctness, as the exact attention and processing mechanisms are not fully elucidated
- **Low Confidence**: Generalizability of findings to other domains or larger question sets, given the limited sample size

## Next Checks
1. Replicate the study with a larger, more diverse set of questions across multiple domains to test generalizability
2. Implement inter-annotator agreement checks to quantify and reduce evaluation subjectivity
3. Conduct ablation studies to isolate the specific components of prompt formatting and evidence presentation that most influence model outputs
