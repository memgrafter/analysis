# Anticipate, Ensemble and Prune: Improving Convolutional Neural Networks via Aggregated Early Exits

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.12168
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12168
- Reference count: 29
- Primary result: Improves CNN accuracy by up to 15% through weighted ensemble of early-exit classifiers with pruning

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the Anticipate, Ensemble and Prune (AEP) method to improve convolutional neural network accuracy and efficiency. The approach adds multiple early-exit classifiers to standard ConvNet architectures and trains them jointly with weighted loss functions. During inference, outputs from active exits are combined using learned weights to produce the final prediction. A pruning step removes unnecessary exits, reducing model size and latency while maintaining accuracy. Experiments on various ConvNet architectures and image datasets demonstrate significant accuracy improvements over single-output models, along with reduced parameters, operations, and inference time.

## Method Summary
The AEP method involves three key steps: anticipation through adding multiple early-exit classifiers to intermediate layers of ConvNets, ensemble learning by training these exits jointly with weighted loss functions and combining their outputs during inference, and pruning to remove underperforming exits after training. The technique trains the network with weighted losses from all exits, uses weighted outputs from active exits for final prediction, and then applies a post-processing pruning step to optimize the sub-network. This approach improves accuracy by allowing intermediate layers to learn useful classification features while reducing model complexity through exit removal.

## Key Results
- Accuracy improvements of up to 15% compared to single-output models
- Pruned models achieve up to 41% fewer parameters, 18% fewer operations, and 16% lower latency
- Particularly effective for models trained from scratch and for lower resolution images
- Consistent improvements across multiple ConvNet architectures (ResNet50, VGG16, DenseNet169, MobileNetV3Small, EfficientNetB5)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Joint training of multiple early-exit classifiers with weighted loss functions improves overall network performance by allowing intermediate layers to learn useful classification features.
- Mechanism: Each early-exit classifier is trained with a weighted loss that contributes to the total network loss. By backpropagating through all exits, the network learns to optimize feature extraction at each stage for classification, not just at the final layer.
- Core assumption: Intermediate features learned by early exits are discriminative enough to contribute meaningfully to classification accuracy when properly weighted and combined.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method also includes a pruning step to remove unnecessary exits and reduce model size and latency."
  - [section] "Unlike the other approaches, in AEP the last exit is not treated differently from the intermediate ones, since its contribution to the final loss is weighted following the same weight assignment strategy as the others."

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Ensemble of early-exit predictions weighted by learned coefficients improves accuracy over single-output models by combining complementary information from different abstraction levels.
- Mechanism: During inference, outputs from active exits are combined using learned weights to produce the final prediction. This allows the model to leverage diverse feature representations learned at different depths.
- Core assumption: Different early exits capture complementary information, and their outputs can be effectively combined through learned weights to outperform any single exit.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The key idea is to train the network with weighted losses from all exits and then use weighted outputs from active exits to make the final prediction."
  - [section] "The predictions obtained after the ensemble, from now on referred to as ˆy, are calculated as a weighted sum of the outputs of each i-th output, namely Oi."

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Pruning removes redundant or underperforming exits, reducing model size and latency while maintaining or improving accuracy by extracting the optimal sub-ensemble.
- Mechanism: After training, the full ensemble is validated in all possible combinations of active exits. The best-performing sub-ensemble is selected, removing unnecessary exits and their associated parameters and computations.
- Core assumption: Not all early exits contribute equally to performance, and a subset can achieve comparable or better results with fewer resources.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "The method also includes a pruning step to remove unnecessary exits and reduce model size and latency."
  - [section] "Upon completion of training the multi-output network, a pruning step is applied as a post-processing technique to optimize its performance."

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input layer → Convolutional feature extraction blocks → Multiple early-exit classifiers (one after each stage) → Weighted ensemble layer → Output. Each early exit consists of a classifier head (e.g., global pooling + dense layer) attached to intermediate feature maps.
- Critical path: Forward pass through convolutional layers until the last active exit, then weighted combination of all active exit outputs to produce final prediction.
- Design tradeoffs: More early exits provide more opportunities for early prediction and diverse feature learning but increase model size and computation. Fewer exits reduce overhead but may limit performance gains.
- Failure signatures: If early exits perform significantly worse than the final exit, the ensemble may degrade performance. If pruning removes too many exits, accuracy may drop.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement a simple CNN with one early exit and test accuracy improvement over single-output baseline.
  2. Add multiple early exits and test different weighting strategies for loss and ensemble.
  3. Apply pruning to remove underperforming exits and measure impact on accuracy and model size.

## Limitations

- The optimal placement of early exits within different ConvNet architectures is not fully specified, particularly for deeper networks like ResNet50 and DenseNet169 where feature extraction varies significantly across layers.
- The exact weight assignment strategies for both loss functions and ensemble outputs are described generally but lack specific implementation details, particularly for non-linear weighting schemes.
- The paper does not explore the impact of different optimization techniques or hyperparameter tuning on AEP performance.

## Confidence

- High confidence in the core mechanism of weighted ensemble learning combining early-exit predictions
- Medium confidence in the effectiveness of the pruning strategy, as results show significant parameter reduction but the validation methodology for identifying optimal sub-ensembles is not fully detailed
- Medium confidence in the generalization across different datasets and architectures, though the specific architectural modifications for each ConvNet are not fully specified
