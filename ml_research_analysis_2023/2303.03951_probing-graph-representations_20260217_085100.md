# Probing Graph Representations

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.03951
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03951
- Reference count: 32
- Key outcome: This work investigates what information is encoded in the representations learned by graph neural networks and graph transformers using a probing framework, showing that transformer-based models learn richer representations capturing more chemically relevant knowledge compared to message-passing GNNs.

## Executive Summary
This study investigates what information is encoded in graph representations learned by GNNs and graph transformers, focusing on molecular datasets. Using a probing framework with linear classifiers, the authors quantify the amount of chemically relevant information captured in these representations. Experiments show that transformer-based models learn richer representations compared to message-passing GNNs, capturing more chemically relevant knowledge. The study also examines the effect of architectural choices like skip connections and virtual nodes, and advocates for probing as a diagnostic tool to understand and improve graph-based models.

## Method Summary
The authors pretrain various graph models (GCN, GIN, Graphormer, GRPE, GraphGPS, TokenGT) on the PCQM4Mv2 dataset, then extract hidden representations for molecules from probing datasets. Linear probes (logistic regression for binary/categorical, linear regression for continuous) are trained on these representations to predict various molecular properties such as atom counts, functional groups, 3D structure, and odor. Performance is evaluated using AUC for binary/categorical properties and R² for continuous properties. Bayesian Mutual Information (BMI) is used as an information-theoretic metric, and pairwise probing is employed to isolate causal effects.

## Key Results
- Transformer-based models (Graphormer, GRPE, GraphGPS, TokenGT) consistently outperform message-passing GNNs (GCN, GIN) across multiple molecular properties in probing experiments.
- Randomly initialized models can serve as useful feature extractors, as probing performance does not drop to random baseline levels.
- Architectural choices like skip connections and virtual nodes contribute to improved probing performance, though systematic ablations were not performed.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
Linear probes reveal encoded information because a high-performing linear classifier indicates the property is linearly separable in the representation space. Probing trains a linear model to predict a property from fixed graph representations. If the linear probe achieves high accuracy, it means the representations lie on one side of a hyperplane that distinguishes molecules with the property from those without it. Core assumption: The probe is trained on a sufficiently large, independent dataset so that high accuracy reflects true separability rather than overfitting. Evidence anchors: [abstract] "If we can reliably extract a given property... from a given representation... we can conclude that the information about that property is encoded in the representation." [section 2.2] "Good test performance is taken as evidence that the representation contains information about the property." Break condition: If the probing dataset is too small or correlated with the training data, high probe accuracy could be due to memorization rather than genuine encoding.

### Mechanism 2
Bayesian mutual information (BMI) measures usable information by quantifying what a rational agent could extract given partial knowledge. BMI estimates the reduction in uncertainty about a property given the representations, factoring in computational constraints. It generalizes classical mutual information by breaking the data-processing inequality, allowing subsequent layers to retain or even increase usable information. Core assumption: The agent's prior beliefs and posterior approximation (e.g., Gaussian prior on weights) are reasonable for the task. Evidence anchors: [section 2.2] "BMI estimates the information a rational agent could obtain from a random variable given only partial knowledge of its distribution." Break condition: If the prior or posterior approximation is misspecified, BMI estimates may not reflect true usable information.

### Mechanism 3
Pairwise probing isolates causal effects by creating molecule pairs that differ only in the target property. By constructing source-target pairs where only the functional group is removed, and centering the representations, the difference vectors align with the property direction. High cosine similarity across pairs indicates a consistent causal direction. Core assumption: The SCM DAG holds (F → M → Z) and intervening on F blocks backdoor paths, making the difference vectors causally interpretable. Evidence anchors: [section 2.2] "To better isolate the effect of a certain property on the representation we propose pairwise probing... the dataset is now balanced w.r.t. pi, making the probe models easier to train." [section 2.2] "Similar to Yang et al. (2022), we assume our generative process is based on the causal DAG... we can block the backdoor path." Break condition: If the SCM assumptions are violated (e.g., unmeasured confounders), the estimated treatment effect may be biased.

## Foundational Learning

- **Weisfeiler-Lehman (WL) isomorphism hierarchy**: Defines theoretical bounds on what graph neural networks can distinguish; understanding it clarifies why some models capture more chemical information. Quick check: If a GNN is 1-WL equivalent, can it distinguish graphs that require 2-WL for isomorphism testing? Answer: No, it cannot.

- **Mutual Information (MI) and Bayesian Mutual Information (BMI)**: Quantify the information shared between representations and properties, but MI is limited by the data-processing inequality, while BMI generalizes it. Quick check: Does classical MI guarantee that a deeper layer retains at least as much information about the input as an earlier layer? Answer: No, it can only decrease.

- **Probing dataset construction and independence**: To avoid confounding effects from the training data, probing datasets must be independent and sufficiently large. Quick check: If the probing dataset is drawn from the same distribution as the training data, what risk arises? Answer: Risk of overfitting or capturing spurious correlations rather than genuine encoding.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: Graph encoders (GCN, GIN, Graphormer, GRPE, GraphGPS, TokenGT) -> Representation extraction layer -> Probing models (linear regression, logistic regression, Poisson regression) -> Evaluation metrics (AUC, R², BMI) -> Dataset handlers (PCQM4Mv2, MoleculeNet, odor datasets)

- **Critical path**: 1. Pretrain graph model on PCQM4Mv2. 2. Generate hidden representations for probing dataset. 3. Train and evaluate linear probes for each property. 4. Optionally compute BMI and/or run pairwise probing. 5. Aggregate results across properties and models.

- **Design tradeoffs**: Linear vs. non-linear probes: Linear probes are interpretable but may underestimate information; non-linear probes may overfit. Dataset size: Larger probing datasets improve BMI estimates but increase compute cost. Pairwise construction: Guarantees balance but requires property manipulation; not all properties are easily pairable.

- **Failure signatures**: Linear probe performance near random baseline → property not linearly encoded. BMI plateaus early → representations lack rich information or prior is mismatched. Pairwise cosine similarity low → property not consistently represented in the same direction.

- **First 3 experiments**: 1. Run linear probing for atom counting on GCN representations; check if AUC > 0.5. 2. Compare BMI estimates for GCN vs. Graphormer with 1000-sample probing dataset. 3. Generate pairwise probing dataset for benzene presence and compute PCA alignment.

## Limitations
- The study assumes linear probes adequately reveal what information is encoded, but cannot rule out non-linear interactions that linear models miss.
- The choice of Bayesian Mutual Information as an information-theoretic metric depends on the correctness of the prior/posterior approximations, which are not empirically validated in this work.
- The pairwise probing approach assumes the causal structure (functional group → molecule → representation) holds, but unmeasured confounders or violations of the SCM assumptions could bias the estimated treatment effects.

## Confidence
- **High**: Transformer models (Graphormer, GRPE, GraphGPS, TokenGT) learn richer representations than message-passing GNNs, as evidenced by consistently higher probing performance across multiple molecular properties.
- **Medium**: Random initialization can serve as useful feature extraction, since probing performance does not drop to random baseline levels, but the practical utility of such features is not demonstrated on downstream tasks.
- **Low**: Skip connections and virtual nodes improve probing performance, since results are not reported for ablated models, and architectural effects are not systematically studied.

## Next Checks
1. Test whether non-linear probes (e.g., small MLPs) significantly outperform linear probes for the same properties; if not, the linear probing assumption is validated.
2. Perform ablation studies on Graphormer by removing skip connections and virtual nodes; check if probing performance drops, confirming architectural benefits.
3. Apply the probing framework to a non-molecular graph dataset (e.g., social or citation networks) to test generalizability beyond chemistry.
