# Backchannel Detection and Agreement Estimation from Video with Transformer Networks

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01656
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01656
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: One-layer transformer architecture achieves 66.4% accuracy for backchannel detection and 0.0604 MSE for agreement estimation, setting new state-of-the-art on the MultiMediate challenge.

## Executive Summary
This work presents a comprehensive evaluation of transformer-based multi-modal fusion architectures for automatic backchannel analysis from body pose and facial behavior. A single one-stream transformer layer emerged as the best-performing architecture for backchannel detection, reaching 66.4% accuracy and outperforming the previous state of the art on the MultiMediate challenge. For agreement estimation, an architecture consisting of two subsequent one-stream transformer layers performed best, reaching a new state of the art with 0.0604 MSE. The experiments prove the effectiveness of transformer-based approaches over recurrent neural networks for automatic backchannel analysis, but also point to the need to carefully evaluate sophisticated architectures against more simple ones that might reach competitive-or even better performance.

## Method Summary
The method involves extracting facial keypoints via OpenFace 2.0 and body keypoints via OpenPose from 10-second video clips of the MPIIGroupInteraction dataset. The input to models is the absolute difference of features between consecutive frames for the last 3 seconds. Six transformer architectures were evaluated, including one-stream, one-to-one stream, one-to-two stream, two-to-one stream, cross-attention, and cross-to-one stream variants. Models were trained with Adam optimizer (lr=0.0005, wd=0.0005) for 350 epochs, using BCE loss for detection and MSE loss for agreement, with intermediate losses for multi-layer models. Positional encoding was only applied in the first layer, with 4/2/10 attention heads for face/pose/concatenated input.

## Key Results
- One-layer one-stream transformer network achieves 66.4% accuracy for backchannel detection, outperforming previous state of the art.
- Two subsequent one-stream transformer layers achieve 0.0604 MSE for agreement estimation, setting new state of the art.
- Transformer-based approaches outperform recurrent neural networks for both backchannel detection and agreement estimation tasks.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Multi-modal transformer networks can fuse pose and facial behavior features more effectively than early concatenation or recurrent networks for backchannel analysis.
- Mechanism: The self-attention mechanism in transformers allows the model to dynamically weigh the importance of different input features and their temporal relationships, enabling more nuanced integration of visual cues from face and body posture compared to static or sequential approaches.
- Core assumption: Pose and facial features contain complementary information that can be better leveraged through learned attention patterns than through fixed concatenation or LSTM memory.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Current state-of-the-art approaches for backchannel analysis from visual behavior make use of two types of features: features based on body pose and features based on facial behavior... transformer neural networks have been established as an effective means to fuse input from different data sources"
  - [section]: "The key component of the transformer layer is the self-attention mechanism, which allows the model to selectively focus on certain parts of the input sequence while processing it"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus shows related work on backchannel prediction but does not directly validate transformer fusion effectiveness.
- Break condition: If pose and facial features are highly redundant or if their temporal patterns are too simple for attention mechanisms to provide meaningful differentiation.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: A single one-stream transformer layer outperforms more complex multi-modal architectures for backchannel detection.
- Mechanism: The one-stream architecture concatenates all modalities early and applies a single transformer layer, reducing architectural complexity and potential overfitting while still capturing cross-modal interactions through attention.
- Core assumption: The complexity of the one-stream approach is sufficient to model the relationships between pose and facial features for backchannel detection without the need for hierarchical or modality-specific processing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "we reach 66.4% accuracy with a one-layer transformer architecture, outperforming the previous state of the art"
  - [section]: "the best validation performance was achieved by a single-layer, one-stream transformer network (0.736 ACC)"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus shows related work on multi-modal fusion but does not directly validate single-layer superiority.
- Break condition: If the relationship between modalities is too complex for a single layer to capture, or if the dataset is large enough that more complex architectures can be trained without overfitting.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Two subsequent one-stream transformer layers are optimal for agreement estimation from backchannels.
- Mechanism: The first transformer layer learns initial cross-modal representations, while the second layer refines these representations for the regression task of agreement estimation, allowing for hierarchical feature learning.
- Core assumption: Agreement estimation requires a more complex representation of the backchannel behavior than binary detection, necessitating deeper processing.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "for the task of agreement estimation from backchannels, we set a new state of the art (0.0604 MSE) with a two-layer transformer architecture"
  - [section]: "the best performance (0.0644 MSE) was achieved by two subsequent single-stream transformer layers"
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence - corpus shows related work on agreement estimation but does not directly validate two-layer effectiveness.
- Break condition: If the additional layer does not provide significant improvement over a single layer, or if the dataset is too small to train a deeper network effectively.

- Failure signatures: Poor performance on validation set compared to baseline; overfitting indicated by large gap between training and validation performance; failure to converge during training.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Evaluate baseline LSTM performance on backchannel detection task.
  2. Test one-stream transformer with single layer on both detection and agreement estimation tasks.
  3. Compare one-stream with two-layer one-stream architecture on agreement estimation task.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How well do transformer-based models for backchannel detection and agreement estimation generalize to group interactions in languages other than German?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes that the MPIIGroupInteraction dataset is limited to German discussions and suggests collecting corpora involving different languages and cultural backgrounds.
- Why unresolved: The study only evaluated on German-language data; no cross-linguistic validation was performed.
- What evidence would resolve it: Testing the same transformer architectures on datasets from other languages and cultures, and comparing performance to ensure robustness across linguistic and cultural contexts.

### Open Question 2
- Question: Does incorporating audio features improve backchannel detection and agreement estimation when using transformer-based multi-modal fusion architectures?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states that previous work found minimal improvement from adding audio, but suggests it is worthwhile to investigate this in the context of transformer-based multi-modal fusion.
- Why unresolved: Audio was excluded in the current study; prior work with non-transformer models did not show significant gains, but transformer models may leverage audio differently.
- What evidence would resolve it: Training and evaluating transformer models with combined audio-visual features on the same datasets, and comparing their performance to vision-only models.

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the optimal transformer architecture for multi-modal fusion when integrating pose and facial features for backchannel analysis?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper compares multiple transformer architectures (one-stream, one-to-one stream, one-to-two stream, etc.) and finds that simpler one-stream models often outperform more complex ones, but the evaluation is limited to the tested set.
- Why unresolved: The study tested a subset of possible architectures and combinations; more complex or hybrid designs were not explored.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experimentation with additional transformer designs, such as deeper cross-attention layers, modality-specific encoders, or adaptive fusion strategies, and comparing their performance on the same tasks.

## Limitations

- Limited dataset generalizability due to evaluation on only one dataset (MPIIGroupInteraction) with a small test set (200 samples).
- Frame differencing preprocessing may discard absolute feature values that could be informative for backchannel analysis.
- Only compares against recurrent neural networks as baselines, omitting direct comparisons with other state-of-the-art transformer-based approaches.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The superiority of transformer networks over RNNs for backchannel analysis is well-supported by the experimental results, with clear improvements in both detection accuracy and agreement estimation MSE.
- **Medium Confidence**: The claim that a single one-stream transformer layer is optimal for backchannel detection is supported by the ablation study but may be dataset-specific.
- **Low Confidence**: The assertion that two subsequent one-stream layers are optimal for agreement estimation requires further validation on larger datasets to rule out overfitting to the current dataset's characteristics.
