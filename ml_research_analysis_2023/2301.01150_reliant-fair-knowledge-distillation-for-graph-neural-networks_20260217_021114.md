# RELIANT: Fair Knowledge Distillation for Graph Neural Networks

## Executive Summary
This paper tackles the challenge of achieving fair knowledge distillation in graph neural networks (GNNs), addressing the issue of bias propagation from teacher to student models. The authors propose RELIANT, a framework that learns a proxy of bias as extra input attributes to account for and mitigate bias in the student model. By manipulating this proxy during inference, RELIANT effectively reduces bias while maintaining high prediction utility. Experiments on multiple real-world datasets demonstrate that RELIANT achieves less biased GNN knowledge distillation compared to baseline methods, with improvements in fairness metrics such as ∆SP and ∆EO, while preserving model performance.

## Method Summary
RELIANT introduces a learnable proxy of bias as additional node attributes for the student model. During training, the proxy is optimized to account for the exhibited bias in the student model, while the student model itself is optimized for both utility and attribution losses. The framework approximates non-differentiable fairness metrics using orthogonal polynomials to enable end-to-end learning. During inference, the proxy is manipulated (e.g., by replacing it with an averaged proxy) to remove bias information from the input. RELIANT is designed to be student-agnostic, decoupling from specific teacher and student model structures.

## Key Results
- RELIANT achieves less biased GNN knowledge distillation compared to baseline methods.
- The framework maintains high prediction utility while reducing bias metrics (∆SP, ∆EO).
- RELIANT is easily adaptable to various GNN-based KD frameworks without requiring modifications to student model architecture.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Teacher GNN -> Student Model -> Proxy of Bias -> RELIANT Framework

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize the proxy of bias.
  2. Optimize the student model parameters using the utility and attribution loss terms.
  3. Optimize the proxy of bias using the proxy loss term.
  4. Repeat steps 2-3 until convergence.
  5. During inference, manipulate the proxy to remove bias information.

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Utility vs. Fairness: RELIANT aims to balance prediction utility and bias mitigation. The hyperparameter λ controls the trade-off between these two objectives.
  - Approximation Accuracy vs. Differentiability: Using polynomial approximations for fairness metrics enables end-to-end learning but may introduce approximation errors.

- Failure signatures:
  - High bias in student model despite training with RELIANT.
  - Significant drop in prediction utility after applying RELIANT.
  - Poor convergence or instability during training.

## Limitations
- The effectiveness of the proxy-based bias mitigation approach relies heavily on the assumption that the learnable proxy can accurately capture and represent bias in the student model.
- The use of polynomial approximations for fairness metrics introduces potential approximation errors that could affect the debiasing performance.
- The paper does not provide extensive analysis on the long-term stability and generalization of the debiased student models.
