# Opportunities for Large Language Models and Discourse in Engineering Design

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.09169
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09169
- Reference count: 40
- This paper argues that LLMs can assist in engineering design by participating in goal-oriented, argumentative discourse, proposing explicit documentation of reasoning steps as digital artifacts to improve collaboration and enable machine participation.

## Executive Summary
This paper explores how large language models can transform engineering design by participating in the argumentative discourse that underlies the design process. The authors propose making design reasoning explicit as digital artifacts, which would improve documentation, increase collaboration, and enable machines to participate in reasoning. LLMs are particularly well-suited for this role due to their natural language capabilities, rich world representations, and strong reasoning performance across tasks. The paper outlines research directions including formalizing engineering design discourse, creating machine-actionable interfaces for engineering software, developing better representations for engineering skills, and establishing evaluation metrics and datasets.

## Method Summary
The paper provides a conceptual framework rather than a specific implementation. It proposes collecting textual engineering design problems, requirements, and concepts to fine-tune large language models using few-shot learning or instruction fine-tuning. The fine-tuned models would then be integrated into a formalized engineering design discourse framework that breaks down the design process into stages and components. This integration would allow models to provide suggestions, ask clarifying questions, and reason about design problems. However, the paper does not detail specific training procedures or provide a concrete framework structure.

## Key Results
- LLMs possess capabilities (natural language processing, reasoning, knowledge representation) that are relevant to engineering design tasks
- Making design reasoning explicit as digital artifacts can improve documentation and enable machine participation
- Formalizing engineering design discourse into stages and components could increase the detail at which reasoning processes can be documented and verified

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The paper argues that engineering design is fundamentally a discourse-based process involving argumentation and reasoning about design alternatives. LLMs can participate in this discourse because they can understand and generate natural language, possess rich representations of engineering concepts and the physical world, and demonstrate strong reasoning capabilities across various domains. By formalizing the design process into explicit stages and components, the reasoning steps become machine-readable artifacts that enable LLMs to contribute meaningfully to the design conversation rather than just providing isolated suggestions.

## Foundational Learning
- Engineering design discourse: Goal-oriented argumentative conversations about design problems and solutions - needed because design is fundamentally about reasoning and communication
- Quick check: Can identify the recurring stages and components in a sample engineering design discussion

- Design representation: Formal structures that capture design problems, requirements, and concepts - needed because machines require explicit representations to participate effectively
- Quick check: Can map natural language design descriptions to structured representations

- Few-shot learning: Training approach where models learn from very few examples - needed because engineering design data is scarce and expensive to collect
- Quick check: Can perform design tasks with minimal training examples

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map:** Design Problem Description -> Formalized Discourse Framework -> Fine-tuned LLM -> Design Suggestions/Reasoning

**Critical Path:** The path from design problem input through formalization to LLM output and back to human designers is critical for ensuring meaningful participation in the design discourse.

**Design Tradeoffs:** The paper suggests balancing model autonomy with human oversight, as well as choosing between comprehensive frameworks that capture all design aspects versus simpler frameworks that are easier to implement and verify.

**Failure Signatures:** The model may generate irrelevant or incorrect suggestions if the training data doesn't adequately represent the target design domain, or if the formalized framework doesn't capture the essential aspects of engineering design discourse.

**3 First Experiments:**
1. Fine-tune a language model on a small dataset of engineering patent texts and evaluate its ability to generate design concept descriptions
2. Create a simple two-stage design discourse framework and test LLM participation in basic design discussions
3. Develop a prototype interface that allows designers to interact with the fine-tuned model within a formalized discourse structure

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out
- Can large language models effectively participate in the engineering design discourse by automating parts that involve creativity and reasoning?
- What are the specific evaluation metrics and datasets required to assess the performance of LLMs in the engineering design discourse?
- How can the engineering design discourse be formalized within a framework of stages and components to improve the interpretability and accuracy of LLMs?

## Limitations
- The paper does not provide a concrete framework for engineering design discourse, only proposing the need for one
- Specific evaluation metrics and datasets for assessing LLM performance in design contexts are not specified
- No empirical evidence or case studies demonstrating LLM effectiveness in actual engineering design scenarios

## Confidence
- High confidence: LLMs possess relevant capabilities (natural language processing, reasoning, knowledge representation) for engineering design tasks
- Medium confidence: LLMs can meaningfully participate in goal-oriented discourse about design problems when properly integrated
- Low confidence: The specific mechanisms for formalizing engineering design discourse and creating machine-actionable interfaces are fully specified

## Next Checks
1. Develop and publish a detailed specification for the engineering design discourse framework, including stage definitions, component relationships, and interaction protocols between humans and LLMs
2. Create a public dataset of engineering design problems with ground truth solutions and establish evaluation metrics specifically designed for assessing LLM performance in design contexts
3. Build a working prototype that demonstrates LLM integration into a simplified version of the formalized design framework, showing concrete examples of how the model participates in design discussions and provides value
