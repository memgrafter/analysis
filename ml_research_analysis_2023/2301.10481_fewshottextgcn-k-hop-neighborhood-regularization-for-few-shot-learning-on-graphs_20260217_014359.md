# FewShotTextGCN: K-hop neighborhood regularization for few-shot learning on graphs

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.10481
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10481
- Reference count: 14
- Key outcome: 17% higher accuracy than TextGCN baseline using only 20 labeled samples across 8 languages

## Executive Summary
FewShotTextGCN addresses few-shot text classification in low-resource languages by introducing K-hop Neighborhood Regularization (K-NR) for heterogeneous word-document graphs. The method significantly improves performance in low-resource settings while using only ~1% of parameters compared to large pretrained language models. It achieves this through graph simplification, novel regularization, and adaptive pseudo-labeling, making it particularly effective when labeled data is scarce.

## Method Summary
FewShotTextGCN is a GCN-based method for few-shot text classification that operates on word-document graphs. It simplifies the traditional TextGCN graph construction by removing word-word edges, reducing graph density by ~7x. The method introduces K-hop Neighborhood Regularization (K-NR) using triplet margin loss to stabilize learning in low-resource settings, and incorporates an adaptive pseudo-labeling variant tailored for word-document graphs. The model uses two GCN layers with 64-dimensional hidden size, trained with Ranger optimizer and dropout=0.5.

## Key Results
- Achieves 17% higher accuracy than TextGCN baseline when using only 20 labeled samples across 8 typologically diverse languages
- Performs on par with large pretrained language models while using only ~1% of their parameters
- Reduces graph density by ~7x through simplified graph construction without word-word edges

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: K-hop Neighborhood Regularization stabilizes learning by forcing document embeddings to incorporate vocabulary information
- Core assumption: Documents sharing vocabulary are more likely to belong to the same topic class
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "we introduce K-hop Neighborhood Regularization" and [section 5] "documents that share large parts of their vocabulary are more likely to be about the same topic"
- Break condition: If vocabulary overlap becomes noisy or dominated by stop words

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Removing word-word edges reduces graph density while maintaining/improving performance
- Core assumption: Word-word PMI edges provide redundant information recoverable through message passing
- Evidence anchors: [section 5] "simplification results in improved performance" and [section 8] "average performance using graphs without word-word edges is always higher"
- Break condition: In tasks requiring precise word co-occurrence patterns (e.g., sentiment analysis)

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Adaptive pseudo-labeling improves few-shot performance by leveraging graph structure
- Core assumption: There exist words highly distinctive for certain classes allowing confident pseudo-label generation
- Evidence anchors: [abstract] "new variant of Adaptive Pseudo-Labeling" and [section 5] "directly optimize unlabeled document embedding to be a good predictor"
- Break condition: If pseudo-label confidence threshold is too low (noisy labels) or too high (too few documents labeled)

  - Why needed here: The entire method relies on GCNs to propagate information between word and document nodes
  - Quick check question: In a 2-layer GCN on a word-document graph, how many hops away can a document node influence another document node?

- Concept: Heterogenous graph structures
  - Why needed here: The method exploits the bipartite word-document structure specifically
  - Quick check question: Why can't standard Node2Vec-style sampling be directly applied to word-document graphs?

- Concept: Semi-supervised learning and pseudo-labeling
  - Why needed here: The method uses unlabeled documents to improve few-shot learning performance
  - Quick check question: What is the key difference between standard pseudo-labeling and the variant proposed here?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Graph construction: Tokenization → TF-IDF weighting → adjacency matrix (word-document edges only)
  - K-NR module: Triplet sampling → margin loss computation → gradient update
  - GCN backbone: Two GCN layers → linear classifier
  - Pseudo-labeling: Confidence thresholding → cross-entropy loss on selected nodes
  - Training loop: Combines main loss + K-NR + pseudo-labeling losses

- Critical path:
  1. Graph construction (preprocessing)
  2. GCN forward pass
  3. Loss computation (main + K-NR + pseudo-labeling)
  4. Backward pass and parameter update

- Design tradeoffs:
  - K-NR slows convergence but improves generalization
  - Removing word-word edges speeds computation but may lose some co-occurrence signal
  - Pseudo-labeling adds complexity but leverages unlabeled data

- Failure signatures:
  - High validation loss with K-NR: learning rate too high or margin too aggressive
  - No improvement from pseudo-labeling: confidence threshold too strict or graph too sparse
  - Memory issues: graph density too high (add word-word edges back)

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Baseline TextGCN vs simplified graph (no word-word edges) on MLDoc with 2% training data
  2. K-NR ablation: TextGCN + K-NR vs TextGCN baseline on same setting
  3. Full FewShotTextGCN vs TextGCN + K-NR + pseudo-labeling on R8/R52 datasets

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the optimal value of K for K-hop Neighborhood Regularization across different graph types?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates K=2 for word-document graphs and suggests K-NR could be applied to other heterogeneous graphs
- Why unresolved: Only K=2 is empirically validated; different graph structures may benefit from different K values
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments varying K across diverse graph types while measuring performance and computational efficiency

### Open Question 2
- Question: How does FewShotTextGCN compare to modern few-shot PLM approaches with prompt engineering or adapter-based fine-tuning?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper compares to vanilla mBERT and XLM-R but doesn't explore recent few-shot PLM techniques
- Why unresolved: Limited comparison to standard PLM training; modern few-shot techniques could bridge performance gaps
- What evidence would resolve it: Head-to-head comparison against state-of-the-art few-shot PLM approaches under identical settings

### Open Question 3
- Question: What is the impact of different word tokenization strategies across languages with varying morphological complexity?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper notes Russian's inflectional complexity but uses basic tokenization
- Why unresolved: Doesn't systematically investigate how tokenization strategies affect performance for morphologically rich languages
- What evidence would resolve it: Controlled experiments comparing different tokenization strategies across languages with varying morphological complexity

## Limitations

- Lacks ablation studies isolating individual contributions of K-NR, graph simplification, and pseudo-labeling
- No comparison against strong few-shot baselines specifically designed for low-resource text classification
- Limited discussion of scalability to larger datasets or longer documents

## Confidence

- **High**: The claim that removing word-word edges improves performance while reducing graph density - directly supported by experimental results
- **Medium**: The effectiveness of K-hop Neighborhood Regularization in stabilizing few-shot learning - results show improvement but mechanism contribution is unclear
- **Medium**: The overall performance advantage over TextGCN baseline - significant improvements reported but lack of ablation studies prevents clear attribution
