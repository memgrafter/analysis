# SAM operates far from home: eigenvalue regularization as a dynamical phenomenon
## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2302.08692
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.08692
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: SAM provides eigenvalue regularization throughout training trajectories, not just near minima, through a modified edge of stability effect

## Executive Summary
This work demonstrates that Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) induces a modified edge of stability (EOS) effect that stabilizes large eigenvalues at lower values than pure gradient descent throughout the entire learning trajectory. The authors analyze a quadratic regression model to show that SAM modifies the neural tangent kernel by adding a term (1 + ρJJ⊤), increasing eigenvalues and shifting the EOS threshold to η(λmax + ρλ²max) ≈ 2. CIFAR-10 experiments with WideResNet confirm this prediction, revealing that SAM's effectiveness stems from dynamical phenomena beyond just minima behavior.

## Method Summary
The authors combine theoretical analysis of a quadratic regression model with empirical validation on CIFAR-10 using WideResNet 28-10. The quadratic model simulates SAM dynamics through update rules that capture the modified neural tangent kernel structure, while tracking the largest eigenvalue of JJ⊤ over training steps. The CIFAR-10 experiments compare SGD and SAM across different learning rates and SAM radii, computing Hessian eigenvalues at each step using Lanczos methods to verify the predicted SAM-EOS boundary.

## Key Results
- SAM induces eigenvalue stabilization at a modified edge of stability (SAM-EOS), where eigenvalues stabilize at lower values than pure gradient descent
- SAM-normalized eigenvalues η(λmax + ρλ²max) stabilize near the critical value of 2 in CIFAR-10 experiments
- SAM's regularization effect depends primarily on the ratio of SAM radius to learning rate (ρ/η)

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: SAM induces eigenvalue stabilization at a modified edge of stability (SAM-EOS), where eigenvalues stabilize at lower values than pure gradient descent.
- Mechanism: SAM modifies the neural tangent kernel (NTK) by adding a term (1 + ρJJ⊤), increasing the eigenvalues and shifting the EOS threshold to η(λmax + ρλ²max) ≈ 2. This causes the largest eigenvalues to stabilize at a lower magnitude than pure GD.
- Core assumption: The dynamics of SAM can be approximated by a modified NTK with increased eigenvalues due to the SAM radius parameter ρ.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "A quadratic regression model analysis reveals that SAM induces a modified edge of stability (EOS) effect, stabilizing large eigenvalues at lower values than pure gradient descent."
  - [section 2.2.2] "We will show that SAM also induces an EOS stabilization effect, but at a smaller eigenvalue than GD. We can understand the shift intuitively by analyzing un-normalized SAM on a loss 1/2 x⊤Hx. Direct calculation gives the update rule: xt+1−xt = −η(H + ρH²)xt. For positive definite H, xt converges exponentially to 0 iff η(λmax + ρλ²max) < 2."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct mention of SAM-EOS in corpus, but related work on EOS dynamics exists (e.g., 'The Implicit Regularization of Dynamical Stability in Stochastic Gradient Descent').

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: SAM provides eigenvalue regularization throughout the learning trajectory, not just near minima.
- Mechanism: SAM dynamically regularizes large eigenvalues by inducing a non-linear feedback between the largest curvature eigenvalue and the displacement in the largest eigendirection, leading to stabilization at the SAM-EOS.
- Core assumption: The regularization effect of SAM is not limited to the vicinity of minima but extends throughout the entire training trajectory.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our work reveals that SAM provides a strong regularization of the eigenvalues throughout the learning trajectory."
  - [section 2.2.1] "This analysis suggests that on average, at early times, the change in the singular value is negative. However, the change also depends linearly on (wα·z0)². This suggests that if the component of z in the direction of the singular vector becomes small, the stabilizing effect of SAM becomes small as well."
  - [section 3.1] "For larger learning rates, SAM has a strong suppressing effect on the largest eigenvalues... The eigenvalues stabilize at the modified EOS boundary η(λmax + ρλ²max) = 2."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct mention of trajectory-wide regularization in corpus, but related work on EOS dynamics exists.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The effectiveness of SAM depends on the ratio of the SAM radius to the learning rate (r = ρ/η).
- Mechanism: Rescaling the dynamics shows that the ratio r determines the amount of regularization SAM provides, with larger values of r leading to stronger regularization effects.
- Core assumption: The key dimensionless parameter governing SAM's regularization effect is the ratio of the SAM radius to the learning rate.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section A.1] "This suggests that, at least for gradient descent, the ratio of the SAM radius to the learning rate determines the amount of regularization which SAM provides."
  - [section 3.1] "In Appendix A.3 we conduct additional experiments which confirm that the boundary predicts the largest eigenvalue for a range of ρ, and that consequently generally increasing ρ leads to decreased λmax."
  - [corpus] Weak: No direct mention of the ρ/η ratio in corpus, but related work on SAM hyperparameters exists.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does SAM's eigenvalue regularization mechanism differ between full batch and mini-batch training settings?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that for full batch training, SAM induces a modified EOS effect that stabilizes large eigenvalues at lower values than pure gradient descent. However, the analysis of SAM under SGD is noted as an important open question.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides theoretical analysis for full batch settings and some insights into SGD through Theorem 2.3, a complete understanding requires analysis of E_P[(z·vi)^2] for different eigenmodes vi, which has only recently been analyzed for quadratic loss functions.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed experimental and theoretical analysis comparing eigenvalue dynamics across various batch sizes, particularly focusing on how the SAM-EOS prediction holds up under different sampling regimes and how the eigenvalue regularization mechanism scales with batch size.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the optimal SAM radius scheduling strategy for different training phases?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper shows that both early SAM and late SAM schedules provide benefits over SGD-only training, with generally better results when turning SAM on later or for the full trajectory. However, the authors note that a more detailed understanding of SAM-EOS stabilization could lead to proposals for ρ-schedules.
- Why unresolved: While the paper demonstrates that SAM schedules can successfully move between GD-EOS and SAM-EOS regimes, the optimal timing and magnitude of ρ adjustments throughout training remains unexplored. The complex dependence of SAM behavior on ρ and the model suggests that a one-size-fits-all approach may not be optimal.
- What evidence would resolve it: Systematic experiments testing various ρ-scheduling strategies (e.g., linear decay, cosine annealing, adaptive scheduling based on eigenvalue measurements) across different model architectures and datasets, coupled with theoretical analysis of how different scheduling approaches affect the SAM-EOS dynamics.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How do different loss functions affect SAM's ability to control large eigenvalues and its relationship to generalization?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper demonstrates that SAM's eigenvalue regularization works for both MSE and cross-entropy losses, but notes qualitative differences in behavior. For cross-entropy, eigenvalue decrease starts earlier in training, and gradient magnitudes show larger variation.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides preliminary evidence that SAM-EOS effects exist for cross-entropy loss, the underlying mechanisms differ from MSE loss. The authors note that the origin of early eigenvalue decrease for cross-entropy may be due to interactions between logit magnitude and the softmax function, but this requires further investigation.
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of SAM's eigenvalue regularization across multiple loss functions (MSE, cross-entropy, hinge loss, etc.) including detailed characterization of how loss function properties (e.g., gradient scaling, saturation behavior) affect the SAM-EOS dynamics and generalization performance.

## Limitations
- The theoretical analysis relies on a quadratic approximation that may not capture all relevant dynamics in deep networks
- The exact relationship between the SAM-EOS in the quadratic model and its manifestation in WideResNet remains incompletely characterized
- The impact of stochastic gradients and finite batch sizes on the eigenvalue regularization effect is not fully explored
