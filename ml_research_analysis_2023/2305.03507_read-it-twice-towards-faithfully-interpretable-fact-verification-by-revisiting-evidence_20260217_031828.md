# Read it Twice: Towards Faithfully Interpretable Fact Verification by Revisiting Evidence

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.03507
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03507
- **Reference count**: 40
- **Primary result**: 4.30% and 4.32% improvements in Micro F1 and Macro F1 on CHEF dataset

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of fact verification by focusing on the quality of retrieved evidence. The authors identify a key limitation in existing approaches: retrieved evidence often lacks faithfulness (reflecting the model's actual reasoning) and plausibility (convincing to humans). To address this, they propose ReRead, a two-stage model that first trains an evidence retriever using faithfulness and plausibility criteria, then uses this optimized retriever to improve claim verification accuracy.

## Method Summary
ReRead employs a two-stage approach to fact verification. First, it trains an evidence retriever using a combination of faithfulness and plausibility losses. Faithfulness is measured through fullness (whether evidence covers all relevant information) and sufficiency (whether evidence alone is enough to verify the claim). Plausibility is assessed by whether the retrieved evidence would convince humans. In the second stage, the optimized retriever is used to retrieve evidence for claim verification. The model is trained on the Chinese real-world dataset CHEF, with evaluations showing improvements in both verification accuracy and interpretability of retrieved evidence.

## Key Results
- Achieves 4.30% and 4.32% improvements in Micro F1 and Macro F1 respectively over state-of-the-art baseline on CHEF
- Improves interpretability of retrieved evidence
- Human evaluation shows 5.87% increase in verification accuracy
- Ablation studies confirm importance of each loss function

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The two-stage approach works by first optimizing the evidence retrieval process using faithfulness and plausibility criteria, rather than just retrieval accuracy. By training the retriever to find evidence that is both faithful to the model's decision process and plausible to humans, the system ensures that the evidence used for verification is high-quality and interpretable. This addresses the key limitation of existing approaches where retrieved evidence may be accurate but not faithful or plausible, leading to poor interpretability and potentially incorrect verification decisions.

## Foundational Learning

**Faithfulness Metrics**
- Why needed: To ensure evidence reflects the model's actual reasoning process
- Quick check: Measure fullness (coverage of relevant information) and sufficiency (evidence alone sufficient for verification)

**Plausibility Criteria**
- Why needed: To ensure evidence is convincing to human readers
- Quick check: Human evaluation of whether evidence would support the verification decision

**Two-Stage Training**
- Why needed: To separate evidence retrieval optimization from claim verification
- Quick check: Compare performance of jointly trained vs. two-stage approaches

## Architecture Onboarding

**Component Map**
Evidence Retriever -> Evidence Verifier -> Verification Decision

**Critical Path**
1. Input claim processed by evidence retriever
2. Retriever selects relevant documents based on faithfulness and plausibility
3. Selected evidence passed to verifier for final decision
4. Verification output generated

**Design Tradeoffs**
- Two-stage approach increases computational complexity but improves evidence quality
- Separate training stages prevent interference between retrieval and verification objectives
- Focus on Chinese language may limit generalizability but allows for specialized optimization

**Failure Signatures**
- Poor evidence retrieval leading to cascading errors in verification
- Over-optimization for faithfulness metrics at expense of actual verification accuracy
- Computational overhead making real-time application impractical

**3 First Experiments**
1. Ablation study removing each loss function to assess individual contributions
2. Human evaluation comparing evidence quality with baseline approaches
3. Cross-dataset validation on non-Chinese fact verification datasets

## Limitations
- Evaluation limited to single Chinese dataset (CHEF), limiting generalizability
- Faithfulness metrics rely heavily on automated measures rather than human assessment
- Two-stage approach increases computational complexity and may introduce cascading errors

## Confidence
- **High confidence**: Technical approach of using faithfulness and plausibility criteria is sound
- **Medium confidence**: Generalizability to other languages and domains
