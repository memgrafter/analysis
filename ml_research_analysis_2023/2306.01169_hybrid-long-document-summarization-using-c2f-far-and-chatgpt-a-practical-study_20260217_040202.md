# Hybrid Long Document Summarization using C2F-FAR and ChatGPT: A Practical Study

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.01169
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01169
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: Hybrid pipeline combining C2F-FAR extraction with ChatGPT paraphrasing shows comparable automated metrics to human summaries but reveals critical coherence and faithfulness issues in human evaluation.

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a hybrid summarization pipeline that combines the C2F-FAR extractive model with ChatGPT for long document summarization. The approach first extracts important sentences using C2F-FAR's block-based semantic grouping, then uses ChatGPT to summarize and paraphrase the extracted content. Experiments on 20 business articles and 20 books demonstrate that while automated metrics like ROUGE and BERTScore suggest comparable performance to human-written summaries, human evaluation reveals significant problems with text coherence, faithfulness, and style—particularly in later summary sections. The results indicate that current models, while promising, require substantial human editing before practical deployment.

## Method Summary
The hybrid approach processes long documents by first cleaning PDF/text content, then applying C2F-FAR to extract important sentences through semantic block formation and centrality scoring. The extracted sentences are chunked into manageable pieces for ChatGPT input, which generates paraphrased summaries. These chunks are concatenated into final summaries. The method addresses ChatGPT's token limitations while attempting to maintain summary quality through the extractive preprocessing stage.

## Key Results
- Automated metrics (ROUGE-1: 0.44-0.46, BERTScore) show machine-generated summaries comparable to human-written ones
- Human evaluation reveals critical issues with text coherence, faithfulness, and style in machine-generated summaries
- Quality degradation occurs particularly in later parts of summaries when processing chunked content
- Despite moderate automated metric performance, summaries require substantial human editing before practical use

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: C2F-FAR captures high-level document structure by grouping semantically similar sentences into blocks before selection
- Mechanism: Sentences are encoded via pre-trained language models; adjacent sentences are grouped into blocks based on similarity thresholds; centrality estimators filter blocks and select top-scoring sentences within blocks
- Core assumption: Semantic blocks preserve topical coherence, so key sentences from each block represent overall document meaning
- Break condition: Documents lacking clear adjacent sentence similarity will have misrepresented topical structure and degraded summary quality

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: ChatGPT abstracts and paraphrases extracted sentences, increasing text fluency and reducing redundancy
- Mechanism: Extracted sentences are fed in chunks to ChatGPT with prompts like "Please rewrite the text in your own words"
- Core assumption: ChatGPT's paraphrasing preserves core facts while smoothing mechanical sentence boundaries from C2F-FAR
- Break condition: Over-paraphrasing by ChatGPT may omit key details or numerical facts, harming faithfulness

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Two-stage pipeline mitigates ChatGPT's token limit by extracting manageable subsets before generation
- Mechanism: Long documents are reduced to ~100 sentences via C2F-FAR, then fed to ChatGPT in chunks
- Core assumption: Extracted sentences are sufficient to reconstruct coherent summaries without full document access
- Break condition: Key information outside extracted sentences or chunking disrupting logical flow will produce incomplete or incoherent summaries

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Semantic block formation in extractive summarization
  - Why needed here: C2F-FAR's effectiveness depends on correctly grouping topically coherent sentences
  - Quick check question: If a document's adjacent sentences are topically unrelated, what happens to block quality and downstream summary relevance?

- Concept: Zero-shot generation and paraphrasing
  - Why needed here: ChatGPT is used without fine-tuning, so understanding its general language capabilities is critical
  - Quick check question: What metrics best capture whether ChatGPT's paraphrasing preserves factual accuracy versus merely improving fluency?

- Concept: Chunked long-document processing
  - Why needed here: Overcoming ChatGPT's token limit requires breaking documents into manageable pieces without breaking narrative flow
  - Quick check question: How can one measure coherence loss introduced by summarizing each chunk independently and concatenating results?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → PDF/Text Cleaner → C2F-FAR Extractor → Sentence Chunk Generator → ChatGPT Paraphraser → Output
- Critical path:
  1. Clean document text (remove headers, footers, non-ASCII, etc.)
  2. Run C2F-FAR to extract top sentences
  3. Split extracted sentences into ChatGPT-sized chunks
  4. Generate paraphrased summary chunks
  5. Concatenate chunks into final summary
- Design tradeoffs:
  - C2F-FAR parameter tuning vs. runtime cost
  - Number of extracted sentences vs. faithfulness vs. ChatGPT capacity
  - Chunk size vs. coherence vs. avoiding context loss
- Failure signatures:
  - Low ROUGE-2/ROUGE-L → C2F-FAR losing phrase-level matches
  - High ESTIME alarms → ChatGPT introducing new words not in source
  - Human flags on "lack of coherence" → chunking disrupting flow
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Vary C2F-FAR block size parameter and measure ROUGE-1/2 changes on sample articles
  2. Compare ChatGPT paraphrase quality with/without chunk boundaries using human evaluation
  3. Test GPT-4 vs. GPT-3.5 on the same pipeline to quantify hallucination reduction

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does the quality of machine-generated summaries compare to human-written summaries when evaluated using automated metrics like ROUGE and BERTScore?
- Basis in paper: The paper presents experiments comparing machine-generated summaries using C2F-FAR and ChatGPT to human-written summaries using automated metrics like ROUGE and BERTScore.
- Why unresolved: The paper shows automated metrics suggest comparable performance, but human evaluation reveals critical issues not captured by these metrics.
- What evidence would resolve it: Additional human evaluation studies comparing machine-generated and human-written summaries using different metrics and datasets.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the key challenges in using large language models like ChatGPT for summarizing long documents?
- Basis in paper: The paper identifies challenges like text coherence, faithfulness, and style in machine-generated summaries, especially in later parts of summaries.
- Why unresolved: The paper highlights these issues but does not provide solutions or investigate root causes.
- What evidence would resolve it: Further research into causes of these challenges and potential solutions, such as improving model architecture or incorporating human feedback.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can human editors effectively use machine-generated summaries to improve their work efficiency?
- Basis in paper: The paper suggests current models can serve as inspiration but not full replacement for human editors, with a lack of guidelines for efficient human-machine collaboration.
- Why unresolved: The paper identifies potential for human-machine collaboration but does not provide concrete guidelines or best practices.
- What evidence would resolve it: Studies on practical application of machine-generated summaries in editorial workflows, including guidelines and metrics for evaluating effectiveness.

## Limitations
- Small dataset (20 business articles, 20 books) limits generalizability to other domains
- Automated metrics show moderate performance but human evaluation reveals significant quality issues
- Hybrid approach requires substantial human editing before practical deployment
- Chunking mechanism introduces coherence breaks that automated metrics cannot detect

## Confidence
- High confidence: The pipeline architecture (C2F-FAR + ChatGPT) is correctly implemented and produces measurable outputs
- Medium confidence: Automated metric results (ROUGE, BERTScore) are reliable but incomplete for evaluating long document summaries
- Low confidence: Claims about "practical" application and "impressive" results are overstated given critical human evaluation findings
