# Automated Reading Passage Generation with OpenAI's Large Language Model

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.04616
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04616
- Reference count: 10
- Primary result: GPT-3 can generate reading passages similar in quality to human-written ones for fourth-grade assessments

## Executive Summary
This study explores the application of OpenAI's GPT-3 large language model for automated generation of reading passages in primary school assessments. Researchers used carefully engineered prompts based on original PIRLS passages to generate new passages, which were then filtered by text difficulty and edited for errors. Human expert judges evaluated both AI-generated and original passages on coherence, appropriateness for fourth graders, and engagement. Results showed that GPT-3 could produce passages of comparable quality to human-written ones, though some AI passages were found to be more distracting and harder to follow. The study demonstrates promising potential for automating passage generation in reading assessments while highlighting the need for human oversight.

## Method Summary
The study employed GPT-3 (text-davinci-002) to generate reading passages through carefully designed prompts based on original PIRLS passages. Researchers used zero-shot and one-shot prompting with varying temperature settings (0.5, 0.7, 0.9) to generate multiple passages. Generated passages were filtered based on their Lexile score similarity to original passages, with selections made within one standard deviation of the original score. Human editors then corrected grammatical and factual errors in the selected passages. Finally, expert judges evaluated the AI-generated passages alongside original passages using a 4-point Likert scale questionnaire covering coherence, appropriateness for fourth graders, readability, and engagement.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 generated passages demonstrated coherence and age-appropriateness comparable to original passages
- AI passages were sometimes more distracting and harder to follow than original passages
- Expert evaluation showed similar ratings between AI-generated and human-written passages across most quality dimensions
- Human editing was critical for correcting factual errors, particularly in informational passages

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: GPT-3 can generate coherent and age-appropriate reading passages when given carefully designed prompts and age indicators.
- Mechanism: The large language model uses few-shot learning from the provided prompt examples to generate passages that match the desired style, structure, and reading level. Including the target audience's age helps the model calibrate the complexity and content appropriateness.
- Core assumption: The model has sufficient training data on reading passages and age-appropriate content to draw from when generating new passages.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Human post-generation quality control and editing is critical for ensuring factual accuracy and coherence in AI-generated passages.
- Mechanism: While GPT-3 can generate grammatically correct and coherent text, it may produce factual errors or illogical statements. Human editors review the passages and correct any errors, improving overall quality and suitability for the target audience.
- Core assumption: Human judgment is needed to assess factual accuracy and logical coherence in generated text.

- Break condition: Human editing is insufficient to catch all factual errors or the editing process is not rigorous enough.

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Few-shot learning in large language models
  - Why needed here: GPT-3 relies on few-shot learning from the provided prompts to generate passages that match the desired style and structure. Understanding how this works is crucial for designing effective prompts.
  - Quick check question: What is the difference between zero-shot, one-shot, and few-shot learning in the context of large language models?

- Concept: Text difficulty metrics and their relationship to reading level
  - Why needed here: The generated passages are selected and evaluated based on their text difficulty scores, which need to align with the target grade level. Understanding how these metrics work is important for assessing passage appropriateness.
  - Quick check question: How do text difficulty metrics like Lexile scores measure reading level, and what factors contribute to a passage's difficulty?

- Concept: Human evaluation methods for educational content
  - Why needed here: The study relies on expert human evaluation to assess the quality and appropriateness of the generated passages. Familiarity with common evaluation methods and criteria is important for interpreting and building upon the results.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: GPT-3 API -> Prompt design -> Passage generation -> Text difficulty scoring -> Passage selection -> Human editing -> Expert evaluation
- Critical path: prompt design -> passage generation -> text difficulty scoring -> passage selection -> human editing -> human evaluation
- Design tradeoffs: One key tradeoff is between the amount of information provided in the prompt (e.g. zero-shot vs. one-shot learning) and the model's ability to generate relevant, appropriate content. Another tradeoff is between the speed of the automated generation process and the quality assurance provided by human editing and evaluation.
- Failure signatures: Common failure modes include the model generating off-topic or inappropriate content, factual errors slipping through the editing process, or the human evaluation not catching issues with coherence or engagement.
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test the effect of different prompt structures (e.g. zero-shot vs. one-shot) on the quality and relevance of the generated passages.
  2. Evaluate the impact of different text difficulty thresholds for selecting passages on the overall quality and appropriateness of the generated passages.
  3. Compare the results of expert human evaluation with automated text analysis tools to assess the validity and reliability of the evaluation process.

## Limitations
- The study did not directly compare GPT-3 generated passages with passages written by professional assessment developers, making it difficult to assess the true quality of AI-generated content
- The evaluation was conducted by a small team of four human experts, raising questions about the reliability and generalizability of the assessment
- The study did not report quantitative measures of inter-rater reliability among the human evaluators

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: GPT-3 can generate grammatically correct and coherent reading passages when given appropriate prompts and age indicators.

**Medium Confidence**: AI-generated passages are comparable in quality to human-written passages for fourth-grade reading assessments.

**Low Confidence**: GPT-3 can reliably generate factually accurate reading passages without extensive human editing.

## Next Checks

1. **Expand the expert evaluation**: Conduct the human evaluation with a larger, more diverse group of education experts and report inter-rater reliability statistics to strengthen the validity of the quality assessment.

2. **Compare against professional assessment items**: Generate AI passages using the same methodology but compare them directly to passages written by professional assessment developers rather than just the original PIRLS passages, to better establish the relative quality of AI-generated content.

3. **Test the system with different passage types**: Apply the automated generation process to a wider variety of passage genres (e.g., narrative, expository, poetry) and difficulty levels to evaluate the generalizability and limitations of the approach across different assessment contexts.
