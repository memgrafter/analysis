# Approximate information state based convergence analysis of recurrent Q-learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.05991
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05991
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: RQL-AIS outperforms ND-R2D2 baseline on MiniGrid POMDPs by incorporating AIS losses

## Executive Summary
This paper analyzes recurrent Q-learning (RQL) for partially observable Markov decision processes (POMDPs) where the agent state is non-Markovian. The authors first prove that RQL converges in the tabular setting without requiring stationarity or ergodicity of the agent state. They then introduce the concept of approximate information state (AIS) to quantify how representation quality affects the converged solution. Based on this theoretical analysis, they propose RQL-AIS, a variant that incorporates AIS losses during training. Empirical evaluation on MiniGrid environments demonstrates that RQL-AIS outperforms the state-of-the-art ND-R2D2 baseline, particularly in more challenging scenarios.

## Method Summary
The method combines n-step Q-learning with AIS losses to improve learning in POMDPs. The AIS framework predicts rewards and next observations from the current agent state and action, with losses computed using integral probability metrics (IPMs). The RQL-AIS algorithm uses an LSTM to maintain agent state from history, predicts rewards and observations, and updates Q-values using n-step returns. AIS losses are backpropagated to the representation network but not to the Q-network. The approach is evaluated against ND-R2D2 baseline on MiniGrid benchmark environments with pre-trained autoencoder observation compression.

## Key Results
- RQL converges in tabular POMDPs without requiring stationarity or ergodicity of the agent state
- AIS approximation errors (εt, δt) provide theoretical bounds on the sub-optimality of the converged Q-function
- RQL-AIS outperforms ND-R2D2 baseline on 22 MiniGrid environments, with larger gains in more challenging tasks
- Strong correlation between AIS loss reduction and performance improvement during training

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** Recurrent Q-learning converges in POMDPs without requiring stationarity or ergodicity of the agent state.
- **Mechanism:** By adapting stochastic approximation theory, the paper splits the Q-function error into two state components (W1 and W2). Component W2, driven by immediate approximation errors, converges almost surely. Component W1, driven by value function errors, decreases monotonically once below a threshold Cϵ, ensuring overall convergence.
- **Core assumption:** The agent state space and action space are finite, and the exploration policy induces a unique stationary distribution over (state, observation, agent state, action) tuples.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "it is shown that in spite of the lack of the Markov property, recurrent Q-learning (RQL) converges in the tabular setting."
  - [section 3.1] "We show that it is possible to adapt the standard convergence arguments to show that RQL converges."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no related paper explicitly proves convergence under non-stationarity. Assumption: This is novel theoretical contribution.
- **Break condition:** If the state space is infinite or the exploration policy fails to induce a positive stationary distribution, the convergence proof breaks down.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** The quality of the converged Q-function depends on the quality of the approximate information state (AIS) representation.
- **Mechanism:** The AIS framework quantifies approximation error via two terms: εt (reward prediction error) and δt (state transition error). These errors bound the sub-optimality of the converged Q-function using integral probability metrics (IPMs).
- **Core assumption:** An AIS-generator exists that approximates the history-based dynamics and rewards within bounded error.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "it is shown that the quality of the converged limit depends on the quality of the representation which is quantified in terms of what is known as an approximate information state (AIS)."
  - [section 3.2] "Our key observation is that for any F, (σt, Pξ, rξ) is an (εt, δt)t≥1 AIS-generator..."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; assumption: The AIS framework is assumed to be applicable here without external validation.
- **Break condition:** If the AIS approximation errors grow unbounded over time, the bounds become meaningless and the converged policy may be arbitrarily sub-optimal.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** Adding AIS losses during training improves RQL performance in practice.
- **Mechanism:** By jointly minimizing εt and δt via AIS losses (using MMD or ℓ2 distances), the representation becomes more predictive, enabling Q-learning updates to propagate through histories more effectively. This accelerates learning, especially in sparse-reward environments.
- **Core assumption:** The AIS loss is a useful auxiliary signal that correlates with downstream RL performance.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract] "Based on this characterization of the approximation error, a variant of RQL with AIS losses is presented. This variant performs better than a strong baseline..."
  - [section 4.2] "there is a strong correlation between the performance of RQL over time and the loss associated with the AIS representation."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence; no related paper reports AIS-based auxiliary losses improving RQL. Assumption: The empirical correlation is assumed to be causal.
- **Break condition:** If the AIS loss optimization interferes with Q-learning or the chosen IPM is ill-suited to the problem, performance may degrade.

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:**
  - History compression (σ) -> LSTM mapping history to agent state
  - Reward predictor (ˆr) -> Predicts expected reward from (z,a)
  - Observation predictor (ˆP y) -> Predicts next observation from (z,a)
  - Q-function (ˆQθ) -> Estimates action values from (z,a)
  - Replay buffer -> Stores sequences of (observation, action, reward) with burn-in histories

- **Critical path:**
  1. Collect experience using epsilon-greedy policy
  2. Store sequences in R2D2 replay buffer with burn-in
  3. Sample batch, reconstruct agent states via σ
  4. Compute AIS loss (reward + observation prediction)
  5. Compute n-step Q-learning loss
  6. Backpropagate AIS loss to σ, ˆr, ˆP y; backpropagate Q-loss to ˆQθ only
  7. Update target network periodically

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - AIS loss vs. Q-learning interference: AIS losses must not backpropagate into Q-function
  - Sequence length vs. representation drift: Shorter main sequences reduce drift but may hurt learning
  - Uniform vs. prioritized sampling: PER helps in sparse rewards but can hurt early in training

- **Failure signatures:**
  - High AIS loss + flat reward curve: Representation not learning meaningful features
  - High Q-learning loss + low AIS loss: AIS may be overfitting or Q-network underfitting
  - Performance drops after many steps: Representation drift or catastrophic forgetting

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Train on a simple MiniGrid environment (e.g., Unlock) with uniform sampling; verify AIS loss decreases and reward increases
  2. Remove AIS loss block; compare learning speed and final performance on same environment
  3. Switch to prioritized sampling; observe impact on convergence in a harder environment (e.g., KeyCorridorS3R3)

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- **Open Question 1:** Can the convergence analysis of recurrent Q-learning be extended to the function approximation setting where both the state space and recurrent state space are continuous?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper's theoretical analysis is restricted to the tabular setting with finite state and recurrent state spaces.
  - **Why unresolved:** Extending the convergence proof to the function approximation setting requires handling the additional complexity of infinite state spaces and ensuring that the Bellman operator remains a contraction under function approximation.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** A rigorous convergence proof for recurrent Q-learning with function approximation, along with empirical validation on continuous state space problems.

- **Open Question 2:** How does the performance of recurrent Q-learning with AIS losses compare to other exploration strategies in sparse reward environments?
  - **Basis in paper:** [explicit] The paper shows that RQL-AIS outperforms ND-R2D2 in challenging MiniGrid environments, suggesting that AIS losses provide a form of exploration bonus.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper does not directly compare RQL-AIS to other exploration strategies such as intrinsic motivation or curiosity-driven exploration.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Empirical comparison of RQL-AIS with other exploration strategies on a range of sparse reward environments, measuring both sample efficiency and final performance.

- **Open Question 3:** What is the impact of using an AIS-predictor instead of an observation predictor on the performance of recurrent Q-learning?
  - **Basis in paper:** [inferred] The paper uses an observation predictor as a proxy for the AIS-predictor, which may not be necessary for a good AIS.
  - **Why unresolved:** The paper does not explore the performance difference between using an AIS-predictor and an observation predictor.
  - **What evidence would resolve it:** Implementation and comparison of recurrent Q-learning with both AIS-predictor and observation predictor on a range of POMDPs, measuring the quality of the learned representations and the final performance.

## Limitations

- Theoretical convergence proof is limited to tabular settings with finite state and action spaces
- AIS approximation error bounds are theoretical and not quantified for practical problem scales
- Empirical validation is limited to MiniGrid benchmark environments, which may not represent real-world POMDP challenges
- Superior performance is demonstrated only against a single strong baseline (ND-R2D2)

## Confidence

- RQL convergence without stationarity/ergodicity: **Medium** - The theoretical proof appears sound but relies on assumptions that may not hold in practice
- AIS error bounds determining performance: **Low-Medium** - The theoretical framework is established, but practical bounds are not quantified
- AIS losses improve learning in practice: **Medium** - Empirical results show improvement, but the mechanism is not fully explained and could be due to other factors
