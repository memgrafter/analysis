# Evaluation of Social Biases in Recent Large Pre-Trained Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2304.06861
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.06861
- Reference count: 6
- Three recent pre-trained language models (ELECTRA, DeBERTa, DistilBERT) show lower bias than BERT on bias benchmarks.

## Executive Summary
This study evaluates social biases in three recent pre-trained language models—ELECTRA, DeBERTa, and DistilBERT—against two bias benchmarks (StereoSet and CrowS-Pairs), comparing their performance to BERT as a baseline. The models were tested on multiple social bias categories such as gender, race, religion, and others. Results showed that while all models exhibited bias, the newer models generally performed better than BERT, indicating a trend toward reduced bias in recent pre-trained language models. DeBERTa, in particular, demonstrated a strong inclination toward anti-stereotypical responses, especially in intersentence tasks. The study highlights the importance of addressing social bias in model development alongside improvements in speed, efficiency, and performance.

## Method Summary
The study evaluates three recent pre-trained language models (ELECTRA, DeBERTa, DistilBERT) using two bias benchmarks (StereoSet and CrowS-Pairs) and compares their performance to BERT as a baseline. The models are tested on multiple social bias categories, and bias is quantified using metrics such as Language Modeling Score, Stereotype Score, and Idealized CAT Score. The evaluation pipeline involves loading and preprocessing the benchmark datasets, running the models, computing the specified metrics, and comparing results across models.

## Key Results
- Newer pre-trained language models (ELECTRA, DeBERTa, DistilBERT) exhibit lower stereotypical bias than older models like BERT.
- DeBERTa demonstrates a strong inclination toward anti-stereotypical responses, especially in intersentence tasks.
- While all models exhibit bias, the newer models generally performed better than BERT, indicating a trend toward reduced bias in recent pre-trained language models.

- Core assumption: The training corpora and techniques used for newer models have improved over time, resulting in less biased representations.

- Break condition: If newer models are trained on similar or more biased data than older models, or if architectural improvements do not target bias mitigation, the observed trend may not hold.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: DeBERTa's lower bias scores are due to its training on a carefully curated Wikipedia corpus and its smaller parameter count, which forces it to learn anti-stereotypical associations.
- Mechanism: DeBERTa's NSP fine-tuning on Wikipedia, a source with editorial oversight, combined with its parameter efficiency, leads to learning less biased associations.
- Core assumption: Wikipedia's content is less biased than general web data, and DeBERTa's architecture is more sensitive to such curated training.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Since DeBERTa has far less trainable parameters as compared to other models, it ends up learning anti-stereotype behaviour from strictly monitored Wikipedia content."
  - [abstract] "DeBERTa, in particular, demonstrated a strong inclination toward anti-stereotypical responses, especially in intersentence tasks."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence comparing Wikipedia's bias level to other training corpora.
- Break condition: If Wikipedia itself contains significant biases, or if DeBERTa's architecture does not inherently favor anti-stereotypical learning, the observed effect may be due to other factors.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The observed reduction in bias across models is an artifact of benchmark design or evaluation methodology rather than true bias reduction.
- Mechanism: Changes in benchmark datasets, metrics, or evaluation procedures over time may artificially inflate the appearance of bias reduction.

## Limitations

- The study does not control for differences in training data composition across models, making it difficult to isolate whether architectural improvements or data curation drive bias reduction.
- The benchmarks used (StereoSet and CrowS-Pairs) are not validated for temporal consistency, raising the possibility that observed trends reflect changes in evaluation methodology rather than true bias reduction.
- The attribution of DeBERTa's low bias to Wikipedia's "editorial oversight" is speculative, as no direct evidence is provided comparing bias levels in Wikipedia versus other corpora.

## Confidence

- High: Observation that newer models (ELECTRA, DeBERTa, DistilBERT) show lower bias than BERT on the evaluated benchmarks.
- Medium: Interpretation that architectural improvements are the primary driver of bias reduction.
- Low: Attribution of DeBERTa's low bias specifically to Wikipedia's editorial oversight and parameter efficiency.
