# Unsupervised Task Graph Generation from Instructional Video Transcripts

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2302.09173
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09173
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Unsupervised method achieves 72.1% graph prediction accuracy on ProceL and 68.4% on CrossTask, outperforming supervised baseline

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an unsupervised approach for generating task graphs from instructional video transcripts. The method leverages language models to summarize transcripts, cluster key steps, relabel sequences, and rank them to produce task graphs. Unlike supervised approaches, it doesn't require labeled training data, making it more scalable for real-world applications. The method demonstrates superior performance compared to supervised baselines on two benchmark datasets while producing more detailed task graphs in certain cases.

## Method Summary
The approach processes video transcripts through a pipeline of language model operations. First, it generates summaries of each transcript to condense the instructional content. These summaries are then clustered to identify distinct key steps in the task. The clustered summaries are relabeled with their corresponding key steps, and another language model ranks these relabeled sequences based on their likelihood of representing the correct task structure. Finally, an off-the-shelf graph generation algorithm creates the task graph from the highest-ranked sequences. This unsupervised method eliminates the need for labeled training data while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Key Results
- Achieves 72.1% graph prediction accuracy on ProceL dataset
- Achieves 68.4% graph prediction accuracy on CrossTask dataset
- Outperforms supervised learning baseline (62.9% on ProceL, 60.8% on CrossTask)
- Produces more detailed task graphs compared to human-annotated ground truth in some cases

## Why This Works (Mechanism)
The method works by leveraging language models' ability to understand and summarize instructional content without requiring task-specific training. By clustering summaries, the approach identifies distinct procedural steps across multiple videos. The ranking step ensures that the most representative sequences are used for graph generation, while the final graph algorithm structures these steps into a coherent task representation. This unsupervised approach bypasses the need for expensive annotation while maintaining accuracy through multiple filtering and ranking stages.

## Foundational Learning

Language Model Summarization - Why needed: To condense lengthy video transcripts into manageable, representative content; Quick check: Verify summaries capture essential steps without losing critical information

Clustering Algorithms - Why needed: To group similar procedural steps across different videos; Quick check: Ensure clusters represent distinct task steps with minimal overlap

Graph Generation Algorithms - Why needed: To structure ranked sequences into coherent task graphs; Quick check: Validate generated graphs follow logical task flow and capture dependencies

## Architecture Onboarding

Component Map: Transcript -> Summarization -> Clustering -> Relabeling -> Ranking -> Graph Generation

Critical Path: Summarization and Ranking are most critical - poor summaries lead to incorrect clusters, and poor ranking produces suboptimal graph inputs

Design Tradeoffs: The method trades supervised accuracy for unsupervised scalability, accepting that ground truth annotations may be less detailed than generated graphs

Failure Signatures: Performance drops when transcripts contain ambiguous language, when clustering fails to separate distinct steps, or when ranking incorrectly prioritizes sequences

First Experiments:
1. Test summarization quality by comparing generated summaries against human-written abstracts
2. Evaluate clustering effectiveness by measuring intra-cluster similarity and inter-cluster differences
3. Assess ranking accuracy by comparing top-ranked sequences against known correct task structures

## Limitations

The evaluation metric comparing against human annotations may be inappropriate since the unsupervised method can produce more detailed graphs than ground truth. Performance still leaves significant room for improvement with nearly 30% incorrect predictions. The method shows lower performance on CrossTask (68.4%) compared to ProceL (72.1%), suggesting dataset-specific limitations.

## Confidence

Method effectiveness on ProceL: Medium-High
Method effectiveness on CrossTask: Medium
Comparison with human annotations: Low-Medium
Generalizability across domains: Low
