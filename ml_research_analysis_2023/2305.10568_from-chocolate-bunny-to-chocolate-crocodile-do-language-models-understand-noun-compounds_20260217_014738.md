# From chocolate bunny to chocolate crocodile: Do Language Models Understand Noun Compounds?

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.10568
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10568
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 9
- Key outcome: GPT-3 achieves near-perfect performance on interpreting existing noun compounds and outperforms humans on novel compound conceptualization.

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates how well large language models can interpret noun compounds (NCs), both existing and novel ones. The authors clean and expand the SemEval 2013 dataset for existing NCs, then evaluate T5 and GPT-3 on this task. GPT-3 achieves near-perfect performance, likely due to memorization of web-scale text containing common compound definitions. For novel NCs (NCC), GPT-3 outperforms humans, presumably thanks to its vast knowledge base and ability to generalize without the cognitive constraints humans face. The authors also find that GPT-3 often parrots its training data, though this strategy is less beneficial for novel compounds.

## Method Summary
The authors evaluate noun compound interpretation using two tasks: NCI (interpreting existing compounds) and NCC (conceptualizing novel ones). For NCI, they clean and expand the SemEval 2013 dataset by removing train-test overlap, correcting paraphrases, removing catch-all templates, and augmenting with WordNet synonyms. They fine-tune T5-large and use few-shot prompting with GPT-3 to generate paraphrases. For NCC, they use rare or novel compounds from Google Ngram and Twitter datasets with GPT-3 only. Evaluation includes automatic metrics (METEOR, ROUGE-L, BERTScore) and human evaluation via Amazon Mechanical Turk. They also analyze n-gram overlap with the C4 corpus to quantify parroting behavior.

## Key Results
- GPT-3 achieves near-perfect performance on existing noun compound interpretation (NCI)
- GPT-3 outperforms humans on novel noun compound conceptualization (NCC)
- GPT-3's outputs show significant overlap with training corpus, especially for common compounds

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- **Claim:** GPT-3 achieves near-perfect performance on interpreting existing noun compounds (NCI) due to its access to vast amounts of pre-training data that includes many compound definitions and contexts.
- **Mechanism:** GPT-3 leverages memorization of web-scale text to recall precise or near-precise paraphrases of common noun compounds that appeared in its training corpus.
- **Core assumption:** The training corpus contains sufficient instances of common noun compounds with their explicit or implicit meanings expressed in natural language.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "it tends to memorize texts seen during pre-training (Carlini et al., 2022), likely including partial or complete definitions of common NCs."
  - [section]: "GPT-3 achieved near perfect performance on this new test set."
  - [corpus]: Weak evidence; relies on external citation of Carlini et al. (2022) but no direct quantitative overlap analysis provided in this paper for NCI.
- **Break condition:** If the target noun compound is absent or extremely rare in the pre-training corpus, memorization will fail and GPT-3 must rely on generalization.

### Mechanism 2
- **Claim:** GPT-3 outperforms humans on noun compound conceptualization (NCC) due to its ability to generalize from semantically similar observed compounds and its lack of cognitive effort constraints.
- **Mechanism:** GPT-3 combines learned knowledge of constituent noun meanings with pattern generalization to propose plausible interpretations for novel compounds, without the cognitive load humans face.
- **Core assumption:** Semantic similarity between novel and observed compounds in the training data is sufficient for GPT-3 to infer plausible interpretations.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "it is better than that of humans -- likely thanks to its access to vast amounts of knowledge, and because conceptual processing is effortful for people."
  - [section]: "GPT-3 outperforms humans on NCC, presumably thanks to its fast access to a huge 'knowledge base', and compared to the relative human slowness on this task."
  - [corpus]: Weak; no explicit similarity analysis or retrieval examples shown, but implied by the comparison with human effortful processing.
- **Break condition:** If the novel compound is too semantically distant from any observed compound, GPT-3's generalization may fail.

### Mechanism 3
- **Claim:** GPT-3 often parrots its training data, which helps on NCI but is less beneficial for NCC.
- **Mechanism:** Generated paraphrases contain n-grams that frequently appear in the C4 corpus, indicating direct copying or close paraphrasing of training text.
- **Core assumption:** Overlap between GPT-3 outputs and large web corpus indicates parroting rather than novel reasoning.
- **Evidence anchors:**
  - [abstract]: "We find that the outputs from GPT-3 often have significant overlap with a large web corpus, but that the parroting strategy is less beneficial for novel noun compounds."
  - [section]: "Figure 2 displays the percents of n-grams among the generated paraphrases (for n ={3, 4, 5}) that occur in the C4 corpus 0, 1-5, or 5+ times..."
  - [corpus]: Present; specific quantitative overlap data provided for both NCI and NCC, showing higher parroting in NCI and lower in NCC.
- **Break condition:** If generated paraphrases for novel compounds have little to no overlap with the corpus, parroting is not the source of performance.

## Foundational Learning

- **Concept:** Noun compound interpretation as a paraphrasing task
  - **Why needed here:** The paper focuses on free-text paraphrasing to express semantic relations between compound constituents, rather than classification into predefined relation labels.
  - **Quick check question:** Can you paraphrase "chocolate bunny" in a way that explicitly shows the relationship between "chocolate" and "bunny"?

- **Concept:** Difference between existing and novel noun compounds
  - **Why needed here:** NCI deals with interpreting known compounds, while NCC requires reasoning about rare or unseen compounds.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might "chocolate crocodile" be harder to interpret than "chocolate bunny"?

- **Concept:** Evaluation metrics for generative tasks
  - **Why needed here:** The paper uses both automatic metrics (METEOR, ROUGE-L, BERTScore) and human evaluation to assess paraphrase quality.
  - **Quick check question:** Why might automatic metrics favor T5 over GPT-3 even if GPT-3's paraphrases are more correct?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map:** Data cleaning pipeline -> Model interface (T5-large or GPT-3) -> Evaluation suite (automatic metrics + human MTurk) -> Overlap analysis with C4 corpus

- **Critical path:**
  1. Clean and augment NCI dataset.
  2. Train T5 or set up GPT-3 prompt.
  3. Generate paraphrases for test set.
  4. Evaluate with automatic and human metrics.
  5. Analyze overlap with web corpus.

- **Design tradeoffs:**
  - T5 vs GPT-3: T5 can mimic reference style but may generate generic paraphrases; GPT-3 is more creative but may parrot or hallucinate.
  - Human evaluation: high cost and time, but more reliable than automatic metrics for open-ended generation.
  - Dataset augmentation: increases training size but may introduce noise if synonyms alter meaning.

- **Failure signatures:**
  - Low human evaluation score despite high automatic metric score → model overfitting to reference style rather than meaning.
  - High corpus overlap for incorrect NCC paraphrases → model defaulting to memorization when generalization fails.
  - Poor performance on rare compounds → model lacks relevant training signal.

- **First 3 experiments:**
  1. Run T5 on cleaned NCI test set and compare automatic metrics with GPT-3 outputs.
  2. Perform human evaluation on a small subset of NCI paraphrases to validate automatic metric bias.
  3. Compute n-gram overlap for NCI and NCC outputs to quantify parroting effect.

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How can we improve the human evaluation process for noun compound conceptualization to better compare human and model outputs?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The paper mentions that the human evaluation setup had limitations, such as crowdsourcing workers being incentivized to provide any answer quickly, and the evaluation being binary (correct/incorrect) rather than nuanced.
- Why unresolved: The paper acknowledges these limitations but does not propose a specific improved evaluation methodology.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study implementing a more nuanced human evaluation process, such as using a rating scale instead of binary judgments, providing more time or incentives for workers, and comparing the results to the current evaluation method.

### Open Question 2
- Question: To what extent can large language models generalize their knowledge of noun compounds to low-resource languages?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that the methods presented can be adapted to other languages that use noun compounds, but notes that languages like German, Norwegian, Swedish, Danish, and Dutch write noun compounds as single words, requiring an additional step of separating the compounds into constituent nouns.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the performance of large language models on noun compound interpretation and conceptualization in low-resource languages.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments evaluating large language models on noun compound interpretation and conceptualization tasks in various low-resource languages, comparing their performance to high-resource languages.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can we better distinguish between a large language model's reasoning about the world and its parroting of training data when interpreting novel noun compounds?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper investigates the extent to which GPT-3 copies from its pre-training corpus when generating paraphrases for noun compounds, finding that copying is less beneficial for novel noun compounds.
- Why unresolved: While the paper provides evidence that GPT-3's performance on novel noun compounds is not solely due to parroting, it does not propose a definitive method to distinguish between reasoning and memorization.
- What evidence would resolve it: A study developing and evaluating metrics or methods to quantify the degree of reasoning versus memorization in a model's outputs for novel noun compounds, potentially by comparing the outputs to various corpora or using ablation studies.

## Limitations
- The exact GPT-3 few-shot prompt format is not fully specified, affecting reproducibility
- Methodology for distinguishing copying from legitimate knowledge use is not explicitly defined
- Human evaluation sample size and inter-annotator agreement statistics are not reported

## Confidence
**High confidence:** GPT-3's near-perfect performance on NCI, GPT-3's superior performance on NCC compared to humans, GPT-3's parroting behavior for common compounds.

**Medium confidence:** Specific mechanisms of GPT-3's generalization to novel compounds, extent to which automatic metrics under-estimate GPT-3 performance relative to human evaluation.
