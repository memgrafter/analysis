# Efficient Bayesian Physics Informed Neural Networks for Inverse Problems via Ensemble Kalman Inversion

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2303.07392
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07392
- Reference count: 40
- Key outcome: EKI B-PINNs achieve 8-30x speedup over HMC-based B-PINNs while providing comparable uncertainty quantification for physical parameter inference

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces an efficient approach for Bayesian inference in Physics Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) using Ensemble Kalman Inversion (EKI). Traditional Bayesian PINNs rely on computationally expensive Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods, particularly for high-dimensional parameter spaces. The proposed EKI B-PINN method reformulates the inference problem as a Bayesian inverse problem and leverages EKI's gradient-free optimization, linear computational scaling with parameter dimension, and rapid convergence. The method demonstrates significant computational speedups (8-30x) compared to Hamiltonian Monte Carlo while maintaining comparable accuracy in parameter estimation and uncertainty quantification across various PDE examples including diffusion-reaction systems and Burgers' equation.

## Method Summary
The method combines PINNs with EKI for Bayesian inverse problems. The PINN serves as a forward surrogate that approximates PDE solutions, while EKI performs efficient Bayesian inference on both physical parameters and neural network weights. The measurement operator G(ξ) combines forward solution evaluations, residual points, and boundary conditions. An ensemble of J=1000 particles is initialized from the prior distribution and updated iteratively using the Kalman gain based on sample covariances. The algorithm stops when the relative improvement in discrepancy over a window W=25 falls below threshold τ=0.05. The approach is implemented using JAX for efficient computation and leverages the neural network surrogate to avoid expensive PDE solver evaluations.

## Key Results
- EKI B-PINN achieves 8-30x speedup compared to HMC-based B-PINNs on tested PDE examples
- Comparable parameter estimation accuracy with relative errors similar to HMC baseline
- Provides informative uncertainty estimates for both physical parameters and forward predictions
- Demonstrates linear computational scaling with parameter dimension
- Shows robust performance across linear and nonlinear PDEs including Burgers' equation

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Measurement operator G(ξ) -> Ensemble of parameter samples -> Covariance computation -> Update rule -> Stopping criterion

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize ensemble from prior
  2. Compute measurement operator for all ensemble members
  3. Calculate sample covariances
  4. Apply Kalman update to obtain posterior ensemble
  5. Check stopping criterion
  6. Return final ensemble for uncertainty quantification

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Ensemble size J vs. computational cost: Larger J provides better posterior approximation but increases memory and computation
  - Observation covariance R choice: Affects uncertainty quantification and convergence behavior
  - Artificial dynamics covariance Q: Controls ensemble collapse vs. exploration

- Failure signatures:
  - Ensemble collapse: Small spread in ensemble members, poor uncertainty estimates
  - Slow convergence: Discrepancy metric plateaus slowly, many iterations needed
  - Numerical instability: Singular covariance matrices, poor conditioning

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Simple 1D Poisson equation with known analytical solution to verify basic implementation
  2. Nonlinear 1D problem with moderate noise to test convergence behavior
  3. 2D diffusion-reaction equation to evaluate scalability and uncertainty quantification

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

- Question: How does EKI B-PINN perform on large datasets or problems with a high number of residual points, and what are the computational challenges in such cases?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper mentions that naive approaches to EKI would be computationally expensive for large datasets due to cubic scaling with the size of the dataset, and suggests mini-batching techniques may help.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate this case and leaves it as future work.

- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper acknowledges that EKI requires storing an ensemble of J neural network parameter sets, which can be memory-demanding, and suggests dimension reduction techniques may help.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate this strategy and leaves it as future work.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies applying dimension reduction techniques to EKI B-PINN, and analysis of the impact on memory usage, computational cost, and accuracy.

### Open Question 3
- Question: How can the stopping criterion for EKI B-PINN be improved to better balance computational efficiency and accuracy?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper uses a relative change in discrepancy over a fixed iteration window as the stopping criterion, but acknowledges that the choice of the threshold is dependent on the observation covariance matrix.
- Why unresolved: The paper does not investigate alternative stopping criteria or their impact on the performance of EKI B-PINN.
- What evidence would resolve it: Empirical studies comparing different stopping criteria, and analysis of their impact on the convergence, accuracy, and computational cost of EKI B-PINN.
