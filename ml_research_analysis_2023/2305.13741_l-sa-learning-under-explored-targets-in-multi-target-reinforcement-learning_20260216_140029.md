# L-SA: Learning Under-Explored Targets in Multi-Target Reinforcement Learning

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.13741
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13741
- Reference count: 15
- One-line primary result: L-SA improves success rates by up to 490% and achieves 289.6% sample-efficiency improvement in multi-target RL with under-explored targets.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the Under-Explored Target Problem (UTP) in multi-target reinforcement learning, where difficult targets receive insufficient learning due to low success rates. The authors propose the L-SA framework that combines adaptive sampling and active querying to focus learning on under-explored targets. The framework dynamically samples targets with the highest success rate improvements and actively queries under-represented goal states, creating a virtuous cycle that enhances sample richness without requiring additional learnable parameters or prior knowledge.

## Method Summary
L-SA combines adaptive sampling with active querying in a multi-target RL setting. Adaptive sampling focuses representation learning on targets showing the highest relative increase in success rates, while active querying increases goal state collection for under-explored targets by over-sampling their instructions. The framework uses A3C with LSTM and gated-attention as the base, incorporating supervised contrastive learning (SupCon) on goal states collected from successful episodes. The two components interact cyclically: active querying enriches goal storage for hard targets, which adaptive sampling then uses to boost their representation learning, improving success rates and enabling more efficient future querying.

## Key Results
- L-SA achieved 289.6% sample-efficiency improvement over baseline methods
- Success rate improvements up to 490% for under-explored targets
- State-of-the-art performance on visual navigation tasks with varying target difficulties

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Adaptive sampling targets the "steepest increase" in success rate to focus representation learning where it has the most impact.
- Mechanism: At each update step, the method computes wx/t / wx/t-1 for each target x, selecting the target with the maximum ratio as the focused target. This target then receives a higher sampling proportion in the contrastive learning updates.
- Core assumption: The rate of change in success rate is a reliable proxy for learning progress and prioritization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "adaptive sampling dynamically samples targets with the highest increase of success rates"
  - [section] "the focused target is selected as the target whose relative increase in success rate at t-th update is the greatest"
- Break condition: If success rate curves flatten or become noisy, the ratio-based metric could mislead sampling toward already saturated targets or noise.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Active querying balances the distribution of goal states in storage by over-sampling under-represented targets.
- Mechanism: Queries are sampled multinomially with probability proportional to exp(-dx * τa), where dx is the proportion of target x in storage. This biases instruction selection toward targets with fewer stored experiences.
- Core assumption: The proportion of collected goal states accurately reflects the learning need of each target.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "active querying prompts the agent to interact more frequently with under-explored targets"
  - [section] "instructions are set more often for the targets that occupy a small proportion of the goal states collected"
- Break condition: If storage capacity is too small or goal collection is slow, the proportion dx may not reflect true learning need, causing over-querying of already hard targets.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: The cyclic interaction between adaptive sampling and active querying creates a virtuous feedback loop that progressively enriches data for under-explored targets.
- Mechanism: Active querying increases goal state collection for under-explored targets, which adaptive sampling then uses to boost their representation learning. This improves success rates, enabling more efficient future querying.
- Core assumption: The two mechanisms operate at compatible timescales and their effects compound rather than conflict.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "the cyclic relationship between adaptive sampling and active querying effectively improves the sample richness"
  - [section] "Our framework achieves these results through a virtuous cycle structure of experience and goal state collection through querying, and representation learning through adaptive sampling."
- Break condition: If one mechanism dominates or if their update intervals misalign, the cycle could break, leading to stagnation or oscillation.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  Base A3C module -> Goal storage -> Adaptive sampling module -> Active querying module -> Feature extractor -> SupCon loss

- Critical path:
  1. Agent interacts with environment, collects goal states on success
  2. Every 50 updates, compute adaptive sampling ratios and active querying probabilities
  3. Sample mini-batch from goal storage according to adaptive ratios
  4. Compute SupCon loss and backpropagate to update feature extractor
  5. Use active querying to set next instruction for exploration

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Adaptive sampling vs. random sampling: focuses learning but may neglect rare but important targets if ratios stagnate
  - Active querying vs. uniform querying: balances exploration but can over-query if storage is too small
  - Shared goal storage vs. per-thread storage: enables synchronized sampling but requires careful synchronization

- Failure signatures:
  - Low success rates on hard targets despite high active querying ratio: possible storage overflow or insufficient capacity
  - High variance in success rate curves: unsynchronized updates or noisy success rate estimates
  - Sampling ratio stuck on easy targets: success rate saturation not detected due to metric choice or update frequency

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run baseline A3C + SupCon with random sampling and querying on Studio-2N 2H; measure baseline success rates
  2. Replace random sampling with adaptive sampling only; observe impact on hard target learning
  3. Replace random querying with active querying only; measure changes in goal state distribution and success rates

## Limitations
- Empirical validation limited to controlled ViZDoom navigation environments with specific difficulty structures
- Adaptive sampling relies on success rate ratios as proxy for learning progress, which may not generalize to domains with noisy or delayed success metrics
- Active querying assumes goal state distribution in storage accurately reflects learning needs, which may break down in sparse reward environments

## Confidence

- High confidence: The mechanism of adaptive sampling based on success rate increases is well-grounded and the mathematical formulation is clear and reproducible
- Medium confidence: The sample efficiency improvements and relative performance gains across different target difficulties are supported by the presented results
- Low confidence: The claimed generality of the cyclic relationship between adaptive sampling and active querying across different RL domains and task types
