# Mitigating Approximate Memorization in Language Models via Dissimilarity Learned Policy

## Quick Facts
- **arXiv ID**: 2305.01550
- **Source URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01550
- **Reference count**: 28
- **Primary result**: RL-based fine-tuning reduces approximate memorization in GPT-Neo models while maintaining fluency

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces a reinforcement learning framework to mitigate approximate memorization in large language models (LLMs). The approach uses a negative similarity score (SacreBLEU or BERTScore) as a reward signal to fine-tune LLMs via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), encouraging generation of suffixes that are semantically dissimilar to the true suffix. Experiments on GPT-Neo models show effective reduction in approximate memorization, as measured by lower SacreBLEU scores, while preserving generation quality and fluency. The method is robust across different settings and does not require assumptions about data structure.

## Method Summary
The framework fine-tunes pretrained LLMs using PPO, where the reward is a negative similarity score between the generated suffix and the true suffix. A KL penalty ensures the fine-tuned policy stays close to the original pretrained model, preventing degenerate outputs. The method is evaluated on GPT-Neo models of varying sizes, using The Pile dataset, and measures effectiveness via SacreBLEU and perplexity.

## Key Results
- Negative SacreBLEU reward effectively reduces approximate memorization as measured by SacreBLEU scores.
- Larger models (2.7B) converge faster and achieve higher dissimilarity scores than smaller models (125M).
- The framework maintains generation fluency, as evidenced by stable perplexity scores across all model sizes.
- The approach is robust to longer contexts and does not require data partitioning or structural assumptions.

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Negative SacreBLEU as reward directly penalizes semantic similarity between generated and true suffixes, pushing the model to generate dissimilar outputs.
- Mechanism: By maximizing negative SacreBLEU (i.e., minimizing positive SacreBLEU), the model is incentivized to paraphrase or replace memorized phrases with semantically similar but different tokens.
- Core assumption: The similarity metric (SacreBLEU) is sensitive enough to detect approximate memorization but general enough to not over-constrain the model's fluency.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "Our approach utilizes a negative similarity score, such as BERTScore or SacreBLEU, as a reward signal to learn a dissimilarity policy."
  - [section 4.3]: "We experimented with three different reward functions: negative SacreBLEU, negative SacreBLEU combined with perplexity, and negative BERTScore."
  - [corpus]: Weak—no direct evidence in neighbors, but the mechanism is well-supported by the literature on reward shaping in RL for NLP.
- Break condition: If SacreBLEU becomes insensitive (e.g., low n-gram overlap but high semantic similarity), the reward signal may fail to guide the policy toward true dissimilarity.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PPO fine-tuning with a KL penalty ensures the learned policy remains close to the original pretrained model while improving dissimilarity.
- Mechanism: KL penalty prevents the model from drifting too far from the reference LM, avoiding degenerate outputs (e.g., whitespace repetition) while optimizing the dissimilarity reward.
- Core assumption: The pretrained LM is a good prior and should not be drastically altered, only adjusted to reduce memorization.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2]: "The KL penalty is applied per token using a reference model (the pretrained model before fine-tuning.) This prevents the fine-tuned model from generating a suffix that deviates too much from the reference language model."
  - [section 5.2]: Model remains fluent as measured by perplexity staying comparable.
  - [corpus]: Weak—neighbors discuss LoRA and other fine-tuning, but not PPO with KL.
- Break condition: If KL weight is too high, the policy may not change enough to reduce memorization; if too low, outputs may become incoherent.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Larger models converge faster and achieve higher dissimilarity scores because they have richer representations and can more easily learn paraphrasing strategies.
- Mechanism: With more parameters, the model can represent more diverse generation strategies, enabling faster learning of the dissimilarity policy.
- Core assumption: Model size correlates with representational capacity for paraphrasing and context understanding.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 5.2]: "As the size of the language model increases, the convergence rate becomes faster" and "the dissimilarity score increase."
  - [section 6]: "the model began to modify multiple words in the generated suffix in an attempt to increase dissimilarity."
  - [corpus]: Weak—no direct evidence in neighbors; inference based on empirical results.
- Break condition: If larger models overfit to the reward signal, they may sacrifice fluency or coherence despite high dissimilarity.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Pretrained GPT-Neo model (base LM)
  - Token-level MDP environment (prefix/suffix generation)
  - Reward function (negative SacreBLEU, BERTScore, or combination)
  - PPO optimizer with KL penalty
  - Value network (for advantage estimation)

- Critical path:
  1. Sample prefix from dataset
  2. Generate suffix with LM
  3. Compute reward (negative similarity)
  4. Update policy via PPO
  5. Repeat until convergence

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Reward function choice: SacreBLEU is fast but n-gram limited; BERTScore is richer but slower.
  - KL penalty weight: Balances fluency vs. dissimilarity.
  - Epochs: More epochs increase dissimilarity but may hurt fluency or cause degenerate outputs.

- Failure signatures:
  - Perplexity spikes: KL penalty too low or reward too aggressive.
  - No improvement in dissimilarity: Reward function not sensitive or optimization stuck.
  - Degenerate outputs (e.g., repeated words): KL penalty too low or reward mis-specified.

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Run PPO with negative SacreBLEU on GPT-Neo 125M for 4 epochs; monitor SacreBLEU and perplexity.
  2. Compare negative SacreBLEU vs. BERTScore as reward on the same model/size.
  3. Test longer context setting (prefix + 100 tokens) to see if framework robustness holds.

## Limitations

- The framework's effectiveness on larger models (e.g., trillion-parameter scale) and other model architectures is untested.
- SacreBLEU may not fully capture semantic similarity, potentially leaving some approximate memorization unaddressed.
- The KL penalty's optimal weight is heuristic, and the balance between fluency and dissimilarity is not systematically validated.

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The framework effectively reduces approximate memorization as measured by SacreBLEU scores, and larger models converge faster due to richer representations. The mechanism of using negative similarity as a reward signal is well-supported by the experimental results.
- **Medium Confidence**: The KL penalty ensures fluency by preventing drastic deviations from the pretrained LM. However, the exact balance between dissimilarity and fluency is heuristic and not systematically validated.
- **Low Confidence**: The framework's generalizability to other model architectures, datasets, or larger models is untested. The sensitivity of SacreBLEU to approximate memorization and the long-term stability of the learned policy are not fully established.

## Next Checks

1. **Sensitivity Analysis of KL Penalty**: Systematically vary the KL weight and evaluate its impact on both dissimilarity (SacreBLEU) and fluency (perplexity, human evaluation). Identify the optimal range that balances memorization mitigation and generation quality.

2. **Cross-Architecture Testing**: Apply the framework to a different model family (e.g., LLaMA or BLOOM) and a distinct dataset (e.g., C4 or Wikipedia) to assess generalizability. Compare convergence rates and final dissimilarity scores across architectures.

3. **Long-Term Stability Evaluation**: After fine-tuning, generate suffixes over multiple epochs and monitor for degradation in fluency or emergence of degenerate patterns (e.g., repeated phrases). This ensures the learned policy remains stable during deployment.
