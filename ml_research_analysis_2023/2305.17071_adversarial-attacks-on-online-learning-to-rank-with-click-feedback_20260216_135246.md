# Adversarial Attacks on Online Learning to Rank with Click Feedback

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17071
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17071
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: Attacks can misguide OLTR algorithms to choose target items T - o(T) times with cumulative cost o(T) under position-based and cascade click models.

## Executive Summary
This paper introduces the first adversarial attack strategies against online learning to rank (OLTR) algorithms using click feedback. The work addresses unique challenges posed by bounded binary feedback and combinatorial action spaces in OLTR. Three attack algorithms are developed: one for general UCB-based OLTR under the position-based click model, one specific to the cascade model, and a general probabilistic attack for arbitrary OLTR algorithms. The attacks achieve sublinear cumulative cost while successfully manipulating the recommendation system to favor target items.

## Method Summary
The paper develops three attack algorithms for OLTR with click feedback. The first uses conservative estimation against UCB-based algorithms under the position-based click model, maintaining timestamp-based lower bounds of target arm rewards. The second adapts the approach for cascade click models using regret-based analysis to handle partial feedback. The third provides a general probabilistic attack for arbitrary OLTR algorithms without knowledge of the underlying mechanism. All attacks work within the constraints of binary feedback while achieving sublinear cumulative attack costs.

## Key Results
- Attack algorithms successfully manipulate OLTR systems to choose target items T - o(T) times
- Cumulative attack cost remains sublinear at o(T) under all three click models
- Theoretical guarantees validated on both synthetic data and real MovieLens 20M dataset
- Attack success demonstrated across position-based and cascade click models

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Conservative estimation of the target arm enables attack success under bounded binary feedback
- Mechanism: The algorithm maintains a timestamp-based lower bound of the target arm's expected reward, updating it only when a feasible attack value exists. This ensures valid binary feedback while satisfying attack constraints.
- Core assumption: The difference between the conservative lower bound and the actual target arm reward is bounded by the confidence radius β(NL(t))
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our first result provides an attack strategy against the UCB algorithm on classical stochastic bandits with binary feedback, which solves the key issues caused by bounded and discrete feedback that previous works can not handle."
  - [section 3.2] "Compared with Eq. (1), Eq. (4) uses a more conservative lower bound of µL, µL(hat(t − 1)), instead of µL(t), on the right hand side of inequality. We call this conservative estimation of the target arm L with respect to arm at, where the estimated lower bound of L will only be updated when there exists feasible αt to ensure Eq. (1)."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; assumption based on theoretical construction
- Break condition: If the conservative estimate diverges significantly from the actual reward, or if the confidence radius becomes too large relative to the reward gap, the attack may fail to maintain the required inequality

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Position bias-corrected counters enable effective attacks in the position-based click model
- Mechanism: The attack algorithm uses position bias-corrected counters to compute UCB indices, allowing it to manipulate the empirical means of items in a way that favors the target item despite position-based examination probabilities
- Core assumption: The position bias-corrected counters accurately reflect the true expected rewards of items across different positions
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.1] "The PBM-UCB algorithm uses position bias-corrected counters other than simple click counters to compute UCB indices; we provide the theoretical analysis of our attack algorithm against PBM-UCB by carefully treating these unusual counters."
  - [section 4.1] "Since ai,t is chosen by the PBM-UCB algorithm, there must exist a ∈ a∗ with its UCB value less than that of ai,t"
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; assumption based on theoretical construction
- Break condition: If the position bias-corrected counters become inaccurate or if the examination probabilities change dynamically, the attack may fail to manipulate the UCB indices effectively

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Regret-based analysis enables attack success in the cascade model despite partial feedback
- Mechanism: The attack algorithm uses a regret-based approach to bound the expected number of recommendations of non-target items, ensuring the target item is recommended frequently while maintaining valid binary feedback
- Core assumption: The regret caused by recommending non-target items can be bounded by the expected number of recommendations and the reward gap with respect to the optimal list
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section 4.2] "To tackle this problem, we use a new regret-based analysis on the expected number of recommendations."
  - [section 4.2] "Based on the regret upper bound 12 logT /∆0 given in [5], we can get the upper bound of the expected number of recommendations is 12p∗∆20 log T."
  - [corpus] No direct evidence; assumption based on theoretical construction
- Break condition: If the regret bounds become too loose or if the reward gaps change dynamically, the attack may fail to maintain the required frequency of target item recommendations

  - Quick check question: How do the position-based and cascade click models differ in their feedback generation and censoring mechanisms?

  - Why needed here: The attack algorithms use conservative estimation of target arm rewards and regret-based analysis to handle the challenges of bounded binary feedback and partial feedback in OLTR

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Player (chooses item/list) -> Attack algorithm (observes, computes attack values) -> Feedback (pre-attack) -> Attack algorithm (returns post-attack feedback)
- Critical path: The critical path involves observing the player's chosen item/list and pre-attack feedback, computing the attack value(s) based on the specific attack algorithm and click model, ensuring the post-attack feedback is valid (binary), and returning the post-attack feedback to the player
- Design tradeoffs: The attack algorithms trade off between the effectiveness of the attack (how often the target item is chosen) and the cost of the attack (how much the feedback is manipulated). Conservative estimation and regret-based analysis are used to minimize the attack cost while maintaining attack success
- Failure signatures: Failure of the attack algorithms may manifest as the target item not being chosen frequently enough, or the attack cost becoming too high relative to the target item's frequency. This could indicate issues with the conservative estimation, regret bounds, or the click model assumptions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Implement the attack algorithm for stochastic bandits with binary feedback and test it on a simple 2-armed bandit problem, verifying that the target arm is chosen frequently while the attack cost remains sublinear
  2. Extend the attack algorithm to the position-based click model and test it on a small OLTR problem with known position-based examination probabilities, ensuring the target item is recommended frequently while the attack cost is minimized
  3. Adapt the attack algorithm to the cascade click model and test it on a small OLTR problem, verifying that the target item is recommended frequently despite the partial feedback and the attack cost remains sublinear

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the theoretical lower bound on cumulative attack cost for adversarial attacks on OLTR with binary click feedback?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "there is no known lower bound of the cumulative attack cost even for the stochastic bandit setting in literature, so it is unclear whether our attack strategies are (asymptotically) order-optimal."
- Why unresolved: Current theoretical analysis only provides upper bounds for attack costs, and no matching lower bounds exist to establish optimality
- What evidence would resolve it: Proving matching lower bounds on cumulative attack costs that match the O(log T) upper bounds established in the paper would demonstrate optimality

### Open Question 2
- Question: How do adversarial attacks perform under different OLTR feedback models like top-K feedback?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper states "One limitation of our work is that it cannot be applied to feedback models without clicks such as top-K feedback [13]."
- Why unresolved: The current attack strategies specifically exploit properties of click feedback and cannot be directly applied to non-click feedback models
- What evidence would resolve it: Designing and analyzing attack strategies that can successfully attack OLTR algorithms under top-K feedback or other non-click feedback models

### Open Question 3
- Question: How robust are OLTR algorithms to adversarial attacks when the attacker has incomplete knowledge of the algorithm?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The paper introduces "a general attack strategy that can misguide any OLTR algorithm without knowing the underlying algorithm" but notes it "may pay more cost (still sublinear) than the others since it cannot take advantage of the details of the algorithm."
- Why unresolved: While the general attack strategy exists, its performance relative to algorithm-specific attacks and the trade-off between attack cost and knowledge about the target algorithm remains unclear
- What evidence would resolve it: Comparative analysis of attack costs and success rates between algorithm-specific attacks and the general attack strategy across different OLTR algorithms and click models

## Limitations

- Theoretical analysis assumes known click model parameters and item quality distributions
- Cannot be applied to feedback models without clicks such as top-K feedback
- No known lower bounds on cumulative attack cost to establish optimality of the attack strategies

## Confidence

- High confidence: Theoretical bounds on attack cost (o(T)) and success rate (T-o(T)) for all three models
- Medium confidence: Practical effectiveness on synthetic data with uniform item quality distribution
- Low confidence: Performance on real-world data due to unspecified parameter extraction from MovieLens dataset
