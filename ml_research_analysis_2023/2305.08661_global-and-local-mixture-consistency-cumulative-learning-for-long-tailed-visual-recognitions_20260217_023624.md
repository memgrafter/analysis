# Global and Local Mixture Consistency Cumulative Learning for Long-tailed Visual Recognitions

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08661
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08661
- Reference count: 40
- Top-1 accuracy: 87.75% (CIFAR10-LT), 55.88% (CIFAR100-LT), 56.3% (ImageNet-LT)

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of long-tailed visual recognition by proposing a Global and Local Mixture Consistency (GLMC) framework that learns contrastive consistency between globally mixed and locally mixed augmented images. The method combines supervised classification with self-supervised mixture consistency learning in a single training stage, using MixUp and CutMix augmentations to generate two views whose representations are aligned via cosine similarity. A cumulative head-tail soft label reweighted loss progressively balances conventional and reweighted classification losses to mitigate head class bias.

## Method Summary
GLMC generates two augmented batches from the same input batch using global MixUp and local CutMix, then minimizes the cosine similarity between their projected representations as an auxiliary consistency loss. Simultaneously, it applies cumulative class-balanced reweighting where the conventional classification loss and reweighted classification loss are combined with a coefficient α that accumulates over epochs. This one-stage approach eliminates the need for separate representation learning and classifier fine-tuning stages while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Key Results
- Achieves 87.75% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR10-LT with imbalance factor 100
- Achieves 55.88% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR100-LT with imbalance factor 50
- Achieves 56.3% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-LT with imbalance factor 10
- Improves generalization on balanced ImageNet and CIFAR datasets

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Input → MixUp/CutMix augmentation → Encoder → Projection → Predictor → Cosine similarity (consistency) AND Input → MixUp/CutMix augmentation → Encoder → Classifier → Classification loss → Total loss

- Critical path: Augmentation → Encoder → Projection → Predictor → Cosine similarity (consistency) AND Augmentation → Encoder → Classifier → Classification loss → Total loss

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using both MixUp and CutMix provides complementary global and local consistency learning but increases computation
  - The cumulative rebalancing strategy gradually shifts focus from head to tail classes but requires careful scheduling
  - One-stage training simplifies the pipeline but may limit the maximum achievable performance compared to two-stage methods

- Failure signatures:
  - If accuracy on head classes drops significantly while tail classes improve, the rebalancing may be too aggressive
  - If overall accuracy plateaus early, the mixture consistency weight γ may be too high or too low
  - If training becomes unstable, the stop-gradient implementation may be incorrect

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Train with only the supervised loss (no mixture consistency, no rebalancing) to establish baseline performance
  2. Add mixture consistency loss with γ=0 (only MixUp or only CutMix) to test individual augmentation effectiveness
  3. Add cumulative rebalancing with α=1 (only reweighted loss) to test rebalancing impact

## Limitations
- Limited comparison with recent state-of-the-art long-tail methods like BBN, LDAM, and DROC
- Lack of ablation studies on the relative contributions of MixUp versus CutMix
- No comprehensive analysis of performance across different imbalance factors

## Confidence

- **High confidence**: The overall framework design and the use of cosine similarity for mixture consistency learning are well-established techniques adapted appropriately to the long-tailed setting.
- **Medium confidence**: The empirical results on CIFAR and ImageNet-LT are promising, but the lack of comparison to recent state-of-the-art methods limits confidence in the claimed superiority.
- **Low confidence**: The generalization claims to balanced datasets are based on a single experiment and need more thorough validation across multiple datasets and imbalance factors.
