# Mathematical conjecture generation using machine intelligence

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.07277
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07277
- Reference count: 7
- Introduces framework for generating mathematical conjectures about strict inequalities using machine intelligence and geometric gradient descent

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a novel framework for generating mathematical conjectures about strict inequalities using machine intelligence. The authors formalize the space of inequalities as a Banach manifold and study its symmetries, showing that it admits free group actions. They propose an algorithmic pipeline using geometric gradient descent that incorporates these symmetries to sample new conjectures. As proof of concept, they generate conjectures involving the prime counting function and diameters of Cayley graphs of non-abelian simple groups. Some conjectures were proved, while others remain open. The approach demonstrates the potential for AI-driven discovery of meaningful mathematical inequalities.

## Method Summary
The framework consists of defining a conjecture space C as an open subset of a Banach space R, studying its linear automorphisms and free group actions, and using geometric gradient descent with an invariant metric to sample new conjectures. The oracle algorithm takes a compact feature space bD and a parameterization P mapping parameters θ to conjectures (f_θ, g_θ) ∈ C. The loss function measures violation of f < g, and the algorithm uses gradient descent with a Riemannian metric informed by the conjecture space symmetries to find parameters yielding valid conjectures.

## Key Results
- The conjecture space C is proven to be a Banach manifold isomorphic to an open subset of the Banach space R = C(bD) × C(bD)
- Linear automorphisms of C form a 3-parameter Lie subgroup G of GL(2,R), providing symmetry structure for efficient search
- Geometric gradient descent using invariant metric M_θ exploits the conjecture space symmetries to efficiently sample new conjectures
- Generated conjectures involving prime counting functions and Cayley graph diameters were both proved and remain open, demonstrating the method's potential

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: The conjecture space C is a Banach manifold isomorphic to an open subset of the Banach space R = C(bD) × C(bD), enabling geometric gradient descent.
- Mechanism: By proving C is open in R and R is a Banach space, the authors can endow C with a Banach manifold structure, allowing the use of Riemannian optimization techniques.
- Core assumption: The compact domain bD ensures continuity and boundedness properties needed for the Banach space structure.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "associate them with a vector space. By geometerising this space, which we refer to as a conjecture space, we prove that this space is isomorphic to a Banach manifold."
  - [section 2.1]: Detailed proof showing C is open in R using supremum norm properties.
- Break condition: If bD is not compact, the supremum norm may not be well-defined, breaking the Banach space structure.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Linear automorphisms of C form a 3-parameter Lie subgroup G of GL(2,R), providing symmetry structure for efficient search.
- Mechanism: The authors identify G = T ⋊ H where T are dilations and H are specific 2x2 matrices, then show G acts freely on C, allowing quotient space reduction.
- Core assumption: The specific form of H (with p+q≠1) ensures it leaves C invariant while not fixing any points.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "study its linear automorphisms... show that this space admits several free group actions."
  - [section 2.2]: Construction of G and proof that it's a 3-parameter subgroup.
  - [section 2.5]: Proof that G, T, H, J1, J2 are Lie groups.
- Break condition: If H contained elements that fixed points in C, the free action property would fail, invalidating the quotient approach.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Geometric gradient descent using invariant metric M_θ exploits the conjecture space symmetries to efficiently sample new conjectures.
- Mechanism: The oracle uses gradient descent on the parameter space Θ with a metric informed by the invariances of the conjecture space, specifically the free group actions.
- Core assumption: The loss function L is invariant under the group G, making gradient descent on the quotient space well-defined.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract]: "propose an algorithmic pipeline to generate novel conjectures using geometric gradient descent, where the metric is informed by the invariances of the conjecture space."
  - [section 3]: Algorithm description showing L(c_θ) is invariant and M_θ is a Riemannian metric.
- Break condition: If the loss function is not properly invariant under G, the gradient descent may not converge to valid conjectures.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  bD (compact subset of R^n) -> Θ (open connected subset of R^m) -> P(θ) (parameterization) -> (f_θ, g_θ) ∈ C (conjecture space) -> L(c_θ) (loss function) -> M_θ (Riemannian metric)

- Critical path:
  1. Initialize random θ
  2. Compute c_θ = P(θ)
  3. Evaluate loss L(c_θ)
  4. Compute gradient ∇L(θ)
  5. Update θ ← θ - η M_θ^(-1) ∇L(θ)
  6. Repeat until L ≤ tol or max epochs reached

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Using Banach manifold structure enables geometric methods but requires compact bD
  - Free group actions reduce search space but limit the types of symmetries exploitable
  - Linear parameterization P(θ) is simple but may miss nonlinear patterns
  - Invariant loss function ensures valid conjectures but may slow convergence

- Failure signatures:
  - Loss not decreasing: Could indicate poor initialization or metric M_θ not aligned with conjecture structure
  - Generated conjectures not meaningful: May need richer function class F_{d1,d2} or better parameterization P
  - Algorithm stuck in local minima: Could benefit from multiple random restarts or different initialization

- First 3 experiments:
  1. Test on simple inequality conjectures like f(x) = x^2 < g(x) = x^2 + 1 to verify basic functionality
  2. Generate conjectures about π(ab) vs π(a) + π(b) to validate on number theory case study
  3. Attempt to reproduce known conjectures about Cayley graph diameters to test group theory application

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: What is the largest subgroup of GL(2, R) that leaves the space of conjectures C invariant?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors discuss subgroups T and H that leave C invariant and ask what the largest such subgroup is
- Why unresolved: The authors prove that if A ∈ GL(2, R) leaves C invariant, then A can be decomposed as A = BC where B ∈ T and C ∈ H, but they don't prove this is the largest possible subgroup
- What evidence would resolve it: A proof that no larger subgroup of GL(2, R) leaves C invariant would resolve this

### Open Question 2
- Question: What are the invariants of free group actions on the conjecture space?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] The authors mention this as an open direction and note that further work is needed to understand invariants of free group actions on C
- Why unresolved: The authors only identify some free group actions on C (by T, J1, and J2) but don't characterize all possible free group actions or their invariants
- What evidence would resolve it: A complete characterization of all free group actions on C and their invariants would resolve this

### Open Question 3
- Question: Can the conjecture generation framework be extended to more complex function classes and optimization problems?
- Basis in paper: [inferred] The authors note that their case studies involve low-dimensional optimization problems and suggest that including more complex machine architectures would lead to more difficult optimization problems
- Why unresolved: The authors haven't implemented the oracle in the proposed geometric setting for more complex function classes
- What evidence would resolve it: Implementing the oracle with more complex function classes (e.g. neural networks) and demonstrating its effectiveness would resolve this

## Limitations

- Limited empirical validation: The sample size of generated conjectures is relatively small (4 in case study 1, 3 in case study 2), making it difficult to assess the method's reliability.
- Domain restrictions: The approach relies on compact domains bD and specific function classes F_{d1,d2}, which may struggle with conjectures involving discontinuous functions or non-compact domains.
- Proof verification: The paper does not detail the proof methodology or provide systematic validation of generated conjectures, making it unclear how conjecture generation quality relates to proof likelihood.

## Confidence

**High confidence**: The mathematical foundations regarding Banach manifolds, group actions, and Lie groups appear rigorous based on the proofs provided in sections 2.1-2.5. The geometric gradient descent framework is well-defined and theoretically sound.

**Medium confidence**: The algorithmic implementation and case study results are convincing for the specific examples chosen, but broader applicability remains to be demonstrated. The effectiveness of the symmetry-exploiting approach needs validation on more diverse conjecture types.

**Low confidence**: Claims about the general utility of this framework for mathematical discovery are largely aspirational at this stage, given the limited empirical evaluation and lack of comparison with alternative approaches.
