# Monotonicity for AI ethics and society: An empirical study of the monotonic neural additive model in criminology, education, health care, and finance

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.07060
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07060
- Reference count: 10
- Key outcome: Monotonic Neural Additive Models (MNAMs) enforce monotonicity constraints to improve fairness and interpretability in high-stakes domains, reducing violations without sacrificing accuracy.

## Executive Summary
This paper addresses fairness concerns in AI systems by introducing Monotonic Neural Additive Models (MNAMs), which enforce both individual and pairwise monotonicity constraints in neural networks. The authors demonstrate that MNAMs significantly reduce monotonicity violations compared to traditional models while maintaining competitive accuracy across four real-world datasets from criminology, education, health care, and finance. The approach represents a fairness-aware machine learning solution that integrates domain knowledge about monotonic relationships into model architecture.

## Method Summary
The method introduces Monotonic Neural Additive Models (MNAMs) that extend Neural Additive Models (NAMs) by integrating monotonicity constraints through regularization terms. The approach uses a hybrid training algorithm that starts with standard NAM training and gradually increases penalty weights for monotonicity violations until constraints are satisfied. The method enforces both individual monotonicity (each feature's effect on the outcome should be monotonic) and pairwise monotonicity (relative importance of features should respect domain knowledge). The training process iteratively adjusts penalty parameters λ and η to balance accuracy with constraint satisfaction.

## Key Results
- MNAMs significantly reduce monotonicity violations compared to FCNNs and NAMs across all four datasets
- Models maintain competitive accuracy (classification accuracy and AUC) while enforcing fairness constraints
- Visualizations confirm monotonic behavior in marginal effects, demonstrating improved interpretability
- The hybrid training approach successfully finds solutions that satisfy monotonicity constraints without severe accuracy degradation

- Evidence anchors: Domain examples include criminal justice system and temporal weighting; corpus lacks specific pairwise monotonicity evidence

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Hybrid training balances accuracy with fairness through gradual constraint enforcement
- Mechanism: Iterative process starts with NAM (λ=0, η=0) and increases penalties until monotonicity violations vanish
- Core assumption: Gradual constraint enforcement allows finding solutions balancing accuracy and fairness
- Evidence anchors: Algorithm 1 specifies the two-step procedure; corpus lacks specific evidence about this approach
- Break condition: Iterative process fails to converge or accuracy degrades severely

## Limitations

- Empirical evaluation uses relatively small sample sizes (6,500 to 150,000 instances) that may not represent complexity of high-stakes domains
- Comparison limited to only two baseline models (FCNN and NAM), restricting generalizability of performance claims
- Lacks extensive ablation studies to isolate effects of individual vs. pairwise monotonicity constraints

## Confidence

- High confidence: Theoretical framework and hybrid training algorithm are well-specified and mathematically sound
- Medium confidence: Empirical results showing reduced monotonicity violations, limited by dataset diversity and baseline comparisons
- Medium confidence: Claim that MNAMs improve fairness without significant accuracy trade-offs, pending broader validation
