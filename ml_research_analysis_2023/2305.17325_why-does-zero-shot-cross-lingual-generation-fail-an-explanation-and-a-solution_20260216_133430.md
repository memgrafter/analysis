# Why Does Zero-Shot Cross-Lingual Generation Fail? An Explanation and a Solution

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.17325
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17325
- Reference count: 25
- Key outcome: Reduces accidental translation problem by 68% and improves ROUGE-L score by 1.5 on average

## Executive Summary
This paper investigates why zero-shot cross-lingual generation often produces outputs in the wrong language, a phenomenon the authors term the "accidental translation problem." Through systematic experiments, they demonstrate that fine-tuning on a single source language increases the cosine similarity between sentence representations across languages (XLRS), which helps classification tasks but harms generation tasks. The authors propose using an auxiliary source language to implicitly regularize XLRS and a checkpoint selection method based on XLRS that doesn't require target language development data. Their approach significantly improves generation quality across three tasks while reducing accidental translation by 68%.

## Method Summary
The method involves fine-tuning a multilingual T5-base model on English data for generation tasks, then using XLRS (cosine similarity between sentence representations) to monitor and control the learning process. The key innovation is using an auxiliary source language during training to prevent XLRS from becoming too high. The authors also propose selecting model checkpoints based on XLRS between source and target languages rather than requiring target language development data. They evaluate on summarization, story completion, and title generation tasks using datasets like WikiLingua and MTG benchmark.

## Key Results
- Reduces accidental translation problem by 68% across target languages
- Improves ROUGE-L score by 1.5 on average across generation tasks
- XLRS-based checkpoint selection performs better than random selection
- Multi-source training (English + auxiliary) outperforms single-source training in 13 out of 15 zero-shot directions

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Fine-tuning on a single source language increases the cosine similarity between sentence representations of different languages (XLRS).
- Mechanism: During fine-tuning, the model optimizes for cross-entropy loss on the source language while simultaneously minimizing the distance between parallel sentences across languages. This dual optimization causes representations to converge, increasing XLRS.
- Core assumption: The model's training objective implicitly encourages language-invariant representations to improve classification performance.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "We show that the fine-tuning process learns language invariant representations, which is beneficial for classification tasks but harmful for generation tasks."
  - [section 4] "We plot the average cosine similarity between representations of parallel sentences (XLRS) for each training iteration in two classification tasks: POS tagging and paraphrase identification (PAWS-X) at Figure 1 and Figure 2, respectively. In both tasks, the plot displays an increasing trend of XLRS between parallel sentences between English and all the other languages."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - the corpus neighbors don't directly address XLRS mechanics but discuss cross-lingual transfer broadly.
- Break condition: If the model architecture or training objective explicitly prevents representation convergence, or if the source and target languages are sufficiently distant that shared representations are impossible.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Increased XLRS degrades zero-shot cross-lingual generation performance because generation requires language-specific outputs.
- Mechanism: In generation tasks, the model must predict different words in different languages. High XLRS causes the model to produce similar outputs regardless of language, resulting in incorrect language generation or code-switching.
- Core assumption: Generation can be viewed as classification over a large vocabulary where different languages require different label distributions.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we show that the fine-tuning process learns language invariant representations, which is beneficial for classification tasks but harmful for generation tasks."
  - [section 5] "In a generation task, if the model maps the source and the target into identical representations, the model predicts the next word to be the same. Even if this is correct in semantics and possibly results in the code-switched results as shown in Figure 1, the model fails to generate in the correct language."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss cross-lingual transfer but don't specifically address XLRS degradation in generation.
- Break condition: If the generation task requires similar outputs across languages (e.g., generating numbers or named entities), or if the model uses explicit language identification mechanisms.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Using an auxiliary source language implicitly regularizes XLRS, preventing it from becoming too high and improving generation quality.
- Mechanism: The presence of a second source language with different linguistic properties creates conflicting optimization pressures, preventing the model from over-converging representations across all languages.
- Core assumption: Different source languages provide complementary regularization effects that balance the representation learning process.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "we propose a simple method to regularize the model from learning language invariant representations and a method to select model checkpoints without a development set in the target language, both resulting in better generation quality."
  - [section 6.1] "We observe that adding Chinese to English data improves the performance in 13 out of 15 zero-shot directions compared to only using English. We point out that our improvement is not due to an increase in the amount of training data since we used the same amount of training data for all experiments."
  - [corpus] Weak evidence - corpus papers discuss multi-source approaches but don't specifically address XLRS regularization.
- Break condition: If the auxiliary language is too similar to the primary source language, or if the model architecture prevents effective multi-language training.

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: Multilingual pre-trained model (mT5-base) -> Fine-tuning pipeline with source language data -> XLRS computation module (cosine similarity between sentence representations) -> Multi-source training support -> Checkpoint selection logic based on XLRS

- Critical path: Source language data → Model fine-tuning → XLRS monitoring → Checkpoint selection → Target language evaluation

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Single source vs. multi-source training: Single source maximizes source language performance but increases XLRS; multi-source provides regularization but may reduce source language performance
  - XLRS threshold selection: Too high causes generation failures; too low may hurt classification performance
  - Checkpoint selection criteria: XLRS-based vs. source language performance vs. target language development set

- Failure signatures:
  - XLRS approaching 1.0: Model likely generating in wrong language
  - XLRS staying constant: Model not learning cross-lingual transfer
  - Performance gap between source and target languages: XLRS may be too high or too low
  - Degraded source language performance: Multi-source regularization may be too strong

## Limitations

- The causal relationship between XLRS and generation quality, while mechanistically plausible, remains somewhat indirect and could have alternative explanations
- The proposed solution of using an auxiliary source language shows promise but raises questions about scalability and generalizability to more distant language pairs
- The checkpoint selection method based on XLRS lacks direct comparison against simpler alternatives like random selection or source language performance-based selection

## Confidence

**High Confidence**: The observation that fine-tuning on a single source language increases XLRS between parallel sentences is well-supported by the presented experiments and visualizations. The degradation of zero-shot cross-lingual generation performance when XLRS becomes too high is also convincingly demonstrated through multiple experiments showing accidental translation problems.

**Medium Confidence**: The claim that XLRS regularization through multi-source training improves generation quality is supported by empirical results but could have alternative explanations. While the paper rules out increased training data as the cause of improvement, it does not fully explore other potential mechanisms such as improved lexical coverage or better handling of language-specific phenomena.

**Low Confidence**: The assertion that the proposed checkpoint selection method based on XLRS consistently outperforms random selection or source language-based selection lacks direct comparison. The paper demonstrates that XLRS-based selection works in practice but does not establish it as superior to simpler alternatives.

## Next Checks

1. **Ablation Study on XLRS Contribution**: Design an experiment that explicitly manipulates XLRS independently of multi-source training to isolate its causal effect on generation quality. This could involve training models with different regularization strengths or using adversarial training to control XLRS while keeping other factors constant.

2. **Cross-Lingual Pair Generalization Test**: Evaluate the proposed method across a wider range of language pairs, including very distant languages (e.g., English-Japanese) and languages with different scripts. This would test whether the benefits of XLRS regularization generalize beyond the Indo-European language pairs used in the main experiments.

3. **Alternative Checkpoint Selection Comparison**: Implement and compare XLRS-based checkpoint selection against simpler methods such as random selection, source language performance-based selection, and ensemble methods. This would establish whether the additional complexity of XLRS monitoring provides meaningful improvements over more straightforward approaches.
