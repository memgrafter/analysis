# sustain.AI: a Recommender System to analyze Sustainability Reports

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.08711
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08711
- Reference count: 17
- Primary result: BERT-based recommender system outperforms baselines by >10 percentage points in MAP for matching sustainability report text to regulatory requirements

## Executive Summary
sustain.AI is a BERT-based recommender system that automatically matches text passages from sustainability reports to relevant regulatory requirements. The system leverages weighted random sampling to handle class imbalance and is trained end-to-end on German reporting datasets. On two novel datasets—GRI (92 documents, 89 requirements) and DNK (1779 documents, 33 requirements)—the model consistently outperforms strong baselines by over 10 percentage points in mean average precision. The tool is publicly available and improves auditing efficiency by quickly identifying relevant segments for specific GRI indicators, supporting both auditors and the general public in analyzing complex sustainability reports.

## Method Summary
The system uses a BERT-based encoding module with a multi-label classification head to match text segments from sustainability reports to regulatory requirements. During training, weighted random sampling addresses class imbalance by oversampling relevant segments. The model is trained end-to-end on German sustainability report datasets, jointly optimizing BERT parameters and the classification layer. Evaluation metrics include Mean Sensitivity (MS) and Mean Average Precision (MAP) for top-3 and top-5 recommendations.

## Key Results
- sustain.AI outperforms strong baselines by over 10 percentage points in mean average precision on both GRI and DNK datasets
- Weighted random sampling enables faster training convergence and improves MAP by 3 percentage points on the GRI dataset
- End-to-end training of BERT and MLP components yields better performance than freezing BERT embeddings

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
BERT provides contextual embeddings that capture semantic meaning beyond surface-level term matching, allowing the model to understand regulatory requirements that share similar terminology but differ in scope. This works because the German BERTBASE model has been trained on sufficient domain-specific language from sustainability reports.

### Mechanism 2
Weighted random sampling (WRS) counteracts class imbalance by oversampling relevant segments during training, ensuring the model sees sufficient positive examples to learn matching patterns effectively. This mechanism works because only 9% of segments in GRI data are relevant to any requirement, making uniform sampling ineffective.

### Mechanism 3
End-to-end training of BERT and MLP components allows the model to adapt contextual embeddings specifically to the sustainability reporting domain. This works because the domain-specific language in sustainability reports differs sufficiently from general German text that adaptation improves performance.

## Foundational Learning

- **Multi-label classification**: Each text segment can be relevant to multiple regulatory requirements simultaneously. Quick check: If a paragraph discusses both energy consumption and emissions, how should it be labeled for requirements related to environmental impact?

- **Binary Cross Entropy loss**: Each requirement is treated as an independent binary classification problem. Quick check: Why can't we use softmax activation with categorical cross-entropy for this multi-label problem?

- **Weighted random sampling**: Addresses class imbalance by altering the probability of selecting relevant segments during training. Quick check: How does WRS alter the probability of selecting a segment during training?

## Architecture Onboarding

- **Component map**: PDF Parser -> Text Preprocessing -> BERT Encoder -> MLP Classifier -> Ranking System -> Recommendation Engine

- **Critical path**: 1. Parse document → 2. Preprocess segments → 3. Encode with BERT → 4. Classify with MLP → 5. Rank and recommend

- **Design tradeoffs**: End-to-end training vs frozen BERT (fine-tuning improves domain adaptation but requires more data); segment granularity (larger segments capture more context but may mix relevant and irrelevant content); number of recommendations (higher K increases recall but may include more irrelevant segments)

- **Failure signatures**: Low MAP but high MS (model finding relevant segments but poor ranking); poor performance on GRI vs DNK (struggles with sparse annotations and longer documents); degradation on new reports (overfitting to training data structure or vocabulary)

- **First 3 experiments**: 1) Compare end-to-end training vs frozen BERT on a small subset; 2) Test different segment sizes to optimize context vs noise tradeoff; 3) Implement and compare uniform sampling vs weighted random sampling

## Limitations
- Evaluation relies on a single German-language dataset (DNK) with full annotations, limiting generalization to other regulatory frameworks or languages
- GRI dataset has sparse annotations (9% relevance rate) that could introduce bias in the model's understanding of requirement matching
- System's performance on entirely unseen regulatory requirements remains untested as all evaluation requirements were present during training

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: Architectural design combining BERT with multi-label classification is sound; quantitative improvements over baselines (>10 percentage points in MAP) are well-documented; weighted random sampling benefits are empirically demonstrated
- **Medium Confidence**: Claim that end-to-end fine-tuning improves performance assumes sufficient domain-specific language exists in training data; magnitude of gains relative to training data size warrants caution
- **Low Confidence**: System's robustness to document structure variations untested; ability to handle requirements not present during training unverified; potential bias from weighted random sampling unaddressed

## Next Checks

1. **Cross-Regulatory Transfer Test**: Evaluate the model on sustainability reports from a different regulatory framework (e.g., SASB) to assess generalization beyond German DNK requirements

2. **Annotation Sparsity Sensitivity**: Systematically vary annotation density in the GRI dataset (e.g., 5%, 15%, 25%) to determine minimum annotation threshold required for reliable performance

3. **OOV Requirement Handling**: Test the model's ability to match text segments to completely new regulatory requirements not present during training, measuring both recall and precision to understand generalization limits
