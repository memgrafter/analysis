# Rationalization for Explainable NLP: A Survey

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2301.08912
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08912
- Reference count: 19
- Primary result: Survey of rationalization techniques in NLP from 2007-2022, introducing Rational AI as a new subfield

## Executive Summary
This survey systematically analyzes rationalization techniques in NLP from 2007-2022, introducing Rational AI (RAI) as a new subfield. The paper categorizes rationalization into abstractive and extractive methods, finding that extractive approaches are more common due to their simplicity and reliability. Key insights include the observation that rationalization techniques enable models to justify predictions through natural language explanations, making them more accessible to non-expert users.

The survey identifies critical challenges including lack of standardized evaluation metrics, limited dataset diversity, and insufficient human-centered evaluations. The authors propose integrating human-centered evaluations with NLP Assurance to improve model trustworthiness and transparency. The survey also highlights that rationalization methods can potentially be applied beyond NLP to fields like Computer Vision and Speech.

## Method Summary
The authors conducted a comprehensive literature review of 33 peer-reviewed papers on rationalization techniques across NLP tasks including Machine Reading Comprehension, Commonsense Reasoning, Natural Language Inference, Fact-Checking, Sentiment Analysis, Text Classification, and Neural Machine Translation. The methodology involved systematic literature review with inclusion-exclusion criteria, categorization by NLP tasks, and analysis of techniques. Papers were selected based on their focus on rationalization, explanation, or justification in the NLP domain from 2007-2022, with particular attention to explanation models, XAI metrics, datasets, and code availability.

## Key Results
- Extractive rationalization methods are more common than abstractive methods due to their simplicity and reliability
- Rationalization enables models to justify predictions through natural language explanations, improving accessibility for non-expert users
- The field faces significant challenges including lack of standardized evaluation metrics and insufficient human-centered evaluations

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Rationalization techniques provide human-comprehensible explanations by generating natural language rationales that justify model predictions.
- Mechanism: The approach leverages natural language generation capabilities of modern language models (BERT, GPT-2, T5) to produce textual explanations that non-experts can understand without specialized domain knowledge.
- Core assumption: Natural language explanations are inherently more accessible and interpretable to non-experts than numerical or visualization-based methods.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Rationalization justifies a model's output by providing a natural language explanation (rationale)."
  - [section] "Rationalization justifies a model's output by providing a natural language explanation. This is accomplished by either extracting text fragments from the input (extractive rationalization) or by generating a novel explanation (abstractive rationalization)."
- Break condition: If generated explanations become too technical or require domain expertise to understand, the accessibility advantage is lost.

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Extractive rationalization works by identifying and selecting the most salient input features as rationales, similar to extractive summarization techniques.
- Mechanism: The approach uses scoring algorithms to identify and extract the most important text fragments from the input that are sufficient to justify the model's prediction, while ignoring irrelevant information.
- Core assumption: The most important input features for prediction can be reliably identified and extracted as rationales that provide sufficient justification.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "In extractive rationalization, important features or sentences from the input data are extracted as rationales to support the prediction."
  - [section] "In extractive summarization, meaningful sentences are extracted to form a summary of the original text while still retaining the overall subject matter."
- Break condition: If the extracted rationales don't capture the full reasoning process or if they're insufficient to explain the prediction, the approach fails.

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Abstractive rationalization leverages the power of advanced language models to generate novel explanations that go beyond simple extraction.
- Mechanism: The approach uses transformer-based language models to generate new sentences that explain the prediction, potentially paraphrasing existing content or creating new explanations using novel words and phrases.
- Core assumption: Modern language models can generate coherent, accurate, and faithful explanations that capture the model's reasoning process.
- Evidence anchors:
  - [section] "Abstractive rationalization is a generative task in which novel sentences are generated using new words or paraphrasing existing sentences."
  - [section] "Abstractive rationalization is still relatively new, with limited research available. However, there have been promising and pioneering approaches."
- Break condition: If generated explanations are inaccurate, hallucinated, or fail to capture the true reasoning process, the approach becomes unreliable.
