# A Scalable and Adaptive System to Infer the Industry Sectors of Companies: Prompt + Model Tuning of Generative Language Models

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2306.03313
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03313
- Authors: 
- Reference count: 8
- Primary result: >80% accuracy and high recall in mapping millions of companies to industry sectors using prompt + model tuning

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a production system that maps millions of companies to industry sectors using a prompt + model tuning approach with a medium-sized T5-based generative language model. The system addresses challenges of scarce, imbalanced, and evolving annotations, noisy textual features, and high inference demands. Deployed for over a year, it serves hundreds of PE professionals with high precision and recall, particularly in lower-level sectors, while scaling to daily re-inference of over 100 million companies.

## Method Summary
The system standardizes a sector framework and employs annotation attribution to propagate labels up the hierarchy, then balances the dataset via augmentation. A T5-based generative language model is trained using a novel co-tuning approach that starts with prompt tuning for parameter efficiency, then jointly tunes prompt and model parameters. The pipeline uses Airflow orchestration with daily inference triggered by company selection rules, publishing results via Kafka to the investment platform.

## Key Results
- Achieves >80% accuracy and strong recall across industry sectors
- Outperforms baselines even with relatively small PLM size
- Successfully scales to daily re-inference of 100M+ companies
- Maintains high precision and recall in lower-level sectors over two-year deployment

## Why This Works (Mechanism)

### Mechanism 1
- Claim: Prompt tuning preserves pre-trained knowledge while being more parameter-efficient than full model tuning
- Mechanism: By prepending soft prompts and freezing PLM weights, the model retains general language understanding while adapting to sector-specific mapping with fewer parameters
- Core assumption: Pre-trained T5 has sufficient sectoral and business domain knowledge to generalize to unseen sectors
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions generative methods can predict sectors beyond predefined ones; section notes generative models can harness pre-learned knowledge to avoid overfitting on smaller datasets
- Break condition: If pre-trained model lacks domain-specific knowledge, prompt tuning may underperform full model tuning

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: Co-tuning prompt and model parameters leverages both prompt efficiency and model adaptation
- Mechanism: Start with prompt tuning for t' steps, then jointly tune both prompt and model weights to refine task-specific adaptation and general language knowledge
- Core assumption: Joint tuning after prompt initialization leads to better convergence than either method alone
- Evidence anchors: Abstract mentions co-tuning PLM from later stage of prompt tuning; section states this approach outperforms all compared methods
- Break condition: If learning rate schedule or warm-up phases are poorly tuned, co-tuning may lead to unstable training or catastrophic forgetting

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: Annotation attribution and augmentation address data scarcity and imbalance
- Mechanism: Propagate annotations up sector hierarchy to include more sectors, then balance dataset via EDA (synonym replacement, insertion, swapping, deletion)
- Core assumption: Hierarchically related sectors share sufficient semantic similarity to justify annotation propagation
- Evidence anchors: Abstract explicitly mentions annotation attribution and sample balancing via augmentation; section discusses algorithm to collect eligible sectors and augmenting samples for minority sectors
- Break condition: If hierarchy is too flat or propagated annotations are semantically distant, this could introduce noise and degrade performance

## Foundational Learning

- Concept: Hierarchical sector framework and annotation attribution
  - Why needed here: Sector framework is tree-structured with lower-level sectors lacking annotations; attribution enables leveraging parent sector data to train on more sectors
  - Quick check question: How does annotation attribution algorithm determine which sectors are eligible for modeling?

- Concept: Generative vs discriminative modeling for few-shot learning
  - Why needed here: With scarce labels, generative models can leverage pre-trained knowledge to predict unseen sectors, whereas discriminative models may overfit
  - Quick check question: What is the key advantage of generative models over discriminative ones in the context of few-shot learning?

- Concept: Prompt tuning and its parameter efficiency
  - Why needed here: Model needs to scale to millions of companies with scarce labels; prompt tuning offers parameter-efficient alternative to full model tuning
  - Quick check question: How many parameters are typically tuned in prompt tuning versus full model tuning?

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map: BigQuery → Annotation attribution → Sample balancing → Training → T5 Large PLM → Prompt + Model Tuning → QA checks → GCS release → Company selection → Sector inference → Kafka → Investment platform
- Critical path: Annotation attribution → Prompt + Model Tuning → Inference → Kafka publish
- Design tradeoffs:
  - Small PLM + prompt tuning vs Large PLM + full tuning: Parameter efficiency vs raw performance
  - Daily vs real-time inference: Scalability vs freshness of predictions
  - Hierarchical vs flat sector framework: Granularity vs annotation scarcity
- Failure signatures:
  - Low precision in fine-grained sectors: May indicate annotation scarcity or noisy text features
  - Inference latency spikes: May indicate inefficient company selection or unoptimized Dataflow pipeline
  - Model QA failures: May indicate poor training data quality or incorrect sector framework definitions
- First 3 experiments:
  1. Benchmark prompt tuning vs model tuning on small subset of sectors to validate mechanism 1
  2. Test annotation attribution on toy hierarchy to ensure correct propagation of annotations
  3. Run incremental inference on sample of companies to verify company selection logic and overall pipeline

## Open Questions the Paper Calls Out

### Open Question 1
- Question: How does the system handle conflicts between inferred sectors and human-curated annotations when they differ significantly?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper mentions system triggers re-inference when textual features change, but doesn't discuss conflict resolution between automated inferences and manual annotations
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on system performance and scalability but doesn't detail process of reconciling discrepancies between automated predictions and human judgments
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed documentation of conflict resolution process, including examples of how discrepancies are identified, resolved, and whether there's feedback loop to improve model accuracy

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the long-term impact of annotation attribution algorithm on diversity and granularity of sector framework?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper discusses annotation attribution process but doesn't explore its long-term effects on sector framework's diversity or granularity
- Why unresolved: While algorithm ensures sectors with sufficient annotations are included, it doesn't address whether this approach might lead to narrowing of sector framework over time
- What evidence would resolve it: Longitudinal studies tracking changes in sector framework's diversity and granularity over time, including metrics on emergence of new sectors and decline of others

### Open Question 3
- Question: How does the system ensure privacy and security of company data used for sector inference?
- Basis in paper: [explicit] Paper doesn't mention any specific measures for data privacy and security
- Why unresolved: Paper focuses on technical aspects but doesn't address ethical and legal implications of handling sensitive company information
- What evidence would resolve it: Comprehensive overview of data privacy and security protocols in place, including compliance with relevant regulations and measures to protect against data breaches

## Limitations

- Data quality and domain specificity concerns due to reliance on proprietary data from single PE firm without external validation
- Scalability claims lack detailed resource utilization metrics and operational details under peak loads
- Evaluation methodology gaps with missing benchmark comparisons and detailed breakdown by sector hierarchy levels
