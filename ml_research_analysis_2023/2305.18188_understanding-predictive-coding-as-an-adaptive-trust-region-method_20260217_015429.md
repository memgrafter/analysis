# Understanding Predictive Coding as an Adaptive Trust-Region Method

## Quick Facts
- arXiv ID: 2305.18188
- Source URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18188
- Reference count: 40
- Primary result: PC can train deeper networks with saddle-inducing activations significantly faster than BP

## Executive Summary
This paper presents a theoretical framework interpreting predictive coding (PC) as an adaptive trust-region optimization method that uses second-order information. The key insight is that PC's inference dynamics can be viewed as solving a trust-region problem on the backpropagation loss landscape, where the trust region is defined by the Fisher information matrix of the generative model. This creates a different energy landscape geometry that allows PC to escape saddle points faster than traditional backpropagation. The theory predicts and experimental results confirm that PC shows faster convergence in deep networks with certain activation functions while maintaining robustness to weight perturbations near minima.

## Method Summary
The method involves implementing predictive coding with two phases: inference dynamics that minimize a free energy function using local second-order information (defined by the Fisher information matrix), and learning dynamics that update weights based on gradients of this free energy. The core implementation requires solving the inference dynamics to equilibrium before each weight update, using a second-order Taylor expansion of the free energy. The approach was tested on synthetic regression tasks, MNIST classification with deep linear networks, and shallow networks with various activation functions (ReLU, Tanh, linear), comparing performance against standard backpropagation using SGD with grid-searched learning rates.

## Key Results
- PC escapes saddle points faster than BP in shallow networks due to reshaped landscape geometry with smaller Hessian eigenvalues at saddles
- PC shows slower convergence near minima but greater robustness to weight perturbations compared to BP
- PC can train deeper networks with saddle-inducing activations (Tanh) significantly faster than BP, though both methods fail on very deep chains (L=15) likely due to vanishing/exploding gradients

### Mechanism 2
- Claim: PC escapes saddle points faster than BP due to reshaped landscape geometry
- Mechanism: The equilibrated PC energy landscape has smaller Hessian eigenvalues at saddle points compared to the BP loss, making saddles more "unstable" and accelerating escape in gradient-based dynamics
- Core assumption: The local curvature around saddle points determines convergence speed
- Evidence anchors:
  - [abstract] "Our theory suggests that PC should escape saddles faster than BP"
  - [section] "Because the equilibrated energy has smaller Hessian eigenvalues than the loss at the saddle (Eq. 16), PC will be simultaneously less attracted to and more repelled from it than BP"

### Mechanism 3
- Claim: PC shows slower convergence near minima but greater robustness to weight perturbations
- Mechanism: The PC energy landscape has flatter minima than the BP loss landscape, causing slower convergence but increased stability to weight perturbations near optima
- Core assumption: Flatter minima correspond to increased robustness
- Evidence anchors:

## Architecture Onboarding

- Component map:
  - Free energy function (Eq 1) -> Hierarchical Gaussian generative model
  - Inference dynamics (Eq 2) -> Minimizes free energy w.r.t. neural activities
  - Learning dynamics (Eq 3) -> Updates weights based on gradient of free energy
  - Trust region solver -> Implicit in the inference step through Fisher information matrix

- Critical path: Inference → Weight update → Repeat
  - The inference step must reach equilibrium before weight updates occur
  - Each weight update depends on the current inference equilibrium

- Design tradeoffs:
  - Computational cost: PC requires solving inference dynamics to equilibrium, which is more expensive than BP's single forward/backward pass
  - Biological plausibility: PC uses only local information, making it more biologically plausible than BP
  - Convergence properties: PC may escape saddles faster but converge slower near minima

- Failure signatures:
  - Slow training with vanishing gradients - likely due to high learning rates or insufficient inference steps
  - No improvement in deep networks with ReLU - suggests saddle-breaking activations may not benefit from PC's properties
  - Divergence in highly overparameterized networks - may indicate need for more inference iterations or adaptive learning rates

- Why unresolved: The paper does not explore the behavior of PC in networks deeper than 10 layers, leaving the question of how PC's trust-region adaptation scales with depth unanswered.
- What evidence would resolve it: Experiments showing PC's performance and saddle-escaping behavior in networks with more than 10 layers, comparing it to BP.

### Open Question 2
- Question: What is the precise mechanism by which the Fisher information matrix defines the trust region geometry in predictive coding, and how does this change across different activation functions?

- Why unresolved: While the paper suggests that the Fisher information matrix plays a crucial role, it does not provide a detailed analysis of how this mechanism varies with different activation functions or network architectures.
- What evidence would resolve it: Detailed studies showing the relationship between Fisher information, activation functions, and trust region geometry in various network configurations.

## Limitations
- The theory relies on second-order Taylor approximations that may break down in deep networks with highly non-linear activations
- Empirical validation is limited to relatively shallow networks (up to 5 layers), with theoretical advantages for deeper architectures remaining unproven
- Comparison with BP uses grid search over learning rates but doesn't account for potential differences in optimal learning rate schedules between methods

## Confidence

- **High Confidence**: The mathematical derivation of PC as a trust-region method is rigorous and internally consistent
- **Medium Confidence**: The empirical results on shallow networks demonstrate the predicted saddle-escaping behavior
- **Low Confidence**: The extrapolation to deep networks and the claim of significant practical advantages over BP in realistic scenarios
